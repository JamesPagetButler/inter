## Paper text

The theory of condensed mathematics by Dustin Clausen and Peter Scholze claims that topological spaces should be replaced by the definition of condensed sets. The main purpose of this paper is to investigate in which way the theory of homotopy groups on topological spaces can be extended to the theory of condensed sets. We show that there exist functors from the category of condensed sets to the
category of pro-groups, s.t. restriction to CW-spaces coincides with the ordinary notion of homotopy groups on topological spaces. These functors can be extended to the $\infty$-categories of condensed anima and pro-anima. On our way to these results we will prove that the compact projective objects in the category of condensed sets are given by the extremally disconnected profinite sets.

Definition and Proposition 1.1.2: Stone-Čech compactification To every topological space X X , one can assign a compact Hausdorff space β ​ X \beta X and a continuous map ι X : X → β ​ X \iota_{X}\colon X\rightarrow\beta X satisfying the following universal property: For every compact Hausdorff space K K and every continuous map f : X → K f\colon X\rightarrow K there exists a unique continuous map

Corollary 1.1.3: Every limit of compact Hausdorff spaces is compact Hausdorff.

Lemma 1.1.4: Compact Hausdorff spaces are closed under finite coproducts and retracts.

Definition 1.1.5: Totally and extremally disconnected 1. A topological space is called totally disconnected if all connected subspaces are singletons. 2. A topological space is called extremally disconnected if the closure of every open subset is open.

Remark 1.1.6: Hausdorff extremally disconnected spaces are totally disconnected. Indeed for any two points in a connected subspace, one can find disjoint open neighbourhoods. Then, the closure of such an open neighbourhood still does not contain the other point and is a proper open and closed subset, in contradiction to the connectedness. Hence, the connected subspace cannot contain more than one
point.

Proposition 1.1.7: Let X X be a compact Hausdorff space. Then the following are equivalent. (i) The space X X is extremally disconnected. (ii) For any surjective continuous map f : Y → X f\colon Y\rightarrow X with Y compact Hausdorff, there exists a continuous section. (iii) For any map X → Z X\rightarrow Z and surjective map Y → Z Y\rightarrow Z of compact Hausdorff spaces, there exists a
continuous map X → Y X\rightarrow Y s.t. the diagram X {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X} Y {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y} Z {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z} commutes.

Definition 1.2.1: Small functors Let 𝒞 \mathcal{C} be a possibly large category. A functor F : 𝒞 → 𝒮 ​ et F\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et} is said to be small if it is the left Kan extension of some presheaf with a small domain. Lemma 1.2.2: The small functors are precisely given by the small colimits of representables.

Remark 1.2.3: The totality of small presheaves on a fixed category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} forms an ordinary category which is cocomplete, i.e. it contains all small colimits, as small colimits of small colimits are still small. colimits.

Warning 1.2.4: Dealing with large categories Throughout this master thesis, we will consider large categories and want, amongst other things, to work with their presheaf categories. In order to handle set theoretic problems arising from this consideration in a simple way, we will implicitly assume functors to be small. For our purposes, it is enough to know that there is a way to deal with these
problems. Nevertheless, we will mention the existence of set theoretic problems at relevant points and refer to some possibility of solving this issue.
