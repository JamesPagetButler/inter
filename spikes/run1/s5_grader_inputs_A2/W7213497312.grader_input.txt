## Paper text

We construct an involutive duality on condensed spectra with locally compact homotopy groups, extending classical Pontryagin duality. We prove that dualization induces Pontryagin duality on homotopy groups, with degrees reversed, and that the canonical biduality map is an equivalence. In particular, every ordinary spectrum, viewed as a discrete condensed spectrum, satisfies biduality, without any
finiteness assumptions on its homotopy groups.

1 Introduction This result addresses a difficulty already identified by Brown and Comenetz at the outset of their theory. In their 1974 account, they explicitly set aside the natural topology on the character groups and explained that their inability to incorporate it led to finiteness assumptions in most of their results [ 2 , Introduction] . The issue was already present with ℝ / ℤ
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} as the character group: its topology was forgotten in passing to spectra. Condensed spectra allow us to retain this topology and establish biduality for locally compact homotopy groups. 1.1 Motivation and statement of the main result Pontryagin duality and Brown–Comenetz duality are two instances of the same principle: an object can sometimes be recovered from its characters
into a suitable dualizing object. The purpose of this paper is to construct a common generalization of these two dualities in the category of condensed spectra. Let A A be a locally compact abelian group. Its Pontryagin dual is A ^ = Hom cont ⁡ ( A , 𝕋 ) , \widehat{A}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}\left(A,\mathbb{T}\right), endowed with the compact-open topology, where 𝕋 = ℝ / ℤ
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} is the circle group. The Pontryagin duality theorem states that A ^ \widehat{A} is again locally compact and that the evaluation map d A : A → A ^ ^ d_{A}:A\to\widehat{\widehat{A}} is an isomorphism. Moreover, Pontryagin duality exchanges compact and discrete abelian groups. We use the formulation in [ 9 , Theorem 4.1(ii)–(iii)] . If topological abelian groups are
regarded as condensed abelian groups, then the condensed internal Hom recovers the compact-open topology: for a compactly generated Hausdorff topological abelian group A A and a Hausdorff topological abelian group B B , there is a natural isomorphism Hom ¯ Cond ⁡ ( Ab ) ​ ( A , B ) ≅ Hom cont ⁡ ( A , B )
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}(A,B)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(A,B) of condensed abelian groups, where the right-hand side is the condensed abelian group associated with the compact-open topology [ 9 , Proposition 4.2] . Thus, Pontryagin duality can be written intrinsically in condensed mathematics as A ^ = Hom ¯ Cond ⁡ ( Ab ) ​ ( A , 𝕋 ) .
\widehat{A}=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}\left(A,\mathbb{T}\right). For finite abelian groups, Pontryagin duality reduces to the usual character duality A ↦ Hom ⁡ ( A , ℚ / ℤ ) , A\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}), since every character has finite image and 𝕋 tors ≅ ℚ / ℤ \mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} . There is a parallel
construction in stable homotopy theory. Since ℚ / ℤ \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} is injective in Ab \mathrm{Ab} , it admits an injective lift, relative to the standard t t -structure on spectra, to a spectrum I ℚ / ℤ I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}} . In Lurie’s terminology, this follows from the classification of injective objects in a stable category with a t t -structure [ 7 , Definition C.5.7.2, Proposition
C.5.7.3, and Theorem C.5.7.4] . We write X ^ := Hom ¯ Sp ​ ( X , I ℚ / ℤ ) \widehat{X}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Sp}}\left(X,I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}\right) for the Brown–Comenetz dual of a spectrum X X . The resulting spectrum satisfies π n ​ X ^ ≅ Hom ¯ Ab ​ ( π − n ​ X , ℚ / ℤ )
\pi_{n}\widehat{X}\cong\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Ab}}\left(\pi_{-n}X,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\right) naturally in every spectrum X X and every integer n n . If all homotopy groups of X X are finite, the evaluation map X ⟶ X ^ ^ X\longrightarrow\widehat{\widehat{X}} is an equivalence; this is the Brown–Comenetz duality theorem [ 8 , Theorem 7.4.1] . Passing to condensed spectra allows us
to remove this finiteness hypothesis. For the duality constructed below, every ordinary spectrum, regarded as a discrete condensed spectrum, is canonically equivalent to its double dual. Its homotopy groups may be arbitrary abelian groups: their discrete topology makes them locally compact, while their duals carry the compact topology supplied by Pontryagin duality.
