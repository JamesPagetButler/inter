# Restatement

## Abstract

We compare homological and homotopical epimorphisms of associative algebras in monoidal $\infty$-categories. Assume that geometric realizations and pushouts of associative algebras exist and that tensoring preserves geometric realizations. Then every homological epimorphism is homotopical. If, in addition, the ambient category is additive and tensoring preserves finite coproducts, the converse holds. This extends the result of Chuang and Lazarev for dg-algebras. In the symmetric monoidal setting, we discuss consequences for centers and commutative refinements, as well as applications to derived analytic geometry.

## Introduction

Let $f:A\to B$ be a morphism of associative algebras in a monoidal $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$. The canonical maps
$$B\ast_{A}B\longrightarrow B,\qquad B\otimes_{A}B\longrightarrow B$$
give rise to two notions of epimorphism. We call $f$ *homotopical* if the first map is an equivalence and *homological* if the second is an equivalence. Here $\ast_{A}$ denotes the pushout of associative algebras and $\otimes_{A}$ the relative tensor product of modules.

Chuang and Lazarev prove that these notions agree for unbounded dg-algebras [14, Theorem 4.4] and ask whether this equivalence reflects a general categorical phenomenon [14, Remark 4.2].

We show that the equivalence can fail for associative algebras in spaces, equipped with the cartesian monoidal structure (see Remark 4.4). Theorem 3.8 establishes the equivalence in additive monoidal $\infty$-categories, provided the relevant algebra pushouts and geometric realizations exist and tensoring preserves finite coproducts and geometric realizations.

Our motivation comes from bornological algebra and analytic geometry. See [3, 4, 5, 8, 12] for the bornological approach to derived analytic geometry, and [7, 8, 10, 11, 13, 19, 20] for further developments. In derived analytic geometry, localizations are naturally expressed by a tensor idempotence condition
$$B\widehat{\otimes}_{A}^{\mathbb{L}}B\simeq B.$$
This condition occurs, for instance, in the homological description of affinoid, dagger, and Stein localizations [3, 4, 8, 12, 13]. See Section 4 for a more detailed account.

On the other hand, in the noncommutative geometric framework of [6], localization is formulated intrinsically in the category of associative algebras through the condition
$$B\ast_{A}B\simeq B.$$
The equivalence between the pushout and tensor conditions is a key input in that work. Extending this localization formalism from the algebraic to the analytic setting therefore requires understanding when the two conditions agree. Establishing this comparison in a general monoidal setting is the main goal of this paper.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects the preliminaries on algebras, modules, central structures, square-zero extensions, and derivations. Section 3 proves our main theorem. The proof that homotopical epimorphisms are homological epimorphisms is inspired by Silver's argument for ordinary rings [32]: we construct a relative derivation with values in $B\otimes_{A}B$ and show that its triviality forces relative multiplication to be an equivalence. Section 4 discusses the cotangent complex, examples motivating the theorem, and geometric applications. Appendix A gives an alternative proof under the additional assumption that $\mathcal{C}$ is closed.

## Notation

We follow the conventions for simplicial sets and $\infty$-categories established in [24]. Let $\Delta$ denote the simplex category, and let $\mathbf{sSet}=\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set})$ denote the category of simplicial sets. For each $n\geq 0$, we write $\Delta^{n}$ for the standard $n$-simplex and, for $0\leq i\leq n$, we denote by $\Lambda_{i}^{n}\hookrightarrow\Delta^{n}$ the inclusion of the $i$-th horn. A simplicial set $\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}$ is an $\infty$-category, or equivalently a weak Kan complex, if every inner horn in $\mathcal{X}_{\bullet}$ admits a filler, that is, if it has the right lifting property with respect to $\Lambda_{i}^{n}\hookrightarrow\Delta^{n}$ whenever $0<i<n$; see [24, Definition 1.1.2.4]. Given two objects $x,y\in\mathcal{X}_{0}$, we denote by $\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{X}}(x,y)$ the corresponding mapping space, in the sense of [24, Definition 1.2.2.1]. For an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, we write $X\in\mathcal{C}$ to mean that $X$ is an object of $\mathcal{C}$. We denote by $\mathbf{Cat}$ the $\infty$-category of small $\infty$-categories. Finally, if two objects $x,y\in\mathcal{C}$ are equivalent, we write $x\simeq y$.

An $\infty$-category is called *additive* if it has finite biproducts and its canonical commutative monoid structures on mapping spaces are group-like. In particular, subtraction of morphisms is defined coherently. Additivity of $\mathcal{C}$ does not by itself assert that a chosen tensor product preserves these biproducts; this compatibility will always be stated separately. All fibers, pullbacks, and mapping spaces below are understood in the $\infty$-categorical sense. Throughout this paper, we use cohomological conventions for complexes.

## Section 2: Preliminaries

**Definition 2.1.** A *monoidal $\infty$-category* is a coCartesian fibration of $\infty$-operads
$$p:\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}.$$
Its underlying $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$ is the fiber over the unique color of $\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}$. The operadic Segal condition identifies the fiber over $\langle n\rangle$ with $\mathcal{C}^{n}$. CoCartesian transport along the ordered binary multiplication and the nullary operation determines the tensor product $\otimes:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}$ and its unit $1_{\mathcal{C}}$ (see [23, §4.1.1]). A *symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category* is a coCartesian fibration of $\infty$-operads $\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\mathrm{sym}}\to\mathbf{Fin}_{*}$ (see [23, Definition 2.0.0.7]). It determines a monoidal $\infty$-category by the pullback
$$\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}=\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}\times_{\mathbf{Fin}_{*}}\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\mathrm{sym}}.$$

**Definition 2.2.** For a monoidal $\infty$-category $p:\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}$, define
$$\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{inert}}_{/\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}}(\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes},\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}),$$
the full subcategory of the functor $\infty$-category over $\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}$ spanned by functors preserving inert morphisms. Thus its objects are inert-preserving sections of $p$, called *associative algebra objects* in $\mathcal{C}$.

**Example 2.3.** For a commutative dg-algebra $A$ over a commutative ring $k$, let $\mathbf{Mod}_{A}$ denote the derived $\infty$-category of dg $A$-modules, obtained by inverting quasi-isomorphisms and equipped with the derived tensor product. Then $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathbf{Mod}_{A})$ is modeled by associative dg $A$-algebras with quasi-isomorphisms inverted. If $\mathbf{Sp}$ denotes spectra with the smash product, $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathbf{Sp})$ is the $\infty$-category of associative ring spectra.

**Lemma 2.4.** Let $(\mathcal{C},\otimes)$ be a monoidal $\infty$-category with countable coproducts, and assume that $\otimes$ preserves countable coproducts in each variable. Then the forgetful functor $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}$ admits a left adjoint, called the *free algebra functor*.

**Lemma 2.5.** Let $F^{\otimes}:\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}$ be a lax monoidal functor between monoidal $\infty$-categories, and let $F:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}$ be the underlying functor. Assume that:
- $F$ is fully faithful;
- for every $X,Y\in\mathcal{C}$, the lax monoidal structure map $F(X)\otimes F(Y)\longrightarrow F(X\otimes Y)$ is an equivalence.

Then the induced functor $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{D})$ is fully faithful.

**Remark 2.6.** We do not assume that the unit map $1_{\mathcal{D}}\longrightarrow F(1_{\mathcal{C}})$ is an equivalence. Thus $F$ need not be strongly monoidal: under the hypotheses of the lemma, the only possible failure of strong monoidality occurs at the unit.

**Notation 2.7.** Let $\mathcal{L}\mathrm{Mod}^{\otimes}$ and $\mathcal{R}\mathrm{Mod}^{\otimes}$ denote the left- and right-module $\infty$-operads of [23, §4.2.1]. Their canonical input orders determine maps to $\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}$: the module input is last in a left-module operation and first in a right-module operation. We write
$$\mathcal{L}\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{inert}}_{/\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}}(\mathcal{L}\mathrm{Mod}^{\otimes},\mathcal{C}^{\otimes})$$
and define $\mathcal{R}\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{C})$ analogously using $\mathcal{R}\mathrm{Mod}^{\otimes}$. Here the superscript $\mathrm{inert}$ denotes the full subcategory spanned by functors preserving inert morphisms (see [23, Example 4.2.1.16]). Restriction along the algebra-color inclusion $\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}\mathrm{Mod}^{\otimes}$ gives $\mathcal{L}\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$, and similarly for right modules.

**Definition 2.8.** Let $A\in\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$. The $\infty$-category of left $A$-modules, denoted ${}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}$, is the fiber over $A$ of the canonical functor $\mathcal{L}\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$. Similarly, the $\infty$-category of right $A$-modules, denoted $\mathbf{Mod}_{A}$, is the fiber over $A$ of $\mathcal{R}\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$.

**Notation 2.9.** Let $\mathcal{B}\mathrm{Mod}^{\otimes}$ denote the bimodule $\infty$-operad of [23, §4.3.1]. Its canonical map to $\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}$ records the input order: the left algebra inputs precede the module input, and the right algebra inputs follow it. We set
$$\mathcal{B}\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{inert}}_{/\mathrm{Assoc}^{\otimes}}(\mathcal{B}\mathrm{Mod}^{\otimes},\mathcal{C}^{\otimes});$$
see [23, Example 4.3.1.15].

**Remark 2.10.** The bimodule operad contains the left- and right-module operads and two copies of the associative operad, selecting the two algebra objects [23, Remarks 4.3.1.9–4.3.1.10]. Bimodules can also be described as right-module objects in the category of left modules, using its right $\mathcal{C}$-action [23, Theorem 4.3.2.7].

**Proposition 2.11.** The forgetful functors ${}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}_{A}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{A}$ and ${}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}_{A}\to{}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}$ are conservative.

**Proposition 2.12.** Let $(\mathcal{C},\otimes)$ be a monoidal $\infty$-category, and let $A\in\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$. Assume that $\mathcal{C}$ admits geometric realizations of simplicial objects and that $\otimes$ preserves geometric realizations separately in each variable. Then there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathbf{Alg}({}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}_{A})\simeq\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})_{A/},$$
where ${}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}_{A}$ is endowed with its monoidal structure given by the relative tensor product $\otimes_{A}$.

**Definition 2.13.** Let $B\in\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$. We define the *center space* of $B$ as
$$\mathcal{Z}(B)={\rm Map}_{{}_{B}\mathbf{Mod}_{B}}(B,B).$$
If $\mathcal{C}$ is closed symmetric monoidal and admits totalizations of cosimplicial objects, the *center object* (or just *center*) of $B$ is defined by
$$\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(B)=\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{{}_{B}\mathbf{Mod}_{B}}(B,B),$$
the internal endomorphism object of $B$ viewed as a $B$-$B$-bimodule.

**Remark 2.14.** Under the hypotheses ensuring the enriched mapping objects above, and assuming that the relative tensor product commutes with geometric realizations, the center object carries a canonical $E_{2}$-algebra structure: it is the endomorphism object of the monoidal unit $B$ in $({}_{B}\mathbf{Mod}_{B},\otimes_{B})$. See [23, Theorem 5.3.1.30] and Dunn additivity [23, Theorem 5.1.2.2]. More generally, if $\mathcal{C}$ is presentable symmetric monoidal and its tensor product preserves small colimits separately, the center of an $E_{k}$-algebra exists and carries a canonical $E_{k+1}$-algebra structure, see [23, Corollary 5.3.1.15].

**Definition 2.15.** Let $A\in{\mathbf{C}}\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$, and suppose that the relative tensor products defining the symmetric monoidal category $(\mathbf{Mod}_{A},\otimes_{A})$ exist. A *central $A$-algebra* is an associative algebra object of $(\mathbf{Mod}_{A},\otimes_{A})$. Such an object has an underlying associative algebra $B$ in $\mathcal{C}$, and its unit induces an associative algebra morphism $f:A\to B$. This construction defines a natural functor
$$\Phi_{A}:\mathbf{Alg}(\mathbf{Mod}_{A})\longrightarrow\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})_{A/}.$$
For a specified associative algebra morphism $f:A\to B$, a *central structure* on $f$ is an object $\widetilde{B}\in\mathbf{Alg}(\mathbf{Mod}_{A})$ together with an equivalence $\Phi_{A}(\widetilde{B})\simeq(f:A\to B)$ in $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})_{A/}$.

**Example 2.16.** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic zero, and put
$$A=k[t],\qquad B=k[x]\otimes_{k}\Lambda_{k}(\epsilon),\qquad |t|=|x|=0,\quad|\epsilon|=-1,$$
with zero differentials. Fix the associative algebra morphism $f:A\longrightarrow B$, $t\longmapsto 0$. For $\lambda\in\{0,1\}$, define a dg $A$-algebra $C_{\lambda}$ by adjoining generators $x,\epsilon,h$, with
$$|x|=0,\qquad|\epsilon|=|h|=-1,\qquad d(x)=d(\epsilon)=0,\quad d(h)=t.$$
The element $t$ is central, and the remaining relations are
$$x\epsilon=\epsilon x,\qquad\epsilon^{2}=h^{2}=0,\qquad h\epsilon=-\epsilon h,\qquad hx-xh=\lambda\epsilon.$$
The differential respects these relations. Since $t$ is central, each $C_{\lambda}$ is an associative algebra object of $\mathbf{Mod}_{A}$. The relations give
$$C_{\lambda}\cong A[x]\oplus A[x]\epsilon\oplus A[x]h\oplus A[x]\epsilon h$$
as graded $A$-modules. Thus, as complexes,
$$C_{\lambda}\cong B\otimes_{k}\bigl(k[t]\otimes_{k}\Lambda_{k}(h),\ d(h)=t\bigr).$$
It follows that the inclusion $i_{\lambda}:B\longrightarrow C_{\lambda}$ is a quasi-isomorphism of dg $k$-algebras. Moreover, the structure map $A\to C_{\lambda}$ is homotopic, as an associative algebra morphism, to the composite $A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{i_{\lambda}}C_{\lambda}$. Indeed, $A=k[t]=k\langle t\rangle$ is free as an associative algebra on one degree-zero generator, and $d(h)=t$ supplies the required nullhomotopy of its image. Thus, after choosing this homotopy, we obtain an equivalence $\Phi_{A}(C_{\lambda})\simeq(f:A\to B)$ in $\mathbf{Alg}(D(k))_{A/}$. Both $C_{0}$ and $C_{1}$ therefore define central structures on the same morphism $f$.

These central structures are inequivalent. To see this, apply derived extension of scalars along $A\to k$, $t\mapsto 0$. Each $C_{\lambda}$ is a bounded complex of free $A$-modules, so
$$k\otimes_{A}^{\mathbb{L}}C_{\lambda}\simeq C_{\lambda}/(t).$$
These quotient dg-algebras have zero differential. For $\lambda=0$, the quotient is graded-commutative. For $\lambda=1$, it satisfies
$$hx-xh=\epsilon\neq 0,$$
and is therefore not graded-commutative. Their graded homology algebras are therefore nonisomorphic, and $C_{0}$ and $C_{1}$ cannot be equivalent in $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathbf{Mod}_{A})$.

**Lemma 2.17.** Let $B\in\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$, and assume that the enriched bimodule endomorphism object $\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(B)=\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{{}_{B}\mathbf{Mod}_{B}}(B,B)$ exists. Regard it as an associative algebra by composition. There is a canonical morphism of associative algebras
$$e_{B}:\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(B)\longrightarrow B,$$
whose underlying morphism is
$$\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(B)\simeq\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(B)\otimes 1_{\mathcal{C}}\xrightarrow{{\rm Id}\otimes\eta_{B}}\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(B)\otimes B\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}}B.$$
We call $e_{B}$ *evaluation at the unit of $B$*.

**Proposition 2.18.** Assume that $\mathcal{C}$ is presentable symmetric monoidal, satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma 2.17, and that the tensor product commutes with small colimits in each variable. For $A\in{\mathbf{C}}\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$ and $f:A\to B$, the space of central structures on $f$ is equivalent to the homotopy fiber at $f$ of
$${\rm Map}_{\operatorname{Alg}_{E_{2}}(\mathcal{C})}(A,\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(B))\longrightarrow{\rm Map}_{\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})}(A,B),\qquad g\longmapsto e_{B}g.$$
Here $A$ is regarded as an $E_{2}$-algebra by restriction of its commutative structure.

**Proposition 2.19.** The functor $\Phi_{A}$ identifies $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathbf{Mod}_{A})$ with the $\infty$-category of $A$-algebras equipped with central structures. The multiplication of such an algebra is $B\otimes_{A}B\longrightarrow B$ in $\mathbf{Mod}_{A}$, and its underlying multiplication in $\mathcal{C}$ is obtained by precomposing with $B\otimes B\to B\otimes_{A}B$.

**Definition 2.20.** Let $B\in\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$ and $M\in{}_{B}\mathbf{Mod}_{B}$, and write $p_{M}:B\oplus M\longrightarrow B$ for the augmentation of the split square-zero extension. A *derivation* of $B$ into $M$ is a section of $p_{M}$ in $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$. Its space is
$$\operatorname{Der}(B,M)=\operatorname{fib}_{{\rm Id}_{B}}\left({\rm Map}_{\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})}(B,B\oplus M)\xrightarrow{p_{M}\circ-}{\rm Map}_{\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})}(B,B)\right).$$
Thus a derivation consists of an algebra morphism $s:B\to B\oplus M$ together with an identification $p_{M}s\simeq{\rm Id}_{B}$. For an algebra morphism $f:A\to B$, an *$A$-relative derivation* is a section of $p_{M}$ in $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})_{A/}$. Its space is
$$\operatorname{Der}_{A}(B,M)=\operatorname{fib}_{{\rm Id}_{B}}\left({\rm Map}_{\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})_{A/}}(B,B\oplus M)\xrightarrow{p_{M}\circ-}{\rm Map}_{\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})_{A/}}(B,B)\right).$$
The zero derivation is the canonical section $z_{B}:B\to B\oplus M$.

**Proposition 2.21 (Addition of derivations).** The spaces $\operatorname{Der}(B,M)$ and $\operatorname{Der}_{A}(B,M)$ carry canonical grouplike $E_{\infty}$-structures, naturally in the coefficient bimodule $M$. Their zero elements are the zero sections, and their addition is induced by addition in $M$.

## Section 3: Homological and homotopical epimorphisms

**Definition 3.1.** A morphism $f:A\to B$ in $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$ is said to be:
- a *homotopical epimorphism* if the canonical morphism $B\ast_{A}B\to B$ is an equivalence;
- a *homological epimorphism* if the canonical morphism $B\otimes_{A}B\to B$ is an equivalence of $B$-bimodules.

**Lemma 3.2.** Let $f:A\to B$ be an associative algebra morphism, and suppose that the pushout $B\ast_{A}B$ exists. The following conditions are equivalent:
1. The morphism $f$ is a homotopical epimorphism.
2. For every $R\in\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$, restriction induces a $(-1)$-truncated map
$$f^{*}:{\rm Map}_{\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})}(B,R)\longrightarrow{\rm Map}_{\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})}(A,R).$$
3. For every $A$-algebra $R$, the space ${\rm Map}_{\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})_{A/}}(B,R)$ is either empty or contractible. In particular, whenever a specified algebra morphism $A\to R$ extends along $f$, its space of extensions is contractible.

**Proposition 3.3.** Let $f:A\to B$ be a morphism in $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$. The following conditions are equivalent:
- the restriction of scalars functor $\mathbf{Mod}_{B}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{A}$ is fully faithful;
- the restriction of scalars functor ${}_{B}\mathbf{Mod}\to{}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}$ is fully faithful;
- the restriction of scalars functor ${}_{B}\mathbf{Mod}_{B}\to{}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}_{A}$ is fully faithful.

Equivalently, each of these conditions holds if and only if $f$ is a homological epimorphism.

**Lemma 3.4.** Let $f:A\to B$ be a homological epimorphism in $\mathbf{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$. Then the restriction of scalars functor $U_{f}:{}_{B}\mathbf{Mod}_{B}\to{}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}_{A}$ is lax monoidal and the canonical natural transformation
$$U_{f}(M)\otimes_{A}U_{f}(N)\longrightarrow U_{f}(M\otimes_{B}N)$$
is an equivalence.

**Remark 3.5.** The lax monoidal unit of $U_{f}$ is $A\to B$, which need not be an equivalence. This is exactly the situation covered by Lemma 2.5.

**Lemma 3.6.** Assume that $\mathcal{C}$ is additive and that tensoring preserves finite coproducts. If $f:A\to B$ is homotopical, then for every $B$-bimodule $M$ the space $\operatorname{Der}_{A}(B,M)$ is contractible. In particular, every relative derivation is homotopic to the zero section as an algebra map under $A$.

**Lemma 3.7.** Now suppose also that $\mathcal{C}$ is additive and that tensoring preserves finite coproducts separately. Let $f:A\to B$, and put $M=B\otimes_{A}B$, with its outer $B$-bimodule structure. Define
$$\sigma_{1}:\;B\simeq B\otimes_{A}A\xrightarrow{{\rm Id}_{B}\otimes_{A}f}B\otimes_{A}B,\qquad \sigma_{2}:\;B\simeq A\otimes_{A}B\xrightarrow{f\otimes_{A}{\rm Id}_{B}}B\otimes_{A}B.$$
There exists an $A$-relative derivation, represented by an algebra section $s:B\longrightarrow B\oplus M$, whose underlying $A$-bimodule morphism is
$$\delta:=\pi_{M}s\simeq\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}:B\longrightarrow M.$$
If $\delta$ is nullhomotopic in ${\rm Map}_{{}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}_{A}}(B,M)$, then the multiplication $\mu_{B/A}:B\otimes_{A}B\longrightarrow B$ is an equivalence. In particular, this holds if $s$ is homotopic to the zero section as an algebra morphism under $A$.

**Theorem 3.8.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a monoidal $\infty$-category admitting geometric realizations, and suppose that tensoring preserves geometric realizations separately in each variable. Suppose also that the algebra pushout $B\ast_{A}B$ exists for the morphism $f:A\to B$ under consideration.
1. Every homological epimorphism $f$ is homotopical.
2. If $\mathcal{C}$ is additive and tensoring preserves finite coproducts separately in each variable, then $f$ is homotopical if and only if it is homological.

**Corollary 3.9.** A homological epimorphism $f:A\to B$ induces a morphism of composition monoids
$$\mathcal{Z}(A)\longrightarrow\mathcal{Z}(B).$$
In particular, this holds for homotopical epimorphisms under the hypotheses of Theorem 3.8 (2).

**Corollary 3.10.** Assume that $\mathcal{C}$ is presentable symmetric monoidal and that tensoring preserves small colimits separately. A homological epimorphism $f:A\to B$ induces a morphism of $E_{2}$-algebras
$$\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(A)\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(B).$$
The induced map on center spaces, obtained by applying ${\rm Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(1_{\mathcal{C}},-)$, agrees with the map constructed in Corollary 3.9. Thus the same conclusion holds for homotopical epimorphisms when $\mathcal{C}$ is additive.

**Corollary 3.11.** Assume that $\mathcal{C}$ is presentable symmetric monoidal and tensoring preserves small colimits separately. Let $f:A\to B$ be a homological epimorphism, and fix a commutative structure on $A$ refining its associative structure. Then $B$ admits a commutative refinement such that $f$ lifts to a morphism of commutative algebras. The space of such refinements and lifts, with their identifications with the given associative morphism, is contractible.

**Remark 3.12.** Corollary 3.11 extends a classical theorem of Silver. If $f:A\to B$ is an epimorphism in the category of unital associative rings and $A$ is commutative, then $B$ is commutative [32, Corollary 1.2].

**Remark 3.13.** Under the hypotheses of Corollary 3.10, the enriched center is the Hochschild cochain object
$$\underline{\mathcal{Z}}(A)\simeq\underline{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(A)=\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{{}_{A}\mathbf{Mod}_{A}}(A,A).$$
Corollary 3.10 shows that homological epimorphisms induce morphisms $\underline{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(A)\to\underline{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(B)$ of $E_{2}$-algebras.

## Section 4: Cotangent complex, examples, and applications

**Corollary 4.1.** Under the hypotheses fixed on $\mathcal{C}$, the following are equivalent:
1. $f$ is homotopical;
2. $f$ is homological;
3. $\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{nc}}_{B/A}\simeq 0$.

**Remark 4.2.** The associative cotangent complex $\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{nc}}_{B/A}$ represents associative derivations with values in arbitrary $B$-bimodules. It should be distinguished from Illusie's commutative cotangent complex $\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{comm}}$, which governs commutative deformation theory. Vanishing of the commutative cotangent complex does not imply that a morphism is a homotopical epimorphism. For example, let $k$ be a field and $B=k\times k$. The diagonal map $k\to B$ is finite étale, hence $\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{comm}}_{B/k}\simeq 0$, whereas
$$\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{nc}}_{B/k}\simeq{\rm Ker}\bigl(B\otimes_{k}B\xrightarrow{\mu}B\bigr)\neq 0.$$
Indeed, writing $e_{1},e_{2}$ for the standard idempotents of $B$, this kernel is spanned by $e_{1}\otimes e_{2}$ and $e_{2}\otimes e_{1}$. Thus $k\to B$ is not homological and, by Theorem 3.8, is not homotopical.

**Example 4.3.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be simplicial abelian groups with weak equivalences inverted and the derived tensor product. Dold–Kan identifies its underlying $\infty$-category with $D^{\leq 0}(\mathbb{Z})$. It is additive and presentable, tensoring preserves small colimits separately, and it is not stable. Consequently, for a map of simplicial associative rings in the derived $\infty$-category,
$$B\ast_{A}B\simeq B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\otimes_{A}B\simeq B.$$
This connective comparison is used (and proved in this specific case) in [6].

**Remark 4.4.** Consider the $\infty$-category of spaces $\mathcal{S}$ with cartesian tensor product and a nontrivial acyclic discrete group $G$, regarded as a grouplike $E_{1}$-space. The map $G\to\star$ is not homological, since
$$\star\times_{G}\star\simeq BG,$$
whose fundamental group is $G$. It is nevertheless homotopical. The pushout $\star\ast_{G}\star$ in $E_{1}$-spaces is grouplike: $\pi_{0}$ preserves colimits into ordinary monoids, and this pushout of monoids is trivial. Thus it is also the pushout in grouplike $E_{1}$-spaces. Their equivalence with pointed connected spaces [23, §5.2.6] gives
$$\star\ast_{G}\star\simeq\Omega\Sigma BG.$$
The suspension of the connected acyclic CW-space $BG$ is simply connected and acyclic, hence contractible by the Hurewicz and Whitehead theorem. The codiagonal is therefore an equivalence.

**Remark 4.5.** More generally, if $T$ is an ordinary $H$-comodule algebra and $s\in T$ is central and coinvariant, the same telescope argument gives
$$T[s^{-1}]\otimes_{T}^{\mathbb{L}}T[s^{-1}]\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}T[s^{-1}].$$
Its coaction extends by $\rho(s^{-1})=s^{-1}\otimes 1$. Thus central coinvariant localizations are homotopical epimorphisms of associative algebras in $\mathcal{C}$. Indeed, for a commutative $k$-algebra $R$, put $A_{R}=R\otimes_{k}H$, with coaction ${\rm Id}_{R}\otimes\Delta$ and $A_{R}^{\operatorname{co}H}=R$. Coinvariants induce an equivalence
$$\mathbf{Mod}_{A_{R}}(\mathcal{C})\simeq D^{\leq 0}(R),\qquad M\longmapsto M^{\operatorname{co}H},$$
whose inverse is $N\mapsto N\otimes_{R}A_{R}$. Indeed, on ordinary compatible right modules and comodules, the action map
$$M^{\operatorname{co}H}\otimes_{k}H\longrightarrow M,\qquad m\otimes g\longmapsto m(1\otimes g),$$
is an isomorphism: its inverse sends a homogeneous element $m_{g}$ to $m_{g}(1\otimes g^{-1})\otimes g$. The equivalence of ordinary module categories is a special case of Dade's theorem [16, Theorem 2.8]: the algebra $A_{R}$ is strongly $G$-graded, with components $(A_{R})_{g}=R(1\otimes g)$ and identity component $R$, and coinvariants correspond to the identity component of a graded module. Since this equivalence is exact, it induces the equivalence of derived categories.

**Proposition 4.6.** Under these hypotheses, for each $A$ there is only a set of equivalence classes of homotopical epimorphisms $A\to B$.
