# Restatement

## Abstract

Condensed mathematics, developed by Clausen and Scholze, studies the interplay between algebra and geometry. It replaces topological spaces by condensed sets. Its central objects are solid abelian groups and liquid vector spaces, which are analogues of complete topological groups. Nöbeling's theorem (1960s) states something about the structure of the abelian group of continuous maps from a profinite space to the integers. It is a crucial ingredient in the theory of solid abelian groups: without it, no nonzero examples of solid abelian groups can be given. The paper discusses a formalisation of this result in the Lean theorem prover and gives a more detailed proof than those previously in the literature. The proof requires induction over ordinals.

## 1 Introduction

Nöbeling's theorem states that the abelian group $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$ of continuous maps from a profinite space $S$ to the integers is a free abelian group. The original statement [14, Satz 1] is the more general result that bounded maps from any set to the integers form a free abelian group. The special case has been applied [16, Theorem 5.4] in condensed mathematics (see also [15, 5, 3]).

The formalisation uses the Lean 4 theorem prover [12] and the Mathlib library [11, 10]. The proof uses the well-ordering principle and induction over ordinals. This is the first use of the induction principle for ordinals in Mathlib outside the directory containing the theory of ordinals.

The proof does not use any ordinal arithmetic. It uses induction over ordinals with a case split between limit ordinals and successor ordinals.[^1]

The proof in [14] is in German. The proofs of the result in [7, 16] follow the same argument as the one presented here, with less detail.

[^1]: Footnote 1 of the paper: The proof does not use any ordinal arithmetic. However, it uses the principle of induction over ordinals with a case split between limit ordinals and successor ordinals.

Footnote 3 of the paper: This definition has also been formalised by the author in Lean 3 in [2].

## 2 Definitions, lemmas and results

**Definition 1.** Let $S=\varprojlim_{i}S_{i}$ be a profinite space. Define a condensed abelian group
$$\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\rule[0.36pt]{3.5pt}{3.5pt}}:=\varprojlim_{i}\mathbb{Z}[S_{i}].$$
There is a natural map $\mathbb{Z}[S]\to\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\rule[0.36pt]{3.5pt}{3.5pt}}$. A condensed abelian group $A$ is *solid* if for every profinite space $S$ and every morphism $f:\mathbb{Z}[S]\to A$ of condensed abelian groups, there is a unique morphism $g:\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\rule[0.36pt]{3.5pt}{3.5pt}}\to A$ making the obvious triangle commute.

**Definition 2.** Let $I$ and $X$ be sets and let $r$ be a binary relation on $X$. An $I$-indexed family $(x_{i})$ in $X$ is *directed* if for all $i,j\in I$ there exists $k\in I$ such that $r(x_{i},x_{k})$ and $r(x_{j},x_{k})$.

**Lemma 3.** A monotone map on a poset with a join operation (i.e. a least upper bound of two elements) is directed.

**Remark 4.** Taking the union of two sets is an example of a join operation.

**Definition 5.** A category $\mathcal{C}$ is *filtered* if it satisfies the following three conditions:
1. $\mathcal{C}$ is nonempty.
2. For all objects $X,Y$, there exists an object $Z$ and morphisms $f:X\to Z$ and $g:Y\to Z$.
3. For all objects $X,Y$ and all morphisms $f,g:X\to Y$, there exists an object $Z$ and a morphism $h:Y\to Z$ such that $h\circ f=h\circ g$.

A category is *cofiltered* if the opposite category is filtered.

**Remark 6.** A poset is filtered if and only if it is nonempty and directed.

**Remark 7.** The poset of finite subsets of a given set is filtered.

**Lemma 8.** If $(X_{i})$ is a family of linearly independent subsets of a module over a ring $R$, which is directed with respect to the subset relation, then its union is linearly independent.

**Lemma 9.** Suppose there is a commutative diagram with $0$, $N$, $M$, $P$ in the top row and $I$, $I\sqcup J$, $J$ in the bottom row, with maps labelled $f$, $g$, $v$, $u$, $w$. Here $N,M,P$ are modules over a ring $R$, the top row is exact, and the bottom maps are the inclusion maps. If $v$ and $w$ are linearly independent, then $u$ is linearly independent.

**Theorem 10** (Cantor's intersection theorem). Let $(Z_{i})_{i\in I}$ be a nonempty family of nonempty, closed and compact subsets of a topological space $X$, which is directed with respect to the superset relation $(V,W)\mapsto V\supseteq W$. Then the intersection $\bigcap_{i\in I}Z_{i}$ is nonempty.

**Remark 11.** Cantor's intersection theorem is often stated only for decreasing nested sequences of nonempty compact, closed subsets. The generalisation in Theorem 10 can be proved by slightly modifying the standard proof of that special case.

**Definition 12.** A *profinite space* is a totally disconnected compact Hausdorff space.

**Lemma 13.** Every profinite space has a basis of clopen subsets.

**Lemma 14.** Every profinite space is totally separated, i.e. any two distinct points can be separated by clopen neighbourhoods.

**Remark 15.** A topological space is profinite if and only if it can be written as a cofiltered limit of finite discrete spaces. See Section 6 for further discussion.

**Lemma 16.** Any continuous map from a cofiltered limit of profinite spaces to a discrete space factors through one of the components.

**Remark 17.** In particular, a continuous map from a profinite space $S=\varprojlim_{i}S_{i}$ to a discrete space factors through one of the finite quotients $S_{i}$.

**Theorem 18** (Nöbeling's theorem). Let $S$ be a profinite space. Then the abelian group $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$ of continuous maps from $S$ to $\mathbb{Z}$ is free.

**Lemma 19.** Let $I$ be a set and let $S$ be a closed subset of $\prod_{i\in I}\{0,1\}$. Then $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$ is a free abelian group.

**Lemma 20.** For $p\in P$ and $x\in S$,
$$\operatorname{ev}_{S}(p)(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\forall i\in p,\ x_{i}=1,\\ 0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

**Lemma 21.** Let $J$ be a subset of $I$ and let $p\in P$ be such that $i\in p$ implies $i\in J$. Then $\pi_{J}^{*}(\operatorname{ev}_{S_{J}}(p))=\operatorname{ev}_{S}(p)$.

**Remark 22.** The hypothesis in Lemma 21 holds in particular if $p\in E(S_{J})$. Indeed, suppose $i\in p$. If $i\notin J$, then $\operatorname{ev}_{S_{J}}(p)=0$.

**Lemma 23.** If $\mu',\mu$ are ordinals satisfying $\mu'<\mu$, then $E(S_{\mu'})\subseteq E(S_{\mu})$.

**Lemma 24.** The set $P$ is well-ordered.

**Remark 25.** The proof sketch of Lemma 24 is ill-suited for formalisation. Kim Morrison gave a formalised proof, following similar ideas, which used close to 300 lines of code. Junyan Xu then found a proof that was ten times shorter, directly using the inductive datatype `WellFounded`. This is the only result whose proof indicated in this paper differs significantly from the one used in the formalisation.

**Lemma 26.** If $\operatorname{ev}_{S}(P)$ spans $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$, then $\operatorname{ev}_{S}(E(S))$ spans $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$.

**Lemma 27.** Let $F$ denote the contravariant functor from the (filtered) poset of finite subsets of $I$ to the category of profinite spaces, which sends $J$ to $S_{J}$. Then $S$ is homeomorphic to the limit of $F$.

**Lemma 28.** Let $J$ be a finite subset of $I$. Then $\operatorname{ev}_{S_{J}}(E(S_{J}))$ spans $C(S_{J},\mathbb{Z})$.

**Lemma 29.** $P$ spans $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$.

**Notation 30.** Regard $I$ with its well-ordering as an ordinal. Let $Q$ denote the following predicate on an ordinal $\mu\leq I$: for all closed subsets $S$ of $\prod_{i\in I}\{0,1\}$ such that, for all $x\in S$ and $i\in I$, $x_{i}=1$ implies $i<\mu$, the set $E(S)$ is linearly independent in $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$.

**Lemma 31.** The base case of the induction, $Q(0)$, holds.

**Lemma 32.** Let $\mu'<\mu$ and let $p\in P$ have all entries $<\mu'$. Then
$$\pi_{\mu'}^{*}\left(\operatorname{ev}_{S_{\mu'}}\left(\{q\in P\mid q<p\}\right)\right)=\operatorname{ev}_{S_{\mu}}\left(\{q\in P\mid q<p\}\right).$$

**Lemma 33.** $E(S_{\mu})=\bigcup_{\mu'<\mu}E(S_{\mu'})$.

**Lemma 34.** 
$$\operatorname{ev}_{S_{\mu}}\left(E(S_{\mu})\right)=\bigcup_{\mu'<\mu}\pi_{\mu'}^{*}\left(\operatorname{ev}_{S_{\mu'}}\left(E(S_{\mu'})\right)\right).$$

**Construction 35.** Let
$$S_{0}=\{x\in S\mid x_{\mu}=0\},\qquad S_{1}=\{x\in S\mid x_{\mu}=1\},$$
and
$$S'=S_{0}\cap\pi_{\mu}(S_{1}).$$
Then $S'$ satisfies the inductive hypothesis.

**Construction 36.** Let $g_{0}:S'\to S$ denote the inclusion map, and let $g_{1}:S'\to S$ denote the map that swaps the $\mu$-th coordinate to $1$. Since $S'\subseteq\pi_{\mu}(S_{1})$, this map lands in $S$. Both maps are continuous, and they give a linear map
$$g=g_{1}^{*}-g_{0}^{*}:C(S,\mathbb{Z})\to C(S',\mathbb{Z}).$$

**Lemma 37.** The top row in diagram (1) is exact.

**Lemma 38.** If $p\in P$ starts with $\mu$, then $g(\operatorname{ev}_{S}(p))=\operatorname{ev}_{S'}(p^{t})$.

**Remark 39.** If $p\in E(S)$ and $\mu\in p$, then $p$ satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma 38.

**Lemma 40.** If $p\in E(S)$ and $\mu\in p$, then $p^{t}\in E(S')$.

**Lemma 41.** The set $E(S)$ is the disjoint union of $E(S_{\mu})$ and
$$E'(S)=\{p\in E(S)\mid \mu\in p\}.$$

**Theorem 42.** A topological space is profinite if and only if it can be written as a cofiltered limit of finite discrete spaces.
