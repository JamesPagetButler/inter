# Restatement

## Abstract

The paper describes a category whose objects are models for homotopy theories. It shows that for such models, functors between two homotopy theories form a homotopy theory, more precisely that the category of such models has a well-behaved internal hom-object.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.0 Background

Quillen introduced closed model categories [Qui67]: a category with a distinguished subcategory of weak equivalences and additional structure allowing homotopy theory to be done. Examples include topological spaces with the usual weak equivalences, and bounded-below chain complexes with quasi-isomorphisms as weak equivalences. A model category has an associated homotopy category and higher homotopy structure. For instance, Quillen observed that homotopy groups and Toda brackets can be defined in a closed model category. Dwyer and Kan showed [DK80] that for any two objects of a model category one can define a function complex.

Quillen's criterion for two models to give equivalent homotopy theories is now called a "Quillen equivalence" of closed model categories. Topological spaces and simplicial sets are alternate models for the same homotopy theory. (In Quillen's case the problem was algebraic models for rational homotopy theory [Qui69].) A "model" for a homotopy theory is distinguished from the homotopy theory itself. A model could be a closed model category, but other kinds of models are also considered.

Dwyer and Kan [DK80] clarified the notion of an abstract homotopy theory:

1. The minimal data specifying a homotopy theory is a category with a distinguished subcategory of weak equivalences.
2. Any such data gives rise to a simplicial localization, a category enriched over simplicial sets. If the data came from a model category, its homotopy category and higher composition structure can be recovered from the simplicial localization.
3. Dwyer and Kan define a notion of equivalence of simplicial localizations, answering Quillen's question on equivalence of homotopy theories. The simplicial localizations with this notion of equivalence form a "homotopy theory of homotopy theory" (see [DS95, §11.6]).

On the other hand, one can study abstract homotopy theory through diagrams in a homotopy theory. Under mild hypotheses, a category of functors from a fixed domain category into a closed model category is itself a closed model category. The domain category may itself be a closed model category, or a subcategory of one. Thus one expects that functors from one homotopy theory to another form a new homotopy theory. Such functor categories are of practical interest, with applications including models for spectra, simplicial sheaf theory, and the Goodwillie calculus of functors.

The paper studies a model for a homotopy theory called a **complete Segal space**. A complete Segal space is an object of a certain Quillen closed model category, and the category of complete Segal spaces has internal hom-objects. The main results are:

- **(0)** A complete Segal space has invariants such as a "homotopy category" and "function complexes", together with "higher composition" structure (§5).
- **(1)** There exists a simplicial closed model category in which the fibrant objects are precisely the complete Segal spaces (7.2). This is a "homotopy theory of homotopy theories".
- **(2)** This category is cartesian closed, and the cartesian closure is compatible with the model category structure. In particular, if $X$ is any object and $W$ is a complete Segal space, then the internal hom-object $W^X$ is a complete Segal space (7.3). This says that functors between two homotopy theories form another homotopy theory.

The category in question is the category of simplicial spaces with an appropriate closed model category structure.

A complete Segal space is a modification of Graeme Segal's notion of a $\Delta$-space, a simplicial space that serves as a model for loop spaces. "Complete Segal space" (Section 6) is a special case of "Segal space" (Section 4).

### 1.1 Natural examples

- Any category gives rise to a complete Segal space via a classifying diagram construction.
- A closed model category gives rise to a complete Segal space via a classification diagram construction, a generalization of the classifying diagram.
- A pair $(C,W)$, where $C$ is a category and $W$ a subcategory, gives rise to a complete Segal space via a localization of the classification diagram.

Given a closed model category $\mathbf{M}$ and a small category $C$, the category $\mathbf{M}^C$ of functors from $C$ to $\mathbf{M}$ is often again a closed model category. One can then ask whether the classification diagram of $\mathbf{M}^C$ is equivalent to the complete Segal space obtained as the internal hom-object of maps from the classifying diagram of $C$ to the classification diagram of $\mathbf{M}$. A consequence (8.12) of a result of Dwyer and Kan gives this equivalence when $\mathbf{M}$ is the category of simplicial sets, or more generally a category of diagrams of simplicial sets. The paper does not prove it for a general closed model category.

### 1.2 Classifying diagrams and classification diagrams

To any category $C$ one associates its classifying space $BC$: a vertex for each object, a 1-simplex for each morphism, a 2-simplex for each commutative triangle, and so on.

- If $C$ is a groupoid, then it is characterized up to equivalence of categories by $BC$. For a groupoid $C$, $BC$ has the homotopy type of a disjoint union of spaces $K(\pi_X,1)$, where $X$ ranges over representatives of isomorphism classes of objects of $C$ and $\pi_X$ is the automorphism group of $X$.
- A general category cannot be recovered from its classifying space.
- Let $\iso C$ denote the subcategory of $C$ with all objects and all isomorphisms. It is the maximal subgroupoid of $C$. From the homotopy type of $B(\iso C)$ one recovers the set of isomorphism classes of objects of $C$ and the automorphism group of any object. So $B(\iso C)$ may be viewed as a "moduli space" for $C$.

**Proposition 3.7 (cited in 1.2):** A category $C$ is determined, up to equivalence, by the simplicial diagram of spaces $[n]\mapsto B\iso(C^{[n]})$, called the classifying diagram of $C$. Here $[n]$ denotes the category with $n+1$ objects and $n$ composable arrows, and $C^{[n]}$ the category of functors $[n]\to C$.

The classifying diagram of a category is a complete Segal space.

The homotopy-theoretic analogue of $B(\iso C)$ is the classification space of a model category, due to Dwyer and Kan. For a closed model category $\mathbf{M}$, let $\we\mathbf{M}\subset\mathbf{M}$ be the subcategory of all objects and weak equivalences. The **classification space** of $\mathbf{M}$, denoted $\class(\mathbf{M})$, is $B(\we\mathbf{M})$.

This is analogous to $B(\iso C)$. In particular, $\class(\mathbf{M})$ has the homotopy type of a disjoint union of spaces $B(\haut X)$, where $X$ ranges over representatives of weak equivalence classes of objects of $\mathbf{M}$, and $\haut X$ is the simplicial monoid of self-homotopy equivalences of $X$ (8.7).

Classification spaces arise in realization problems, for example realizing a diagram in the homotopy category of spaces by an actual diagram of spaces; see [DK84b], [DK84a].

For a closed model category $\mathbf{M}$, the simplicial space $[n]\mapsto\class(\mathbf{M}^{[n]})$ is the **classification diagram** of $\mathbf{M}$. By (8.3), the classification diagram of a closed model category is essentially a complete Segal space, where "essentially" means up to an easy fibrant replacement.

### 1.3 Applications

- **Diagram categories.** Diagram categories in a model category can be modeled as the internal function complex in the category of simplicial spaces. The proof is given here only for the case where the model category is simplicial sets.
- **Homotopy inverse limits.** Let $W=\class(\mathcal{T}_*)$, the classification diagram of pointed topological spaces. $W$ is a complete Segal space. Let $\omega\colon W\to W$ be the self-map induced by the loop-space functor $\Omega\colon\mathcal{T}_*\to\mathcal{T}_*$. Let $W_\infty$ be the homotopy inverse limit, in simplicial spaces, of the tower
$$\dots\xrightarrow{\omega}W\xrightarrow{\omega}W\xrightarrow{\omega}W.$$
Then $W_\infty$ is a complete Segal space, and it is weakly equivalent to the classification space of the category of spectra. (This is stated without proof.)
- **Sheaves of homotopy theories.** There is a model category for sheaves of spaces (= sheaves of simplicial sets) over a base space, or more generally over a Grothendieck topology [Jar87], [Jar96]. Hence there is a model category structure on sheaves of simplicial spaces. A sheaf of simplicial spaces $W$ is a **complete Segal sheaf** if each stalk is a complete Segal space in the sense of this paper.

### 1.4 Other models

- Dwyer and Kan proposed a model, discussed above. Since complete Segal spaces are objects of a closed model category, the construction gives another model for a homotopy theory of homotopy theory. The paper states a belief that its model is equivalent to that of Dwyer and Kan, via a suitable notion of equivalence: there should be constructions taking complete Segal spaces to simplicially enriched categories and vice versa, inverse to each other up to appropriate equivalence. This is not proved.
- A. Heller [Hel88] proposed modeling a homotopy theory by a contravariant 2-functor from the category of small categories to the category of large categories. For a closed model category $\mathbf{M}$, one assigns to each small category $C$ the homotopy category $\ho(\mathbf{M}^C)$, and to each functor $C\to D$ restriction functors $\ho(\mathbf{M}^D)\to\ho(\mathbf{M}^C)$, which admit both left and right adjoints arising from homotopy Kan extensions. Heller's models require such homotopy Kan extensions and seem less general than those considered here. The relation between his theory and the others is not known.

## 3. Classifying diagrams

**Proposition 3.2.** The nerve of $[n]$ is $\Delta[n]$. For categories $C$ and $D$ there are natural isomorphisms
$$\nerve(C\times D)\approx\nerve C\times\nerve D\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nerve(D^{C})\approx\nerve(D)^{\nerve(C)}.$$
The functor $\nerve\colon\mathcal{C}at\to\mathcal{S}$ is a full embedding of categories. Furthermore, if $C$ is a groupoid then $\nerve(C)$ is a Kan complex.

**Theorem 3.7.** Let $C$ and $D$ be categories. There are natural isomorphisms of simplicial spaces
$$N(C\times D)\approx NC\times ND\qquad\text{and}\qquad N(D^{C})\approx(ND)^{NC}.$$
The functor $N\colon\mathcal{C}at\to s\mathcal{S}$ is a full embedding of categories. Furthermore, a functor $f\colon C\to D$ is an equivalence of categories if and only if $Nf$ is a weak equivalence of simplicial spaces.

**Lemma 3.8.** For a category $C$ there are natural isomorphisms of simplicial spaces
$$N([m]\times C)\approx F(m)\times NC\qquad\text{and}\qquad N(C^{I[n]})\approx(NC)^{\Delta[n]}.$$

**Lemma 3.9.** If $C$ is a category, then $NC$ is a Reedy fibrant simplicial space.

**Proposition 3.11.** Let $C$ and $D$ be categories, and let $W\subset D$ be a subcategory such that $\iso D\subset W$. Then there are natural isomorphisms
$$N(D^{C},\we(D^{C}))\approx N(D,W)^{NC}\approx N(D,W)^{\discnerve C}.$$

**Lemma 3.12.** Let $C$ be a category and $W$ a subcategory with $\operatorname{ob}W=\operatorname{ob}C$. Then there is a natural isomorphism
$$N\bigl(\widetilde{C^{[n]}},\we(\widetilde{C^{[n]}})\bigr)\approx(NC)^{\Delta[n]},$$
where $\widetilde{C^{[n]}}\subset C^{[n]}$ is the full subcategory whose objects are the functors $[n]\to C$ that factor through $W\subset C$, and $\we(\widetilde{C^{[n]}})=\we(C^{[n]})\cap\widetilde{C^{[n]}}$.

## 5. Segal spaces as homotopy theories

**Remark 5.2.** If $C$ is a category and $W=\discnerve C$ or $W=NC$, then $\operatorname{ob}W\approx\operatorname{ob}C$ and $\map_W(x,y)\approx\hom_C(x,y)$.

**Proposition 5.4.** Given points $f\in\map(w,x)$, $g\in\map(x,y)$, and $h\in\map(y,x)$, we have
$$(h\circ g)\circ f\sim h\circ(g\circ f)\qquad\text{and}\qquad f\circ\id_w\sim f\sim\id_x\circ f.$$

**Remark 5.6.** Recall (3.5), where $N\colon\mathcal{C}at\to s\mathcal{S}$ was defined via the classifying diagram construction; by (4.4) it lands in the subcategory of Segal spaces. By (5.2), $\ho NC\approx C$. The functor $N$ admits a left adjoint $L\colon s\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{C}at$, and $L(W)\approx\ho W$ whenever $W$ is a Segal space. (The paper states that this is possible to show.)

**Lemma 5.8.** If $g\in W_1$ is a vertex that can be connected by a path in $W_1$ to a homotopy equivalence $g'\in W_1$, then $g$ is itself a homotopy equivalence.

## 6. Complete Segal spaces

**Proposition 6.1.** If $C$ is a small category, then the classifying diagram $NC$ of (3.5) is a complete Segal space.

**Theorem 6.2.** If $W$ is a Segal space, then $\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(E,W)\to W_1$ factors through $W_{\hoequiv}\subseteq W_1$, and induces a weak equivalence $\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(E,W)\to W_{\hoequiv}$.

**Proposition 6.4.** Let $W$ be a Segal space. The following are equivalent.

1. $W$ is a complete Segal space.
2. The map $W_0\to\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(E,W)$ induced by $E\to F(0)$ is a weak equivalence.
3. Either of the maps $\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(E,W)\to W_0$ induced by a map $F(0)\to E$ is a weak equivalence.
4. For each pair $x,y\in\operatorname{ob}W$, the space $\hoequiv(x,y)$ is naturally weakly equivalent to the space of paths in $W_0$ from $x$ to $y$.

**Corollary 6.5.** Let $\operatorname{ob}W/{\sim}$ denote the set of homotopy equivalence classes of objects of $\ho W$. If $W$ is a complete Segal space, then $\pi_0W_0\approx\operatorname{ob}W/{\sim}$.

**Corollary 6.6.** Let $W$ be a complete Segal space. Then $\ho W$ is a groupoid if and only if $W$ is Reedy weakly equivalent to a constant simplicial space.

## 7. Model category structures

**Theorem 7.1.** There exists a simplicial closed model category structure on the category $s\mathcal{S}$ of simplicial spaces, called the **Segal space model category structure**, with the following properties.

1. The cofibrations are precisely the monomorphisms.
2. The fibrant objects are precisely the Segal spaces.
3. The weak equivalences are precisely the maps $f$ such that $\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(f,W)$ is a weak equivalence of spaces for every Segal space $W$.
4. A Reedy weak equivalence between any two objects is a weak equivalence in the Segal space model category structure, and if both objects are Segal spaces then the converse holds.

Moreover, this model category structure is compatible with the cartesian closed structure on $s\mathcal{S}et$ in the sense of Section 2.

**Theorem 7.2.** There exists a simplicial closed model category structure on the category $s\mathcal{S}$ of simplicial spaces, called the **complete Segal space model category structure**, with the following properties.

1. The cofibrations are precisely the monomorphisms.
2. The fibrant objects are precisely the complete Segal spaces.
3. The weak equivalences are precisely the maps $f$ such that $\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(f,W)$ is a weak equivalence of spaces for every complete Segal space $W$.
4. A Reedy weak equivalence between any two objects is a weak equivalence in the complete Segal space model category structure, and if both objects are complete Segal spaces then the converse holds.

Moreover, this model category structure is compatible with the cartesian closed structure on $s\mathcal{S}et$ in the sense of Section 2.

**Corollary 7.3.** If $W$ is a complete Segal space (resp. a Segal space) and $X$ is any simplicial space, then $W^X$ is a complete Segal space (resp. a Segal space).

**Lemma 7.5.** Let $U\xrightarrow{f}V\xrightarrow{g}W$ be maps of Segal spaces such that $f$ and $g$ are Dwyer–Kan equivalences. Then $gf$ is a Dwyer–Kan equivalence. Let $U\xrightarrow{f}V\xrightarrow{g}W\xrightarrow{h}X$ be maps of Segal spaces such that $gf$ and $hg$ are Dwyer–Kan equivalences. Then each of $f$, $g$, and $h$ is a Dwyer–Kan equivalence.

**Proposition 7.6.** A map $f\colon U\to V$ of complete Segal spaces is a Dwyer–Kan equivalence if and only if it is a Reedy weak equivalence.

**Theorem 7.7.** Let $f\colon U\to V$ be a map between Segal spaces. Then $f$ is a Dwyer–Kan equivalence if and only if it becomes a weak equivalence in the complete Segal space model category structure.

**Remark 7.8.** If $C$ is a category, then the natural inclusion $\discnerve(C)\to N(C)$ of simplicial spaces is a Dwyer–Kan equivalence. By (7.7), this map is a weak equivalence in the complete Segal space model category structure.

**Corollary 7.9.** The homotopy category of complete Segal spaces may be obtained by formally inverting the Dwyer–Kan equivalences in the homotopy category of Segal spaces.

## 8. Classification diagrams of model categories

**Theorem 8.3.** Let $\mathbf{M}$ be a simplicial closed model category, and let $\mathbf{W}\subset\mathbf{M}$ denote the subcategory of weak equivalences. Then $V=N^f(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W})$ is a complete Segal space. Furthermore, there is an equivalence of categories $\ho V\approx\ho\mathbf{M}$, and there are weak equivalences of spaces $\map_V(X,Y)\approx\map_{\mathbf{M}}(X,Y)$.

**Remark 8.4.** The result (8.3) presumably generalizes to an arbitrary closed model category, not necessarily simplicial. There the function complex $\map_V(X,Y)$ would be taken to be one of those described by Dwyer and Kan [DK80].

**Remark 8.5.** Any category $C$ having finite limits and colimits can be made into a closed model category in which the weak equivalences are precisely the isomorphisms, and all maps are fibrations and cofibrations. In this case $N(C)=N(C,\iso C)$ coincides with the classifying diagram construction of (3.5), and (6.1) shows that it is already a complete Segal space.

**Proposition 8.7 (Dwyer–Kan [DK84a, 2.3, 2.4]).** Let $\mathbf{M}$ be a simplicial closed model category, and let $X\in\mathbf{M}$ be fibrant and cofibrant. Let $\haut X\subset\map_{\mathbf{M}}(X,X)$ be its simplicial monoid of weak equivalences. Then the classifying complex $\bar W\haut X$ is weakly equivalent to $\sclass X$. In fact, $\bar W\haut X$ and $\sclass X$ can be connected by a finite string of weak equivalences that is natural with respect to simplicial functors $f\colon\mathbf{M}\to\mathbf{N}$ between closed model categories which preserve weak equivalences and are such that $fX\in\mathbf{N}$ is fibrant and cofibrant.

**Remark 8.8.** For any two fibrant-and-cofibrant objects $X,Y\in\mathbf{M}$, the space of paths from $X$ to $Y$ in $\class(\mathbf{M})$ is naturally weakly equivalent to the space $\hoequiv_{\mathbf{M}}(X,Y)\subset\map_{\mathbf{M}}(X,Y)$ of homotopy equivalences from $X$ to $Y$. (The notation $\class(\mathbf{M})$ was defined in (1.2).) Compare with (6.4, 4).

**Theorem 8.11.** The map $f$ of (8.10) is a Reedy weak equivalence when $\mathbf{M}=\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{J}}$, where $\mathcal{S}$ denotes the category of simplicial sets and $\mathbf{J}$ is a small indexing category.

**Corollary 8.12.** There is a natural weak equivalence of complete Segal spaces
$$N(\mathbf{M}^I)\xrightarrow{\sim}N^f(\mathbf{M})^{N(I)}$$
if $\mathbf{M}=\mathcal{S}^J$ and $I$ and $J$ are small categories.

**Remark 8.13.** The theorem of Dwyer and Kan, and hence the statements of (8.11) and (8.12), should hold for any "reasonable" model category $\mathbf{M}$. The class of "reasonable" closed model categories includes at least the "cofibrantly generated" simplicial closed model categories.

**Theorem 8.14 (Dwyer–Kan).** Let $I$ be a small category. The natural map
$$\class(\mathcal{S}^I)\approx\lim_{([k]\to I)\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\operatorname{op}}I}\class(\mathcal{S}^{[k]})\longrightarrow\holim_{([k]\to I)\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\operatorname{op}}I}\class(\mathcal{S}^{[k]})^f$$
is a weak equivalence. Here $X^f$ denotes the fibrant replacement of a space $X$, and $\holim$ is the homotopy inverse limit construction of [BK72].

**Lemma 8.15.** Let $I$ be a small category and $W$ a Reedy fibrant simplicial space. Then the natural map
$$\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(\discnerve I,W)\approx\lim_{([k]\to I)\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\operatorname{op}}I}W_k\longrightarrow\holim_{([k]\to I)\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\operatorname{op}}I}W_k$$
is a weak equivalence.

## 9. Localized model structures

**Proposition 9.1.** Let $f\colon A\to B$ be an inclusion in $s\mathcal{S}$. There exists a cofibrantly generated simplicial model category structure on $s\mathcal{S}$ with the following properties.

1. The cofibrations are exactly the inclusions.
2. The fibrant objects (called $f$-local objects) are exactly the Reedy fibrant $W\in s\mathcal{S}$ such that $\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(B,W)\to\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(A,W)$ is a weak equivalence of spaces.
3. The weak equivalences (called $f$-local weak equivalences) are exactly the maps $g\colon X\to Y$ such that for every $f$-local object $W$, the induced map $\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(Y,W)\to\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(X,W)$ is a weak equivalence.
4. A Reedy weak equivalence between two objects is an $f$-local weak equivalence, and if both objects are $f$-local then the converse holds.

**Proposition 9.2.** Suppose that for each $f$-local object $W$, the simplicial space $W^{F(1)}$ is also $f$-local. Then the $f$-local model category structure on $s\mathcal{S}$ is compatible with the cartesian closure.

## 10. Covers and products

**Lemma 10.1.** Let $G\subset F(n)$ be a cover. Then the inclusions
$$G(n)\xrightarrow{i}G\xrightarrow{j}F(n)$$
are weak equivalences in the Segal space model category structure.

**Remark 10.2.** The class of subobjects weakly equivalent to $F(n)$ is not exhausted by the coverings. For example, for $0<i<n$, the subobject $\dot F(n)\setminus d^iF(n-1)$, the boundary of $F(n)$ with a face removed that is neither the first nor the last face, is weakly equivalent to $F(n)$ in the Segal space model category structure, but is not a cover.

**Lemma 10.3.** The inclusion $F(1)\times G(n)\to F(1)\times F(n)$ is a weak equivalence in the Segal space model category structure.

## 11. Segal space computations

**Proposition 11.1.** If $W$ is a Segal space and $n\geq 3$, then the map $\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(E^{(n)},W)\to W_1$ factors through the subspace $W_{\hoequiv}\subseteq W_1$, and induces a weak equivalence $\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(E^{(n)},W)\to W_{\hoequiv}$.

**Proposition 11.3.**

1. Given $f\in\map(x,y)$ and $g\in\map(y,z)$, and $g\circ f$ the result of their composition, we have $(g\circ f)_*\sim g_*\circ f_*$ and $(g\circ f)^*\sim f^*\circ g^*$.
2. Given $x\in\operatorname{ob}W$, we have $(\id_x)_*\sim(\id_x)^*\sim\id_{\map(x,x)}$.

**Proposition 11.4.** Let $f,g\in\map(x,y)$. Then $f\sim g$ if and only if the maps $f_*,g_*\colon\map(w,x)\to\map(w,y)$ are homotopic for all $w\in\operatorname{ob}W$, if and only if the maps $f^*,g^*\colon\map(y,z)\to\map(x,z)$ are homotopic for all $z\in\operatorname{ob}W$.

**Corollary 11.5.** If $f\in\map(x,y)$ is a homotopy equivalence (in the sense of (5.5)), then $f_*$ and $f^*$ are weak equivalences of spaces.

**Lemma 11.6.** Let $W$ be a Segal space, $f\in\map(x,y)$ and $g\in\map(y,z)$, such that $f$ is a homotopy equivalence. Then the induced map
$$\map(x,y,z)_{f,g}\xrightarrow{(d_1,d_2)}\map(x,z)_{g\circ f}\times\map(x,y)_f$$
is a weak equivalence.

**Lemma 11.10.** Let $W$ be a Segal space. Then for $k\geq 2$ the induced map
$$\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(F(k),W)_{\hoequiv}\rightarrow\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(H(k),W)_{\hoequiv}$$
is a weak equivalence.

**Lemma 11.11.** There is a natural weak equivalence
$$\Map_{s\mathcal{S}}(C(k),W)\approx\Map(d^1H(k-1),W)\times_{W_0}W_1.$$

## 13. Categorical homotopy and equivalences

**Proposition 13.2.** Let $U$ be a Segal space and $W$ a complete Segal space. A pair of maps $f,g\colon U\rightrightarrows W$ is categorically homotopic if and only if they are homotopic in the usual sense. That is, there exists a map $K\colon U\times\Delta[1]\to W$ which restricts to $f$ and $g$ on the endpoints of $\Delta[1]$.

**Proposition 13.4.** A map $g\colon U\to V$ between complete Segal spaces is a categorical equivalence if and only if it is a simplicial homotopy equivalence, if and only if it is a Reedy weak equivalence.

**Proposition 13.5.** Let $A$, $B$, and $W$ be Segal spaces.

1. If $f,g\colon A\rightrightarrows B$ are categorically homotopic maps, then the induced maps $W^B\rightrightarrows W^A$ are categorically homotopic.
2. If $f\colon A\to B$ is a categorical equivalence, then the induced map $W^B\to W^A$ is a categorical equivalence.

**Proposition 13.6.** If $f\colon U\to V$ is a categorical equivalence between Segal spaces, then it is a weak equivalence in the complete Segal space model category structure.

**Proposition 13.8.** If $g\colon U\to V$ is a categorical equivalence of Segal spaces, then it is a Dwyer–Kan equivalence.

**Lemma 13.9.** If $W$ is a Segal space, then the map $W\to W^E$ and both maps $W^E\to W$ are Dwyer–Kan equivalences.

## 14. Completion

**Remark 14.1.** This completion is a generalization of the classifying space construction. Suppose $W$ is a Segal space such that $\ho W$ is a groupoid; equivalently, $W_1=W_{\hoequiv}$. Then the arguments below show that $\widehat W$ is weakly equivalent to a constant simplicial space, which in each degree is the realization $\diag W$. For instance, if $W$ is a "$\Delta$-space" (i.e., $W_0=*$), and thus a model for a loop space with underlying space equivalent to $W_1$, then $\widehat W$ is equivalent to the constant object $BW_1$, the classifying space of the "loop space" $W_1$, in each degree.

**Lemma 14.2.** If $C$ is a category, then $\widetilde{\discnerve C}$ is isomorphic to $NC$. In particular, $NE\approx\widetilde E$ and $\widehat E$ are weakly equivalent to the terminal object in $s\mathcal{S}$.

**Lemma 14.3.** If $U\to V$ is a categorical equivalence between Segal spaces, then $\widehat U\to\widehat V$ is a Reedy weak equivalence.
