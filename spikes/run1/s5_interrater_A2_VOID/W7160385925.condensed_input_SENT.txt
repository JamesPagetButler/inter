This is a slightly updated version of lectures notes for a course on analytic geometry taught in the winter term 2019/20 at the University of Bonn. The material presented is part of joint work with Dustin Clausen. This is intended as a stable citable version of the material. In the first half of this course, we develop the basic theory of liquid real vector spaces, which we used in another course
to give a new approach to complex-analytic geometry. In the second half, we gave a tentative definition of a category of analytic spaces that contains (for example) adic spaces and complex-analytic spaces. While the precise definition of analytic spaces represents an abandoned stepping stone on our way to define analytic stacks and hence should be seen as a historical artifact, much of the
surrounding discussion stays very relevant.

Lectures on Analytic Geometry Preface This is a slightly updated version of lectures notes for a course on analytic geometry taught in the winter term 2019/20 at the University of Bonn. The material presented is part of joint work with Dustin Clausen. This is intended as a stable citable version of the material. In the first half of this course, we develop the basic theory of liquid real vector
spaces, which is used in [ CS22 ] to give a new approach to complex-analytic geometry. In the second half, we gave a tentative definition of a category of analytic spaces that contains (for example) adic spaces and complex-analytic spaces. While the precise definition of analytic spaces represents an abandoned stepping stone on our way to define analytic stacks in [ CS26 ] and hence should be seen
as a historical artifact, much of the surrounding discussion stays very relevant. May 2026, Peter Scholze 0.1. Lecture I: Introduction Mumford writes in Curves and their Jacobians: "[Algebraic geometry] seems to have acquired the reputation of being esoteric, exclusive, and very abstract." The goal of this course is to launch a new attack, turning functional analysis into a branch of commutative algebra, and various types of analytic geometry (like manifolds) into algebraic geometry. [Standard background on condensed sets, profinite sets, the pro-étale site, solid abelian groups, Z[S]^square, measures, solidification, and profinite F_p-vector spaces follows, establishing the definitions and propositions cited by the claims below: Proposition 0.2.1, Remark 0.2.2, Definition 0.2.4, Theorem 0.2.5, Theorem 0.2.9, Proposition 0.2.6, Corollary 0.2.7, Proposition 0.2.8, Proposition 0.1.2, Lemma 0.1.3, Definition 0.1.1, the short exact sequence 0 -> (R,discrete) -> (R,natural) -> Q -> 0 of condensed abelian groups in Section 0.1.]
