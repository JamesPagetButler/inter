## Paper text

A fuzzy geometry is a certain type of spectral triple whose Dirac operator crucially turns out to be a finite matrix. This notion incorporates familiar examples like fuzzy spheres and fuzzy tori. In the framework of random noncommutative geometry, we use Barrett's characterization of Dirac operators of fuzzy geometries in order to systematically compute the spectral action S(D)=Tr f(D) for 2n-dimensional fuzzy geometries. In contrast to the original Chamseddine–Connes spectral action, we take a polynomial f with f(x)->infinity as |x|->infinity in order to obtain a well-defined path integral that can be stated as a random matrix model with action of the type S(D)=N·tr F + sum_i tr A_i · tr B_i, being F, A_i and B_i noncommutative polynomials in 2^{2n-1} complex NxN matrices that parametrize the Dirac operator D. For arbitrary signature formulas for 2-dimensional fuzzy geometries are given up to a sextic polynomial, and up to a quartic polynomial for 4-dimensional ones, with focus on the octo-matrix models for Lorentzian and Riemannian signatures. The noncommutative polynomials F, A_i and B_i are obtained via chord diagrams and satisfy: independence of N; self-adjointness of the main polynomial F (modulo cyclic reordering); also up to cyclicity, either self-adjointness or anti-self-adjointness of A_i and B_i simultaneously, for fixed i. This favors a free probabilistic perspective for the large-N limit.

Introduction [background on noncommutative geometry, the Standard Model derivation from the Chamseddine-Connes spectral action, gauge networks, path-integral quantization of noncommutative geometries Z=∫_M e^{-Tr f(D)} dD (1.1), fuzzy geometries as finite-dimensional geometries distinct from lattice/finite geometries (Krajewski diagrams), motivated by Barrett-Glaser's polynomial spectral action for Markov chain Monte Carlo statistics of eigenvalues of D.] Definition 2.1: A real, even spectral triple of KO-dimension s in Z/8Z consists of an algebra A with involution, a Hilbert space H with faithful *-representation rho, an antilinear real structure J, a chirality gamma with gamma^2=1, [rho(a),J rho(b) J^-1]=0, a self-adjoint D satisfying the order-one condition, and relations J^2=epsilon, JD=epsilon' DJ, J gamma = epsilon'' gamma J, with signs determined by s mod 8 per the table. Definition 2.2 (paraphrased from [Bar15]): a fuzzy geometry of signature (p,q) is given by a simple matrix algebra A=M_N(C), a Hermitian Cl(p,q)-module V with chirality gamma, a Hilbert space H=V⊗M_N(C), a left-A representation, three signs epsilon,epsilon',epsilon'' determined by s:=q-p via the same mod-8 table, a real structure J=C⊗*, and a self-adjoint D satisfying the order-one condition. Example 2.3 (Fuzzy d=2 geometries, from [Bar15]): Type (0,2): D^(0,2) = gamma^1·[L_1,-]+gamma^2·[L_2,-]. Type (1,1): D^(1,1)=gamma^1·{H,-}+gamma^2·[L,-]. Type (2,0): D^(2,0)=gamma^1·{H_1,-}+gamma^2·{H_2,-}. Proposition 3.1 gives the formula for Tr_{M_N(C)}(k_{I_1}...k_{I_r}) as a sum over subsets with signs and traces (eq 3.7). Proposition 3.2 gives the even powers of D, Tr(D^{2t})/dim V, as a sum over chord diagrams chi in CD_{2n} (eq 3.8). Definition 3.3 defines the action functional a_n(chi)[K] of a chord diagram (eq 3.10), the bi-trace functional b_n(chi) (eq 3.11), and the single-trace functional s_n(chi) via a_n(chi)=N s_n(chi)+b_n(chi). Corollary 3.4: for even d=p+q, (1/dim V) Tr f(D) = N sum_{2t<=m} f_{2t} S_{2t} + B, where B stands for products of two traces with N-independent coefficients (eq in Cor 3.4). Proposition 3.5 gives the formula for (1/dim V)Tr([D^{(p,q)}]^2) for odd and even d. Proposition 4.1: for a 2-dimensional fuzzy geometry with Dirac operator D, (1/2)Tr(D^6) = N·S_6[K_1,K_2] + B_6[K_1,K_2], with explicit single-trace and bi-trace parts given in terms of traces of products of K_1, K_2. Proposition 5.1 gives explicit chord-diagram formulas chi^{...} for the types tau_1..tau_4 using the Levi-Civita-type symbol delta_{alpha mu nu rho} (= 1 iff {alpha,mu,nu,rho}=Delta_4, else 0, eq 5.5).
