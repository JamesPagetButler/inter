# Restatement

## Introduction

**Setting.** A noncommutative geometry is given by a spectral triple $({\cal A},{\cal H},D)$ (1), where ${\cal A}$ is a noncommutative algebra with involution $*$ acting on the Hilbert space ${\cal H}$, and $D$ is a self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent such that

$$[D,a]\ \text{is bounded}\ \forall\,a\in{\cal A}. \tag{2}$$

The operator $D$ determines the metric on the state space of ${\cal A}$,

$$d(\varphi,\psi)=\mathop{\rm Sup}\nolimits\,\{|\varphi(a)-\psi(a)|;\ \|[D,a]\|\leq 1\}, \tag{3}$$

and the $KK$-homology fundamental class of $D$ [6].

**Index pairing.** In the even case the pairing between $K_0({\cal A})$ and the $KK$-homology class of $D$ is

$$[e]\in K_{0}({\cal A})\rightarrow \text{Index}\ D_{e}^{+}\in{\mathbb Z}. \tag{4}$$

Here $[e]$ is the class of an idempotent

$$e\in M_{r}({\cal A}),\quad e^{2}=e,\quad e=e^{*}, \tag{5}$$

and

$$D_{e}^{+}=e\,D^{+}e, \tag{6}$$

where $D^{+}=D\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{2}\right)$ is the restriction of $D$ to the range ${\cal H}^{+}$ of $\frac{1+\gamma}{2}$, and $\gamma$ is the ${\mathbb Z}/2$ grading of ${\cal H}$. In block form,

$$D=\begin{bmatrix}0&D_{+}^{*}\\ D_{+}&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad \gamma=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\ 0&-1\end{bmatrix}. \tag{7}$$

**Index formula.** The index pairing (4) is expressed by local cyclic cocycles on ${\cal A}$ [6], [12], [16]. When only the top component of the Chern character ${\rm Ch}(e)$ in cyclic homology is nonvanishing, the formula reduces to

$$\text{Index}\,D_{e}^{+}=(-1)^{m}\int\!\!\!\!\!\!-\ \gamma\left(e-\frac{1}{2}\right)[D,e]^{2m}\,D^{-2m}, \tag{8}$$

provided

$$\mathrm{ch}_{j}(e)=0\qquad j<m. \tag{9}$$

In (8), $\gamma$ is the ${\mathbb Z}/2$ grading, the resolvent of $D$ is of order $\frac{1}{2m}$ (its characteristic values $\mu_k$ satisfy $\mu_k=0(k^{-\frac{1}{2m}})$), and $\int\!\!\!\!\!\!-$ is the coefficient of the logarithmic divergence in the ordinary operator trace [15], [26].

**Cyclic-homology vanishing.** The vanishing (9) of the Chern character of $e$ in the cyclic homology of ${\cal A}$ is a stronger condition at the chain level than vanishing of the usual Chern differential form. For $m=1$ and $r=2$ in (5), commutative solutions with ${\cal A}=C^{\infty}(S^{2})$ are obtained, generated by the matrix components

$$e=[e_{ij}]\in M_{2}({\cal A}). \tag{10}$$

For $m=1$, commutativity is forced by $e^{2}=e$, $e=e^{*}$ and $\mathrm{ch}_{0}(e)=0$. For $m=2$ and $r=4$ in (5), commutative solutions with ${\cal A}=C^{\infty}(S^{4})$ were also found, but those computations used an ansatz and did not analyse the general solution, so noncommutative solutions were left open.

**Quartic equation and volume.** For any Riemannian metric on $S^{4}$ whose volume form equals that of the round metric, the corresponding Dirac operator satisfies

$$\left\langle\left(e-\frac{1}{2}\right)[D,e]^{4}\right\rangle=\gamma_{5}, \tag{11}$$

where $\langle\ \rangle$ denotes the projection on the commutant of $4\times 4$ matrices. Combined with the index formula, (11) gives a quantization of the volume,

$$\int\!\!\!\!\!\!-\ ds^{4}\in{\mathbb N},\qquad ds=D^{-1}. \tag{12}$$

It also fixes the leading term of the spectral action [3],

$$\text{Trace}\left(f\left(\frac{D}{\Lambda}\right)\right)=\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{2}\ \int\!\!\!\!\!\!-\ ds^{4}+\cdots \tag{13}$$

## Theorems

**Theorem 1.**
a) $E\in C^{\infty}(S^{2m},M_{r}(\mathbb C))$ satisfies $E=E^{2}=E^{*}$ and $\mathrm{ch}_{j}(E)=0$ for all $j<m$.
b) The Hochschild cycle $\omega=\mathrm{ch}_{m}(E)$ is the volume form of the round sphere $S^{2m}$.
c) Let $g$ be a Riemannian metric on $S^{2m}$ with volume form $\sqrt{g}\,d^{2m}x=\omega$. Then the corresponding Dirac operator $D$ satisfies
$$\left\langle\left(e-\frac{1}{2}\right)[D,e]^{2m}\right\rangle=\gamma,$$
where $e=E$ as above and $\langle\ \rangle$ denotes the projection on the commutant of $M_{r}(\mathbb C)$.

**Theorem 2.**
a) $e\in C^{\infty}(S^{4}_{\theta},M_{4}(\mathbb C))$ satisfies $e=e^{2}=e^{*}$ and $\mathrm{ch}_{j}(e)=0$ for all $j<2$.
b) ${\rm Gr}_{2,4}$ is a noncommutative space and $S^{4}_{\theta}\subset{\rm Gr}_{2,4}$.

**Theorem 3.**
a) The spectral triple $(C^{\infty}(S^{4}_{\theta}),{\cal H},D)$ fulfills all axioms of noncommutative manifolds.
b) Let $e\in C^{\infty}(S^{4}_{\theta},M_{4}(\mathbb C))$ be the canonical idempotent given in (III. 48). The Dirac operator $D$ satisfies
$$\left\langle\left(e-\frac{1}{2}\right)[D,e]^{4}\right\rangle=\gamma,$$
where $\langle\ \rangle$ denotes the projection on the commutant of $M_{4}(\mathbb C)$ and $\gamma$ is the grading operator.

**Theorem 6.** Let $M$ be a compact spin Riemannian manifold whose isometry group has rank $\geq 2$. Then $M$ admits a natural one-parameter isospectral deformation to noncommutative geometries $M_{\theta}$.

## Lemmas

**Lemma 4.**
a) Let $x$ be a homogeneous operator of bidegree $(n_1,n_2)$ and $y$ a homogeneous operator of bidegree $(n^{\prime}_{1},n^{\prime}_{2})$. Then
$$l(x)\,r(y)-r(y)\,l(x)=(xy-yx)\,\lambda^{n^{\prime}_{1}n_{2}}\,\lambda^{n_{2}p_{1}+n^{\prime}_{1}p_{2}}. \tag{89}$$
In particular, $[l(x),r(y)]=0$ if $[x,y]=0$.
b) Let $x$ and $y$ be homogeneous operators as before, and define
$$x*y=\lambda^{n^{\prime}_{1}n_{2}}\,xy. \tag{90}$$
Then $l(x)\,l(y)=l(x*y)$.
