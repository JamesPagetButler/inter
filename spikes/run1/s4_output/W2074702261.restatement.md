# Restatement

## Abstract

We show that the first five of the axioms we had formulated on spectral triples suffice (in a slightly stronger form) to characterize the spectral triples associated to smooth compact manifolds. The algebra, which is assumed to be commutative, is shown to be isomorphic to the algebra of all smooth functions on a unique smooth oriented compact manifold, while the operator is shown to be of Dirac type and the metric to be Riemannian.

## 1. Introduction

The problem of spectral characterization of manifolds was initially formulated as an open question in [11]. Given a spectral triple $({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)$ with ${\mathcal{A}}$ commutative, the problem is to show that ${\mathcal{A}}$ is the algebra $C^{\infty}(X)$ of smooth functions on a (unique) smooth compact manifold $X$. The five conditions of [11], in dimension $p$, are:

1. The $n$-th characteristic value of the resolvent of $D$ is $O(n^{-1/p})$.
2. $\left[[D,a],b\right]=0 \quad \forall\,a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}$.
3. For any $a\in{\mathcal{A}}$, both $a$ and $[D,a]$ belong to the domain of $\delta^{m}$, for any integer $m$, where $\delta$ is the derivation $\delta(T)=[|D|,T]$.
4. There exists a Hochschild cycle $c\in Z_{p}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{A}})$ such that $\pi_{D}(c)=1$ for $p$ odd, while for $p$ even, $\pi_{D}(c)=\gamma$ is a ${\mathbb{Z}}/2$ grading.
5. Viewed as an ${\mathcal{A}}$-module, the space ${\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}=\cap{\rm Dom}\,D^{m}$ is finite and projective. Moreover the following equality defines a hermitian structure $(\ |\ )$ on this module:
$$\langle\xi,a\,\eta\rangle={\int\!\!\!\!\!\!-}\,a(\xi|\eta)\,|D|^{-p}\,,\ \forall a\in{\mathcal{A}},\ \forall\xi,\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}.$$

The notation is recalled in § 2. The strategy of [11] uses the components $a_{\alpha}^{j}$ ($j>0$) of the cycle $c=\sum a_{\alpha}^{0}\otimes a_{\alpha}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{\alpha}^{p}$ as tentative local charts. Three difficulties are identified:

- a) Show that the spectrum $X$ of ${\mathcal{A}}$ is large enough that the range of the "local charts" $a_{\alpha}$ contains an open set in ${\mathbb{R}}^{p}$.
- b) Show that the joint spectral measure of the components $a_{\alpha}^{j}$ ($j>0$) of a "local chart" is the Lebesgue measure.
- c) Apply the basic inequality ([8], [9], Proposition IV.3.14), giving an upper bound on the Voiculescu obstruction [25], and use [25] Theorem 4.5 to show that the "local charts" are locally injective.

The paper [22] considers this problem. The implicit function theorem is used for a). Under the assumption that continuous $*$-derivations of ${\mathcal{A}}$ exponentiate, i.e. are generators of one-parameter groups of automorphisms of ${\mathcal{A}}$, §§ 5 and 6 show that enough self-adjoint derivations exponentiate. Proposition 6.16 is used for b), and a local form of the inequality of [8], [9] (Proposition IV.3.14) is proved in § 9 for c). The proof is concluded in § 11.

**Theorem 1.1.** Let $({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)$ be a spectral triple, with ${\mathcal{A}}$ commutative, fulfilling the first five conditions of [11] (cf. § 2) in a slightly stronger form, i.e. we assume that:

- The regularity holds for all ${\mathcal{A}}$-endomorphisms of $\cap{\rm Dom}\,D^{m}$.
- The Hochschild cycle $c$ is antisymmetric.

Then there exists a compact oriented smooth manifold $X$ such that ${\mathcal{A}}$ is the algebra $C^{\infty}(X)$ of smooth functions on $X$. Moreover every compact oriented smooth manifold appears in this spectral manner.

**Theorem 1.2.** Let $({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)$ be a spectral triple with ${\mathcal{A}}$ commutative, fulfilling the first five conditions of [11] (cf. § 2) with the cycle $c$ antisymmetric. Assume that the multiplicity of the action of ${\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}$ in ${\mathcal{H}}$ is $2^{p/2}$. Then there exists a smooth oriented compact ($spin^c$) manifold $X$ such that ${\mathcal{A}}=C^{\infty}(X)$.

It follows from [11] (cf. [14] for the proof) that the operator $D$ is then a Dirac operator. The reality condition selects spin manifolds among $spin^c$, and the spectral action ([3]) selects the Levi-Civita connection.

The introduction also contains remarks in § 12 on the interpretation of Theorem 1.2 via the coexistence of a commutative von Neumann algebra $M$ and an operator $(\mathcal{H}, D)$ related by a unitary $F$, with the Yukawa coupling parameters of the Standard Model ([6]), and a result of M. Hilsum on finite propagation ([17]). Appendices § 13 (equivalent formulations of the regularity condition) and § 14 (the Dixmier trace and its relation with the heat expansion) are mentioned.

## 2. Preliminaries

**Lemma 2.1.** The following conditions are equivalent for $T\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime\prime}$:

1. $T\in{\mathcal{A}}$.
2. $[D,T]$ is bounded and both $T$ and $[D,T]$ belong to the domain of $\delta^{m}$, for any integer $m$.
3. $T$ belongs to the domain of $\delta^{m}$, for any integer $m$.
4. $T{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$.

**Proposition 2.2.**

1. The unbounded derivation $\delta$ is a closed operator in ${\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})$.
2. The algebra ${\mathcal{A}}$ endowed with the norms $p_{k}$ is a Frechet algebra.
3. The semi-norms $p_{k}([D,a])=p^{\prime}_{k}(a)$ are continuous.

**Proposition 2.3.**

1. When endowed with the norms (13), ${\mathcal{A}}$ is a Frechet separable nuclear space.
2. One has Sobolev estimates of the form
$$p_{k}(a)\leq c_{k}\|a\|_{s_{k}}^{\rm sobolev}\,,\qquad p_{k}([D,a])\leq c^{\prime}_{k}\|a\|_{s^{\prime}_{k}}^{\rm sobolev}\,,\qquad \forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}$$
with $c_{k}<\infty$, $c^{\prime}_{k}<\infty$ and suitable sequences $s_{k}>0$, $s^{\prime}_{k}>0$.
3. The spectrum $X={\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}})$ is metrizable.
4. Any $T\in{\mbox{End}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$ is continuous in ${\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$ and extends continuously to a bounded operator in ${\mathcal{H}}$.
5. The algebraic isomorphism ${\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}=e{\mathcal{A}}^{n}$ is topological.
6. The map $(a,\xi)\mapsto a\xi$ and the ${\mathcal{A}}$-valued inner product are jointly continuous ${\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$ and ${\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}\times{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}\to{\mathcal{A}}$.

**Proposition 2.4.** Let $a_{j}=a_{j}^{*}$ be $n$ self-adjoint elements of ${\mathcal{A}}$, and let $f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mapsto{\mathbb{C}}$ be a smooth function defined on a neighborhood of the joint spectrum of the $a_{j}$. Then the element $f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in A$ belongs to ${\mathcal{A}}\subset A$.

## 3. Exponentiation of derivations

**Definition 3.1.** Let ${\mathcal{A}}$ be a Frechet pre-$C^{*}$-algebra. A continuous $*$-derivation $\delta_{0}\in{\mbox{Der}}{\mathcal{A}}$ exponentiates iff there is a unique solution, depending continuously on $(t,a)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathcal{A}}$, of the differential equation
$$\partial_{t}\,y(t,a)=\delta_{0}(y(t,a))\,,\qquad y(0,a)=a\,. \tag{21}$$
We say that ${\mathcal{A}}$ is expable when any continuous $*$-derivation $\delta_{0}\in{\mbox{Der}}{\mathcal{A}}$ exponentiates.

**Proposition 3.2.** For any $a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}$,
$$y(t,ab)=y(t,a)y(t,b)\,,\qquad y(t,a^{*})=y(t,a)^{*}\,,\qquad y(t,a+b)=y(t,a)+y(t,b)\,. \tag{23}$$
$$y(t_{1}+t_{2},a)=y(t_{1},y(t_{2},a))\,,\qquad y(t,\delta_{0}(a))=\delta_{0}(y(t,a))\,. \tag{24}$$
Moreover $y(t,a)$ is a smooth function of $(t,a)$, with $n$-th derivative given by
$$D^{n}y(t,a,s_{1},h_{1},\ldots,s_{n},h_{n})=\delta_{0}^{n}(y(t,a))\prod s_{j}+\sum_{i}\delta_{0}^{n-1}y(t,h_{i})\prod_{j\neq i}s_{j}\,. \tag{25}$$

**Lemma 3.3.** Let ${\mathcal{A}}$ be commutative, and let $a=(a^{j})$ be $p$ self-adjoint elements of ${\mathcal{A}}$. Let $\chi$ be a character of ${\mathcal{A}}$. Assume that there exist $p$ derivations $\delta_{j}\in{\mbox{Der}}{\mathcal{A}}$ such that:

- Each $\delta_{j}$ exponentiates.
- The determinant of the matrix $\chi(\delta_{j}(a^{k}))$ does not vanish.

Then the image under $a$ of any neighborhood of $\chi$ in the spectrum ${\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}})$ of ${\mathcal{A}}$ contains a neighborhood of $a(\chi)$ in ${\mathbb{R}}^{p}$.

**Lemma 3.4.** Under the hypothesis of Lemma 3.3, there exist a smooth family $\sigma_{t}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{A}})$, $t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}$, a neighborhood $Z$ of $\chi$ in $X={\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}})$, and a neighborhood $W$ of $0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}$ such that, for any $\kappa\in Z$, the map $t\mapsto a(\kappa\circ\sigma_{t})$ is a diffeomorphism, depending continuously on $\kappa$, of $W$ with a neighborhood of $a(\kappa)$ in ${\mathbb{R}}^{p}$.

## 4. Commutators and the local charts

**Definition 4.1.** Let $T_{j}\in{\mathcal{B}}$ be elements of a noncommutative algebra ${\mathcal{B}}$. One sets
$$[T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n}]=\sum_{\sigma}\epsilon(\sigma)\,T_{\sigma(1)}T_{\sigma(2)}\cdots T_{\sigma(n)}$$
where $\sigma$ varies through all permutations of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and $\epsilon(\sigma)$ is its signature.

**Proposition 4.2.** Let $T_{j}\in{\mathcal{B}}$ be elements of a noncommutative algebra ${\mathcal{B}}$.

a) For any permutation $\alpha$ of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, $[T_{\alpha(1)},T_{\alpha(2)},\ldots,T_{\alpha(n)}]=\epsilon(\alpha)\,[T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n}]$.

b) If two of the $T_{j}$ are equal, then $[T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n}]=0$.

c) Let ${\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}$ be a commutative subalgebra and ${\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}$ its relative commutant in ${\mathcal{B}}$. Let $a_{k}^{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}$ and $\gamma_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}$. Then, with $T_{k}=\sum a_{k}^{j}\gamma_{j}$,
$$[T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n}]={\rm Det}((a_{k}^{j}))\,[\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots,\gamma_{n}]\tag{28}$$

d) Equality (28) extends to the case of a rectangular matrix $a_{k}^{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}$ as follows:
$$[T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n}]=\sum_{F}{\rm Det}((a_{k}^{j}(F)))\,[\gamma_{1}(F),\gamma_{2}(F),\ldots,\gamma_{n}(F)]\tag{29}$$
where the sum is over all subsets $F\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}$ with $\#F=n$, the matrix $a_{k}^{j}(F)$ is the restriction of $a_{k}^{j}$ to $j\in F$, and the $\gamma_{j}(F)$ are the $\gamma_{i}$, $i\in F$, ordered with increasing index in $F$.

**Lemma 4.3.** Let ${\mathcal{B}}$ be the algebra of endomorphisms of ${\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$. One has a finite decomposition
$$[D,a]=\sum_{j}\delta_{j}(a)\,\gamma_{j}\,,\qquad \forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}\,, \tag{31}$$
where $\gamma_{j}\in{\mathcal{B}}$ and the $\delta_{j}$ are derivations of the form
$$\delta_{j}(a)=i(\xi_{j}|[D,a]\xi_{j})\,,\qquad \forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}\,, \tag{32}$$
for some $\xi_{j}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$.

**Lemma 4.4.** The $U_{\alpha}$ form an open cover of $X={\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}})$. Each $U_{\alpha}$ is the disjoint union of the two open subsets $U_{\alpha}^{\pm}$ corresponding to the sign of $\rho_{\alpha}$:
$$\pm\rho_{\alpha}(x)>0\,,\qquad \forall x\in U_{\alpha}^{\pm}\,.$$

**Lemma 4.5.** Assume that derivations of the form (32) exponentiate. Let $\chi\in U_{\alpha}$.

- There exist $p$ derivations $\delta_{j}\in{\mbox{Der}}({\mathcal{A}})$ such that $\chi({\rm Det}((\delta_{j}(a_{\alpha}^{k}))))\neq 0$.
- The map $a_{\alpha}$ from $U_{\alpha}$ to ${\mathbb{R}}^{p}$ is open.
- There exist a smooth family $\sigma_{t}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{A}})$, $t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}$, a neighborhood $Z$ of $\chi$ in $X={\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}})$, and a neighborhood $W$ of $0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}$ such that, for any $\kappa\in Z$, the map $t\mapsto s_{\alpha}(\kappa\circ\sigma_{t})$ is a diffeomorphism, depending continuously on $\kappa$, of $W$ with a neighborhood of $a(\kappa)$ in ${\mathbb{R}}^{p}$.

## 5. Dissipativity and regularity

**Lemma 5.1.** Assume that the derivations of the form (32) exponentiate. Then, for any $h=h^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}$, the commutator $[D,h]$ vanishes where $h$ reaches its maximum.[^1] Conversely, if this property holds, the derivations $\pm\delta_{j}$ of the form (32) are dissipative (cf. [2], Definition 1.4.6), i.e.
$$\|x+\lambda\delta_{j}(x)\|\geq\|x\|\,,\qquad \forall x\in{\mathcal{A}}\,,\ \lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\,. \tag{42}$$

[^1]: This makes sense since $[D,h]$ commutes with $h$.

**Lemma 5.2.** Let $T$ preserve ${\rm Dom}\,D$ and let $[D,T]$ be bounded. Then the function $t\mapsto\alpha_{t}(T)$ is norm continuous,
$$\|\alpha_{s}(T)-\alpha_{t}(T)\|\leq|s-t|\,\|[D,T]\| \tag{46}$$
and, as $s\to 0$, the difference quotient
$$\frac{\alpha_{s}(T)-T}{s}=\frac{i}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\alpha_{t}([D,T])\,dt \tag{47}$$
converges to $i[D,T]$ in the strong topology.

**Lemma 5.3.** Let $h=h^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}$ and $\chi\in{\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}})$. If $[[D^{2},h],h]$ vanishes at $\chi$, then $[D,h]$ vanishes at $\chi$.

**Lemma 5.4.** For any $h=h^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}$, $h\geq 0$, reaching its maximum at $\chi\in{\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}})$, and any sequence $b_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}$, $\|b_{n}\|\leq 1$, with support tending to $\{\chi\}$, one has
$$\|b_{n}^{*}[|D|,h]b_{n}\|\to 0\,. \tag{51}$$

**Remark 5.5.** In the example (43), $[D,\phi]$ does not map ${\rm Dom}\,D^{2}$ to ${\rm Dom}\,D$, so $D\,[D,\phi]\xi$ does not make sense in that case. Regularity fails, and ${\rm Dom}\,D^{2}$ is not invariant under $\phi$, unless $[D,\phi]$ vanishes on the boundary. The boundary condition for $D^{2}$ is
$$\xi=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\ \xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in{\rm Dom}\,D^{2}\Leftrightarrow \xi_{1}(0)=0\,,\ \partial_{x}\xi_{2}(0)=0\,,\ \xi_{2}(1)=0\,,\ \partial_{x}\xi_{1}(1)=0\,. \tag{57}$$
This contains the Neumann condition $\partial_{x}\xi_{2}(0)=0$ while $\xi_{2}(0)$ is arbitrary. Thus $\partial_{x}\phi\,\xi_{2}(0)=\partial_{x}\phi(0)\,\xi_{2}(0)$ vanishes only when $\partial_{x}\phi(0)=0$.

**Lemma 5.6.** Assume that, as $\tau\to\infty$, the following limit holds in the strong topology:
$$\lim_{\tau\to\infty}e^{i\tau h}[|D|,h]e^{-i\tau h}=T\,.$$
Then, with strong convergence on ${\rm Dom}\,D$,
$$\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\tau^{-1}\,e^{i\tau h}|D|e^{-i\tau h}=-iT\,.$$

**Lemma 5.7.** With $h$ as above, one has:

1. $C_{\tau}$ is a completely positive map from ${\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})$ to itself, and $C_{\tau}(1)=1$.
2. $C_{\tau}(aTb)=aC_{\tau}(T)b$ for all $a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}$.
3. $C_{\tau}([|D|,h])=\dfrac{i}{\tau}\,(e^{i\tau h}|D|e^{-i\tau h}-|D|)$.

**Lemma 5.8.** One has, with $X(\epsilon)=\epsilon D-i[D,h]$,
$$|X(\epsilon)|=Y(\epsilon)+f_{0}(X(\epsilon)) \tag{67}$$
where
$$Y(\epsilon)\xi=\frac{2}{\pi}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{X(\epsilon)^{2}}{1+u^{2}+X(\epsilon)^{2}}\,\xi\,du\,,\qquad \forall\xi\in{\rm Dom}\,D \tag{68}$$
and
$$f_{0}(x)=|x|-x^{2}(1+x^{2})^{-1/2}\,,\qquad \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}\,. \tag{69}$$

**Lemma 5.9.** Let $h=h^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}$. There exists $\lambda<\infty$ such that
$$\|\theta_{\lambda}((1+u^{2}+X(0)^{2})^{-1})\|\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+u^{2}\right)^{-1}\,,\qquad \forall u\,. \tag{71}$$

**Lemma 5.10.** Let $h=h^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}$. Then, as $\epsilon\to 0$,
$$Y(\epsilon)\xi\to Y(0)\xi\,,\qquad \forall\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}\,. \tag{75}$$

**Proposition 5.11.** Let $h=h^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}$. Then, with norm convergence,
$$\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\tau^{-1}\,e^{i\tau h}|D|e^{-i\tau h}\xi=|[D,h]|\,\xi\,,\qquad \forall\xi\in{\rm Dom}\,D\,. \tag{77}$$

**Remark 5.12.** Proposition 5.11 shows that, under the regularity hypothesis,
$$[\,|[D,h]|,[D,a]\,]=0\,,\qquad \forall h=h^{*},\ a\in{\mathcal{A}}\,. \tag{78}$$
Indeed, $[e^{i\tau h}|D|e^{-i\tau h},[D,a]]=e^{i\tau h}[|D|,[D,a]]e^{-i\tau h}$, and the norm of $[|D|,[D,a]]$ is finite, so $\tau^{-1}\|[e^{i\tau h}|D|e^{-i\tau h},[D,a]]\|\to 0$ as $\tau\to\infty$. Since $[D,a]$ preserves ${\rm Dom}\,D$, the result follows. Under the strong regularity hypothesis of Definition 6.1 below, the same argument shows that
$$[D,h]^{2}\in{\mathcal{A}}\,,\qquad \forall h=h^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}\,. \tag{79}$$
Indeed $|[D,h]|$ then commutes with all endomorphisms of ${\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$. Its square $[D,h]^{2}$, being itself an endomorphism, belongs to the centre of ${\mbox{End}}_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\infty})$ and is, by (9), an element of ${\mathcal{A}}$.

**Theorem 5.13.** Let $({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)$ be a regular spectral triple with ${\mathcal{A}}$ commutative, fulfilling the order one condition. Then, for any $h=h^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}$, the commutator $[D,h]$ vanishes where $h$ reaches its maximum, i.e. for any sequence $b_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}$, $\|b_{n}\|\leq 1$, with support tending to $\{\chi\}$, where $\chi$ is a character such that $|\chi(h)|$ is maximum,
$$\|[D,h]\,b_{n}\|\to 0\,.$$

**Corollary 5.14.** Let $({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)$ be a spectral triple with ${\mathcal{A}}$ commutative fulfilling the five conditions of § 2. The derivations $\pm\delta_{j}$ of Lemma 4.3 are dissipative.

**Corollary 5.15.** Let $h=h^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}$. The principal symbol of the operator
$${\rm Grad}(h)=[D^{2},h] \tag{83}$$
vanishes where $h$ reaches its maximum.

## 6. Strong regularity and exponentiation

**Definition 6.1.** A spectral triple is strongly regular when all endomorphisms of the ${\mathcal{A}}$-module ${\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$ are regular.

**Lemma 6.2.** Let $A$ be regular. Then $A\,{\rm Dom}\,D\subset{\rm Dom}\,D$, and the adjoint of $A^{*}D$ is the closure of the densely defined operator $T$:
$${\rm Dom}\,T={\rm Dom}\,D\,,\qquad T\xi=D(A\xi)\,,\quad\forall\xi\in{\rm Dom}\,D\,. \tag{85}$$

**Corollary 6.3.** Let $\varphi=\varphi^{*}\in{\mathcal{A}}$. Then the operator $H=\varphi D\varphi$ with domain ${\rm Dom}\,D$ is essentially self-adjoint.

**Lemma 6.4.** Let $A$ be regular. Then $A^{*}D$ is closable, and:

- For any $\xi$ in the domain of the closure $\overline{A^{*}D}$ of $A^{*}D$, and $\epsilon>0$,
$$(1+\epsilon|D|)^{-1}\overline{A^{*}D}\,\xi=A^{*}D(1+\epsilon|D|)^{-1}\xi-(1+\epsilon|D|)^{-1}[|D|,A^{*}]\,\epsilon D(1+\epsilon|D|)^{-1}\xi\,. \tag{90}$$
- The domain of $\overline{A^{*}D}$ is the set of $\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}$ for which $A^{*}D(1+\epsilon|D|)^{-1}\xi$ converges in norm as $\epsilon\to 0$.
- The limit of $A^{*}D(1+\epsilon|D|)^{-1}\xi$ gives $\overline{A^{*}D}\,\xi$.

**Proposition 6.5.** Let $A$ be regular. Then the operator $H=A^{*}DA$ with domain ${\rm Dom}\,D$ is essentially self-adjoint. The domain of the closure of $H$ is the set of $\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}$ for which $A^{*}DA(1+\epsilon|D|)^{-1}\xi$ converges in norm as $\epsilon\to 0$, and the limit of $A^{*}DA(1+\epsilon|D|)^{-1}\xi$ gives $\overline{H}\,\xi$.

**Lemma 6.6.** Let $\xi,\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$. Then the following defines an endomorphism of the ${\mathcal{A}}$-module ${\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$:
$$T_{\xi,\eta}(\zeta)=(\eta|\zeta)\,\xi\,,\qquad \forall\zeta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty} \tag{91}$$
where $(\eta|\zeta)$ is the ${\mathcal{A}}$-valued inner product. One has
$$T_{a\xi,\,b\eta}=ab^{*}\,T_{\xi,\eta}\,,\qquad \forall a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}\,,\qquad T_{\xi,\eta}^{*}=T_{\eta,\xi}\,. \tag{92}$$

**Lemma 6.7.** One has
$$D_{\xi,\,\eta}\zeta=-i\,\delta_{0}((\eta|\zeta))\,\eta+(\xi|D\xi)\,T_{\eta,\,\eta}\zeta\,,\qquad \forall\xi,\eta,\zeta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty} \tag{95}$$
The operator $V_{\eta}(a)=a\eta$, $\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}$, extends to a bounded linear map $V_{\eta}$ from $L^{2}(X,d\lambda)$ to ${\mathcal{H}}$, and one has
$$V_{\eta}^{*}V_{\eta}=(\eta|\eta)\,,\qquad V_{\eta}V_{\eta}^{*}=T_{\eta,\,\eta}\,,\qquad V_{\eta}^{*}(\zeta)=(\eta|\zeta)\,,\qquad \forall\zeta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}\,. \tag{96}$$

**Lemma 6.8.** Let $T$ be regular.

1. The operators ${\mathcal{E}}^{k}_{\lambda}(T)$ are compact for $k>0$ and converge in norm to $0$ as $\lambda\to\infty$.
2. (With $\lambda\geq 1$.)
$$\|{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}(T)D\|\leq 2\|\delta(T)\|+\|\delta^{2}(T)\|\,,\qquad \|D{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}(T)\|\leq 2\|\delta(T)\|+3\|\delta^{2}(T)\|+\|\delta^{3}(T)\| \tag{103}$$
3. For $k>1$, the operators $D\,{\mathcal{E}}^{k}_{\lambda}(T)$ and ${\mathcal{E}}^{k}_{\lambda}(T)\,D$ are compact and converge in norm to $0$ as $\lambda\to\infty$.

**Lemma 6.9.**

1. For any integer $N\in{\mathbb{N}}$, there exist $\lambda<\infty$ and $\epsilon_{0}>0$ such that the following operator with domain ${\rm Dom}\,D$ is closable and invertible in ${\mathcal{H}}$ for any $\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}$:
$$\theta_{\lambda}^{N}(1+i\epsilon S_{\xi,\eta})\,,\qquad S_{\xi,\eta}=D_{\xi,\eta}-(\xi|D\xi)\,T_{\eta,\eta}\,,$$
and the norm of its inverse satisfies
$$\|(\theta_{\lambda}^{N}(1+i\epsilon S_{\xi,\eta}))^{-1}\|\leq 1+N c_{\xi,\eta}\,\epsilon\,, \tag{106}$$
where $c_{\xi,\eta}<\infty$ depends only on $\xi$ and $\eta$.
2. For any integer $N$ there exists $\epsilon_{N}>0$ such that (97) can be solved in ${\mathcal{H}}_{N}={\rm Dom}\,|D|^{N}$.

**Lemma 6.10.** Assuming strong regularity, for $\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}$,
$$\|(\eta|\zeta)\|_{s}^{\rm sobolev}\leq C_{s}\,\|(1+D^{2})^{s/2}\zeta\|\,. \tag{111}$$

**Theorem 6.11.** Let $({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)$ be a strongly regular spectral triple with ${\mathcal{A}}$ commutative, fulfilling the five conditions of § 2. Then any derivation of ${\mathcal{A}}$ of the form (32), i.e. $\delta_{0}(a)=i(\xi|[D,a]\xi)$, $\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}$, is closable for the $C^{*}$-norm of $A$, and its closure is the generator of a one-parameter group of automorphisms $U(t)$ of $A=C(X)$, $X={\rm Spec}({\mathcal{A}})$.

**Lemma 6.12.** Let $x\in A$ have support disjoint from the support of $\xi$. Then $U(t)(x)=x$ for all $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$.

**Lemma 6.13.** Let $S$ be the closure of $S_{\xi,\eta}=D_{\xi,\eta}-(\xi|D\xi)\,T_{\eta,\eta}$ as an unbounded operator in ${\mathcal{H}}$. Then, for any $a\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta_{0}$, one has $a\eta\in{\rm Dom}\,S$ and
$$S(a\eta)=-i\,\delta_{0}(a)\,\eta\,. \tag{115}$$
For any $a\in A$ and $\epsilon>0$, let $b=(1+\epsilon\delta_{0})^{-1}(a)$. Then $b\eta\in{\rm Dom}\,S$ and
$$(1+i\epsilon S)(b\eta)=a\eta\,. \tag{116}$$

**Lemma 6.14.** The one-parameter group $U(t)\in{\rm Aut}(A)$ fulfills, for each $N$, an estimate of the form
$$\|U(t)(a)\|_{N}^{\rm sobolev}\leq c_{1}\,e^{N c_{\xi,\eta}|t|}\,\|a\|_{N}^{\rm sobolev}\,. \tag{117}$$

**Theorem 6.15.** Let $({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)$ be a strongly regular spectral triple with ${\mathcal{A}}$ commutative, fulfilling the five conditions of § 2. Then any derivation of ${\mathcal{A}}$ of the form (32), i.e. $\delta_{0}(a)=i(\xi|[D,a]\xi)$, $\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}$, is the generator of a one-parameter group of automorphisms $\sigma_{t}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{A}})$ such that:

- $\partial_{t}\sigma_{t}(a)=\delta_{0}(\sigma_{t}(a))$.
- The map $(t,a)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\mapsto\sigma_{t}(a)\in{\mathcal{A}}$ is jointly continuous.

**Proposition 6.16.** Let $({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)$, $\delta_{0}$ and $\sigma_{t}$ be as in Theorem 6.15. Then, for each $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$, the measure $\lambda$ of (5) is strongly[^2] equivalent to its transform under $\sigma_{t}$.

[^2]: $\mu$ is strongly equivalent to $\nu$ iff there is $c>0$ with $c\,\nu\leq\mu\leq c^{-1}\nu$.

## 7. Absolute continuity of the spectral measure

**Lemma 7.1.** For any open set $V\subset X$, the following two measures are strongly equivalent:

- The restriction $\lambda|_{V}$ to $V$ of the measure $\lambda$ of (130).
- The restriction to $V$ of the spectral measure associated to a vector $\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\infty}$ whose ${\mathcal{A}}$-valued inner product $(\xi,\xi)$ is strictly positive on $\overline{V}$.

**Lemma 7.2.** Let $V\subset U_{\alpha}$ be an open set and $\chi\in V$. There exist a smooth family $\sigma_{t}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{A}})$, $t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}$, a neighborhood $Z$ of $\chi$ in $V$, and $\epsilon>0$, $\epsilon^{\prime}>0$ such that:

1. For any $\kappa\in Z$, the map $t\mapsto s_{\alpha}(\sigma_{t}(\kappa))=F(\kappa,t)$ is a diffeomorphism, depending continuously on $\kappa$, of $B_{\epsilon}$ with a neighborhood of $s_{\alpha}(\kappa)$ in ${\mathbb{R}}^{p}$, and
$$s_{\alpha}(\kappa)+B_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\subset F(\kappa,B_{\epsilon/2})\,,\qquad \forall\kappa\in\overline{Z}\,. \tag{132}$$
2. For any $t\in B_{\epsilon}$,
$$\frac{1}{2}\,\lambda\leq\sigma_{t}(\lambda)\leq 2\lambda\,. \tag{133}$$
3. $Z_{1}=\cap_{B_{\epsilon}}\sigma_{t}Z$ is a neighborhood of $\chi$.
4. $Z_{2}=\cup_{B_{\epsilon}}\sigma_{t}Z$ is contained in $V$.

**Lemma 7.3.** Let $V\subset X$ be an open set with $\overline{V}\subset U_{\alpha}$. Let $\lambda_{V}$ (respectively $\lambda_{\overline{V}}$) be the spectral measure of the restriction to $V$ (respectively $\overline{V}$) of the representation of $C(X)$ in ${\mathcal{H}}$. Then $s_{\alpha}(\lambda_{V})$ is equivalent to the Lebesgue measure on $s_{\alpha}(V)$, and there exists $c<\infty$ such that
$$\int_{\overline{V}}f\circ s_{\alpha}\,d\lambda_{\overline{V}}\leq c\,\int_{s_{\alpha}(\overline{V})}f(x)\,dx^{p}\,,\qquad \forall f\in C_{c}^{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{p})\,. \tag{136}$$

## 8. Multiplicity

**Lemma 8.1.** Let $s:X\to Y$ be a continuous open map of compact spaces. Then the function $n(y)=\#s^{-1}(y)$ is lower semicontinuous on $Y$.

**Lemma 8.2.** If the measures $\mu_{k}$ are mutually singular, then the spectral multiplicity function $\Sigma(y)$ is equal to $1$ a.e.

**Lemma 8.3.** Let $X$ be a compact space and $\lambda$ a finite positive measure on $X$, and let $\pi$ be the representation[^3] of $C(X)$ in $L^{2}(X,d\lambda)$ by multiplication. Let $a_{j}=a_{j}^{*}\in C(X)$, and let $s$ be the map from $X$ to ${\mathbb{R}}^{p}$ with coordinates $a_{j}$. Let $U\subset X$ be an open set and $\nu$ a measure on ${\mathbb{R}}^{p}$, and assume that:

- The restriction of $s$ to $U$ is an open mapping.
- For every open subset $V\subset U$, the image $s(\lambda|_{V})$ is equivalent to the restriction of $\nu$ to $s(V)$.

Let $V\subset U$ be an open set and consider the operators $T_{j}=\pi(a_{j})|_{V}$, obtained by restricting $\pi(a_{j})$ to the subspace $L^{2}(V,d\lambda)\subset L^{2}(X,d\lambda)$. Then the joint spectral measure of the $T_{j}$ is $\nu|_{s(V)}$, and the spectral multiplicity $\Sigma(y)$ satisfies
$$\Sigma(y)\geq n(y)=\#\{s^{-1}(y)\cap V\}\,,\qquad \forall y\in s(V)\,, \tag{150}$$
almost everywhere modulo $\nu$.
