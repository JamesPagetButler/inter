# Restatement

## Abstract (main result)

We extend Stone duality to a fully faithful embedding of condensed sets into fpqc sheaves over an arbitrary field, which preserves colimits and finite limits. We study how familiar notions from condensed mathematics/topology and algebraic geometry correspond to each other under this form of Stone duality.

## Main results

**Theorem A** (Stone duality for condensed sets; $\mathbf{F}_2$-version). The equivalence of categories (1) induces an adjunction between condensed sets and fpqc sheaves over $\mathbf{F}_2$:
$$\mathrm{Cond}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_{\mathbf{F}_2},$$
whose left adjoint constituent preserves all finite limits and is fully faithful.

**Theorem B** (Theorems 3.11, 3.39; Propositions 3.16, 3.2, 3.21, 3.32; Corollary 2.18). Stone duality gives a dictionary between condensed mathematics and algebraic geometry, under which:

| Condensed side | Algebro-geometric side |
|---|---|
| condensed set $X$ | fpqc sheaf $\mathcal{X}$ |
| $X$ is a profinite set | $\mathcal{X}$ is an affine scheme |
| $X$ is a compact Hausdorff space | $\mathcal{X}$ is a geometric space |
| $X$ is a CGWH space | $\mathcal{X}$ is an ind-geometric space |
| $X$ is an arbitrary condensed set | $\mathcal{X}$ is a quotient of an ind-geometric space by an ind-geometric equivalence relation |
| $X$ is a discrete condensed set | $\mathcal{X}$ is an algebraic space locally of finite presentation |
| open/closed embeddings into $X$ | open/closed immersions into $\mathcal{X}$ |
| the underlying set $X(*)$ | the set of $\mathbf{F}_2$-points $\mathcal{X}(\mathbf{F}_2)$ |
| $\Hom_{\mathrm{Cond}}(X,\mathbf{F}_2)$ | $\Gamma(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})$ |
| $\mathrm{Shv}_{\Mod_{\mathbf{F}_2}}(X)$ | $\QCoh(\mathcal{X})$ |

Here a geometric space is roughly an fpqc-version of an algebraic space.

**Theorem C** (Stone duality for condensed sets; $k$-version, Theorem 2.6). Let $k$ be a field. There is a canonical adjunction between condensed sets and fpqc sheaves over $k$:
$$\mathrm{Cond}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_{k},$$
whose left adjoint constituent preserves all finite limits and is fully faithful.

## Section 1: Stone $k$-algebras

**Definition 1.1.** Fix a base ring $k$. The category of Stone $k$-algebras $\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}\subseteq\mathrm{CAlg}_k$ is the full subcategory of commutative $k$-algebras generated under filtered colimits by the $k$-algebras $k^S=\bigoplus_S k$ for finite sets $S$.

**Theorem 1.2** (Stone duality). Let $k$ be a ring whose only idempotent elements are $0,1\in k$. There is a canonical equivalence of categories
$$\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}\simeq\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{op}},$$
given for any Stone $k$-algebra $A$ and profinite set $S$ by
$$A\mapsto\Hom_{\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}}(A,k),\qquad S\mapsto\Hom_{\mathrm{Cond}}(S,k)\simeq\mathcal{C}(S,k).$$
The Hom sets inherit the respective profinite and $k$-algebra structure from that of $k$.

**Remark 1.3.** The assumption that $0,1\in k$ are the only idempotent elements of $k$ is equivalent to requiring that the underlying topological space $|\Spec(k)|$ of its spectrum is connected. Such a ring is sometimes called a *connected ring*, especially in commutative algebra. This class contains all fields, all local rings and all integral domains, including the universal base $\mathbf{Z}$.

**Proposition 1.4.** Any Stone $k$-algebra is a weakly étale $k$-algebra.

**Proposition 1.5.** If $k$ is absolutely flat, then so is any Stone $k$-algebra $A$.

**Proposition 1.6.** The forgetful functor $U:\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}\to\mathrm{CAlg}_k$ admits a right adjoint.

**Definition 1.7.** The *pearl* of a $k$-algebra $A$, denoted $A^\circ$, is its image under the right adjoint functor $\mathrm{CAlg}_k\to\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}$ of Proposition 1.6. That is, $A^\circ\to A$ is terminal among $k$-algebra homomorphisms from Stone $k$-algebras into $A$.

**Lemma 1.8.** Any finitely generated ideal $I$ in an absolutely flat ring $R$ is of the form $I=(e)$ for some idempotent $e\in R$.

**Proposition 1.9.** Let $k$ be absolutely flat and $A$ a $k$-algebra. The universal map $A^\circ\to A$ is injective.

**Proposition 1.10.** If $k$ is a field, the Stone duality functor $\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}\simeq\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{op}}$ from Theorem 1.2 is given by
$$A\mapsto|\Spec(A)|,$$
the underlying topological space of the spectrum of the Stone $k$-algebra $A$.

**Construction 1.11.** Let $\pi_0(X)$ denote the set of connected components of a topological space $X$, equipped with the quotient topology.¹ It is totally disconnected by [Stacks, Lemma 08ZL], and the resulting functor $\pi_0:\mathrm{Top}\to\mathrm{Top}^{\mathrm{tot.disc.}}$ is left adjoint to the full subcategory inclusion $\mathrm{Top}^{\mathrm{tot.disc.}}\subseteq\mathrm{Top}$. Restricting to the full subcategory $\mathrm{Top}^{\mathrm{sp}}\subseteq\mathrm{Top}$ of spectral spaces in the sense of [Stacks, Definition 08YG], $\pi_0(X)$ is profinite by [Stacks, Lemma 0906]. Thus $\pi_0:\mathrm{Top}^{\mathrm{sp}}\to\mathrm{ProFin}$ is left adjoint to the inclusion $\mathrm{ProFin}\to\mathrm{Top}^{\mathrm{sp}}$, which identifies profinite sets with totally disconnected spectral spaces.

¹ Following the conventions of [Stacks], $\pi_0(X)$ denotes the set of connected components, not path-connected components.

**Proposition 1.12.** Let $k$ be a field and $A$ a $k$-algebra. The universal map of affine schemes $\Spec(A)\to\Spec(A^\circ)$ exhibits a homeomorphism
$$|\Spec(A^\circ)|\simeq\pi_0(|\Spec(A)|),$$
where $\pi_0(|\Spec(A)|)$ carries the quotient topology inherited from $|\Spec(A)|$, and is totally disconnected, i.e. profinite.

**Corollary 1.13.** Over a field $k$, the pearl of a $k$-algebra $A$ can be expressed as
$$A^\circ=\mathcal{C}(|\Spec(A)|,k).$$

**Example 1.14.** Let $L/k$ be any field extension. Using Corollary 1.13,
$$L^\circ=\mathcal{C}(|\Spec(L)|,k)=\mathcal{C}(*,k)=k.$$
In particular, a $k$-algebra $A$ can fail to be Stone when $|\Spec(A)|$ is totally disconnected (i.e. equal to $\pi_0(|\Spec(A)|)$), even if $A$ is additionally assumed to be reduced.

**Example 1.15.** Continuing, let $L/k$ be a finite Galois extension. There is a canonical $k$-algebra isomorphism
$$L\otimes_k L\simeq\prod_{\mathrm{Gal}(L/k)}L.$$
Since taking pearls preserves products,
$$(L\otimes_k L)^\circ\simeq\prod_{\mathrm{Gal}(L/k)}L^\circ=\prod_{\mathrm{Gal}(L/k)}k.$$
On the other hand, $L^\circ\otimes_k L^\circ=k\otimes_k k=k$. So the injective map $L^\circ\otimes_k L^\circ\to(L\otimes_k L)^\circ$ is not bijective whenever $L/k$ is a non-trivial extension.

**Lemma 1.16.** A $k$-algebra homomorphism $A\to B$ between Stone $k$-algebras is faithfully flat if and only if the induced continuous map $|\Spec(B)|\to|\Spec(A)|$ is surjective.

**Lemma 1.17.** Let $k$ be absolutely flat and $A$ a Stone $k$-algebra. A $k$-algebra map $A\to B$ is faithfully flat if and only if it is injective.

**Proposition 1.18.** The forgetful functor $U:\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}\to\mathrm{CAlg}_k$ preserves fpqc covers. If $k$ is absolutely flat, then so does its right adjoint $(-)^\circ:\mathrm{CAlg}_k\to\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}$.

**Proposition 1.19.** Let $k$ be absolutely flat, $A$ a Stone $k$-algebra, and $A\to B$ a faithfully flat $k$-algebra map. The maps $B\rightrightarrows B\otimes_A B$ induce a canonical faithfully flat map on pearls
$$B^\circ\otimes_A B^\circ\to(B\otimes_A B)^\circ.$$

**Proposition 1.20.** Let $k$ be a field. Stone duality (Theorem 1.2) induces an isomorphism of sites
$$(\mathrm{ProFin},\tau_{\mathrm{eff}})\simeq((\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}})^{\mathrm{op}},\tau_{\mathrm{fpqc}}),$$
between profinite sets with the effective epimorphism topology and the opposite of Stone $k$-algebras with the fpqc topology inherited from the inclusion $\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}\subseteq\mathrm{CAlg}_k$.

**Lemma 1.21.** A ring $k$ is both absolutely flat and has no idempotent elements other than $0,1\in k$ if and only if it is a field.

**Proposition 1.22.** Let $k$ be a field. The inclusion $\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}\subseteq\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{wét}}$ is the identity if and only if $k$ is separably closed.

**Remark 1.23.** The pearl of an algebra $A$ over a separably closed field $k$ can be identified with the maximal weakly étale subalgebra $A^\circ=B_{\max}(A)$ of [Stacks, Lemma 0CKS].

**Corollary 1.24.** Let $k$ be a field. The site $((\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}})^{\mathrm{op}},\tau_{\mathrm{fpqc}})$ is equal to the pro-étale site $\Spec(k)_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$ of [BS15] if and only if $k$ is separably closed.

**Remark 1.25.** Corollary 1.24 shows that the Stone $k$-algebra approach to condensed sets, stemming from Proposition 1.20, recovers over a separably closed field the pro-étale approach in which condensed sets are the sheaves on the pro-étale site of a point.

**Proposition 1.26.** Stone $\mathbf{F}_p$-algebras coincide with $p$-Boolean algebras for any prime $p$.

**Remark 1.27.** By the proof of Proposition 1.26, the pearl of an $\mathbf{F}_p$-algebra $A$ is
$$A^\circ=\{a\in A: a^p=a\},$$
equivalently the fixed points $A^{\varphi=1}$ of the Frobenius automorphism. For $p=2$, this is the subring of idempotent elements.

**Corollary 1.28.** Let $p$ be a prime. The forgetful functor $U:\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{Stone}}_{\mathbf{F}_p}\to\mathrm{CAlg}_{\mathbf{F}_p}$ admits a left adjoint $Q:\mathrm{CAlg}_{\mathbf{F}_p}\to\mathrm{CAlg}_{\mathbf{F}_p}^{\mathrm{Stone}}$.

**Question 1.29.** For which fields $k$ does the forgetful functor $U:\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{Stone}}_k\to\mathrm{CAlg}_k$ admit a left adjoint?

## Section 2: Condensed sets and fpqc sheaves

**Definition 2.1.** Let $\kappa$ be an uncountable strong limit cardinal and $k$ a $\kappa$-small ring. The categories of $\kappa$-condensed sets and $\kappa$-fpqc sheaves over $k$ are
$$\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa\coloneqq\mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{ProFin}_\kappa,\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}),\qquad\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_{k,\kappa}\coloneqq\mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{CAlg}_{k,\kappa}^{\mathrm{op}},\tau_{\mathrm{fpqc}}),$$
on the sites of all $\kappa$-small profinite sets $\mathrm{ProFin}_\kappa$ with the effective epimorphism topology, and of the opposite category of $\kappa$-small $k$-algebras $\mathrm{CAlg}_{k,\kappa}$ with the fpqc topology.

**Proposition 2.2** ([CS19, Proposition 2.9], [HP23, Lemma 12.15, Lemma A.8]). Let $\kappa'>\kappa$ be strongly inaccessible cardinals. The left Kan extensions along the subcategory inclusions $\mathrm{ProFin}_\kappa\subseteq\mathrm{ProFin}_{\kappa'}$ and $\mathrm{CAlg}_{k,\kappa}^{\mathrm{op}}\subseteq\mathrm{CAlg}_{k,\kappa'}^{\mathrm{op}}$ restrict to canonical functors
$$\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa\to\mathrm{Cond}_{\kappa'},\qquad\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_{k,\kappa}\to\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_{k,\kappa'},$$
which are fully faithful and commute with all colimits and all $\kappa$-small limits.

**Definition 2.3.** Let $k$ be a ring. The categories of condensed sets and fpqc sheaves over $k$ are the filtered colimits
$$\mathrm{Cond}\coloneqq\varinjlim_\kappa\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa,\qquad\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k\coloneqq\varinjlim_\kappa\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_{k,\kappa},$$
over the filtered poset of strongly inaccessible cardinals.

**Remark 2.4.** A condensed set or fpqc sheaf is always left Kan extended from a $\kappa$-version for some strongly inaccessible $\kappa$. So for practical purposes we may work in the Grothendieck topoi $\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa$ and $\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_{k,\kappa}$, even though $\mathrm{Cond}$ and $\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k$ are too big to be topoi.

**Remark 2.5.** Let $\kappa$ be strongly inaccessible. Precomposing the tentative sheafification functors with the canonical fully faithful embeddings to their $\kappa$-variants (Definition 2.1) recovers the genuine sheafification. That is, the diagram of categories
$$\begin{array}{ccc}\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{ProFin}_\kappa) & \to & \mathrm{Cond}_\kappa\\ \downarrow{\scriptstyle(-)^\dagger} & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle(-)^\dagger}\\ \mathcal{P}(\mathrm{CAlg}_{k,\kappa}^{\mathrm{op}}) & \to & \mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_{k,\kappa}\end{array}$$
commutes, and similarly for $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{acc}}(\mathrm{ProFin})\to\mathrm{Cond}$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{acc}}(\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{op}})\to\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k$. Here the upper horizontal arrows are topos-level sheafifications and the vertical arrows are given by left Kan extension.

**Theorem 2.6.** Let $k$ be a field. The composite of Stone duality (Theorem 1.2) with the subcategory inclusion
$$\mathrm{ProFin}\simeq(\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}})^{\mathrm{op}}\subseteq\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{op}}$$
gives rise to an adjunction
$$f^*:\mathrm{Cond}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k:f_*,$$
whose left adjoint $f^*$ is fully faithful and commutes with all finite limits.

**Remark 2.7.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category with all small limits. For any site $(\mathcal{D},\tau)$, the category of $\mathcal{C}$-valued sheaves on $\mathcal{D}$ may be identified with the full subcategory of $\Fun(\mathrm{Shv}(\mathcal{D},\tau),\mathcal{C})$ of functors preserving all finite limits. Using this, the conclusion of Theorem 2.6 extends, for any uncountable strong limit $\kappa$, to an adjunction between $\kappa$-condensed objects in $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{C}$-valued $\kappa$-fpqc sheaves over $k$. Passing to the filtered colimit over strongly inaccessible $\kappa$ gives an adjunction
$$f^*:\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k(\mathcal{C}):f_*,$$
whose left adjoint is again fully faithful. (See [CS19, Appendix to Lecture II] for the condensed case.)

**Remark 2.8.** Passing to the colimit over strongly inaccessible $\kappa$ in the presheaf-level adjunction for $\kappa$-variants gives, by [HP23, Proposition A.2], an adjunction on accessible presheaves
$$((-)^\circ)^*:\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{acc}}\big((\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}})^{\mathrm{op}}\big)\rightleftarrows\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{acc}}(\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{op}}):U^*.$$
The adjunction of Theorem 2.6 is the restriction of this one to sheaves. In particular, $f^*:\mathrm{Cond}\to\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k$ is the composite
$$\mathrm{Cond}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{acc}}(\mathrm{ProFin})\simeq\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{acc}}\big((\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}})^{\mathrm{op}}\big)\xrightarrow{((-)^\circ)^*}\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{acc}}(\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{op}})\xrightarrow{(-)^\dagger}\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k.$$

**Definition 2.9.** Let $X$ be a condensed set and $\mathcal{X}$ an fpqc sheaf over $k$.
- The *enhanced $k$-points* of $\mathcal{X}$ is the condensed set $\underline{\mathcal{X}(k)}\coloneqq f_*(\mathcal{X})$.
- The *pétra space* of $X$ is the fpqc sheaf $X^{\mathrm{pét}}_k\coloneqq f^*(X)$.
- The *Stone core* (or *petrifaction*) of $\mathcal{X}$ is the fpqc sheaf $\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}}\coloneqq f^*f_*(\mathcal{X})$.

**Proposition 2.10.** The enhanced $k$-points of an fpqc sheaf $\mathcal{X}$ over $k$ is the condensed set $\underline{\mathcal{X}(k)}$ whose value on any $S\in\mathrm{ProFin}$ is
$$\underline{\mathcal{X}(k)}(S)\simeq\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{C}(S,k)).$$
In particular, its set of points is $\underline{\mathcal{X}(k)}(*)\simeq\mathcal{X}(k)$, the usual set of $k$-points of $\mathcal{X}$.

**Example 2.11.** The affine line $\mathbf{A}^1_k$ corresponds, via the functor of points, to the forgetful functor $\mathrm{CAlg}_k\to\mathrm{Set}$. Its enhanced $k$-points $\underline{\mathbf{A}^1_k(k)}$ are the profinite-set-valued functor $S\mapsto\mathcal{C}(S,k)$, which is $k$ itself viewed as a discrete condensed set. The enhanced points functor $\mathcal{X}\mapsto\underline{\mathcal{X}(k)}$ is a right adjoint and so preserves all limits. Hence it sends the affine $n$-space $\mathbf{A}^n_k=\mathbf{A}^1_k\times\cdots\times\mathbf{A}^1_k$ to $\underline{\mathbf{A}^n_k(k)}=k^n$.

**Proposition 2.12.** Let $\mathcal{X}$ be an fpqc sheaf over a ring $k$. It canonically extends to a functor $\underline{\mathcal{X}}:\mathrm{CAlg}_k\to\mathrm{Cond}$ such that:
- the $k$-points of $\underline{\mathcal{X}}$ are the enhanced $k$-points of $\mathcal{X}$, i.e. $\underline{\mathcal{X}}(k)=\underline{\mathcal{X}(k)}$;
- post-composing $\underline{\mathcal{X}}$ with the underlying set functor $\mathrm{Cond}\to\mathrm{Set}$, $X\mapsto X(*)$, recovers $\mathcal{X}:\mathrm{CAlg}_k\to\mathrm{Cond}$.

**Remark 2.13.** From the proof of Proposition 2.12, $\underline{\mathcal{X}}$ is given explicitly by
$$\underline{\mathcal{X}}(A):S\mapsto\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{C}(S,k)\otimes_k A)$$
for any $k$-algebra $A$ and small profinite set $S$. Writing $S=\varprojlim_i S_i$ for a filtered system of finite sets $S_i$,
$$\mathcal{C}(S,k)\otimes_k A=\varinjlim_i k^{S_i}\otimes_k A=\varinjlim_i A^{S_i}=\mathcal{C}(S,A).$$
So $\underline{\mathcal{X}}(A):S\mapsto\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{C}(S,A))$. It follows that $\underline{\mathcal{X}}$ is a $\kappa$-fpqc sheaf of $\kappa$-condensed sets.

**Remark 2.14.** The condensed enhancement $\underline{\mathcal{X}}$ of Proposition 2.12 contrasts with the naive extension of $\mathcal{X}:\mathrm{CAlg}_{k,\kappa}\to\mathrm{Set}$ to a functor into $\kappa$-condensed sets, obtained by post-composing with the inclusion $\mathrm{Set}\to\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa$ of discrete $\kappa$-condensed sets.

**Proposition 2.15.** Let $\mathcal{X}$ be an fpqc sheaf over $k$. The map of condensed sets
$$\underline{\mathcal{X}(k)}\to\underline{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{dR}}(k)},$$
induced by the canonical map of fpqc sheaves $\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{dR}}$, is an isomorphism.

**Proposition 2.16.** The pétra $k$-space $X^{\mathrm{pét}}_k:\mathrm{CAlg}_k\to\mathrm{Set}$ of a condensed set $X$ is the fpqc sheafification of the functor
$$A\mapsto X(\pi_0(|\Spec(A)|)),$$
where $\pi_0$ carries its quotient topology. For any profinite set $S$, viewed as a condensed set, its Stone $k$-space is the affine $k$-scheme
$$S^{\mathrm{pét}}_k\simeq\Spec(\mathcal{C}(S,k)).$$

**Proposition 2.17.** Let $X$ be a condensed set. There is a natural isomorphism of condensed sets
$$X\simeq\underline{X^{\mathrm{pét}}_k(k)},$$
i.e. $X$ is isomorphic to the enhanced $k$-points of its pétra space.

**Corollary 2.18.** There is a natural bijection $X(*)\simeq X^{\mathrm{pét}}_k(k)$ between the underlying set of an arbitrary condensed set $X$ and the $k$-points of its pétra space.

**Remark 2.19.** The passage from topological spaces to condensed sets adds new objects such as $Q=\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{R}^\delta$, the quotient of the real line with its usual topology by the real line with the discrete topology. This condensed set satisfies $Q(*)=*$, yet $Q$ and $*$ are not isomorphic. So the underlying set $X(*)$ contains much less information about a condensed set $X$ than about a topological space. Corollary 2.18 formalizes an analogous situation in algebraic geometry: passing from $k$-varieties to $k$-schemes, the $k$-points carry much less information.

**Proposition 2.20.** Let $X$ be a compactly generated Hausdorff topological space. Its pétra $k$-space is given by
$$\mathrm{CAlg}_k\ni A\mapsto\Hom_{\mathrm{Top}}(|\Spec(A)|,X)\in\mathrm{Set}.$$

**Remark 2.21.** One might wish to apply the formula of Proposition 2.20 to any quasi-separated condensed set $X$, but it is not clear how to do so. For a $k$-algebra $A$, the space $|\Spec(A)|$ can fail to be $T_1$, and so by [CS19, the discussion following Proposition 2.15] it does not (in the naive way) embed into condensed sets. Hence it is unclear in what category to take $\Hom(|\Spec(A)|,X)$.

**Proposition 2.22.** Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a $\kappa$-fpqc sheaf over $k$ for some strongly inaccessible $\kappa$. Its Stone core is the colimit
$$\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}}=\varinjlim_{A\in(\mathrm{CAlg}_{k,\kappa}^{\mathrm{Stone}})_{/\mathcal{X}}}\Spec(A)$$
in $\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k$, where $(\mathrm{CAlg}_{k,\kappa}^{\mathrm{Stone}})_{/\mathcal{X}}$ is the category of pairs $(A,x)$ of a $\kappa$-small Stone $k$-algebra $A$ and a point $x\in\mathcal{X}(A)$. There is a canonical morphism $\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}}\to\mathcal{X}$, and $\mathcal{X}$ is a pétra space (i.e. lies in the essential image of the fully faithful functor $\mathrm{Cond}\to\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k$ of Theorem 2.6) if and only if this morphism is an isomorphism.

**Remark 2.23.** Though the Stone core $\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}}$ is in general difficult to describe more explicitly than in Proposition 2.22, its $k$-points are simple. Combining Proposition 2.10 with $f^*f_*\simeq\mathrm{id}$ (from the proof of Theorem 2.6),
$$\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}}(k)=\underline{\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}}(k)}(*)=f_*f^*f_*(\mathcal{X})(*)\simeq f_*(\mathcal{X})(*)=\underline{\mathcal{X}(k)}(*)=\mathcal{X}(k).$$
Hence the Stone core map $\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}}\to\mathcal{X}$ induces a bijection on $k$-points, and more generally on $A$-points for all Stone $k$-algebras $A$.

**Proposition 2.24.** The Stone core of an fpqc sheaf $\mathcal{X}$ over $k$ is the fpqc sheafification of the functor $A\mapsto\mathcal{X}(A^\circ)$.

**Remark 2.25.** The description in Proposition 2.24 resembles the de Rham space, defined for any fpqc sheaf $\mathcal{X}$ as $\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{dR}}(A)\coloneqq\mathcal{X}(A^{\mathrm{red}})$. Two differences: no sheafification is needed for the de Rham space, and the pearl comes with a map $A^\circ\to A$ while the reduction comes with a map $A\to A^{\mathrm{red}}$. Thus the de Rham space is a quotient $\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{dR}}$, whereas the Stone core is a subspace $\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}}\to\mathcal{X}$.

**Proposition 2.26.** For any fpqc sheaf $\mathcal{X}$ over $k$, there are canonical isomorphisms
$$\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}}\simeq(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{dR}})^{\mathrm{Stone}}\simeq(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{Stone}})_{\mathrm{dR}}.$$

**Proposition 2.27.** Let $k$ be a field for which the forgetful functor $U:\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}\to\mathrm{CAlg}_k$ admits a left adjoint $Q:\mathrm{CAlg}_k\to\mathrm{CAlg}_k^{\mathrm{Stone}}$. Then the Stone core of an affine $k$-scheme is
$$\Spec(A)^{\mathrm{Stone}}=\Spec(Q(A)).$$

**Example 2.28.** Let $k$ be as in Proposition 2.27 and $A$ any $k$-algebra. The enhanced $k$-points of its spectrum are
$$\underline{\Spec(A)(k)}=\underline{\Spec(A)^{\mathrm{Stone}}(k)}=\underline{\Spec(Q(A))(k)}=|\Spec(Q(A))|,$$
and in particular form a profinite set.

**Example 2.29.** Continue with Example 2.28, and assume further that $A$ is of finite type. Then there is a closed immersion, hence a monomorphism, $\Spec(A)\to\mathbf{A}^n_k$. The enhanced points functor is a right adjoint and so preserves monomorphisms. By Example 2.11, $\underline{\Spec(A)(k)}\to\underline{\mathbf{A}^n_k(k)}=k^n$ is a condensed subset of a discrete set. Since $\underline{\Spec(A)(k)}$ is also profinite, it is a profinite subset of a discrete set and hence discrete. Therefore
$$\underline{\Spec(A)(k)}=\Spec(A)(k),$$
the ordinary set of $k$-points viewed as a discrete condensed set.

**Remark 2.30.** Let $k=\mathbf{F}_p$. By the proof of Corollary 1.28, the left adjoint $Q:\mathrm{CAlg}_{\mathbf{F}_p}\to\mathrm{CAlg}_{\mathbf{F}_p}^{\mathrm{Stone}}$ is
$$Q(A)=A/(a-a^p: a\in A).$$
So $\Spec(Q(A))$ is identified with the fixed-point locus $\Spec(A)^F$ of the Frobenius endomorphism $F:\Spec(A)\to\Spec(A)$.

## Section 3: Topological and algebro-geometric notions

**Remark 3.1.** One possible objection is that the correspondence of Theorem 2.6 does not give an equivalence between both sides, as a duality should. Restricting the algebro-geometric side to fpqc sheaves that equal their own Stone core (i.e. pétra spaces) makes it an equivalence, by Proposition 2.22. The paper works with the full category of fpqc sheaves.

**Proposition 3.2.** Let $X$ be a condensed set and $\mathcal{X}=X^{\mathrm{pét}}_k$ its pétra space. There is a canonical isomorphism of $k$-algebras
$$\Gamma(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{Cond}}(X,k).$$

**Proposition 3.3.** The inclusion functor $\mathrm{ProFin}\to\mathrm{Cond}$, obtained by passage to the colimit over uncountable strong limit cardinals $\kappa$ from the Yoneda inclusions $\mathrm{ProFin}_\kappa\to\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa$, admits a left adjoint $\widehat{\pi}_0:\mathrm{Cond}\to\mathrm{ProFin}$.

**Remark 3.4.** The assertion of Proposition 3.3 is non-obvious, since the functor $\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa\to\mathrm{ProFin}$, $X\mapsto\pi_0(\beta(X(*)_{\mathrm{top}}))$, seems to depend on the choice of $\kappa$. There is in general no well-defined functor $\mathrm{Top}\to\mathrm{Cond}$ without restricting to $\kappa$-condensed sets [CS19, Warning 2.14]. The proof of Proposition 3.3 shows that the dependence disappears on connected components with the quotient topology.

**Remark 3.5.** Let $S$ be a set, identified with a discrete condensed set. Then $\widehat{\pi}_0(S)=\beta S$ is the Stone–Čech compactification of $S$, since $S(*)_{\mathrm{top}}=S$. The algebro-geometric formula (6) specializes, for $k=\mathbf{F}_2$, to the standard expression
$$\beta S\simeq|\Spec(P(S))|,\qquad P(S)=\Hom_{\mathrm{Set}}(S,\{0,1\}).$$

**Proposition 3.6.** Let $X$ be a condensed set and $k$ a finite field. The affinization of its pétra space $X^{\mathrm{pét}}_k$ is the pétra space of the profinite set $\widehat{\pi}_0(X)$.

**Remark 3.7.** Without the finiteness assumption on $k$, the affinization of the pétra space of a $\kappa$-condensed set $X$ can still be expressed as
$$\mathrm{Aff}(X^{\mathrm{pét}}_k)\simeq\Spec(\mathcal{C}(\pi_0(X(*)_{\mathrm{top}}),k)).$$
The set $\pi_0(X(*)_{\mathrm{top}})$, with the quotient topology, is totally disconnected. Without assuming $X(*)_{\mathrm{top}}$ is compact Hausdorff (or, for instance, spectral), it need not be profinite.

**Definition 3.8.** An fpqc sheaf $\mathcal{X}$ over $k$ is a *geometric space* if it is isomorphic to a quotient of an affine scheme by an affine equivalence relation in $\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k$. An fpqc sheaf $\mathcal{X}$ over $k$ is an *ind-geometric space* if it is isomorphic in $\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k$ to a filtered colimit $\mathcal{X}\simeq\varinjlim_i\mathcal{X}_i$ of geometric spaces $\mathcal{X}_i$ along closed immersions $\mathcal{X}_i\to\mathcal{X}_j$.

**Remark 3.9.** An accessible functor $\mathcal{X}:\mathrm{CAlg}_k\to\mathrm{Set}$ is a geometric space if and only if:
- (a) $\mathcal{X}$ satisfies fpqc descent;
- (b) the diagonal $\Delta:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}$ is affine;
- (c) there exist a $k$-algebra $A$ and a faithfully flat morphism $\Spec(A)\to\mathcal{X}$.

For an algebraic stack, (c) is modified to ask for an étale cover (or an fppf one, equivalent by [LMB20, Artin slice theorem 10.1]).² An algebraic space has an étale atlas, while a geometric space is only guaranteed an fpqc one. Geometric spaces could therefore be called "fpqc-algebraic spaces". The adjective "geometric" is used instead, in accordance with [SAG, Definition 9.3.0.1]. By [SAG, Proposition 9.1.6.9], the geometric spaces are precisely the $0$-truncated geometric stacks in the sense of [SAG, Definition 9.1.6.2].

² The precise choice between a single affine cover and a scheme cover, and the condition on the diagonal, are treated as conventional.

**Remark 3.10.** Ind-geometric spaces are defined analogously to ind-schemes. They are an fpqc-version of ind-algebraic spaces in the sense of [May23, Definition 41.1].

**Theorem 3.11.** Let $X$ be a condensed set and $\mathcal{X}=X^{\mathrm{pét}}_k$ its pétra space over a field $k$.
1. If $X$ is a profinite set, then $\mathcal{X}$ is affine.
2. If $X$ is a compact Hausdorff space (i.e. a qcqs condensed set), then $\mathcal{X}$ is a geometric space.

When $k$ is a finite field, the converse statements also hold, and moreover:
3. A condensed set $X$ is quasi-separated if and only if $\mathcal{X}$ is an ind-geometric space.

**Lemma 3.12.** A continuous map between compact Hausdorff spaces $X\to Y$ is injective if and only if the induced morphism of pétra spaces $\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}$ over a finite field $k$ is a closed immersion.

**Remark 3.13.** By [CS19, Theorem 2.16], all compactly generated weakly Hausdorff topological spaces are quasi-separated when viewed as condensed sets. Theorem 3.11 therefore implies that their pétra spaces over a finite field are always ind-geometric.

**Corollary 3.14.** Let $k$ be a finite field. The pétra space over $k$ of any condensed space is isomorphic, in $\mathrm{Shv}^{\mathrm{fpqc}}_k$, to a quotient of an ind-geometric space by an ind-geometric equivalence relation.

**Remark 3.15.** The terminology follows [SAG]. Another common convention, from [Sim96], [TV08] and [Ste23], calls the geometric spaces of Definition 3.8 *quasi-compact geometric spaces with affine diagonal*. In that convention, the ind-geometric spaces of Definition 3.8 still fall within the geometric spaces: an ind-geometric space admits a cover by (possibly infinitely many) affines, equivalently by a (possibly non-affine) scheme.

**Proposition 3.16.** Let $X$ be a condensed set. Its pétra space $\mathcal{X}$ over a field $k$ is an algebraic space locally of finite presentation over $\Spec(k)$ if and only if $X$ is discrete.

**Remark 3.17.** The conclusions of Proposition 3.16 and point (1) of Theorem 3.11 might seem at odds. On the condensed side they concern discrete and profinite sets respectively; on the algebro-geometric side they concern affine schemes and algebraic spaces.
