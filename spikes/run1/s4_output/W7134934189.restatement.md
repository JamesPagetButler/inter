# Restatement

## Abstract and introduction (mathematical content)

- A fractured $\infty$-topos structure on the $\infty$-topos of condensed anima $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$ is constructed.
- An explicit collection of jointly conservative points of $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$ is exhibited (Theorem C).
- The category $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ is analyzed with respect to limits. It is shown that it does not admit all fibers, which answers a question of Clausen (Theorem D).

Notation used below:
- $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) \coloneqq \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}_{\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}}(\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc})$.
- $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}$ is the wide subcategory of $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ spanned by open embeddings, with the Grothendieck topology of finitely jointly surjective families of open embeddings.
- $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) \coloneqq \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}})$.
- $\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathrm{open}}\colon \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc} \to \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$ is the sheafification of the Yoneda embedding. (See Remark 3.2.)

## Section 1: Introduction

**Theorem A (3.7).** The left Kan extension of the restriction functor
$$\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) \coloneqq \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}) \longrightarrow \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$$
induces a fractured $\infty$-topos structure. That is, this functor induces an equivalence onto a subcategory of $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$, and the inclusion of this subcategory is a fractured structure.

**Theorem B (3.11).** For every $S \in \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})_{/\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathrm{open}}S} \simeq \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(S).$$

**Theorem C (3.14).** The collection of points
$$\left\{ \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) \longrightarrow \mathscr{A}\mathrm{n},\ F \mapsto \operatorname*{colim}_{\substack{U \subseteq S \text{ clopen} \\ s \in U}} F(U) \right\}_{S \in \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc},\ s \in S}$$
is jointly conservative.

**Theorem D (4.13).** The category $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ does not admit all fibers. Concretely, let $\beta\pi_1\colon \beta(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}) \to \beta\mathbb{N}$ be induced by the projection onto the first coordinate. For $p \in \beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}$, the fiber over $p$ is not extremally disconnected.

## Section 2: Preliminaries on fractured topoi and condensed anima

**Definition 2.1 ([34, Definition 20.1.2.1]).** Let $\mathscr{X}$ be an $\infty$-topos. A subcategory $j_{!}\colon \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}} \to \mathscr{X}$ is a *fracture subcategory* if it satisfies:

1. If $X \in \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$ and $f\colon X \to Y$ in $\mathscr{X}$ is an equivalence, then $f$ belongs to $\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$.
2. The $\infty$-category $\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$ admits pullbacks, and these are preserved by $j_{!}$.
3. The inclusion functor $j_{!}\colon \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}} \to \mathscr{X}$ admits a right adjoint $j^{*}\colon \mathscr{X} \to \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$ which is conservative and preserves small colimits.
4. For every map $U \to V$ in $\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$, the square with vertices $j_!j^*j_!U$, $j_!j^*j_!V$, $j_!U$, $j_!V$, whose maps are induced by the counit $j_{!}j^{*} \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{X}}$, is a pullback in $\mathscr{X}$.

A *fractured $\infty$-topos* is a pair $\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}} \to \mathscr{X}$ where $\mathscr{X}$ is an $\infty$-topos and $\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$ is a fracture subcategory of $\mathscr{X}$. Objects of $\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$ are called *corporeal*.

**Remark 2.2.** Since $j^{*}$ preserves small colimits and $j_{!}\colon \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}} \to \mathscr{X}$ is a map between presentable $\infty$-categories, it admits a right adjoint $j_{*}$ by the adjoint functor theorem.

**Remark 2.3.** For every $X \in \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$, the functor $(j_{!})_{/X}\colon \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}_{/X} \to \mathscr{X}_{/X}$ is fully faithful by [34, Proposition 20.1.3.1].

**Lemma 2.4.** Let $j_{!}\colon \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}} \to \mathscr{X}$ be a fractured $\infty$-topos. The adjunction $(j_{!}, j^{*})$ is monadic, and the adjunction $(j^{*}, j_{*})$ is comonadic. Hence, $j^{*}$ creates all limits and colimits.

**Remark 2.5.** Let $j_{!}\colon \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}} \to \mathscr{X}$ be a fractured $\infty$-topos and let $X \in \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$. There is a triple adjunction $j_{!} \dashv j^{*} \dashv j_{*}$ (by Remark 2.2). Passing to slice categories yields a triple adjunction
$$\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}_{/X} \underset{(j^*)_{/X}}{\overset{(j_!)_{/X}}{\rightleftarrows}} \mathscr{X}_{/X}$$
with further right adjoint $(j_*)_{/X}$. In this setting:
- $(j_{!})_{/X}$ is fully faithful (Remark 2.3) and preserves pullbacks.
- $\mathrm{id}_X \in \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}_{/X}$ by Definition 2.1(1), so it is a terminal object, and $(j_{!})_{/X}$ preserves terminal objects.
- Hence $(j_{!})_{/X}$ preserves finite limits.

If $(j_{!})_{/X}$ admits a further left adjoint that also preserves finite products, then one recovers a cohesive structure in the sense of Lawvere–Schreiber [44, Definition 3.4.1, Remark 3.4.2].

**Definition 2.6 ([34, Definition 20.2.1.1, 20.6.2.1]).** Let $(\mathscr{C}, \tau)$ be an $\infty$-site.

1. An *admissibility structure* on $\mathscr{C}$ is a collection of maps in $\mathscr{C}$, called *admissibility morphisms*, satisfying:
   - (a) Equivalences in $\mathscr{C}$ are admissible.
   - (b) If $f\colon U \to X$ is admissible and $g\colon X' \to X$ is any map in $\mathscr{C}$, then the pullback $f'\colon U\times_X X' \to X'$ exists and $f'$ is admissible.
   - (c) Let $X \xrightarrow{f} Y \xrightarrow{g} Z$ with $h = g\circ f$ be a commuting triangle in $\mathscr{C}$ with $g$ admissible. Then $f$ is admissible if and only if $h$ is admissible.
   - (d) The collection of admissible morphisms is closed under retracts in $\mathrm{Fun}([1], \mathscr{C})$.

   We write $\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{ad}}$ for the wide subcategory of $\mathscr{C}$ spanned by the admissible morphisms.
2. An admissibility structure $\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{ad}} \to \mathscr{C}$ is *compatible with $\tau$* if for every $X \in \mathscr{C}$ and covering sieve $\mathscr{C}^{(0)}_{/X} \to \mathscr{C}_{/X}$ there exists a $\tau$-covering$^{3}$ $\{f_\alpha\colon U_\alpha \to X\}_\alpha$ such that each $f_\alpha$ is admissible and belongs to $\mathscr{C}^{(0)}_{/X}$.
3. A *geometric site* is a triple $(\mathscr{C}, \mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{ad}}, \tau)$ where $(\mathscr{C}, \tau)$ is an $\infty$-site and $\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{ad}}$ is an admissibility structure compatible with $\tau$.

*Footnote 3:* A collection of maps that generates a covering sieve; see [34, Section 20.6.1].

**Theorem 2.7 ([34, Theorem 20.6.3.4]).** Let $(\mathscr{C}, \mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{ad}}, \tau)$ be a geometric site. Precomposition with $j$ induces an adjunction $j_{!} \dashv j^{*}$ between $\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}_{\tau^{\mathrm{ad}}}(\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{ad}})$ and $\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}_{\tau}(\mathscr{C})$, and $j_{!}$ induces an equivalence from $\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}_{\tau^{\mathrm{ad}}}(\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{ad}})$ onto a fracture subcategory $\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}_{\tau}(\mathscr{C})^{\mathrm{corp}} \subset \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}_{\tau}(\mathscr{C})$.

**Proposition 2.8 ([15, Proposition 1.5]).** Let $j_{!}\colon \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}} \to \mathscr{X}$ be a fractured $\infty$-topos with right adjoint $j^{*}$. Let $\{x_i\colon \mathscr{A}\mathrm{n} \to \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}\}_i$ be a collection of jointly conservative points in $\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$. Then $\{j_{*} \circ x_i\colon \mathscr{A}\mathrm{n} \to \mathscr{X}\}_i$ is a collection of jointly conservative points in $\mathscr{X}$, where $j_{*}$ is the right adjoint to $j^{*}$.

**Remark 2.9.** In particular, $\mathscr{X}$ has enough points if $\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$ has enough points.

**Corollary 2.10.** Let $j_{!}\colon \mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}} \to \mathscr{X}$ be a fractured $\infty$-topos such that $\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{corp}}$ has enough points. Then $\mathscr{X}$ is hypercomplete.

**Definition 2.11 ([43, Definition 11.7]).** The $\infty$-topos of condensed anima is
$$\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) \coloneqq \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}_{\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}}(\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}_\kappa).$$

**Remark 2.12.** The category $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ has the following properties:
- Not even binary products of infinite extremally disconnected spaces exist in $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ [29, Proposition 2.54].
- Every convergent sequence in extremally disconnected spaces is eventually constant [20, Theorem 1.3].
- Every extremally disconnected space is a retract of the Stone–Čech compactification of a discrete space [39, Corollary].

**Remark 2.13.** Extremally disconnected spaces are precisely the projective objects in $\mathscr{C}\mathscr{H}\mathrm{aus}$ by Gleason [20, Theorem 2.5]. That is, every continuous surjection onto an extremally disconnected space admits a section.

**Lemma 2.14 ([7, Section 2.2.9]).** A functor $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}$ is a condensed anima if and only if it preserves finite products.

**Example 2.15.** Let $X$ be a topological space. The restricted representable presheaf
$$\text{\char 136\relax}X\colon \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathscr{A}\mathrm{n},\quad Y \mapsto \mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}}(Y, X)$$
is a condensed anima, since $\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}}(-, X)$ preserves finite products. In particular, the Grothendieck topology on $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ is subcanonical.

## Section 3: Fractured structure on condensed anima

**Definition 3.1.** Let $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}$ be the site consisting of the wide subcategory of $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ with maps given by open embeddings, and coverings given by finitely jointly surjective families of open embeddings. These define a Grothendieck topology $\tau_{\mathrm{open}}$. Write
$$\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) \coloneqq \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}})$$
for the resulting $\infty$-category of sheaves.

**Remark 3.2.** The topology on $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}$ is not subcanonical, unlike that of $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$. For the discrete space $\{a,b\}$, the cover by $\{a\}$ and $\{b\}$ gives a map
$$\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}}(\{a,b\}, \{*\}) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}}(\{a\}, \{*\}) \times \mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}}(\{b\}, \{*\})$$
which is injective but not bijective. The notation $\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathrm{open}}(-)$ denotes the representable sheaves in $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$, defined as the sheafifications of the representable presheaves.

**Lemma 3.3.** Let $F\colon I \to \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ be a diagram. If the limit of $F$ exists in $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$, then it is given by the limit in $\mathscr{C}\mathscr{H}\mathrm{aus}$.

**Corollary 3.4.** The pullback of an open embedding along an arbitrary map is an open embedding in $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$, and is evaluated as the pullback in $\mathscr{C}\mathscr{H}\mathrm{aus}$.

**Remark 3.5.** Clopen subspaces of extremally disconnected spaces are again extremally disconnected. This point-set fact is the key input for Corollary 3.4. See 4.13 for an explicit counterexample to the analogous statement for fibers.

**Lemma 3.6.** Open embeddings in $\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ are closed under retracts.

**Theorem 3.7.** The functor $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) \longrightarrow \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$ from 2.7 induces an equivalence onto a fractured subcategory.

**Corollary 3.8.** The $\infty$-topos $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$ is (co)monadic over $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$. In particular, the functor $j^{*}\colon \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) \to \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$ creates all (co)limits.

**Remark 3.9.** Let $S$ be a profinite set. Three sites on open (or clopen) subsets of $S$ are considered:
- $(\mathscr{O}\mathrm{pen}_S, \tau)$: the poset of open subsets of $S$ with the topology of jointly surjective families of maps.
- $(\mathscr{C}\mathrm{lopen}_S, \tau)$: the poset of clopen subsets of $S$ with the topology of jointly surjective families of maps.
- $(\mathscr{C}\mathrm{lopen}_S, \tau^{\mathrm{fin}})$: the poset of clopen subsets of $S$ with the topology of finitely jointly surjective families of maps.

The evident inclusions $\iota_f\colon (\mathscr{C}\mathrm{lopen}_S, \tau^{\mathrm{fin}}) \to (\mathscr{C}\mathrm{lopen}_S, \tau)$ and $\iota_c\colon (\mathscr{C}\mathrm{lopen}_S, \tau) \to (\mathscr{O}\mathrm{pen}_S, \tau)$ induce equivalences
$$\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(S) = \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(\mathscr{O}\mathrm{pen}_S, \tau) \simeq \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(\mathscr{C}\mathrm{lopen}_S, \tau) \simeq \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(\mathscr{C}\mathrm{lopen}_S, \tau^{\mathrm{fin}})$$
via $(\iota_c)^*$ and $(\iota_f)^*$. Since $S$ is compact, every clopen covering of $S$ has a finite subcovering, so $(\iota_f)^*$ is an equivalence. By [3, Proposition 3.1.7], every totally disconnected locally compact Hausdorff space admits a clopen basis, so $\iota_c$ is a basis in the sense of [1, below Definition A.2]. By [1, Corollary A.8], $(\iota_c)^*$ is an equivalence.

**Lemma 3.10.** Let $S$ be an extremally disconnected space. Then the functor
$$\rho_S\colon (\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}})_{/S} \longrightarrow \mathscr{C}\mathrm{lopen}_S,\quad (i\colon U \to S) \mapsto i(U)$$
is an equivalence of categories.

**Proposition 3.11.** Let $S \in \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$ and let $\pi_S\colon \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}_{/S} \to \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}$ be the projection functor. The following functors are equivalences of $\infty$-categories:
$$\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(\mathscr{O}\mathrm{pen}_S, \tau) \xrightarrow{(\iota_f \circ \iota_c)^*} \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(\mathscr{C}\mathrm{lopen}_S, \tau^{\mathrm{fin}}) \xrightarrow{(\rho_S)^*} \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{open}}_{/S}) \xrightarrow{(\pi_S)_!} \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})_{/\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathrm{open}}(S)}.$$
Hence these compose to an equivalence
$$\eta_S\colon \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(S) \xrightarrow{\simeq} \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})_{/\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathrm{open}}(S)}.$$

**Corollary 3.12.** Suppose $X \in \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$ is given by a colimit of representables, i.e. there is an equivalence $f\colon \operatorname*{colim}_i \text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathrm{open}}S_i \xrightarrow{\simeq} X$. Then there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})_{/X} \xrightarrow[\simeq]{f^*} \lim_i \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}^{\mathrm{open}}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})_{/\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathrm{open}}S_i} \xrightarrow[\simeq]{(\eta_{S_i})^{-1}} \lim_i \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(S_i).$$

**Lemma 3.13.** Let $S \in \mathscr{P}\mathrm{ro}\mathscr{F}\mathrm{in}$. Then $\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(S)$ is hypercomplete.

**Theorem 3.14.** The collection of points
$$\left\{ \mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) \longrightarrow \mathscr{A}\mathrm{n},\ F \mapsto \operatorname*{colim}_{\substack{U \subseteq S \text{ clopen} \\ s \in U}} F(U) \right\}_{S \in \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc},\ s \in S}$$
is jointly conservative.

**Corollary 3.15.** The $\infty$-topos $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}) = \mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}_{\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n}}(\mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc})$ is hypercomplete.

**Corollary 3.16.** Let $S \in \mathscr{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathscr{D}\mathrm{isc}$. The shape of $\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(S)$ and the shape of $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})_{/\text{\char 136\relax}S}$ coincide, and are given by the underlying homotopy type of $S$.

**Remark 3.17.** By [36, Theorem 1.2.18, Corollary 1.2.33], the shape of $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})_{/\text{\char 136\relax}X}$ agrees with the shape of $\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(X)$ for every paracompact compactly generated space $X$. For CW complexes [25, Example 2.4] or profinite sets [36, Example 1.2.13], this is the underlying homotopy type of $X$. There exists a locally contractible compact Hausdorff space $X$ such that the shape of $\mathscr{S}\mathrm{hv}(X)$ does not agree with the underlying homotopy type of $X$ [11, Example 2.2.4].

**Corollary 3.18.** Condensed and sheaf cohomology over $\mathbb{Z}$ agree for extremally disconnected spaces.

**Remark 3.19.** Corollary 3.18 is also known for compact Hausdorff spaces [42, Theorem 3.2], for paracompact compactly generated or locally contractible spaces [36, Proposition 1.3.5], and for locally compact Hausdorff spaces [22, Corollary 4.12].

**Corollary 3.20.** Extremally disconnected spaces are compact in $\mathscr{C}\mathrm{ond}(\mathscr{A}\mathrm{n})$.
