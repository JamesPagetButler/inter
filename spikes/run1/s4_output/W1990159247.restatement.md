## Abstract (scope)

The paper studies flows of $G_2$-structures on a 7-manifold $M$. It uses local coordinates to describe the four torsion forms of a $G_2$-structure and derives evolution equations under a general flow of $\varphi$ for the metric $g$, the dual 4-form $\psi$, and the four torsion forms. It gives a new proof of a theorem of Fernández–Gray. It derives a Bianchi-type identity in $G_2$-geometry and, from it, explicit formulas for the Ricci tensor and part of the Riemann curvature tensor in terms of the torsion.

## Section 1: Notation and Conventions

**Forms and metrics.** A $k$-form is written $\alpha=\frac{1}{k!}\alpha_{i_1\cdots i_k}\,dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_k}$ with $\alpha_{i_1\cdots i_k}$ completely skew-symmetric. The induced inner product is

$$g(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{k!}\,\alpha_{i_1\cdots i_k}\beta_{j_1\cdots j_k}g^{i_1j_1}\cdots g^{i_kj_k}\qquad (1.1)$$

**Covariant derivatives.** For a $(0,k)$ tensor $T$,

$$\nabla_m T_{i_1\cdots i_k}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^m}T_{i_1\cdots i_k}-\Gamma^l_{mi_1}T_{l i_2\cdots i_k}-\cdots-\Gamma^l_{mi_k}T_{i_1i_2\cdots l}\qquad (1.2)$$

The exterior derivative of a $k$-form is

$$d\alpha=\frac{1}{k!}(\nabla_m\alpha_{i_1\cdots i_k})\,dx^m\wedge dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_k}\qquad (1.3)$$

**Hodge star and interior product.** For a vector field $v$ and a $k$-form $\alpha$ in dimension 7:

$$\ast(v\lrcorner\alpha)=(-1)^{k+1}(v^\flat\wedge\ast\alpha),\qquad \ast(v\lrcorner\ast\alpha)=(-1)^k(v^\flat\wedge\alpha),\qquad v\lrcorner\mathsf{vol}=\ast v^\flat \qquad (1.4)$$

**Coderivative.** $\delta=d^\ast$ satisfies

$$\delta=(-1)^k\ast d\ast\ \text{ on }\Omega^k \qquad (1.5)$$

$$(\delta\alpha)_{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}}=-g^{lm}\nabla_l\alpha_{m i_1\cdots i_{k-1}} \qquad (1.6)$$

**Lie derivative.** For a tensor $\alpha$ and vector field $Y$,

$$(\mathcal{L}_Y\alpha)_{i_1\cdots i_k}=(\nabla_Y\alpha)_{i_1\cdots i_k}+(\nabla_{i_1}Y^l)\alpha_{l i_2\cdots i_k}+\cdots+(\nabla_{i_k}Y^l)\alpha_{i_1\cdots i_{k-1}l} \qquad (1.7)$$

**Curvature.** $R^m_{ijk}\partial_m=(\nabla_i\nabla_j-\nabla_j\nabla_i)\partial_k$, $R_{ijkl}=R^m_{ijk}g_{ml}$, and the Ricci tensor is $R_{jk}=R_{ijkl}g^{il}$. The following hold:

$$R_{ijkl}=-R_{jikl}=-R_{ijlk}=R_{klij}$$

$$R_{ijkl}+R_{iklj}+R_{iljk}=0 \qquad (1.8)$$

$$\nabla_k\nabla_iX_l-\nabla_i\nabla_kX_l=-R_{kilm}X^m \qquad (1.9)$$

*Citations in this section: [5] (evolution of curvature quantities), [6, 11] (references for curvature conventions), [14] (Hodge star identities).*

## Section 2: $G_2$-Structures

**Theorem 2.1 (Fernández-Gray, 1982).** The $G_2$-structure corresponding to $\varphi$ is torsion-free if and only if $\varphi$ is both closed and co-closed:
$$d\varphi=0\qquad d\ast_\varphi\varphi=d\psi=0$$

**Remark 2.2.** A form $\varphi$ is harmonic if $(dd^*+d^*d)\varphi=0$. On a compact manifold this is equivalent to $d\varphi=0$ and $d\ast\varphi=0$. Since a parallel form is closed and co-closed, Theorem 2.1 implies that on a compact manifold with $G_2$-structure $\varphi$, the 3-form $\varphi$ is parallel if and only if it is harmonic with respect to its induced metric.

**Remark 2.3.** *(See [13].)* For a $G_2$-manifold the holonomy is one of:

- $\mathrm{Hol}_g(M)=\{1\}$ $\Leftrightarrow$ $b_1(M)=7$
- $\mathrm{Hol}_g(M)=\mathrm{SU}(2)$ $\Leftrightarrow$ $b_1(M)=3$
- $\mathrm{Hol}_g(M)=\mathrm{SU}(3)$ $\Leftrightarrow$ $b_1(M)=1$
- $\mathrm{Hol}_g(M)=\mathrm{G_2}$ $\Leftrightarrow$ $b_1(M)=0$

Full holonomy $\mathrm{G_2}$ is considered, so $\pi_1(M)$ is assumed finite.

**Remark 2.4.** *(See [13].)* In the compact case, the following are known obstructions to a torsion-free $G_2$-structure. First, $b_3(M)\geq 1$, since $\varphi$ is a nonzero harmonic 3-form. Second, if full holonomy $\mathrm{G_2}$ is required, then $b_1(M)=0$ (Remark 2.3) and the first Pontryagin class $p_1(M)\neq 0$. Conditions on the cohomology ring are also known.

**Remark 2.5.** *(See [15].)* In the other orientation convention, the eigenvalues of $\beta\mapsto\ast(\varphi\wedge\beta)$ are $+2$ and $-1$ instead of $-2$ and $+1$, respectively.

**Proposition 2.6.** Let $\beta=\frac12\beta_{ij}\,dx^i\wedge dx^j\in\Omega^2$. Then

$$\beta\in\Omega^2_7\ \Leftrightarrow\ \beta_{ij}g^{il}g^{jm}\psi_{lmab}=-4\beta_{ab}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \beta_{ij}=X^k\varphi_{ijk}$$

$$\beta\in\Omega^2_{14}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \beta_{ij}g^{il}g^{jm}\psi_{lmab}=2\beta_{ab}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \beta_{ij}g^{il}g^{jm}\varphi_{lmk}=0$$

**Remark 2.7.** The equivalences in Proposition 2.6 can be established in index notation using Lemmas A.12, A.13, and A.14.

**Remark 2.8.** If $X=X^k\partial_k$ is a vector field, the associated $\Omega^2_7$ form is $\frac12X_{ab}\,dx^a\wedge dx^b$, where $X_{ab}=X^l\varphi_{lab}$. Using Lemma A.12, $X^k$ can be recovered as $X^k=\frac16X_{ab}\varphi_{mpq}g^{ap}g^{bq}g^{mk}$. In summary:

$$X_{ab}=X^l\varphi_{lab}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad X^k=\frac16X_{ab}\varphi_{mpq}g^{ap}g^{bq}g^{mk}\qquad(2.11)$$

**Lemma 2.9.** Let $\beta_{ij}$ be a 2-form. If $\beta_{ij}\in\Omega^2_{14}$, then

$$\beta_{ab}g^{bl}\varphi_{lpq}=\beta_{pl}g^{lm}\varphi_{maq}-\beta_{ql}g^{lm}\varphi_{map}\qquad(2.12)$$

If $\beta\in\Omega^2_7$, then

$$\beta_{ab}g^{bl}\varphi_{lpq}=-\frac12\beta_{pl}g^{lm}\varphi_{maq}+\frac12\beta_{ql}g^{lm}\varphi_{map}-\frac32g_{pa}\beta_q+\frac32g_{qa}\beta_p\qquad(2.13)$$

where $\beta_k=\frac16\beta_{ij}g^{ia}g^{jb}\varphi_{abk}$ as in (2.11).

**Corollary 2.10.** $\Omega^2_{14}$ is a Lie algebra under the matrix commutator
$$[\beta,\mu]_{ij}=\beta_{il}g^{lm}\mu_{mj}-\mu_{il}g^{lm}\beta_{mj}$$

**Remark 2.11.** $\Omega^2_{14}\cong\mathfrak{g}_2$, the Lie algebra of $\mathrm{G_2}$.

**Proposition 2.12.** If $\beta\in\Omega^2_{14}$, then
$$(\beta(X))_{ab}=[\beta,X]_{ab}\qquad(2.14)$$
If $\beta\in\Omega^2_7$, then
$$(\beta(X))_{ab}=-\frac12[\beta,X]_{ab}-\frac32\beta_aX_b+\frac32\beta_bX_a\qquad(2.15)$$
where $[\beta,X]$ is the matrix commutator of $\beta$ and $X$ regarded as elements of $\Omega^2$.

**Proposition 2.13.** The kernel of $D$ is isomorphic to $\Omega^2_{14}$. It is also isomorphic to the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_2$ of the subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})$ preserving $\varphi$.

**Proposition 2.14.** Let $h_{ij}$ be a symmetric tensor and $\eta=i(h_{ij})\in\Omega^3$ be given by
$$\eta=h_{ij}g^{jl}\,dx^i\wedge\left(\partial_l\lrcorner\varphi\right)=\tfrac12h^l_i\varphi_{ljk}\,dx^i\wedge dx^j\wedge dx^k$$
Then
$$\ast\eta=\left(\tfrac14\operatorname{Tr}_g(h)\,g_{ij}-h_{ij}\right)g^{jl}\,dx^i\wedge\left(\partial_l\lrcorner\psi\right)$$
where $\operatorname{Tr}_g(h)=g^{ij}h_{ij}$.

**Proposition 2.15.** Let $f_{ij}$ and $h_{ij}$ be symmetric tensors, with corresponding forms $i(f),i(h)\in\Omega^3$. Then
$$i(f)\wedge\ast(i(h))=g(i(f),i(h))\,\mathsf{vol}=\left(\operatorname{Tr}_g(f)\operatorname{Tr}_g(h)+2f^k_lh^l_k\right)\mathsf{vol}$$

**Corollary 2.16.** The map $i:S^2(T)\to\Omega^3$ is injective, and is an isomorphism onto its image $\Omega^3_1\oplus\Omega^3_{27}$.

**Proposition 2.17.** The map $j:\Omega^3\to S^2(T)$ is an isomorphism between $\Omega^3_1\oplus\Omega^3_{27}$ and $S^2(T)$. Consequently $\Omega^3_7$ is the kernel of $j$. Explicitly, if
$$\eta=h_{ij}g^{jl}\,dx^i\wedge(\partial_l\lrcorner\varphi)+(X\lrcorner\psi)=i(h)+(X\lrcorner\psi)$$
then
$$j(\eta)=-2\operatorname{Tr}_g(h)\,g_{ij}-4h_{ij}$$

**Remark 2.18.** A symmetric tensor decomposes as $h_{ij}=\frac17\operatorname{Tr}_g(h)g_{ij}+h^0_{ij}$, with $h^0$ trace-free. The first term of $i(h)$ can then be written as $\frac37h\varphi+\frac12(h^0)^l_i\varphi_{ljk}\,dx^i\wedge dx^j\wedge dx^k$, which displays the $\Omega^3_1$ and $\Omega^3_{27}$ components explicitly.

**Definition 2.19.** There are four independent torsion forms of a $G_2$-structure $\varphi$, following the notation of [2]:
$$\tau_0\in\Omega^0_1,\quad \tau_1\in\Omega^1_7,\quad \tau_2\in\Omega^2_{14},\quad \tau_3\in\Omega^3_{27}$$
They are defined by
$$d\varphi=\tau_0\psi+3\tau_1\wedge\varphi+\ast\tau_3\qquad(2.22)$$
$$d\psi=4\tau_1\wedge\psi+\ast\tau_2$$

The statement that the $\Omega^4_7$ component of $d\varphi$ and the $\Omega^5_7$ component of $d\psi$ agree up to a constant is proved in Theorem 2.23. A representation-theoretic proof is described in [4, 2].

**Remark 2.20.** $\tau_0$ is called the scalar torsion, $\tau_1$ the vector torsion, $\tau_2$ the Lie algebra torsion, and $\tau_3$ the symmetric traceless torsion.

**Remark 2.21.** In [2] the second equation is written $d\psi=4\tau_1\wedge\psi+\tau_2\wedge\varphi$. This is equivalent, since $\tau_2\wedge\varphi=\ast\tau_2$ for $\tau_2\in\Omega^2_{14}$ in the orientation convention used here (see [14]).

**Remark 2.22.** *(See [14].)* The Lee form is $\theta=-12\tau_1$.

**Theorem 2.23.** The appearance of the same one-form $\tau_1$ in the expressions for both $d\varphi$ and $d\psi$ in (2.22) is justified.

**Lemma 2.24.** For any vector field $X$, the 3-form $\nabla_X\varphi$ lies in $\Omega^3_7$. Therefore $\nabla\varphi$ lies in $\Omega^1_7\otimes\Omega^3_7$, a 49-dimensional space pointwise.

**Remark 2.25.** *(See [8].)* Fernández and Gray study the symmetries of $\nabla\varphi$ and obtain $\nabla\varphi\in\Omega^1_7\otimes\Omega^3_7$, as stated in Lemma 2.24. Their argument uses an extension of the cross product to the full exterior bundle.

**Definition 2.26.** Since $\nabla_l\varphi\in\Omega^3_7$, by (2.7) we can write
$$\nabla_l\varphi_{abc}=T_{lm}g^{mn}\psi_{nabc}$$
for some 2-tensor $T_{lm}$, called the full torsion tensor.

**Theorem 2.27.** The covariant derivative of $\varphi$ is $\nabla_l\varphi_{abc}=T_{lm}g^{mn}\psi_{nabc}$, where
$$T_{lm}=\frac{\tau_0}{4}g_{lm}-(\tau_3)_{lm}+(\tau_1)_{lm}-\frac12(\tau_2)_{lm}$$

**Corollary 2.28.** The 3-form $\varphi$ is parallel if and only if it is closed and co-closed. (This is Theorem 2.1.)

**Remark 2.29.** Starting from $\nabla_l\varphi_{abc}=T_{lm}g^{mn}\psi_{nabc}$, and using (A.11) and Lemma A.13, one obtains
$$\nabla_m\psi_{ijkl}=-T_{mi}\varphi_{jkl}+T_{mj}\varphi_{ikl}-T_{mk}\varphi_{ijl}+T_{ml}\varphi_{ijk}\qquad(2.28)$$
This is used in Section 4.

**Lemma 2.30.** The full torsion tensor is
$$T_{lm}=\frac{1}{24}(\nabla_l\varphi_{abc})\psi_{mijk}g^{ia}g^{jb}g^{kc}\qquad(2.29)$$

**Proposition 2.31.** Writing $T_{pq}=S_{pq}+C_{pq}$, the four torsion forms are
$$\tau_0=\frac47g^{pq}S_{pq}$$
$$(\tau_3)_{pq}=\frac14\tau_0g_{pq}-S_{pq}$$
$$(\tau_1)_{pq}=\frac13C_{pq}-\frac16C_{ij}g^{ia}g^{jb}\psi_{abpq}$$
$$(\tau_2)_{pq}=-\frac43C_{pq}-\frac13C_{ij}g^{ia}g^{jb}\psi_{abpq}$$

**Theorem 2.32.** Let $\widetilde\varphi=f^3\varphi$, where $f$ is any nowhere-vanishing smooth function. Then $\tilde g=f^2g$, and the 4-form satisfies $\tilde\ast\tilde\varphi=f^4\ast\varphi$ as printed (the symbol before $\widetilde\varphi$ is garbled in the source and is presumably $\tilde\psi$). ## Section 3: Evolution Equations

**Theorem 3.1.** Under the flow (3.1), $B_{ij}$ evolves by
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}B_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}_g(h)B_{ij}+h^l_iB_{lj}+h^l_jB_{il}\qquad(3.3)$$
The evolution of $B_{ij}$ depends only on the symmetric 2-tensor $h_{ij}$ and not on the vector field $X^k$.

**Corollary 3.2.** Under the flow (3.1),
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}=2h_{ij}}\qquad(3.5)$$

**Corollary 3.3.** Under the flow (3.1),
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{ij}=-2h^{ij}}\qquad(3.6)$$
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathsf{vol}=\operatorname{Tr}_g(h)\,\mathsf{vol}}\qquad(3.7)$$

**Proposition 3.4.** Under the flow (3.1), the Christoffel symbols evolve by
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Gamma^k_{ij}=g^{kl}\left(\nabla_ih_{jl}+\nabla_jh_{il}-\nabla_lh_{ij}\right)\qquad(3.8)$$

**Theorem 3.5.** Under the flow (3.1), the 4-form $\psi_{ijkl}$ evolves by
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi_{ijkl}=h^m_i\psi_{mjkl}+h^m_j\psi_{imkl}+h^m_k\psi_{ijml}+h^m_l\psi_{ijkm}$$
$$\qquad\qquad -X_i\varphi_{jkl}+X_j\varphi_{ikl}-X_k\varphi_{ijl}+X_l\varphi_{ijk}\qquad(3.10)$$
where $X_k=g_{kl}X^l$.

**Remark 3.6.** Let $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)=\eta_1+\eta_7+\eta_{27}$, with $\eta_k\in\Omega^3_k$ associated to $\varphi(t)$. Then
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(t)=\frac43\left(\ast_{\varphi(t)}\eta_1\right)+\left(\ast_{\varphi(t)}\eta_7\right)-\left(\ast_{\varphi(t)}\eta_{27}\right)$$
This is stated in [2, 12, 13], and an explicit proof is given in [10]. The remark states that Theorem 3.5 agrees with this result. An arbitrary 3-form $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi=\eta$ can be written uniquely as
