# Restatement

## Abstract

The review introduces non-integrable geometric structures on Riemannian manifolds and invariant metric connections with torsion. It also discusses mathematical physics, in particular superstring theory, where these objects arise. Connections with skew-symmetric torsion are presented as a main tool. Intrinsic torsion and the characteristic connection of a $G$-structure are used as unifying principles. Several Weitzenböck formulas for Dirac operators associated with torsion connections are given, together with their relation to Kostant's cubic Dirac operator.

## 1. Background and motivation

### 1.1 Introduction

**Equation (1)** (for trivial $\tau$, on the symmetric space $G/K$, with $G$ non-compact semisimple, $K$ a maximal compact subgroup, and $\mathrm{rank}\,G=\mathrm{rank}\,K$):
$$D^{2}\ =\ \Omega_{G}+\frac{1}{8}\,\mathrm{Scal}.$$

**Equation (2)** (Friedrich's inequality [Fri80], for the first eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ of the Dirac operator on a compact Riemannian manifold $M^n$ of non-negative curvature):
$$(\lambda_{1})^{2}\ \geq\ \frac{n}{4(n-1)}\,\min_{M^{n}}(\mathrm{scal}).$$
Equality holds precisely if the corresponding eigenspinor $\psi$ satisfies the Killing equation
$$\nabla^{g}_{X}\psi\ =\ \pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\min(\mathrm{scal})}{n(n-1)}}\,X\cdot\psi\ =:\ \mu\,X\cdot\psi.$$

**Statements in the introduction (cited results):**

- Berger's theorem (1955) [Ber55] (corrections in [Sim62], status report in [Br96]): the groups in Table 1 are the only possible restricted holonomy groups of a simply connected, irreducible, non-symmetric Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$.
- The existence of metrics with holonomy $\mathrm{SU}(m)$ or $\mathrm{Sp}(m)$ on compact manifolds follows from Yau's solution of the Calabi Conjecture [Yau78].
- Explicit non-compact metrics with holonomy $G_2$ or $\mathrm{Spin}(7)$ are due to Bryant [Br87] and Bryant–Salamon [BrS89]. Compact manifolds with these holonomies were constructed by Joyce [Joy96a], [Joy96b], [Joy96c], [Joy00] and by Kovalev [Kov03].
- Bonan [Bon66] (see Proposition 2.2): the existence of a $\nabla^g$-parallel spinor implies vanishing Ricci curvature. Further restrictions on holonomy are given in [Hit74] and [McKW89].
- Parthasarathy, Wolf, Atiyah and Schmid [Par72], [Wol74], [AS77]: for suitable $\tau$, most discrete series representations of $G$ can be realized on the $L^2$-kernel of the twisted Dirac operator on $G/K$.
- Gray [Gra71] introduced weak holonomy. Manifolds with weak holonomy $\mathrm{U}(n)$ and $G_2$ are nearly Kähler and nearly parallel $G_2$-manifolds, respectively.
- Gruber [Gru90]: a compact, connected, simply connected 6-dimensional Hermitian manifold is nearly Kähler if and only if it admits a Killing spinor with real Killing number $\mu$.
- Similar results hold for Einstein–Sasaki structures in dimension 5 and nearly parallel $G_2$-manifolds in dimension 7 ([FK89], [FK90]).
- Lichnerowicz [Lich88]: on a compact manifold, the space of twistor spinors coincides, up to a conformal change of the metric, with the space of Killing spinors.

### 1.2 Mathematical motivation

Klein [Kl72, p. 34] (Erlangen Programme):
> Let a manifold and on it a transformation group be given; the objects belonging to the manifold ought to be studied with respect to those properties which are not changed by the transformations of the group.

### 1.3 Physical motivation – torsion in gravity

- Einstein–Cartan field equations (Kibble [Kib61], Sciama [In62]). The first is Einstein's equation with an effective energy-momentum tensor $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ depending on torsion. The second is
$$Q(X,Y)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(Q(Y,e_{i})\lrcorner\, e_{i}\big)\cdot X-\big(Q(X,e_{i})\lrcorner\, e_{i}\big)\cdot Y\ =\ 8\pi\, S(X,Y),$$
where $Q$ is the torsion of the connection $\nabla$, $S$ is the spin density, and $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is any orthonormal frame.
- Trautman [Tra73a] gave a formulation in the language of principal fibre bundles.

### 1.4 Physical motivation – torsion in superstring theory

- The configuration space is $Y^{10,11}=V^{3\text{-}5}\times M^{5\text{-}8}$, a product of a low-dimensional spacetime $V$ and an internal space $M$ with special geometric structure.
- By the General Holonomy Principle (Theorem 2.7, referenced but not reproduced in this text), the holonomy group is a subgroup of the stabilizer of the set of parallel spinors in $\mathrm{Spin}(9,1)$.

### 1.5 First developments since 1980

**Strominger's model** [Str86]: a 6-tuple $(M^n,g,\nabla,T,\Phi,\Psi)$ consisting of a Riemannian spin manifold $(M^n,g)$, a 3-form $T$, a dilaton $\Phi$, and a spinor $\Psi$, satisfying
$$\mathrm{Ric}_{ij}-\tfrac{1}{4}T_{imn}T_{jmn}+2\nabla^{g}_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi=0,\qquad \delta\big(e^{-2\Phi}T\big)=0,$$
$$\Big(\nabla^{g}_{X}+\tfrac{1}{4}\,X\lrcorner T\Big)\psi=0,\qquad (2\,d\Phi-T)\cdot\psi=0.$$
The connection with torsion $T$ is
$$\nabla_{X}Y\ :=\ \nabla^{g}_{X}Y+\tfrac{1}{2}\,T(X,Y,-).$$
The third equation is equivalent to $\nabla\Psi=0$.

**Equation (3)** (constant dilaton [IP01]):
$$\mathrm{Ric}^{\nabla}=0,\qquad \delta^{g}(T)=0,\qquad \nabla\Psi=0,\qquad T\cdot\Psi=0.$$
The equation $\delta^g(T)=0$ follows from $\mathrm{Ric}^\nabla=0$.

- [Agr03, Theorem 4.1]: for $M$ compact, a solution of all equations forces $T=0$, i.e. an integrable Ricci-flat geometry with classical holonomy from Berger's list. This was extended to the non-compact case in [AFNP05].
- Friedrich and Ivanov [FI02]: many non-integrable structures (almost contact metric, nearly Kähler, weak $G_2$) admit a unique invariant connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion.
- Hitchin [Hit01]: non-integrable geometries are critical points of certain linear functionals on differential forms.
- In dimension three [SSTP88]: the equation $\nabla\Psi=0$ can be solved only on a compact Lie group with bi-invariant metric, and the torsion of the invariant connection coincides with the Lie bracket.
- In dimension four [IP01]: the Strominger model gives a HKT structure. In the compact case the manifold is a Calabi–Yau manifold or a Hopf surface.
- Ramond and Pengpan: $\mathrm{Spin}(9)$ has an infinite set of irreducible representations partitioned into triplets $\mathcal{S}=\cup_i\{\mu^i,\sigma^i,\tau^i\}$. The infinitesimal character value of the Casimir operator is constant on triplets, and $\dim\mu^i+\dim\sigma^i=\dim\tau^i$ if numbered appropriately [BRX02].
- Kostant's cubic Dirac operator [GKRS98], [Kos99]. The Euler characteristic of $F_4/\mathrm{Spin}(9)$ is three.

## 2. Connections with torsion

**Proposition 2.1** ([Car25, p. 51], [TV83], [Sal89]): The spaces $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{A}^g$ are isomorphic as $\mathrm{O}(n)$-representations. An equivariant bijection is
$$T(X,Y,Z)=A(X,Y,Z)-A(Y,X,Z),\qquad 2\,A(X,Y,Z)=T(X,Y,Z)-T(Y,Z,X)+T(Z,X,Y).$$
For $n\geq 3$, under $\mathrm{O}(n)$,
$$\mathcal{T}\cong TM^{n}\oplus\Lambda^{3}(M^{n})\oplus\mathcal{T}'.$$
The last summand is also denoted $\mathcal{A}'$ when viewed as a subspace of $\mathcal{A}^g$. It is equivalent to the Cartan product of $TM^n\otimes\Lambda^2TM^n$, and
$$\mathcal{T}'=\Big\{T\in\mathcal{T}\ \Big|\ \mathfrak{S}_{X,Y,Z}\,T(X,Y,Z)=0,\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{n}T(e_i,e_i,X)=0\ \ \forall X,Y,Z\Big\}$$
for any orthonormal frame $e_1,\ldots,e_n$. For $n=2$, $\mathcal{T}\cong\mathcal{A}^g\cong\mathbb{R}^2$ is $\mathrm{O}(2)$-irreducible.

**Definition 2.1** (Connection with vectorial torsion): $\nabla$ has vectorial torsion if its torsion tensor lies in the first summand of Proposition 2.1, i.e. it is defined by a vector field $V$ on $M$. Then
$$A(X,Y)=g(X,Y)\,V-g(V,Y)\,X,\qquad T(X,Y,Z)=g\big(g(V,X)Y-g(V,Y)X,\,Z\big).$$
- Tricerri and Vanhecke [TV83]: if $M$ is connected, complete, simply connected, and $V$ is $\nabla$-parallel, then $(M,g)$ is isometric to hyperbolic space.
- Every metric connection on a surface is of this type.
- Miquel [Miq82], [Miq01] studied growth of geodesic balls. Geodesics are treated in [AT04] (Example 2.7). Holonomy and string-theoretic aspects appear in [AF05] and [IPP05].
- Weyl structures (cf. [CP99], [Gau95]): the torsion-free connection
$$\nabla^{\mathrm{w}}_{X}Y\ :=\ \nabla^{g}_{X}Y+g(X,V)\,Y+g(Y,V)\,X-g(X,Y)\,V.$$

**Definition 2.2** (Connection with skew-symmetric torsion): $\nabla$ has (totally) skew-symmetric torsion if its torsion tensor lies in the second summand of Proposition 2.1, i.e. it is given by a 3-form. Such connections are used in string theory and weak holonomy theories (see [Str86], [LT89], [GKMW01], [CKL01], [FP02], [Duf02], [AF04a]).

**Corollary 2.1:** A connection $\nabla$ on $(M^n,g)$ is metric and geodesic-preserving if and only if its torsion $T$ lies in $\Lambda^3(TM^n)$. In this case $2A=T$,
$$\nabla_{X}Y\ =\ \nabla^{g}_{X}Y+\tfrac{1}{2}\,T(X,Y,-),$$
and the $\nabla$-Killing vector fields coincide with the Riemannian Killing vector fields.

**Lemma 2.1:** The lift of any metric connection $\nabla$ on $TM^n$ to the spinor bundle $\Sigma M^n$ satisfies
$$\nabla_{X}(Y\cdot\psi)=(\nabla_{X}Y)\cdot\psi+Y\cdot(\nabla_{X}\psi),\qquad X(\psi_1,\psi_2)=(\nabla_X\psi_1,\psi_2)+(\psi_1,\nabla_X\psi_2).$$

**Example 2.1** (Vectorial torsion): For $V\in TM$, the associated $A_X=2\,X\wedge V$ satisfies
$$Y\lrcorner(2\,X\wedge V)=g(X,Y)\,V-g(Y,V)\,X.$$

**Example 2.2** (Skew-symmetric torsion): For $T\in\Lambda^3(M)$, $A_X=X\lrcorner T$.

**Example 2.3** (Higher-order forms): For forms $\omega^k\in\Lambda^k(M)$ and $\eta^l\in\Lambda^l(M)$ with $k,l\geq 4$, the connection
$$\nabla_{X}\psi:=\nabla^{g}_{X}\psi+(X\lrcorner\,\omega^{k})\cdot\psi+(X\wedge\eta^{l})\cdot\psi$$
is a metric spinorial connection not induced from the tangent bundle. It is used for higher-dimensional membranes [AF03], [Pu06].

**Example 2.4** (General case): Let $n=3$ and let $\nabla$ be the metric connection with vectorial torsion $V=e_1$. Then
$$A_X=X\wedge e_1=g(X,e_2)\,e_2\wedge e_1+g(X,e_3)\,e_3\wedge e_1.$$
The form $\tilde A_X:=g(X,e_2)\,e_2\wedge e_1-g(X,e_3)\,e_3\wedge e_1$ defines a metric linear connection. It is orthogonal to $\Lambda^3(M)\oplus\mathfrak{X}(M)$ and therefore lies in $\mathcal{A}'$. Such connections appear in closed $G_2$-geometry [Br03], [CI03]. The canonical connection of an almost Kähler manifold is of this type.

**Theorem 2.1** ([TV84b, Thm 2.3]): A connected, simply connected and complete Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ is naturally reductive homogeneous if and only if there exists a skew-symmetric tensor field $T$ of type $(1,2)$ such that $\nabla:=\nabla^g-T$ is a metric connection with $\nabla T=\nabla\mathcal{R}=0$.
