# Restatement

## Abstract

The paper studies the partition function of spacetime fields in M-theory and how it depends on the choice of Spin structure. Multiple Spin structures lead to a potential anomaly in the eta invariants under variation of the Spin structure. The main sources of such spaces are manifolds with nontrivial fundamental group. The paper extends the discussion to Spin^c structures, finds the phase of the partition function, and revisits the quantization condition for the C-field.

In type IIA string theory in 10 dimensions, the mod 2 index of the Dirac operator is the obstruction to a well-defined partition function. The paper characterizes geometrically the manifolds with and without this anomaly, and extends this to nontrivial fundamental group. The lift to KO-theory gives the α-invariant, which in general depends on the Spin structure. In the 12-dimensional bounding M-theory, the paper studies similar geometric questions, including choices of metrics and the construction of elements of K-theory in 10 dimensions by pushforward on the disk fiber. This is interpreted through the families index theorem for Dirac operators on the M-theory circle and disk, which involves superconnections, eta forms, and infinite-dimensional bundles, and gives elements of Deligne cohomology in lower dimensions.

## 1 Introduction and Summary

**Index theorems.** The Atiyah–Singer index theorem in even dimensions, without boundary, involves invariants that do not depend on the choice of connection or Spin structure. In the presence of a boundary, the boundary contribution depends on geometric and analytic quantities. M-theory is described in this setting of the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer (APS) index theorem [11] [12] [13] [165].

**Topological part of the M-theory action.** The topological part I_top of the action consists of the Chern–Simons term [42] and the one-loop term [159] [61]. Witten [165] showed that I_top can be written as a sum of the index of a Dirac operator coupled to an E_8 bundle and a Rarita–Schwinger operator. The cohomology class of the degree-four field G_4 contains, as a summand, a characteristic class of an E_8 bundle. The E_8 × E_8 heterotic string on the boundary is also relevant [96].

**Dimensional reduction.** The reduction of the E_8 gauge theory from the total space Y^11 of a circle bundle to ten dimensions was performed in [54]. There the partition function was matched, in a limit, with the K-theoretic partition function of the Ramond–Ramond fields in type IIA string theory.

**Phase of the partition function.** The partition function of the C-field C_3 and the Rarita–Schwinger field ψ_1 factorizes into a modulus and a phase. The phase is given by [54] in Eq. (1.1). Here η̄_V = ½(η_V + dim Ker D_V) = ½(η_V + h_V) is the reduced eta invariant of the Dirac operator D_V coupled to V. The bundle V is either the E_8 bundle or the Rarita–Schwinger bundle RS = TY^11 − 3𝒪, where 𝒪 is a trivial real line bundle.

**Symmetry under Y^11 = X^10 × S^1.** The C-field is a pullback from X^10 and has an orientation-reversing symmetry. Under this symmetry the Chern–Simons term changes sign and the phase of the partition function is complex-conjugated. The Dirac operator changes sign under reflection of one coordinate, so the nonzero eigenvalues occur in pairs (λ, −λ), and the eta invariants vanish [54].

**Further works.** The E_8 gauge theory description of M-theory and type IIA was studied in [116], including the twisted K-theory partition function in the presence of the Neveu–Schwarz H-field H_3 (or its potential, the B-field). Gerbes were studied in [148].

**Scope of this paper.** The paper studies effects related to Spin and Spin^c structures. In the works cited above, both the ten- and eleven-dimensional manifolds are Spin. The paper studies the dependence of the phase on the Spin structure (from Section 2), then extends to Spin^c (from Section 2.6, and more fully in Section 3). It relates Spin and Spin^c structures in eleven and ten dimensions. It first treats the case where the ten-dimensional manifold is Spin^c, and then the case where both are Spin^c.

**Main points** (as stated in the paper):

1. **Spin structures in M-theory.** When multiple Spin structures exist, analytic and geometric entities, and hence physical entities, may differ (Section 2). For example, the mod 2 index of the Dirac operator in dimensionally reduced M-theory admits a KO-theory lift whose value depends on the Spin structure (Section 5). The paper suggests that the full partition function, including metric moduli, should include contributions from the different Spin structures. For the bounding Spin structure on the M-theory circle, connections to eta forms, adiabatic limits and superconnections are discussed (Section 7).

2. **Non-simply connected manifolds.** Manifolds with multiple Spin structures are not simply connected. The main examples are spherical space forms S^n/Γ, which in even dimensions can only be projective spaces. Representations of the finite group Γ give multiple Spin structures. At the level of bundles, this is encoded by flat vector bundles and their holonomy. Fields in ten and eleven dimensions that could support nontrivial fundamental group are identified (Section 2, Sections 6.1 and 6.2). Framed manifolds are discussed at the end of Section 6.2. Second homotopy groups, relevant to Spin^c, are treated in Section 3 and Section 6.3.

3. **Spin vs. Spin^c across dimensions.** M-theory is related to type IIA by quotient by a circle action. The spaces are Spin or Spin^c depending on whether the circle action lifts to the Spin bundle. Starting from an eleven-dimensional Spin manifold: for an action of even type (projectable), the ten-dimensional quotient is Spin; for an action of odd type (nonprojectable), it is Spin^c. These cases are studied in Section 2 (especially 2.5 and 2.6) and Section 3.

4. **Dependence of the phase on the Spin structure.** The phase in Eq. (1.1) is given in terms of exponentiated eta invariants. The paper asks whether it depends on the Spin structure, and analyzes both operators. Examples in eleven and seven dimensions show how the eta function depends on the Spin structure. The number of zero modes of the Dirac operators is also extracted, and these appear in the phase (Section 2.7).

5. **Spin^c extension.** The phase is described for Spin^c in Section 3, with motivation in Section 2.6 and Section 3.2. Adiabatic-limit results are in Section 3.8, building on Section 3.6. Dependence on almost complex structures is in Section 4.1. Obstructions and generalized cohomology are in Section 3.5. The M2- and M5-brane partition functions in the Spin^c case are in Section 3.7.

6. **Ricci and scalar curvature.** The eta invariant and mod 2 index are studied from the viewpoint of curvature, starting in Section 5.1. The mod 2 index appears in the K-theoretic anomaly of the Ramond–Ramond fields in ten dimensions [54]. In Section 5.2 it is characterized topologically, analytically and geometrically. In dimensions 2 and 10 the mod 2 index is an instance of the Â-genus and has a KO-theory lift, the α-invariant, which depends on the Spin structure. Vanishing of α relates to positive scalar curvature metrics via the Gromov–Lawson–Rosenberg conjecture. A Clifford-linear Dirac operator [107] is used. The kernel of α determines when the mod 2 index vanishes, hence when the ten-dimensional partition function is anomaly-free. Total spaces of ℍP^2 bundles represent each cobordism class of ker α, including in the non-simply connected case (Section 5.2). In Section 5.3 the bounding twelve-manifold is studied in the context of the APS index theorem, with preferred metrics compatible with the fibration between type IIA and M-theory.

7. **Families case.** The M-theory circle is treated as a fibre over the ten-dimensional base. Dirac operators on the circle are parametrized by the base.
   - Quillen and Bismut superconnections are discussed in Section 7.2. Superconnections represent the pushforward in K-theory, producing K-theory elements on the base from bundles on the fibre. Infinite-dimensional bundles are characterized.
   - Generalized dimensional reduction is discussed in Section 7.2.2. In the Scherk–Schwarz reduction, the fibre dependence is linear, for example v(x,z) = mz + v(x). The paper considers non-linear fibre dependence and allows nonzero ∇_μ v_z. The second type of generalization was considered in [6], where it was related to metric torsion. The general case involves torsion, which is the RR 2-form F_2.
   - The adiabatic limit of the eta invariant is given by Bismut–Cheeger eta forms (Section 7.3).
   - Eta forms give gerbes and Deligne cohomology classes on the base. The fibre dimension (mostly one or two) gives gerbes of even or odd degree (Section 7.4). Contributions from kernels of vertical Dirac operators are included.
   - Pushforward of bundles from eleven to ten (and lower) dimensions at the level of K-theory is discussed from Section 7.2.2.

8. **Consequences for the fields.** The focus is the M-theory circle. In type IIA, this gives the RR 1-form potential (the connection on the circle bundle) and its curvature F_2. The D0-brane carries the charge under F_2 [138], and such branes carry K-theory charges [122]. Kaluza–Klein modes of the reduction correspond to Adams operations on the line bundle (Section 7.4). The Adams operation raises the brane charge by k units, corresponding to the k-th Kaluza–Klein mode.
   - The B-field is the integral of the C-field over the circle fibre. Section 7.1 characterizes it as the harmonic representative of the Spin^c structure in the adiabatic limit. This is analogous to the description of the C-field as a harmonic representative of the String class in [150].
   - The K-theory class of the family of Dirac operators in ten dimensions gives the RR fields. A K-theoretic formalism relating M-theory and type IIA may be possible without passing through cohomology.
   - The dilaton is the volume of the circle fibre, so its size gives the string coupling. The eigenvalues of the reduced Dirac operators are given in the weak-coupling limit. In the eta-form expression, a scalar parameter is identified essentially with the dilaton, so the eta-form integral is over sizes of the circle (Sections 2.4–2.6, 7.2, 7.3).
   - The dilatino's supersymmetry transformation is the difference between the Lie derivative along the eleventh direction and the covariant derivative. In the families case it becomes the difference between the Bismut and Quillen superconnections (Section 7.2).
   - The C-field has a shifted quantization condition in the Spin case [165]. This is extended to Spin^c in Section 3.7.
   - The parity symmetry of M-theory and its effect on Spin structures and eta forms are considered in Sections 2.3 and 7.3.

**Spin structures.** A Spin structure on an n-manifold M consists of a Spin(n) principal bundle P_Spin(M) over M and a two-fold covering ρ: Spin(n) → SO(n) such that the diagram Eq. (1.2) commutes. The horizontal arrows are given by the principal bundle structure. The set of Spin structures on M is a torsor over H^1(M; ℤ_2). Spin^c structures are defined and studied in Section 3.1. Further discussion is in [107].

**Spin vs. Spin^c and ten vs. eleven dimensions.** Consider the circle bundle S^1 → Y^11 → X^10 with projection π. Let ℒ be the complex line bundle associated to π, with first Chern class e = c_1(ℒ). Since TY^11 ≅ π^*(TX^10) ⊕ TS^1, the fibre tangent bundle is trivialized by the vector field generating the S^1 action on Y^11.

1. If w_2(X^10) = 0, a Spin structure on X^10 induces a Spin structure on Y^11, denoted σ_1.
2. If w_2(X^10) = e mod 2, so that X^10 admits a Spin^c structure, then TX^10 ⊕ ℒ admits a Spin structure. A choice of such a structure gives a Spin structure on the disk bundle 𝔻ℒ. Its restriction to the sphere bundle Sℒ = Y^11 is denoted σ_2.
3. If w_2(X^10) = 0 (X^10 Spin) and e = 0 mod 2, then σ_1 and σ_2 are different Spin structures on Y^11. The restriction of σ_1 to the fibre S^1 is the nontrivial Spin structure, which does not extend over 𝔻^2. The restriction of σ_2 does extend, by construction.

**Reality check 1 (multiple Spin structures and string phenomenology).** Section 2.1 shows that the main source of multiple Spin structures is spaces with nontrivial fundamental group. Heterotic vacua on a Calabi–Yau manifold M require π_1(M) ≠ 1 to produce the standard model gauge group, since only such manifolds admit Wilson lines [163] [153] [64] [38]. Torus-fibred Calabi–Yau threefolds with π_1(M) = ℤ_2 admit stable holomorphic bundles with structure group SU(5) [56] [57] [58]. Those with π_1 = ℤ_2 × ℤ_2 admit stable holomorphic bundles with structure group SU(4) [130] [131] [55]. Examples with π_1(M) = ℤ_3 × ℤ_3 are given in [37]. In all cases the groups break to the standard model group.

**Reality check 2 (multiple Spin structures and cosmology).** The spatial topology of the universe is discussed in the literature. Recent WMAP data is cited as suggesting that the universe is a spherical space form [40] or a hyperbolic space form with "thorned topology" [16]. In both cases the fundamental group is nontrivial. There is no definite answer on whether the universe is simply connected. If it is not, it admits multiple Spin structures.
