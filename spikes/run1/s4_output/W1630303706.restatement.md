# Restatement

## Abstract

The paper considers odd-dimensional, non-orientable Riemannian spaces that admit a pin structure. For these spaces, the twisted adjoint representation of the corresponding pin group is required. The Dirac operator is modified, including on even-dimensional spaces, so that it is equivariant under the action of that group when the twisted adjoint representation is used to define the pin structure. For a hypersurface defined by an immersion into a Euclidean space, an explicit description of the induced pin structure is given and used to derive a Schrödinger-type transform of the Dirac operator. This transform is then applied to determine the spectrum and eigenfunctions of the Dirac operator on spheres and real projective spaces.

**Proposition 1.** For every real quadratic space $(V,h)$, there are two inequivalent central extensions of ${\rm O}(h)$ by ${\mathbb Z}_2$, given by

$$
{\mathbb Z}_2 \to {\rm Pin}(h) \xrightarrow{\ \rho\ } {\rm O}(h)
\quad\text{and}\quad
{\mathbb Z}_2 \to {\rm Pin}(-h) \xrightarrow{\ \rho\ } {\rm O}(h),
\tag{7}
$$

where $\rho$ is as in (2). By restriction to ${\rm Spin}(h)$, each of these extensions reduces to the one given by (5).

**Proposition 2.** Let $(V,h)$ be a quadratic space of dimension $m$, and let $\nu$ denote a positive integer.

(i) If $m$ is even, $m = 2\nu$, then the algebra ${\rm Cl}(h)$ is central simple and, as such, has only one, up to equivalence, faithful and irreducible Dirac representation $\gamma$ in a vector space $S$, which has complex dimension $2^{\nu}$. The restriction of $\gamma$ to ${\rm Cl}^{0}(h)$ decomposes into the direct sum $\gamma_{+}\oplus\gamma_{-}$ of two complex-inequivalent Weyl representations. In a notation adapted to the decomposition $S = S_{+}\oplus S_{-}$ of the space of Dirac spinors into the direct sum of the spaces $S_{+}$ and $S_{-}$ of Weyl spinors, the Dirac matrices are of the form

$$
\gamma_{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & \gamma^{-}_{\mu} \\ \gamma^{+}_{\mu} & 0 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

(ii) If $m$ is odd, $m = 2\nu-1$, then the algebra ${\rm Cl}^{0}(h)$ is central simple and has a faithful and irreducible Pauli representation in a space of complex dimension $2^{\nu-1}$. This representation extends to two representations, $\sigma$ and $\sigma\circ\alpha$, of the full algebra ${\rm Cl}(h)$ in the same space, by putting $\sigma({\rm vol}(h)) = i(h)\,{\rm id}$. These representations, also referred to as Pauli representations of ${\rm Cl}(h)$, are complex-inequivalent and irreducible, but faithful only when $i(h)=i$. A faithful, but reducible, Cartan representation $\gamma$ of ${\rm Cl}(h)$ is defined as $\gamma = \sigma\oplus(\sigma\circ\alpha)$. Therefore, if $\sigma_{\mu}=\sigma(e_{\mu})$, then

$$
\gamma_{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix} \sigma_{\mu} & 0 \\ 0 & -\sigma_{\mu} \end{pmatrix}.
$$

The commutant of the Cartan representation $\gamma$ is generated by $\gamma({\rm vol}(h))$. The elements of the carrier spaces of the representations $\gamma$ and $\sigma$ are called Cartan and Pauli spinors, respectively.

**Proposition 3.** Let $(V,h)$ be a quadratic space of dimension $m = 2\nu$ (resp., $m = 2\nu-1$). There is a faithful representation $\gamma$ of the Clifford algebra ${\rm Cl}(h)$ in a complex vector space $S$ of dimension $2^{\nu}$ such that the representations $\gamma$ and $\gamma\circ\alpha$ are complex-equivalent. The representation is unique, up to complex equivalence, and irreducible (resp., decomposable into two irreducibles). By restriction to the even subalgebra ${\rm Cl}^{0}(h)$, the representation $\gamma$ decomposes into the direct sum of two irreducible representations, each defined in a complex space of dimension $2^{\nu-1}$. The isomorphism $\gamma_{m+1}$ intertwining the representations $\gamma$ and $\gamma\circ\alpha$ can be taken to act on the Dirac (resp., Cartan) spinor $(\varphi,\psi)$ so that

$$
\gamma_{m+1}(\varphi,\psi) = (i\varphi, -i\psi)
\quad\text{(resp., } \gamma_{m+1}(\varphi,\psi) = (-\psi,\varphi)\text{)}.
$$

Irrespective of the parity of $m$, one has

$$
\gamma_{m+1}^{2} = -\,{\rm id}_{S}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\gamma_{m+1}\gamma_{\mu} + \gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{m+1} = 0,
\tag{8}
$$

for $\mu = 1,\dots,m$.

**Proposition 4.** If $\gamma'$ is a representation of ${\rm Cl}_{k,l+1}$ in a complex vector space (of spinors), then $\gamma = \gamma'\circ\iota$ is a representation of ${\rm Cl}_{k,l}$ in the same space. In particular:

(i) If $k+l$ is even and $\gamma'$ is a Pauli representation, then $\gamma$ is the Dirac representation.

(ii) If $k+l$ is odd and $\gamma'$ is the Dirac representation, then $\gamma$ is the Cartan representation.

Moreover, if $\gamma'_{\pm}$ are the Weyl components of $\gamma'$, i.e. $\gamma'|{\rm Cl}^{0}_{k,l+1} = \gamma'_{+}\oplus\gamma'_{-}$, then $\gamma_{\pm} = \gamma'_{\pm}\circ\iota$ are the Pauli components of $\gamma$, i.e. $\gamma = \gamma_{+}\oplus\gamma_{-}$.

**Proposition 5.** The vector bundle $E\to M$, associated with the principal $G$-bundle $Q\to M$ by a representation $\gamma$ of $G$ in $S$, is trivial if and only if there exist a group $G'$, a homomorphism $\iota: G\to G'$, and an extension $\gamma': G'\to{\rm GL}(S)$ of $\gamma$, such that the associated principal $G'$-bundle $Q\times_{\iota} G'\to M$ is trivial.

**Proposition 6.** The classical Dirac operator (20) maps a spinor field of type $\gamma$ into a spinor field of type $\gamma\circ\alpha$. The modified Dirac operator (21) preserves the type of spinor fields.

**Proposition 7.** Let $f$ be an isometric immersion of a hypersurface $M$ in the Euclidean space ${\mathbb R}^{m+1}$. The pin structure on $M$ induced by the immersion,

$$
{\rm Pin}_{0,m}\to Q \xrightarrow{\ \chi\ } P \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ } M,
$$

is such that the bundle of Dirac ($m$ even) or Pauli ($m$ odd) spinors on $M$ is trivial.

**Proposition 8.** Let $m = 2\nu$ (resp., $m = 2\nu+1$), where $\nu$ is a positive integer. The spectrum of the Dirac (resp., Pauli) operator on ${\mathbb S}_m$ is the set

$$
\{\pm(l+\tfrac{1}{2}m) : l = 0,1,\dots\}.
$$

Each of the eigenvalues $l+\tfrac{1}{2}m$ and $-l-\tfrac{1}{2}m$ occurs with multiplicity

$$
2^{\nu}\binom{m+l-1}{l}.
$$

Let $S$ be the $2^{\nu}$-dimensional space of Dirac (resp., Pauli) spinors, and let $\Psi: {\mathbb S}_m\to S$ be an eigenfunction of the Schrödinger transform of the modified Dirac operator.

- If $\mathcal{D}\Psi = (l+\tfrac{1}{2}m)\Psi$, then $\Psi$ is the restriction of $\partial(x\Phi)$ to ${\mathbb S}_m$, where $\Phi$ is an $S$-valued harmonic polynomal on ${\mathbb R}^{m+1}$, homogeneous of degree $l$.
- If $\mathcal{D}\Psi = -(l+\tfrac{1}{2}m)\Psi$, then $\Psi$ is the restriction of $x(\partial\Phi)$, where $\Phi$ is a similar polynomial of degree $l+1$.

**Proposition 9.** Let $m = 2\nu$, where $\nu$ is a positive integer (resp., $m = 2\nu+1$, where $\nu$ is a positive odd integer). The spectrum of the Dirac (resp., Pauli) operator on ${\mathbb P}_m$ is the set

$$
\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\pm(2l+\tfrac{1}{2}m+\tfrac{1}{2}) : l = 0,1,\dots\}.
$$

The eigenvalue $\lambda$ occurs in the spectrum with multiplicity

$$
2^{\nu}\binom{|\lambda|+\tfrac{1}{2}m-1}{|\lambda|-\tfrac{1}{2}m}.
$$
