# Restatement

## Abstract

We construct a six-functor formalism for syntomic cohomology of $p$-adic formal schemes. This generalizes Poincaré duality to general smooth morphisms.

## Introduction: Statements

**Theorem A** (see 4.1.3). There is a six-functor formalism
$$X \mapsto \text{F-Gauge}_{\Delta}^{\square}(X)$$
on the category of (derived) $p$-adic formal schemes satisfying the following:

- **(A)** Morphisms locally of finite type are $!$-able.
- **(B)** Étale morphisms are cohomologically étale.
- **(C)** Any finite composition of closed immersions and proper morphisms¹ is weakly cohomologically proper.
- **(D)** The functor $(\_)^{\text{syn},\square}$ preserves étale covers and sends Zariski covers to open covers of analytic stacks. In particular, the functors $\text{F-Gauge}_{\Delta}^{\square}(\_)^{\ast}$ and $\text{F-Gauge}_{\Delta}^{\square}(\_)^{!}$ are étale sheaves.
- **(E)** It admits Tate twists. That is, for any $p$-adic formal scheme $X$, the object
  $$\mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}}(-1) := \mathrm{cof}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}} \to f_\ast \mathcal{O}_{(\mathbf{P}^1)^{\text{syn}}}\big)$$
  is $\otimes$-invertible, and its inverse identifies with $\mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}}(1) \simeq \mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}}\{1\}[-2]$, a shift of the Breuil–Kisin twist.
- **(F)** Any smooth morphism is cohomologically smooth. Furthermore, for such a smooth morphism $f\colon X \to S$, there is an identification of the dualizing sheaf
  $$f^{!}\mathcal{O}_{S^{\text{syn}}} := \omega_f \simeq \mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}}(d) \simeq \mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}}\{d\}[-2d],$$
  where $d$ is the relative dimension of $f$.
- **(G)** The dualizable objects identify
  $$\big(\text{F-Gauge}_{\Delta}^{\square}(\_)\big)^{\text{dual}} \simeq \text{Perf}((\_)^{\text{syn}})$$
  with perfect F-Gauges as defined in [6] [6.1].² In particular, there is a functorial identification
  $$R\Gamma^{\text{BMS}}_{\text{syn}}(\_, \mathbf{Z}_p(n)) \simeq \Hom_{(\_)^{\text{syn}}}\big(\mathcal{O}_{(\_)^{\text{syn}}}, \mathcal{O}_{(\_)^{\text{syn}}}\{n\}\big)$$
  of the mapping spectrum with the syntomic cohomology of $p$-adic formal schemes as defined in [5].

¹ In this note, proper morphisms are locally of finite presentation.
² There is also a version containing the full category of prismatic F-Gauges (4.3.6).

**Corollary.** The identification in (F) induces a trace map
$$\mathrm{tr}\colon f_! \mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}}\{d\}[-2d] \to \mathcal{O}_{S^{\text{syn}}}$$
such that the composition
$$f_\ast \mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}} \otimes_S f_! \mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}}\{d\}[-2d] \xrightarrow{\ \cup\ } f_! \mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}}\{d\}[-2d] \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{tr}\ } \mathcal{O}_{S^{\text{syn}}}$$
is adjoint to an isomorphism
$$f_\ast \mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}} \simeq \big(f_! \mathcal{O}_{X^{\text{syn}}}\{d\}[-2d]\big)^{\vee}$$
in $\text{F-Gauge}_{\Delta}^{\square}(S)$.

**Theorem B** (see 4.3.10). There is a six-functor formalism
$$X \mapsto \mathcal{D}(X_\eta^{\text{ét},\square})$$
on $p$-adic formal schemes over $\mathbf{Z}_p^{\text{cyc}}$, which satisfies analogous assertions to (A)–(F). Furthermore, the dualizable objects naturally identify
$$\mathcal{D}\big((\_)_\eta^{\text{ét},\square}\big)^{\text{dual}} \simeq \mathcal{D}^{\text{b}}_{\text{lisse}}\big((\_)_\eta, \mathbf{Z}_p\big)$$
with the category of lisse étale sheaves on the generic fiber.

**Theorem C** (see 1.6.5). Let $\mathcal{D}$ be a six-functor formalism on the category of schemes which satisfies the following, and admits the following structures:

- All étale morphisms of schemes are cohomologically étale.
- All proper morphisms are (weakly) cohomologically proper.
- $\mathcal{D}^{\ast}$ is a Nisnevich sheaf.
- It admits an additive orientation.
- It satisfies elementary blow-up excision.

Then any smooth morphism $f\colon X \to S$ of relative dimension $d$ is cohomologically smooth, and there is a canonical identification
$$f^{!}(\mathbf{1}_S) \simeq \mathbf{1}_X(d)$$
of the dualizing sheaf with the Tate twist.³

³ Here the Tate twist is given by the homology of pointed $\mathbf{P}^1$. In the text, one switches to $(d)[-2d]$.

## Section 1: Six-functor formalisms

**1.1.1.** Consider a pair $(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}_E)$, where $\mathcal{C}$ is a category and $\mathcal{C}_E \subset \mathcal{C}$ is a wide subcategory satisfying:

- **(a)** Morphisms in $\mathcal{C}_E$ are closed under pullbacks along morphisms in $\mathcal{C}$.
- **(b)** $\mathcal{C}_E$ admits pullbacks, and the inclusion $\mathcal{C}_E \subset \mathcal{C}$ preserves them.

Such data is called a *geometric setup*. Given such data, one can construct the category $\text{Span}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}_E)$ (see [28] [2.2] for an honest construction):

- Objects are the objects of $\mathcal{C}$.
- A morphism from $X$ to $Y$ is a span $X \leftarrow Z \to Y$ where the right leg lies in $\mathcal{C}_E$.
- Composition of spans is given by fiber products of the inner cospan.

Assuming $\mathcal{C}$ admits finite products, $\text{Span}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}_E)$ carries a symmetric monoidal structure induced by the cartesian product in $\mathcal{C}$.

**Definition 1.1.2.** Let $(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}_E)$ be a geometric setup such that $\mathcal{C}$ admits finite products. A *six-functor formalism* on $(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}_E)$ is a lax symmetric monoidal functor
$$\mathcal{D}\colon \text{Span}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}_E) \to \text{Pr}^L,$$
where $\text{Pr}^L$ denotes the category of presentable categories with colimit-preserving functors as morphisms, equipped with the Lurie tensor product.

**Remark 1.1.3.** Using [28] [3.4], we can and will assume that $\mathcal{C}$ is a topos, such that $\mathcal{D}^\ast$ defines a sheaf on this topos and $\mathcal{D}^!$ descends along effective epimorphisms in $\mathcal{C}_E$.

**1.1.4 (The category of kernels).** Given a six-functor formalism and an object $X \in \mathcal{C}$, we obtain a restricted six-functor formalism
$$\mathcal{D}\colon \text{Span}(\mathcal{C}(X)) \to \text{Pr}^L,$$
where $\text{Span}(\mathcal{C}(X)) := \text{Span}((\mathcal{C}_E)_{/X}, (\mathcal{C}_E)_{/X})$. Furthermore, $\mathcal{D}$ gives a commutative algebra object in $\Fun(\text{Span}(\mathcal{C}(X)), \text{Pr}^L)$, where the latter category carries the Day convolution symmetric monoidal structure. Write
$$\mathcal{K}_X := \text{Mod}_{\mathcal{D}}\big(\Fun(\text{Span}(\mathcal{C}(X)), \text{Pr}^L)\big),$$
called the *category of kernels* of $X$. This can be interpreted as a 2-category: any object $X \in \mathcal{C}_E$ gives rise to an object $[X] \in \mathcal{K}_\ast$; 1-morphisms $[X] \to [Y]$ are described by objects of $\mathcal{D}(X \times Y)$; and 2-morphisms between those are described by morphisms in $\mathcal{D}(X \times Y)$.

**Remark 1.1.5.** The category of kernels will only be used implicitly in this text. For a more detailed description and construction, see [28] [4] and [41] [V].

**Definition 1.1.6.** Let $f\colon X \to S$ be a morphism in $\mathcal{C}_E$ and $\mathcal{E} \in \mathcal{D}(X)$.

- **(a)** $\mathcal{E}$ is called *f-suave* if $\mathcal{E}\colon [X] \to [S]$ is left adjoint in $\mathcal{K}_S$.
- **(b)** $\mathcal{E}$ is called *f-prim* if $\mathcal{E}\colon [S] \to [X]$ is left adjoint in $\mathcal{K}_S$.
- **(c)** $f$ is called *cohomologically smooth* if $\mathbf{1}_X$ is $f$-suave and $\mathrm{D}_f(\mathbf{1}_X) = f^!(\mathbf{1}_S)$ is $\otimes$-invertible.
- **(d)** $f$ is called *cohomologically co-smooth* if $\mathbf{1}_X$ is $f$-prim and $p_{2,\ast}\Delta_!(\mathbf{1}_X) = \mathrm{P}_f(\mathbf{1}_X)$ is $\otimes$-invertible.
- **(e)** $f$ is called *cohomologically étale* if it is $n$-truncated for some finite $n$, $\mathbf{1}_X$ is $f$-suave, and the diagonal $\Delta_f$ is cohomologically étale or an isomorphism.
- **(f)** $f$ is *weakly cohomologically proper* if $\mathbf{1}_X$ is $\Delta_f$-prim, there exists some (non-canonical) isomorphism $\mathrm{P}_{\Delta_f}(\mathbf{1}_X) \simeq \mathbf{1}_X$, and $\mathbf{1}_X$ is $f$-prim.

**Remark 1.1.7.** All of the above notions admit dual versions, which will not appear in this text. All "étale" morphisms considered here will be truncated. In contrast, almost no "proper" morphisms considered here will be truncated.

**Remark 1.1.8.** All the above notions are stable under base change and composition.

**Definition 1.1.9.** Consider a morphism $f\colon X \to S$ in $\mathcal{C}_E$.

- **(a)** $f$ is a *smooth cover* if it is cohomologically smooth and $f^\ast$ is conservative.
- **(b)** $f$ is a *descendable cover* if it is cohomologically co-smooth and $f_\ast \mathbf{1}_X \in \mathcal{D}(S)$ is descendable.
- **(c)** $f$ is a *proper descendable cover* if it is a descendable cover and weakly cohomologically proper.

**Remark 1.1.10.** For all the above covers, $\mathcal{D}^\ast$ as well as $\mathcal{D}^!$ descend along them [28] [4.7.1, 4.7.4].

**Proposition 1.1.11.**
- **(a)** Being cohomologically smooth is $\mathcal{D}^\ast$-local on the target and smooth-local on the source.
- **(b)** Being cohomologically étale is $\mathcal{D}^\ast$-local on the target and étale-local on the source.
- **(c)** Being weakly cohomologically co-smooth is $\mathcal{D}^\ast$-local on the target and proper-descendable-local on the source.
- **(d)** Being a smooth cover is stable under composition and base change, and is $\mathcal{D}^\ast$-local on the target.
- **(e)** Being a descendable cover is stable under composition and base change, and is descendable-local on the target.

**Remark 1.1.12.** If, for a co-smooth map $f\colon X \to S$, the diagonal is weakly cohomologically proper, then $f$ is also weakly cohomologically proper.

**Remark 1.1.13.** If the six-functor formalism is constructed from a suitable decomposition of $E$ [28] [3.3.2], then there are two classes $(I, P)$ of étale and proper maps, which are cohomologically étale resp. cohomologically proper by construction. Then, if a map $f\colon X \to S$ lies in $P$ locally on $S$ for a $\mathcal{D}^\ast$-cover, it is weakly cohomologically proper. This is explained in [16] [3.1.21].

## Section 1.2: Geometrized setups and Chern classes

**Definition 1.2.1.** A geometric setup $(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}_E)$ is *geometrized* if it comes with a functor $\text{Sm}^{\text{sep}}_B \to \mathcal{C}_E$ from the category of separated smooth schemes over some (possibly derived) scheme $B$, which preserves those finite limits that are also finite limits in the category of all (derived) schemes. A six-functor formalism $\mathcal{D}$ on a geometrized geometric setup is called *geometric* if:

- For any $S \in \mathcal{C}$, the category $\mathcal{D}(S)$ is stable.
- Any étale morphism in $\text{Sm}_B$ is cohomologically étale with respect to $\mathcal{D}$.
- Any proper morphism in $\text{Sm}_B$ is weakly cohomologically proper with respect to $\mathcal{D}$.
- Any Nisnevich covering is sent to a $\mathcal{D}^\ast$-cover.

**Remark 1.2.2.** The functor $\text{Sm}_B \to \mathcal{C}$ is normally not written. For example, $\mathbf{A}^n$ denotes the $n$-dimensional affine space viewed as an object in $\mathcal{C}$ via this functor. Affine space over an arbitrary $X \in \mathcal{C}$ can also be defined via base change.

**Remark 1.2.3.** The Nisnevich topology is used in motivic homotopy theory and lies between the Zariski and the étale topology. The étale topology would suffice for all applications in this text.

**Definition 1.2.4.** Let $f\colon \mathbf{P}^1 \to B$ be the projection. $\mathcal{D}$ *admits Tate twists* if the object
$$\mathbf{1}_B(-1) := \mathrm{cof}\big(\mathbf{1}_B \to f_\ast \mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{P}^1_B}\big)$$
is $\otimes$-invertible. In that case, $\mathbf{1}_B(1)$ denotes its $\otimes$-inverse, called the *Tate twist*.

**Remark 1.2.5.** Using proper base change, the Tate twist defines a cartesian section for $\mathcal{D}^\ast$. Thus $\mathbf{1}_S(1)$ can be defined for arbitrary $S \in \mathcal{C}$, either by the above construction over $S$ or via base change.

**Definition 1.2.6.** $\mathcal{D}$ *admits a theory of first Chern classes* if it admits Tate twists and comes with a natural transformation of sheaves of spectra on $\mathcal{C}$
$$c_1\colon R\Gamma_{\text{open}}(\_, \mathbf{G}_m)[-1] \to \Hom(\mathbf{1}_{(\_)}, \mathbf{1}_{(\_)}(1)).$$

**Remark 1.2.7.** There is a natural transformation $R\Gamma_{\text{Zar}}(\_, \mathbf{G}_m)[-1] \to R\Gamma_{\text{open}}(\_, \mathbf{G}_m)[1]$ of sheaves of spectra on $\text{Sm}^{\text{sep}}_B$. Furthermore, $\pi_0$ of the left-hand side computes the Picard group. In particular, any line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ on a smooth separated scheme $S$ over $B$ gives rise to a map $c_1(\mathcal{L})\colon \mathbf{1}_S \to \mathbf{1}_S(1)$. In practice, $\pi_0$ of the right-hand side essentially computes the group of line bundles on an object $S \in \mathcal{C}$.

**Remark 1.2.8.** The assignment of first Chern classes is compatible with base change, and
$$c_1(\mathcal{L}_1 \otimes \mathcal{L}_2) \simeq c_1(\mathcal{L}_1) + c_1(\mathcal{L}_2).$$

**Construction 1.2.9 (The projective bundle formula).** Consider a morphism $f\colon X \to S$ of separated smooth schemes over $B$ and a line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ on $X$. By adjunction, $c_1(\mathcal{L})$ induces a morphism $c_1(\mathcal{L})\colon \mathbf{1}_S \to f_\ast \mathbf{1}_X(1)$, also denoted the same way. Taking $\otimes$-powers iteratively gives
$$c_1(\mathcal{L})^d\colon \mathbf{1}_S \to f_\ast \mathbf{1}_X(d).$$
For $f\colon \mathbf{P}_S(\mathcal{F}) \to S$ the projection from the projective space of a vector bundle $\mathcal{F}$ of rank $d+1$ on $S$, these constructions give a morphism
$$\sum_{i=0}^{d} c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))^i (d-i)\colon \bigoplus_{i=0}^{d} \mathbf{1}_S(d-i) \to f_\ast \mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{P}_S(\mathcal{F})}(d),$$
where $\mathcal{O}(1)$ denotes the universal line bundle, and $c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))^0$ corresponds by adjunction to the identity.

**Definition 1.2.10.** A theory of first Chern classes for $\mathcal{D}$ is an *additive orientation* if, for each $d \geq 1$, the map
$$\sum_{i=0}^{d} c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))^i (d-i)\colon \bigoplus_{i=0}^{d} \mathbf{1}_B(d-i) \to f_\ast \mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{P}^d_B}(d)$$
is an isomorphism.

**Remark 1.2.11.** The projective bundle formula isomorphism base changes to its counterpart over an arbitrary $S \in \mathcal{C}$. In particular, the projective bundle formula holds over any object of $\mathcal{C}$ if there is an additive orientation. Since isomorphisms can be checked locally, one also obtains a projective bundle formula for an arbitrary projective bundle over a smooth separated scheme over $S$.

**Remark 1.2.12.** For any $S \in \mathcal{C}$ and any $\mathcal{E} \in \mathcal{D}(S)$, tensoring the projective bundle formula isomorphism with $\mathcal{E}$ gives an isomorphism
$$\sum_{i=0}^{d} c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))^i (d-i)\colon \bigoplus_{i=0}^{d} \mathcal{E}(d-i) \to f_\ast f^\ast \mathcal{E}(d).$$

## Section 1.3: Regular immersions and cohomological smoothness

**Definition 1.3.1.** A morphism $Z \to X$ in $\mathcal{C}$ is a *regular immersion* if, locally on the target in the open topology, it sits in a cartesian square
$$\begin{array}{ccc} Z & \to & X \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ \{0\} & \to & \mathbf{A}^1_B. \end{array}$$

**Remark 1.3.2.** The wording "regular immersion" is chosen because the above cartesian square should be read as a derived cartesian square.

**Definition 1.3.3.** Given two subcategories $(\text{Ét}, \text{Sm})$ of $\mathcal{C}$, morphisms in $\text{Ét}$ are called *geometrically étale* and morphisms in $\text{Sm}$ are called *geometrically smooth* if they satisfy:

- **(a)** Both classes are stable under base change along arbitrary morphisms between objects in $\text{Sm}$ and $\text{Ét} \subset \text{Sm}$.
- **(b)** Any morphism in $\text{Ét}$ is cohomologically étale.
- **(c)** $\text{Sm}^{\text{sep}}_B \subset \text{Sm}$.
- **(d)** Any morphism in $\text{Sm}$, locally on source and target in the open topology, factors as $X \xrightarrow{e} \mathbf{A}^n_S \xrightarrow{pr} S$ where $e \in \text{Ét}$ and $pr$ is the canonical projection.
- **(e)** Given a morphism $f\colon X \to S$ in $\text{Sm}$, any section $s\colon S \to X$ factors as $S \xrightarrow{i} U \xrightarrow{j} X$, where $j$ is an étale monomorphism and $i$ is a regular immersion.
- **(f)** For any commutative triangle $Z \xrightarrow{i} X \xrightarrow{g} S$ with $f\colon X\to S$ ... (the triangle with $Z \to X$ a regular immersion and $g$, $f$ geometrically smooth, where $f \circ i$ is the composite), locally on $X$ in the open topology, there exist cartesian squares
$$\begin{array}{ccc} Z & \to & Z \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ U & \xrightarrow{t} & \mathbf{A}^n_Z \end{array}\qquad \begin{array}{ccc} Z & \to & Z \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ X & \xleftarrow{\ 0\ } & \mathbf{A}^n_Z \end{array}$$
with the horizontal morphisms étale.
- **(g)** For any commutative triangle $Z \xrightarrow{i} X \xrightarrow{g} S$ with $g$ and $f$ geometrically smooth and $i$ a regular immersion, there exists a geometrically smooth morphism $\mathbf{Bl}_Z(X) \to X$ which, restricted to the open locus of $X$ where there is a cartesian square
$$\begin{array}{ccc} Z & \to & X \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ \{0\} & \to & \mathbf{A}^n_B \end{array}$$
is given by $X \times_{\mathbf{A}^n_B} \mathbf{Bl}_{\{0\}}(\mathbf{A}^n)$. Here $\mathbf{Bl}_{\{0\}}(\mathbf{A}^n_B)$ denotes the blow-up from derived algebraic geometry [31] [43] [A].

**Remark 1.3.4.** Assertion (f) in the definition of geometrically smooth morphisms follows, in practice, from the Jacobian criterion, together with the fact that the diagonal of an étale morphism is an open embedding and that the image of the complement of an open along a Zariski closed immersion has an open complement [39] [Section 3, Lemma 2.28].

**Remark 1.3.5.** Given a Zariski closed immersion $Z \to X$, locally on $X$ the subscheme $Z$ admits a complement, obtained by pulling back the complement of the zero section. As these are monomorphisms, the local complements glue to a global complement $U \subset X$. Furthermore, for any étale morphism $V \to X$ such that the square with $Z$ and $V$ over $X$ is cartesian, the pair $V \amalg U \to X$ is a cohomologically étale covering, since this holds locally. Note also that, as $U \subset X$ is a monomorphism, satisfying descent for this cover is equivalent to sending the square
$$\begin{array}{ccc} U \times_X V & \to & V \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ U & \to & X \end{array}$$
to a pullback.

**Remark 1.3.6.** Condition (h) is a property.

**Definition 1.3.7.** A *trace-cycle theory* on a morphism $f\colon X \to S$ in $\mathcal{C}_E$ consists of a triple $(\omega_f, \mathrm{tr}_f, \mathrm{cl}_\Delta)$ of:

- **(a)** A $\otimes$-invertible object $\omega_f \in \mathcal{D}(X)$.
- **(b)** A trace morphism $\mathrm{tr}_f\colon f_!\omega_f \to \mathbf{1}_S$ in $\mathcal{D}(S)$.
- **(c)** A cycle morphism $\mathrm{cl}_\Delta\colon \Delta_!\mathbf{1}_X \to (p_2)^\ast \omega_f$ in $\mathcal{D}(X \times_S X)$.

Such that the following hold:

- **(1)** The composition
$$\mathbf{1}_X \to (p_1)_!\Delta_!\mathbf{1}_X \xrightarrow{(p_1)_!(\mathrm{cl}_\Delta)} (p_1)_!(p_2)^\ast\omega_f \xrightarrow{\mathrm{tr}_{p_1}} \mathbf{1}_X$$
is the identity, where $\mathrm{tr}_{p_1} \simeq f^\ast(\mathrm{tr}_f)$.
- **(2)** The composition
$$\omega_f \to (p_2)_!\big(p_1^\ast\omega_f \otimes \Delta_!\mathbf{1}_X\big) \xrightarrow{(p_2)_!(\mathrm{id}\otimes \mathrm{cl}_\Delta)} (p_2)_!\big(p_1^\ast\omega_f \otimes p_2^\ast\omega_f\big) \simeq (p_2)_!p_2^\ast\omega_f \otimes \omega_f \xrightarrow{\mathrm{tr}_f \otimes \mathrm{id}} \omega_f$$
is the identity.

**Remark 1.3.8.** The definition unwinds what it means to have a unit and counit of an adjunction $\mathbf{1}_X \dashv \omega_f$ in $\mathcal{K}_S$.

**Theorem 1.3.9 (Zavyalov).** Consider a geometrized geometric setup $(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}_E)$ and a geometric six-functor formalism $\mathcal{D}$ on it. Then a morphism $f\colon X \to S$ in $\mathcal{C}_E$ is cohomologically smooth if and only if it admits a trace-cycle theory, and in that case the dualizing sheaf is computed as
$$f^!\mathbf{1}_S \simeq \omega_f.$$
Furthermore, for any class of geometrically smooth morphisms, the following are equivalent:

- **(a)** Any geometrically smooth morphism is cohomologically smooth.
- **(b)** The morphism $\mathbf{A}^1 \to B$ is cohomologically smooth.
- **(c)** The morphism $\mathbf{P}^1 \to B$ is cohomologically smooth.

## Section 1.4: Cohomology, homology and geometric objects

**1.4.1.** For a morphism $f\colon X \to S$ in $\mathcal{C}$ and an object $\mathcal{E}_S \in \mathcal{D}(S)$, write
$$\mathbf{C}^\ast(X, \mathcal{E}_S) := f_\ast f^\ast \mathcal{E}_S \in \mathcal{D}(S)$$
for its cohomology. If $f$ lies in $\mathcal{C}_E$, write
$$\mathbf{C}_\ast(X, \mathcal{E}_S) := f_! f^! \mathcal{E}_S \in \mathcal{D}(S)$$
for its homology. Coefficients are omitted when $\mathcal{E}_S \simeq \mathbf{1}_S$.

**1.4.2.** Given $S \in \mathcal{C}$, write $(\text{Sm}_S^{\text{geom}})_{\text{open}}$ for the topos generated by geometrically smooth objects over $S$, equipped with the topology generated by families of cohomologically étale monomorphisms which jointly induce a $\mathcal{D}^\ast$-covering. Cohomology and homology induce functors on this topos.

**1.4.3 (Vector bundles).** Given $S \in \mathcal{C}$, write $B\mathbf{GL}_{n,S} \in (\text{Sm}_S^{\text{geom}})_{\text{open}}$ for the stack of rank $n$ vector bundles on $S$, and
$$\mathbf{Vect}_S := \coprod_n B\mathbf{GL}_{n,S} \in (\text{Sm}_S^{\text{geom}})_{\text{open}}$$
for the stack of all vector bundles on $S$. A map $\langle \mathcal{F}\rangle\colon X \to B\mathbf{GL}_{n,S}$ corresponds to a $\mathbf{GL}_n$-torsor $\mathbf{V}_X(\mathcal{F}) \to X$ which trivializes locally in the open topology.

**1.4.4 (Projective bundles).** Given a vector bundle $\mathbf{V}_X(\mathcal{F}) \to X$ of rank $\geq 1$, there is a zero section $0\colon X \to \mathbf{V}_X(\mathcal{F})$. Using the diagonal $\mathbf{G}_m \to \mathbf{GL}_n$, there is a $\mathbf{G}_{m,X}$-action on $\mathbf{V}_X(\mathcal{F})$, which restricts to $\mathbf{V}_X(\mathcal{F}) - \{0\}$. The projective bundle associated to $\mathbf{V}_X(\mathcal{F})$ is defined as
$$\mathbf{P}_X(\mathcal{F}) := \big(\mathbf{V}_X(\mathcal{F}) - \{0\}\big)/\mathbf{G}_m \in (\text{Sm}_S^{\text{geom}})_{\text{open}}.$$
There is a canonical map $\mathbf{P}_X(\mathcal{F}) \to B\mathbf{G}_m$, which defines a line bundle $\mathcal{O}(1)$ on the projective bundle. This line bundle has a first Chern class.

**Remark 1.4.5.** Any projective bundle is weakly cohomologically proper, as this is true locally on the base by assumption.

**1.4.6 (Thom spaces).** Given a vector bundle $\mathbf{V}_X(\mathcal{F})$ of rank $\geq 1$, its Thom space is defined as the cofibre
$$\mathbf{Th}_X(\mathcal{F}) := \mathrm{cof}\big(\mathbf{P}_X(\mathcal{F}) \to \mathbf{P}_X(\mathcal{F} \oplus \mathcal{O}_X)\big) \in (\text{Sm}_S^{\text{geom}})_{\text{open}}.$$
