# Restatement

## Introduction

The correspondence between "spaces" and "commutative algebras" is by now familiar in mathematics and in theoretical physics. This correspondence allows an algebraic translation of various geometrical concepts on spaces in terms of the appropriate algebras of functions on these spaces. Replacing these commutative algebras by noncommutative algebras, i.e. forgetting commutativity, leads then to noncommutative generalizations of geometries where notions of "spaces of points" are not involved.

Gel'fand's transformation associates to each compact topological space $X$ the algebra $C(X)$ of complex continuous functions on $X$. Equipped with the sup norm, $C(X)$ is a commutative unital $C^{\ast}$-algebra. The correspondence $X \mapsto C(X)$ defines an equivalence between the category of compact topological spaces and the category of commutative unital $C^{\ast}$-algebras. The compact space $X$ is identified with the spectrum of $C(X)$, i.e. the set of homomorphisms of unital $\ast$-algebras of $C(X)$ into $\mathbb{C}$ equipped with the weak topology.

Let $X$ be a compact space and let ${\cal E}(X)$ denote the category of finite rank complex vector bundles over $X$. To any vector bundle $E$ of ${\cal E}(X)$ one associates the $C(X)$-module $\Gamma(E)$ of all continuous sections of $E$. The module $\Gamma(E)$ is a finite projective $C(X)$-module, and the Serre-Swan theorem asserts that the correspondence $E \mapsto \Gamma(E)$ defines an equivalence between the category ${\cal E}(X)$ and the category ${\cal P}(C(X))$ of finite projective $C(X)$-modules.

**Remark 1.** Let $X$ be an arbitrary topological space. The algebra $C^{\mathbf{b}}(X)$ of complex continuous bounded functions on $X$ is a $C^{\ast}$-algebra when equipped with the sup norm. By Gel'fand theory, $C^{\mathbf{b}}(X) = C(\hat{X})$ (as $C^{\ast}$-algebras), where $\hat{X}$ denotes the spectrum of $C^{\mathbf{b}}(X)$. The spectrum $\hat{X}$ is a compact space and the evaluation defines a continuous mapping $e: X \mapsto \hat{X}$ with dense image, $\overline{e(X)} = \hat{X}$. The compact space $\hat{X}$ is called the Stone-Čech compactification of $X$, and the pair $(e, \hat{X})$ is characterized (uniquely up to an isomorphism) by the following universal property: for any continuous mapping $f: X \mapsto Y$ of $X$ into a compact space $Y$, there is a unique continuous mapping $\hat{f}: \hat{X} \mapsto Y$ such that $f = \hat{f} \circ e$.

Notice that $e: X \mapsto \hat{X}$ is generally not injective, and that it is an isomorphism, i.e. $X = \hat{X}$, if and only if $X$ is compact. The above universal property means that $\hat{X}$ is the biggest compactification of $X$. For instance, if $X$ is locally compact then $e$ is injective, i.e. $X \subset \hat{X}$ canonically, but $\hat{X}$ is generally much bigger than the one point compactification $X \cup \{\infty\}$ of $X$ (e.g. for $X = \mathbb{R}$, the canonical projection $\hat{\mathbb{R}} \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$ has a huge inverse image of $\infty$).

In the case of measure spaces, the algebras of continuous functions are replaced by algebras of measurable functions. The class of algebras is then the class of commutative $W^{\ast}$-algebras (von Neumann algebras). The noncommutative generalizations are therefore provided by general (noncommutative) $W^{\ast}$-algebras. It has been shown by A. Connes that the corresponding noncommutative measure theory has a very rich structure with no classical (i.e. commutative) counterpart (e.g. the occurrence of a canonical dynamical system) [12].

In differential geometry, the appropriate class of commutative algebras is the class of algebras of smooth functions. If $X$ is a smooth manifold and ${\cal C}$ is the algebra of complex smooth functions on $X$, ${\cal C} = C^{\infty}(X)$, one can reconstruct $X$ with its smooth structure and the objects attached to $X$ (differential forms, etc.) starting from ${\cal C}$ considered as an abstract commutative unital $\ast$-algebra. As a set, $X$ can be identified with the set of characters of ${\cal C}$, i.e. the set of homomorphisms of unital $\ast$-algebras of ${\cal C}$ into $\mathbb{C}$; its differential structure is connected with the abundance of derivations of ${\cal C}$, which identify with the smooth vector fields on $X$.

In [50], J.L. Koszul gave an algebraic generalization of differential geometry in terms of a commutative (associative) algebra ${\cal C}$, of ${\cal C}$-modules and connections (called derivation laws there) on these modules. The space $\mathrm{Der}({\cal C})$ of all derivations of ${\cal C}$ is a Lie algebra and a ${\cal C}$-module, both structures being connected by
$$[X, fY] = f[X,Y] + X(f)Y \qquad \text{for } X, Y \in \mathrm{Der}({\cal C}) \text{ and } f \in {\cal C}.$$
Using the latter property one can extract, by ${\cal C}$-multilinearity, a graded differential algebra generalizing the algebra of differential forms, from the graded differential algebra $C_{\wedge}(\mathrm{Der}({\cal C}), {\cal C})$ of ${\cal C}$-valued Chevalley-Eilenberg cochains of the Lie algebra $\mathrm{Der}({\cal C})$ (with its canonical action on ${\cal C}$). This construction admits a generalization to the noncommutative case; it is the derivation-based differential calculus ([25], [26], [27], [34], [35]), which will be described below. The text states that this is the right differential calculus for quantum mechanics, and that the corresponding noncommutative symplectic geometry is what is needed there (see also [26] and [27]).

For commutative algebras there is another well-known generalization of the calculus of differential forms, the Kähler differential calculus [6], [43], [52], [58]. This differential calculus is "universal" and consequently functorial for the category of (associative unital) commutative algebras. The text announces a generalization of the Kähler differential calculus to noncommutative algebras. This differential calculus is functorial for algebra-homomorphisms mapping centers into centers. It is said to be the universal differential calculus for the category ${\bf Alg}_Z$ whose objects are unital associative $\mathbb{C}$-algebras and whose morphisms are unital algebra homomorphisms mapping centers into centers. It reduces to the Kähler differential calculus for a commutative algebra. The universal differential calculus is universal for the category ${\bf Alg}$ of unital associative $\mathbb{C}$-algebras and all unital algebra homomorphisms.

Concerning generalizations of the notion of module over a commutative algebra ${\cal C}$ to a noncommutative algebra ${\cal A}$: there are right ${\cal A}$-modules, the dual notion of left ${\cal A}$-modules, bimodules over ${\cal A}$, and modules over the center $Z({\cal A})$. For each category of algebras there is a notion of bimodule over its objects. For the category ${\bf Algcom}$ of unital commutative associative $\mathbb{C}$-algebras, the notion of bimodule reduces to the notion of module. For a commutative algebra ${\cal C}$, the notion of bimodule over ${\cal C}$ is the same whether ${\cal C}$ is regarded as an object of ${\bf Algcom}$ or of ${\bf Alg}_Z$, whereas the notion of bimodule over ${\cal C}$ in ${\bf Alg}$ is much wider.

An associative complex $\ast$-algebra is an associative complex algebra ${\cal A}$ equipped with an antilinear involution $x \mapsto x^{\ast}$ such that $(xy)^{\ast} = y^{\ast}x^{\ast}$ for all $x, y \in {\cal A}$. The real subspace ${\cal A}^h$ of hermitian elements of a complex associative $\ast$-algebra is generally not stable under the product, but only under the symmetrized Jordan product $x \circ y = \frac{1}{2}(xy + yx)$. Thus ${\cal A}^h$ is generally not an associative algebra but is a real Jordan algebra. For commutative associative complex $\ast$-algebras ${\cal C}$, the correspondence ${\cal C} \mapsto {\cal C}^h$ defines an equivalence between the category of commutative associative complex $\ast$-algebras (with $\ast$-homomorphisms as morphisms) and the category of commutative associative real algebras. This contrasts with the noncommutative case.

**Remark 2.** Instead of generalizing a commutative associative $\mathbb{R}$-algebra ${\cal C}_{\mathbb{R}}$ by the Jordan algebra ${\cal A}^h$ of hermitian elements of an associative complex $\ast$-algebra ${\cal A}$, one can more generally generalize ${\cal C}_{\mathbb{R}}$ by a real Jordan algebra ${\cal J}_{\mathbb{R}}$ (not a priori a special one), with corresponding Jordan bimodules [44]. The text states that the author refrains from this approach. The lectures are concerned with noncommutative versions of differential geometry in which the algebra of smooth complex functions on a smooth manifold is replaced by a noncommutative associative unital complex $\ast$-algebra ${\cal A}$. The text notes that not every such $\ast$-algebra is a good noncommutative generalization of an algebra of smooth functions, and that several inequivalent noncommutative generalizations of the characterization of smooth function algebras exist [30].

The plan of the notes is as follows. Section 2 recalls graded differential algebras, including Sullivan's result on connected finitely generated free graded commutative differential algebras and Cartan's notion of operation of a Lie algebra in a graded differential algebra. Section 3 explains the equivalence between finite dimensional Lie algebras and free connected graded commutative differential algebras finitely generated in degree 1, together with examples (Chevalley-Eilenberg complexes, the Weil algebra and the Weil homomorphism, and the derivation-based calculus). Section 4 gives the analogous equivalence between finite dimensional associative algebras and free connected graded differential algebras generated in degree 1, with examples such as Hochschild complexes. Section 5 introduces categories of algebras and the associated notions of bimodules. Section 6 recalls first order differential calculi, introduces the generalization of Kähler differentials, and discusses the universal first order calculus. Section 7 introduces higher order differential calculi, including the universal one and the generalization of Kähler exterior forms. Section 8 introduces the diagonal calculus, which is not functorial but has a universal property. Section 9 defines noncommutative Poisson and symplectic structures and their relation with quantum theory. Section 10 describes connections on modules and bimodules, including the generalization of J. Mourad's proposal and its relation with first-order operators. Section 11 discusses relations between noncommutative connections and classical Yang-Mills-Higgs models. Section 12 contains further remarks, in particular on the differential calculus on quantum groups.

Conventions: unless otherwise specified, an algebra means a unital associative complex algebra, and a $\ast$-algebra means a unital associative complex $\ast$-algebra. For algebras ${\cal A}$ and ${\cal B}$, an $({\cal A}, {\cal B})$-bimodule is a vector space ${\cal M}$ with linear maps ${\cal A} \otimes {\cal M} \to {\cal M}$, $a \otimes m \mapsto am$, and ${\cal M} \otimes {\cal B} \to {\cal M}$, $m \otimes b \mapsto mb$, such that $(aa')m = a(a'm)$, $m(bb') = (mb)b'$, $(am)b = a(mb)$, and $\mathbb{1}m = m$, $m\mathbb{1} = m$.

A complex ${\mathfrak{C}}$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-graded vector space over $\mathbb{C}$ with a homogeneous endomorphism $d$ of degree $\pm 1$ such that $d^2 = 0$. If $d$ has degree $-1$, ${\mathfrak{C}}$ is a chain complex, its elements are chains and $d$ is the boundary. If $d$ has degree $+1$, ${\mathfrak{C}}$ is a cochain complex, its elements are cochains and $d$ is the coboundary. The graded vector space $H({\mathfrak{C}}) = \mathrm{Ker}(d)/\mathrm{Im}(d)$ is the homology (chain case) or cohomology (cochain case) of ${\mathfrak{C}}$.

## Statements

**THEOREM 1.** Every connected finitely generated free graded commutative differential algebra is the tensor product of a unique minimal one and a unique contractible one.

**LEMMA 1.** The bilinear antisymmetric product $[\cdot,\cdot]$ on ${\mathfrak{g}}$ satisfies the Jacobi identity if and only if the corresponding antiderivation $d$ of $\bigwedge {\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}$ satisfies $d^2 = 0$.

**THEOREM 2.** The homomorphism $\varphi: {\cal I}_S({\mathfrak{g}}) \to H^{ev}(M)$ does not depend on the choice of the connection $\omega$ on $P$.

**LEMMA 2.** The bilinear product on ${\cal A}$ is associative if and only if the corresponding antiderivation of $T({\cal A}^{\ast})$ satisfies $d^2 = 0$.

**LEMMA 3.** Let ${\cal A}$ be a unital associative algebra over $\mathbb{C}$. Then the cohomology $H(C({\cal A}))$ of $C({\cal A})$ is trivial in the sense that
$$H^0(C({\cal A})) = \mathbb{C} \quad \text{and} \quad H^n(C({\cal A})) = 0 \quad \text{for } n \geq 1.$$

**THEOREM 3.** The basic cohomology $H_B({\cal A})$ of ${\cal A}$ identifies with the algebra ${\cal I}_S({\cal A}_{\mathrm{Lie}})$ of invariant polynomials on the Lie algebra ${\cal A}_{\mathrm{Lie}}$, where the degree $2n$ is given to the homogeneous polynomials of degree $n$, that is $H^{2n}_B({\cal A}) = {\cal I}^n_S({\cal A}_{\mathrm{Lie}})$ and $H^{2n+1}_B({\cal A}) = 0$.

**PROPOSITION 1.** For any first order differential calculus $(\Omega^1, d)$ over ${\cal A}$, there is a unique bimodule homomorphism $i_d$ of $\Omega^1_u({\cal A})$ into $\Omega^1$ such that $d = i_d \circ d_u$.

**LEMMA 4.** Let $(\Omega^1, d)$ be a first order differential calculus over ${\cal A}$. The following conditions are equivalent.
(i) $\Omega^1$ is generated by $d{\cal A}$ as left ${\cal A}$-module.
(ii) $\Omega^1$ is generated by $d{\cal A}$ as right ${\cal A}$-module.
(iii) $\Omega^1$ is generated by $d{\cal A}$ as $({\cal A}, {\cal A})$-bimodule.
(iv) The homomorphism $i_d$ is surjective, i.e. $\Omega^1 = i_d(\Omega^1_u({\cal A}))$.

**PROPOSITION 2.** Let ${\cal A}, {\cal B} \in \mathrm{Ob}({\mathbf{Alg}})$ and let $\varphi \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Alg}}({\cal A}, {\cal B})$. Then there is a unique linear mapping $\Omega^1_u(\varphi)$ of $\Omega^1_u({\cal A})$ into $\Omega^1_u({\cal B})$ satisfying $\Omega^1_u(\varphi)(x\omega y) = \varphi(x)\,\Omega^1_u(\varphi)(\omega)\,\varphi(y)$ for any $x, y \in {\cal A}$ and $\omega \in \Omega^1_u({\cal A})$, and such that $d_u \circ \varphi = \Omega^1_u(\varphi) \circ d_u$.

**PROPOSITION 3.** For any first order differential calculus $(\Omega^1, d)$ over ${\cal A}$ for ${\mathbf{Alg}}_Z$, there is a unique bimodule homomorphism $i_d$ of $\Omega^1_Z({\cal A})$ into $\Omega^1$ such that $d = i_d \circ d_Z$; i.e. there is a unique morphism of first order differential calculi over ${\cal A}$ for ${\mathbf{Alg}}_Z$ from $(\Omega^1_Z({\cal A}), d_Z)$ to $(\Omega^1, d)$.

**PROPOSITION 4.** Let ${\cal A}, {\cal B} \in \mathrm{Ob}({\mathbf{Alg}}_Z)$ and let $\varphi \in \mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbf{Alg}}_Z}({\cal A}, {\cal B})$, i.e. $\varphi$ is a homomorphism with $\varphi(Z({\cal A})) \subset Z({\cal B})$. Then there is a unique linear mapping $\Omega^1_Z(\varphi)$ of $\Omega^1_Z({\cal A})$ into $\Omega^1_Z({\cal B})$ satisfying $\Omega^1_Z(\varphi)(x\omega y) = \varphi(x)\,\Omega^1_Z(\varphi)(\omega)\,\varphi(y)$ for any $x, y \in {\cal A}$ and $\omega \in \Omega^1_Z({\cal A})$, and such that $d_Z \circ \varphi = \Omega^1_Z(\varphi) \circ d_Z$.

**COROLLARY 1.** If ${\cal A}$ is commutative, $\Omega^1_Z({\cal A})$ identifies canonically with the module of Kähler differentials $\Omega^1_{{\cal A}|\mathbb{C}}$, and $d_Z$ identifies with the corresponding universal derivation.

**PROPOSITION 5.** Any homomorphism $\varphi$ of unital algebras of ${\cal A}$ into the subalgebra $\Omega^0$ of elements of degree 0 of a graded differential algebra $\Omega$ has a unique extension $\tilde{\varphi}: \Omega_u({\cal A}) \to \Omega$ as a homomorphism of graded differential algebras.

**PROPOSITION 6.** Let ${\cal M}$ be an $({\cal A}, {\cal A})$-bimodule and let $(a_1, \dots, a_n) \mapsto c(a_1, \dots, a_n)$ be a normalized ${\cal M}$-valued Hochschild $n$-cocycle. Then there is a unique bimodule homomorphism $i_c: \Omega^n_u({\cal A}) \to {\cal M}$ such that
$$c(a_1, \dots, a_n) = i_c(d_u(a_1)\cdots d_u(a_n)), \quad \forall a_i \in {\cal A}.$$
Furthermore, $c$ is a Hochschild coboundary if and only if $i_c$ has an extension $\tilde{\imath}_c$ as a bimodule homomorphism of ${\cal A} \otimes \Omega^{n-1}_u({\cal A})$ into ${\cal M}$.

**LEMMA 5.** The cohomologies of $T_{\cal A}({\cal A} \otimes {\cal A})$ and of $\Omega_u({\cal A})$ are trivial in the sense that
$$H^0(T_{\cal A}({\cal A} \otimes {\cal A})) = H^0(\Omega_u({\cal A})) = \mathbb{C}$$
and
$$H^n(T_{\cal A}({\cal A} \otimes {\cal A})) = H^n(\Omega_u({\cal A})) = 0 \quad \text{for } n \geq 1.$$

**PROPOSITION 7.** Any homomorphism $\varphi$ of unital algebras of ${\cal A}$ into the subalgebra $\Omega^0$ of elements of degree 0 of a graded differential algebra $\Omega$, such that $\varphi(z)\, d(\varphi(x)) = d(\varphi(x))\, \varphi(z)$ for any $z \in Z({\cal A})$ and $x \in {\cal A}$ (where $d$ is the differential of $\Omega$), has a unique extension $\tilde{\varphi}_Z: \Omega_Z({\cal A}) \to \Omega$ as a homomorphism of graded differential algebras.

**COROLLARY 2.** For any differential calculus $\Omega$ over ${\cal A}$ for ${\mathbf{Alg}}_Z$, there is a unique homomorphism $j_\Omega: \Omega_Z({\cal A}) \to \Omega$ of differential algebras which induces the identity mapping of ${\cal A}$ onto itself.

**COROLLARY 3.** Any homomorphism $\varphi: {\cal A} \to {\cal B}$ of unital algebras mapping the center $Z({\cal A})$ into the center $Z({\cal B})$ has a unique extension $\Omega_Z(\varphi): \Omega_Z({\cal A}) \to \Omega_Z({\cal B})$ as a homomorphism of graded differential algebras.

**COROLLARY 4.** If ${\cal A}$ is commutative, $\Omega_Z({\cal A})$ identifies canonically with the graded differential algebra $\Omega_{{\cal A}|\mathbb{C}}$ of Cartan-de Rham-Kähler exterior differential forms.

**LEMMA 6.** The following properties (a) and (b) are equivalent for an $({\cal A}, {\cal A})$-bimodule ${\cal M}$.
(a) The canonical homomorphism $c: {\cal M} \to {\cal M}^{\ast_{\cal A}\ast_{\cal A}}$ is injective.
(b) ${\cal M}$ is isomorphic to a subbimodule of ${\cal A}^I$ for some set $I$.

**PROPOSITION 8.** For any homomorphism of $({\cal A}, {\cal A})$-bimodules $\varphi: {\cal M} \to {\cal N}$ of ${\cal M}$ into a diagonal bimodule ${\cal N}$ over ${\cal A}$, there is a unique homomorphism of $({\cal A}, {\cal A})$-bimodules $\varphi_c: c({\cal M}) \to {\cal N}$ such that $\varphi = \varphi_c \circ c$.

**PROPOSITION 9.** For any first order differential calculus $(\Omega^1, d)$ over ${\cal A}$ such that $\Omega^1$ is diagonal, there is a unique bimodule homomorphism $i_d$ of $\Omega^1_{\mathrm{Diag}}({\cal A})$ into $\Omega^1$ such that $d = i_d \circ d_{\mathrm{Diag}}$.

**PROPOSITION 10.** The homomorphism $i_d: \Omega^1_{\mathrm{Diag}}({\cal A}) \to C^1_{\wedge}(\mathrm{Der}({\cal A}), {\cal A})$ is injective, so that, identifying $\Omega^1_{\mathrm{Diag}}({\cal A})$ with its image under $i_d$, one has canonically
$$\Omega^1_{\mathrm{Diag}}({\cal A}) = \Omega^1_{\mathrm{Der}}({\cal A}), \qquad \big(\Omega^1_{\mathrm{Diag}}({\cal A})\big)^{\ast_{\cal A}} = \mathrm{Der}({\cal A}), \qquad \text{and} \qquad \big(\Omega^1_{\mathrm{Diag}}({\cal A})\big)^{\ast_{\cal A}\ast_{\cal A}} = \underline{\Omega}^1_{\mathrm{Der}}({\cal A}).$$

**PROPOSITION 11.** Any homomorphism $\varphi$ of unital algebras of ${\cal A}$ into the subalgebra $\Omega^0$ of elements of degree 0 of a graded differential algebra $\Omega$, such that $d({\cal A})$ spans a diagonal bimodule over ${\cal A}$ (for the $({\cal A}, {\cal A})$-bimodule structure on $\Omega^1$ induced by $\varphi$), has a unique extension $\tilde{\varphi}_{\mathrm{Diag}}: \Omega_{\mathrm{Diag}}({\cal A}) \to \Omega$ as a homomorphism of graded differential algebras.

**LEMMA 7.** Let ${\cal A}$ be a Poisson algebra. Then $[a, b]\{c, d\} = \{a, b\}[c, d]$, and more generally $[a, b]\, x\, \{c, d\} = \{a, b\}\, x\, [c, d]$, for any elements $a, b, c, d$ and $x$ of ${\cal A}$.

**LEMMA 8.** Let $\omega$ be a symplectic structure for ${\cal A}$, and define an antisymmetric bilinear bracket on ${\cal A}$ by $\{x, y\} = \omega(\mathrm{Ham}(x), \mathrm{Ham}(y))$. Then $(x, y) \mapsto \{x, y\}$ is a Poisson bracket on ${\cal A}$.

**THEOREM 4.** Let ${\cal M}$ and ${\cal N}$ be two $({\cal A}, {\cal B})$-bimodules and let $D$ be a first order operator from ${\cal M}$ into ${\cal N}$. Then there is a unique $({\cal A}, {\cal B})$-bimodule homomorphism $\sigma_L(D)$ of $\Omega^1_u({\cal A}) \otimes_{\cal A} {\cal M}$ into ${\cal N}$, and there is a unique $({\cal A}, {\cal B})$-bimodule homomorphism $\sigma_R(D)$ of ${\cal M} \otimes_{\cal B} \Omega^1_u({\cal B})$ into ${\cal N}$, such that
$$D(amb) = aD(m)b + \sigma_L(D)(d_u a \otimes m)\, b + a\, \sigma_R(D)(m \otimes d_u b)$$
for any $m \in {\cal M}$, $a \in {\cal A}$ and $b \in {\cal B}$.
