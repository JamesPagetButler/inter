# Restatement

## Abstract

Let $G$ be a connected reductive group over a finite extension $F$ of $\mathbf{Q}_p$. The paper concerns Kottwitz's conjecture, which describes the contribution of a supercuspidal representation to the cohomology of a local Shimura variety in terms of the local Langlands correspondence. A natural extension of this conjecture concerns Scholze's more general spaces of local shtukas. Using a new Lefschetz–Verdier trace formula for v-stacks, the paper proves the extended conjecture, disregarding the action of the Weil group, and modulo a virtual representation whose character vanishes on the locus of elliptic elements. As an application, for an irreducible smooth representation of an inner form of $\operatorname{GL}_n$, the $L$-parameter constructed by Fargues–Scholze agrees with the usual semisimplified parameter arising from local Langlands.

## Introduction

### Setup

Let $F$ be a finite extension of $\mathbf{Q}_p$, and let $G$ be a connected reductive group over $F$. Scholze [SW20, §23] introduced the tower of moduli spaces of mixed-characteristic shtukas
$$\Sht_{G,b,\mu}=\varprojlim_K \Sht_{G,b,\mu,K},$$
depending on a $\sigma$-conjugacy class of $b\in G(\breve F)$, where $\breve F$ is the completion of the maximal unramified extension of $F$, and on a conjugacy class of cocharacters $\mu\colon\mathbf{G}_m\to G$ defined over $\overline F$. Here $K$ ranges over open compact subgroups of $G(F)$. Each $\Sht_{G,b,\mu,K}$ is a locally spatial diamond over $\Spd\breve E$, where $E$ is the field of definition of the conjugacy class of $\mu$.

When $\mu$ is minuscule, $\Sht_{G,b,\mu,K}$ is the diamond associated to a rigid-analytic variety $\mathcal{M}_{G,b,\mu,K}$ [SW20, §24]. This is a local Shimura variety, whose general existence was conjectured in [RV14]. Rapoport–Zink spaces [RZ96] give instances of $\mathcal{M}_{G,b,\mu,K}$ with a moduli interpretation, as the generic fibre of a deformation space of $p$-divisible groups.

The Kottwitz conjecture [Rap95, Conjecture 5.1], [RV14, Conjecture 7.3] relates the cohomology of $\mathcal{M}_{G,b,\mu,K}$ to the local Langlands correspondence, in the case that $b$ lies in the unique basic class in $B(G,\mu)$.

Let $G_b$ be the inner form of $G$ associated to $b$. The tower $\Sht_{G,b,\mu,K}$ admits commuting actions of $G_b(F)$ and $G(F)$. The action of $G_b(F)$ preserves each $\Sht_{G,b,\mu,K}$. The action of $g\in G(F)$ sends $\Sht_{G,b,\mu,K}$ to $\Sht_{G,b,\mu,gKg^{-1}}$. There is also a (not necessarily effective) Weil descent datum on this tower from $\breve E$ down to $E$.

Let $\ell\neq p$ be a prime. The geometric Satake equivalence produces an object $\mathcal{S}_\mu$ in the derived category of étale $\mathbf{Z}_\ell$-sheaves on $\Sht_{G,b,\mu,K}$, compatible with the actions of $G(F)$ and $G_b(F)$ on the tower. Let $C$ be the completion of an algebraic closure of $\breve E$. For a smooth representation $\rho$ of $G_b(F)$ with coefficients in $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell$, define
$$R\Gamma(G,b,\mu)[\rho]=\varinjlim_K R\Hom_{G_b(F)}\big(R\Gamma_c(\Sht_{G,b,\mu,K,C},\mathcal{S}_\mu),\rho\big).$$
Then $R\Gamma(G,b,\mu)[\rho]$ lies in the derived category of smooth representations of $G(F)\times W_E$ with coefficients in $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell$, where $W_E$ is the Weil group.

Fargues–Scholze [FS21, Corollary I.7.3] state that if $\rho$ is finite length and admissible, then $R\Gamma(G,b,\mu)[\rho]$ is a complex of finite length admissible representations of $G(F)$ admitting a continuous action of $W_E$.

Let $\Groth(G_b(F))$ be the Grothendieck group of finite length admissible representations of $G_b(F)$ with $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell$ coefficients. Let $\Groth(G(F)\times W_E)$ be the Grothendieck group of finite length admissible representations of $G(F)$ with $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell$ coefficients, equipped with a continuous $W_E$-action commuting with the $G(F)$-action. Following [Shi11], define
$$\Mant_{b,\mu}\colon\Groth(G_b(F))\to\Groth(G(F)\times W_E)$$
by sending $\rho$ to the Euler characteristic of $R\Gamma(G,b,\mu)[\rho]$.

### Local Langlands

The complex dual groups of $G$ and $G_b$ are canonically identified; write $\widehat G$ for either. Let ${}^LG=\widehat G\rtimes W_F$. The basic form of the local Langlands conjecture predicts that the set of isomorphism classes of essentially square-integrable representations of $G(F)$ (resp. $G_b(F)$) is partitioned into $L$-packets $\Pi_\phi(G)$ (resp. $\Pi_\phi(G_b)$), each indexed by a discrete Langlands parameter $\phi\colon W_F\times\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf{C})\to{}^LG$. When $\phi$ is discrete and trivial on $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf{C})$, $\phi$ is called supercuspidal. In this case the packets $\Pi_\phi(G)$ and $\Pi_\phi(G_b)$ are expected to consist entirely of supercuspidal representations.

The generalized Kottwitz conjecture is conditional on the refined local Langlands correspondence for supercuspidal $L$-parameters, in the formulation of [Kal16a, Conjecture G]. It relies on the endoscopic character identities satisfied by $L$-packets, reviewed in Appendix A. The paper does not assume any compatibility between [Kal16a, Conjecture G] and the construction of [FS21].

**Status of [Kal16a, Conjecture G].** The full conjecture is known for regular supercuspidal parameters [Kal19a, Definition 5.2.3] provided $G$ splits over a tame extension of $F$, $F$ has characteristic zero, and $p\ge(e+2)n$, where $e$ is the ramification index of $F/\mathbf{Q}_p$ and $n$ is the smallest size of a faithful algebraic representation of $G$. The proof is in [Kal19a, §5.3] and [FKS19, §4.4]. Parts of the conjecture are known under weaker assumptions:

1. The existence of a finite set $\Pi_\phi$ of representations of rigid inner forms of $G$ for each tempered $L$-parameter $\phi$.
2. The existence and uniqueness of a generic constituent of $\Pi_\phi$ for a fixed Whittaker datum.
3. A bijection between $\Pi_\phi$ and $\mathrm{Irr}(\pi_0(S_\phi^+))$, the set of irreducible representations of the refined centralizer component group.
4. The character identities of ordinary endoscopy, recalled in Appendix A.

A set $\Pi_\phi$ has been constructed in [Kal19b, §§4.1, 4.2] for every supercuspidal $\phi$, provided $G$ splits over a tame extension of $F$ and $p$ does not divide the order of the Weyl group of $G$. Under weaker assumptions on $p$, the construction works for parameters $\phi$ whose wild inertia maps into a torus of $\widehat G$. A bijection between $\Pi_\phi$ and $\mathrm{Irr}(\pi_0(S_\phi^+))$ has been constructed in [Kal19b, §§4.3–4.5] for any supercuspidal $\phi$. Assuming $F$ has characteristic zero and $p\ge(e+2)n$, the existence and uniqueness of the generic constituent and the character identities of ordinary endoscopy are proved in [FKS19, §4.4] for all regular supercuspidal $\phi$. They are also proved for non-regular supercuspidal $\phi$, but only for certain endoscopic elements.

Let $S_\phi=\mathrm{Cent}(\phi,\widehat G)$. For $\pi\in\Pi_\phi(G)$ and $\rho\in\Pi_\phi(G_b)$, the refined local Langlands conjecture implies the existence of an algebraic representation $\delta_{\pi,\rho}$ of $S_\phi$. (It also depends on $b$, which is suppressed from the notation.)

The conjugacy class of $\mu$ determines by duality a conjugacy class of weights of $\widehat G$. Let $r_\mu$ be the irreducible representation of $\widehat G$ of highest weight $\mu$. There is a natural extension of $r_\mu$ to ${}^LG_E$ [Kot84a, Lemma 2.1.2]. Write $r_\mu\circ\phi_E$ for the representation of $S_\phi\times W_E$ given by
$$r_\mu\circ\phi_E(s,w)=r_\mu(s\cdot\phi(w)).$$

**Conjecture 1.0.1.** Let $\phi\colon W_F\to{}^LG$ be a supercuspidal Langlands parameter. Given $\rho\in\Pi_\phi(G_b)$, we have the following equality in $\Groth(G(F)\times W_E)$:
$$\Mant_{b,\mu}(\rho)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_\phi(G)}\pi\boxtimes\mathrm{Hom}_{S_\phi}(\delta_{\pi,\rho},r_\mu\circ\phi_E).\tag{1.0.1}$$

This conjecture is more general than the formulation of Kottwitz's conjecture in [Rap95] and [RV14] in two respects. First, $\mu$ is allowed to be non-minuscule, which requires passing from $\mathcal{M}_{G,b,\mu}$ to $\Sht_{G,b,\mu}$. Second, $G$ is not required to be a $B$-inner form of its quasi-split inner form $G^*$. This condition, reviewed in §2.2, makes the definition of $\delta_{\pi,\rho}$ straightforward. To remove it, the paper uses the refined local Langlands correspondence of [Kal16a, Conjecture G], based on the cohomology sets $H^1(u\to W, Z\to G)$ of [Kal16b]. The definition of $\delta_{\pi,\rho}$ in this setting is in §2.3, see Definition 2.3.2.

**Theorem 1.0.2.** Assume the refined local Langlands correspondence [Kal16a, Conjecture G]. Let $\phi\colon W_F\times\mathrm{SL}_2\to{}^LG$ be a discrete Langlands parameter with coefficients in $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell$, and let $\rho\in\Pi_\phi(G_b)$ be a member of its $L$-packet. After ignoring the action of $W_E$, we have an equality in $\Groth(G(F))$:
$$\Mant_{b,\mu}(\rho)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_\phi(G)}\left[\dim\Hom_{S_\phi}(\delta_{\pi,\rho},r_\mu)\right]\pi+\mathrm{err},$$
where $\mathrm{err}\in\Groth(G(F))$ is a virtual representation whose character vanishes on the locus of elliptic elements of $G(F)$. If the packet $\Pi_\phi(G)$ consists entirely of supercuspidal representations and the semisimple $L$-parameter $\varphi_\rho$ associated with $\rho$ as in [FS21, §I.9.6] is supercuspidal, then $\mathrm{err}=0$.

The paper expects $\varphi_\rho=\phi^{\mathrm{ss}}$. So if $\phi$ is supercuspidal, the error term should vanish, and Conjecture 1.0.1 follows modulo the action of $W_E$. For a discrete, non-supercuspidal $\phi$, the error term is often provably nonzero (cf. [Ima] for examples). For applications to the local Langlands correspondence, however, Theorem 1.0.2 in this generality is needed.

The shtukas here have one leg. Scholze's mixed-characteristic shtukas $\Sht_{G,b,\{\mu_i\}}$ with arbitrarily many legs are fibred over $\prod_{i=1}^r\Spd\breve E_i$. Extending Conjecture 1.0.1 and Theorem 1.0.2 to this setting is said to be straightforward: Theorem 1.0.2 in that generality follows from the one-leg case by letting the legs coalesce and using that cohomology of shtuka spaces forms a local system over $(\mathrm{Div}^1)^I$.

**Theorem 1.0.3.** Let $G$ be any inner form of $\mathrm{GL}_n/F$, and let $\pi$ be an irreducible smooth representation of $G(F)$. Then the $L$-parameter $\varphi_\pi$ associated with $\pi$ by the construction of Fargues–Scholze [FS21, §I.9] agrees with the usual semisimplified $L$-parameter attached to $\pi$.

### Remarks on the proof

The proof of Theorem 1.0.2 uses a Lefschetz–Verdier trace formula. The Lubin–Tate case illustrates the idea. Let $F=\mathbf{Q}_p$, $G=\mathrm{GL}_n$, $\mu=(1,0,\dots,0)$, and let $b$ be basic of slope $1/n$. Let $H_0$ be the $p$-divisible group over $\overline{\mathbf F}_p$ with isocrystal $b$; it has dimension $1$ and height $n$. Then $G_b(F)=\Aut^0H_0=D^\times$, where $D/\mathbf{Q}_p$ is the division algebra of invariant $1/n$.

The spaces $\mathcal{M}_K=\mathcal{M}_{G,b,\mu,K}$ form the Lubin–Tate tower, viewed as rigid-analytic spaces over $C$. The infinite-level space $\mathcal{M}=\varprojlim_K\mathcal{M}_K$ [SW13] is a perfectoid space with an action of $G(\mathbf{Q}_p)\times G_b(\mathbf{Q}_p)$. Its $C$-points classify triples $(H,\alpha,\iota)$, where $H/\mathcal{O}_C$ is a $p$-divisible group, $\alpha\colon\mathbf{Q}_p^n\to VH$ is a trivialization of the rational Tate module, and $\iota\colon H_0\otimes_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p}\mathcal{O}_C/p\to H\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_C}\mathcal{O}_C/p$ is an isomorphism in the isogeny category. Equivalences are quasi-isogenies making both diagrams commute. The action of $G(\mathbf{Q}_p)\times G_b(\mathbf{Q}_p)$ is by composition with $\alpha$ and $\iota$ respectively. The Hodge–Tate period map exhibits $\mathcal{M}$ as a pro-étale $D^\times$-torsor over Drinfeld's upper half-space $\Omega^{n-1}$, the complement in $\mathbf{P}^{n-1}$ of all $\mathbf{Q}_p$-rational hyperplanes. This map is $G(\mathbf{Q}_p)$-equivariant.

**Key observation.** Let $g\in G(\mathbf{Q}_p)$ be a regular elliptic element (irreducible characteristic polynomial). Then $g$ has exactly $n$ fixed points on $\Omega^{n-1}$. For each fixed point $x$, there exists $g'\in G_b(\mathbf{Q}_p)$ such that $(g,g')$ fixes a point in the fibre $\mathcal{M}_x$, and $g$ and $g'$ are related, meaning they become conjugate over $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$. (The argument: $\alpha$ and $\iota$ give an isomorphism $B_{\mathrm{cris}}^n\to VH\otimes B_{\mathrm{cris}}\to M(H_0)\otimes B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ carrying the action of $g$ to that of $g'$, so $g$ and $g'$ are conjugate over $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$.)

For admissible $\rho$ of $D^\times$, there is a $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell$-local system $\mathcal{L}_\rho$ on $\Omega^{n-1}_{C,\mathrm{\acute et}}$. For elliptic $g$, a naïve Lefschetz formula would give
$$\tr\big(g\mid R\Gamma_c(\Omega^{n-1},\mathcal{L}_\rho)\big)=\sum_{x\in(\Omega^{n-1})^g}\tr(g\mid\mathcal{L}_{\rho,x}).$$
By the key observation, $\tr(g\mid\mathcal{L}_{\rho,x})=\tr\rho(g')$ with $g,g'$ related. By Jacquet–Langlands, there is a discrete series $\pi$ of $G(\mathbf{Q}_p)$ with $\tr\pi(g)=(-1)^{n-1}\tr\rho(g')$, where $\tr\pi(g)$ is a Harish-Chandra character. So the Euler characteristic of $R\Gamma_c(\Omega^{n-1},\mathcal{L}_\rho)$ equals $(-1)^{n-1}n\pi$ up to a virtual representation with trace zero on the elliptic locus. Here $\mathcal{S}_\mu=\mathbf{Z}_\ell[n-1]$ up to a Tate twist, and
$$\Mant_{b,\mu}(\rho)=n\pi+\mathrm{err},$$
where the character of $\mathrm{err}$ vanishes on the elliptic locus. This is consistent with Theorem 1.0.2. The argument goes back at least to the 1990s [Har15, Chap. 9] and first appears in [Fal94].

If a suitable Lefschetz formula holds, the equality in Theorem 1.0.2 reduces to an endoscopic character identity relating representations of $G(F)$ and $G_b(F)$ (Theorem 3.2.9). The difficulty in Theorem 1.0.2 therefore lies in proving the Lefschetz formula.

Strauch and Mieda proved Theorem 1.0.2 for the Lubin–Tate tower [Str05], [Str08], [Mie12], [Mie14a], and for a basic Rapoport–Zink space for $\mathrm{GSp}(4)$ [Mie].

For a non-proper rigid space, the boundary must be treated. If $X=\{|T|\le1\}\subset\mathbf{A}^1$ is the affinoid unit disc, then $T\mapsto T+1$ has Euler characteristic $1$ on $X$ despite having no fixed points. The boundary point of $\overline X\setminus X$ is fixed by this automorphism. Mieda [Mie14b] proves a Lefschetz formula for an operator on a rigid space, under the assumption that the operator has no topological fixed points on a compactification.

In the cases above, $\mathcal{M}_{G,b,\mu,K}$ admits a cellular decomposition: it contains a compact open subset whose Hecke translates cover $\mathcal{M}_{G,b,\mu,K}$. This gives the topological fixed-point hypothesis for Mieda's formula. Shen [She14] constructs a cellular decomposition for a basic Rapoport–Zink space for $U(1,n-1)$. For general $(G,b,\mu)$, the $\mathcal{M}_{G,b,\mu,K}$ need not admit one.

The paper uses the geometrization of local Langlands [Far], [FS21]. The stack $\Bun_G$ of $G$-bundles on the Fargues–Fontaine curve geometrizes $B(G)$: there is a bijection $b\mapsto\mathcal{E}^b$ between $B(G)$ and points of $|\Bun_G|$. For basic $b$, the open substack $\Bun_G^b$ classifies $G$-bundles everywhere isomorphic to $\mathcal{E}^b$, and $\Bun_G^b\cong[*/G_b(F)]$. The Hecke stack $\Hecke_{G,\le\mu}$ parametrizes $\mu$-bounded modifications at one point, and the Hecke operator $T_\mu$ acts on $D(\Bun_G,\mathbf{Z}_\ell)$. For $b\in B(G,\mu)$, $\Sht_{G,b,\mu}$ is the fibre of $\Hecke_{G,\le\mu}$ over $(\mathcal{E}^b,\mathcal{E}^1)$. Hence $R\Gamma(G,b,\mu)[\rho]$ is expressed through the Hecke operators $T_\mu$ (Proposition 6.4.5).

Universal local acyclicity (ULA) is a property of $A\in D(X,\mathbf{Z}_\ell)$ for Artin v-stacks $X$. For $X=[*/G_b(F)]$, a ULA object is an admissible complex of representations. [FS21] proves that $T_\mu$ preserves ULA objects, which gives admissibility of $R\Gamma(G,b,\mu)[\rho]$. [LZ22] shows the ULA condition is the right hypothesis for a Lefschetz–Verdier trace formula for the cohomology of $A$. In the counterexample above, $j_!\mathbf{Z}_\ell$ fails to be ULA, where $j\colon X\hookrightarrow\overline X$. [LZ22] is written for schemes, but its formalism applies to rigid-analytic spaces and diamonds. For example, if $H$ is a locally profinite group acting continuously on a proper diamond $X$, and $A\in D(X,\mathbf{Z}_\ell)$ is a $H$-equivariant ULA object, then $R\Gamma(X,A)$ is an admissible $H$-module.

The paper's trace formula for diamonds is in §4. §5 treats the mixed-characteristic affine Grassmannian $\Gr_{G,\le\mu}$. The object $\mathcal{S}_\mu$ is ULA and $G(F)$-equivariant there, so its local term $\loc_g(x,A)$ at a fixed point $x$ of a regular $g\in G(F)$ makes sense. (Such fixed points are isolated.) The result needed for Theorem 1.0.2 would follow if $\loc_g(x,A)$ agreed with the naïve local term $\tr(g\mid A_x)$. This agreement is proved in §5 using the Witt-vector affine Grassmannian as a bridge between diamonds and schemes. §6 proves Theorem 1.0.2 by applying the trace formula to $\Hecke_{G,b,\le\mu}$.

**Lemma 3.2.1.** Suppose $g\in G(F)$ and $g'\in G_b(F)$ are strongly regular elements which are conjugate over an algebraic closure of $\breve F$. Then they are conjugate over $\breve F$.

**Definition 3.2.2.** For strongly regular related elements $g\in G(F)_{\sr}$ and $g'\in G_b(F)_{\sr}$, the invariant $\inv[b](g,g')$ is the class of $y^{-1}by^\sigma$ in $B(T)$, where $y\in G(\breve F)$ satisfies $g'=ygy^{-1}$.

**Fact 3.2.3.** The invariant $\inv[b](g,g')\in B(T)$ depends only on $b$, $g$, and $g'$, and not on the choice of $y$. It depends on the rational conjugacy classes of $g$ and $g'$ as follows:
- For $z\in G(F)$, $\inv[b]((\ad z)(g),g')=(\ad z)(\inv[b](g,g'))$, a class in $B((\ad z)(T))$.
- For $z\in G_b(F)$, $\inv[b](g,(\ad z)(g'))=\inv[b](g,g')$.

The image of $\inv[b](g,g')$ under the composition $B(T)\to B(G)\xrightarrow{\kappa}\pi_1(G)_\Gamma$ equals $\kappa(b)$.

**Definition 3.2.4.** Define a diagram of topological spaces
$$\begin{array}{ccc}\Rel_b & & \\ \downarrow & & \\ G(F)_{\sr}\sslash G(F) & & G_b(F)_{\sr}\sslash G_b(F)\end{array}\tag{3.2.1}$$
