# Restatement

## Abstract

Motivated by applications to duality theorems for $p$-adic pro-étale cohomology of rigid analytic spaces, we study the category of Topological Vector Spaces in the setting of condensed mathematics. We prove that it contains, as full subcategories, both the category of (topologically) bounded algebraic Vector Spaces and the category of perfect complexes on the Fargues-Fontaine curve. Vector Spaces coming from $p$-adic pro-étale cohomology of smooth partially proper rigid analytic varieties are examples of sheaves belonging to the former category.

## Introduction (setting)

Let $p$ be a prime and let $K$ be a complete discrete valuation field with a perfect residue field, of mixed characteristic. Let $\overline{K}$ be an algebraic closure of $K$ and let $C=\widehat{\overline{K}}$ be the $p$-adic completion of $\overline{K}$.

## Theorem 1.1

Let $S\in{\rm sPerf}_{C}$.

(1) (Enriched fully-faithfulness) The canonical functor from Vector Spaces to Topological Vector Spaces $\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}:{\mathcal{D}}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\bf Q}_{p})\to\underline{{\mathcal{D}}}(S,{\bf Q}_{p})$ tends to be fully faithful. More precisely, let ${\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\bf Q}_{p})$ be such that $\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}\in\underline{{\mathcal{D}}}^{b}(S,{\bf Q}_{p})$ and let ${\mathcal{G}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{+}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\bf Q}_{p})$. Then the canonical morphism in $\underline{{\mathcal{D}}}(S,{\bf Q}_{p})$
$$\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}\mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}_{S}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})\to\mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}_{S^{\rm top}}(\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}},\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{G}})$$
is a quasi-isomorphism.

(2) (Fargues-Fontaine fully-faithfulness) The functor $\mathrm{R}\tau_{*}:{\rm QCoh}(X_{\operatorname{FF},S^{\flat}})\to\underline{{\mathcal{D}}}(S,{\bf Q}_{p})$ is fully faithful when restricted to perfect complexes. That is, for ${\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\in{\rm Perf}(X_{\operatorname{FF},S^{\flat}})$, the natural map in ${\mathcal{D}}({\rm Mod}^{\rm cond}_{\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}(S)})$
$$\mathrm{R}{\rm{Hom}}_{{\rm QCoh}(X_{\operatorname{FF},S^{\flat}})}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})\to\mathrm{R}\underline{{\rm{Hom}}}_{S,{\bf Q}_{p}}(\mathrm{R}\tau_{*}{\mathcal{F}},\mathrm{R}\tau_{*}{\mathcal{G}})$$
is a quasi-isomorphism.

(3) (Compatibility of the algebraic and topological projections) The functor $\mathrm{R}\tau^{\prime}_{*}:{\rm QCoh}(X_{\operatorname{FF},S^{\flat}})\to{\mathcal{D}}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\bf Q}_{p})$ is compatible with the functor $\mathrm{R}\tau_{*}$ when restricted to nuclear sheaves. That is, the following diagram commutes:
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
{\rm Nuc}(X_{\operatorname{FF},S^{\flat}}) & \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{R}\tau^{\prime}_{*}\ } & {\mathcal{D}}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\bf Q}_{p}) \\
{\scriptstyle \mathrm{R}\tau_{*}}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle \mathrm{R}\pi_{*}} \\
\underline{{\mathcal{D}}}(S,{\bf Q}_{p}) & & 
\end{array}$$

## Corollary 1.2

Let ${\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\in\{{\mathbb{G}}_{a},\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}\}$. Then we have a natural quasi-isomorphism
$$\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}\mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}_{S,{\bf Q}_{p}}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}_{S^{\rm top},{\bf Q}_{p}}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}).$$

## Remark 1.3

(1) The above theorem and corollary mean that we can compute extensions of Banach-Colmez spaces on the Fargues-Fontaine curve, or in VS's, or in TVS's. Hence, in $\underline{{\mathcal{D}}}(S,{\bf Q}_{p})$:
$$\mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}_{S^{\rm top},{\bf Q}_{p}}({\bf Q}_{p},{\bf Q}_{p})\xleftarrow{\sim}{\bf Q}_{p},\quad \mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}_{S^{\rm top},{\bf Q}_{p}}({\bf Q}_{p},{\mathbb{G}}_{a})\xleftarrow{\sim}{\mathbb{G}}_{a},$$
$$\mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}_{S^{\rm top},{\bf Q}_{p}}({\mathbb{G}}_{a},{\mathbb{G}}_{a})\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}\oplus{\mathbb{G}}_{a}[-1],\quad \mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}_{S^{\rm top},{\bf Q}_{p}}({\mathbb{G}}_{a},{\bf Q}_{p})\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}(-1)[-1].$$
More generally, the first claim of the theorem can be applied to complexes of VS's representing pro-étale cohomology of smooth partially proper rigid analytic varieties (see Corollary 4.8).

(2) The second claim of the theorem holds in greater generality than stated though we did not find a clean general statement.

(3) In the applications to geometric dualities in [10] we work mostly with the solid version of TVS's, which allows us to do computations objectwise in the category of solid ${\bf Q}_{p}$-vector spaces.

(4) There is also a solid version of VS's introduced by Fargues-Scholze in [13] (see also [4]). Certainly, there is a close relationship between these versions of VS's and TVS's but we do not study it here.

## Example 2.1 (selected)

(1) The constant sheaf $\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}$ on $S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}$ is defined via $\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}:\{Y\mapsto{{\mathcal{C}}(|Y|,{\bf Q}_{p})}\}$. It is a sheaf (see [20, Lemma 3.14]). For $Y$ a strictly totally disconnected perfectoid space, one has (see the proof of [20, Cor. 3.15])
$$\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}(Y)=\underline{{\mathcal{C}}(|Y|,{\bf Q}_{p})}\xleftarrow{\sim}\underline{{\mathcal{C}}(\pi_{0}(Y),{\bf Q}_{p})},$$
a Banach space since $\pi_{0}(Y)$ is profinite. Hence $\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}(Y)\in{\rm Solid}$.

(2) The sheaf ${\mathbb{G}}_{a}$. On $S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}$ and $S^{\rm top}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}$ we have the presheaves
$${\mathbb{G}}_{a}:\{Y={\rm Spa}(R_{Y},R^{+}_{Y})\mapsto R_{Y}\},\quad {\mathbb{G}}^{\rm top}_{a}:\{Y={\rm Spa}(R_{Y},R^{+}_{Y})\mapsto\underline{R}_{Y}\},$$
respectively, where $R_{Y}$ is equipped with its natural Banach topology. The first presheaf is a sheaf by [26, Th. 8.7]. Using [22, Cor. 10.5.4] one has a natural isomorphism
$${\mathcal{C}}(T,R_{Y})\xleftarrow{\sim}{\mathbb{G}}_{a}(Y\times{T})=\underline{R}_{Y}\otimes^{\Box}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{\mathcal{C}}(T,{\bf Q}_{p}).$$

(3) Period sheaves. For $i\geq 0$, the presheaves
$$\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\operatorname{dR}}/t^{i}:\{Y\mapsto{{\bf B}}^{+}_{\operatorname{dR}}(Y)/t^{i}\},\quad \mathbb{B}^{+,\varphi=p^{i}}_{\operatorname{cr}}:\{Y\mapsto{{\bf B}}^{+,\varphi=p^{i}}_{\operatorname{cr}}(Y)\}$$
on $S^{\rm top}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}$ are sheaves of (solid) abelian groups, where the period rings carry their canonical topologies. They fit in the exact sequence
$$0\to\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}(i)\to\mathbb{B}^{+,\varphi=p^{i}}_{\operatorname{cr}}\to\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\operatorname{dR}}/t^{i}\to 0.$$
The natural isomorphisms $\pi_{*}\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\operatorname{dR}}/t^{i}\xleftarrow{\sim}\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\operatorname{dR}}/t^{i}$ and $\pi_{*}\mathbb{B}^{+,\varphi=p^{i}}_{\operatorname{cr}}\xleftarrow{\sim}\mathbb{B}^{+,\varphi=p^{i}}_{\operatorname{cr}}$ hold (2.4).

## Lemma 2.5

Let ${\mathcal{F}}\in\{\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p},{\mathbb{G}}_{a},\mathbb{B}^{+}_{\operatorname{dR}}/t^{i},\mathbb{B}^{+,\varphi=p^{i}}_{\operatorname{cr}}\}$ be a sheaf on $S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}$. Then $\mathrm{R}^{i}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}=0$ for $i>0$. In particular, there is a natural quasi-isomorphism $\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}$ in ${\mathcal{D}}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm CondAb})$.

## Proposition 2.7 (Clausen-Scholze [8, Prop. 7.5])

Let $A$ be an analytic ring with an underlying condensed ring $\underline{A}$.

(1) The full subcategory of solid $A$-modules
$${\rm Mod}^{\rm solid}_{A}\subset{\rm Mod}^{\rm cond}_{\underline{A}}$$
is a Grothendieck abelian subcategory, stable under all limits, colimits, and extensions. The inclusion admits a left adjoint
$${\rm Mod}^{\rm cond}_{\underline{A}}\to{\rm Mod}^{\rm solid}_{A}:\ M\mapsto M\otimes_{\underline{A}}A,$$
which preserves all colimits and is symmetric monoidal.

(2) The canonical functor ${\mathcal{D}}({\rm Mod}^{\rm solid}_{A})\to{\mathcal{D}}({\rm Mod}^{\rm cond}_{\underline{A}})$ is fully faithful. It preserves all limits and colimits. A complex $M\in{\mathcal{D}}({\rm Mod}^{\rm cond}_{\underline{A}})$ lies in ${\mathcal{D}}({\rm Mod}^{\rm solid}_{A})$ if and only if $H^{i}(M)$ lies in ${\rm Mod}^{\rm solid}_{A}$ for all $i$. The functor admits a left adjoint $M\mapsto M\otimes^{\rm L}_{\underline{A}}A$, which is the left derived functor of the adjoint in (1). It is symmetric monoidal.

(3) For $M,N\in{\mathcal{D}}({\rm Mod}^{\rm solid}_{A})$, the derived internal Hom $\mathrm{R}\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{A}(M,N)\in{\mathcal{D}}({\rm Mod}^{\rm solid}_{A})$. The natural map $\mathrm{R}\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{A}(M,N)\to\mathrm{R}\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{\underline{A}}(M,N)$ is a quasi-isomorphism.

## Remark 2.12

In the context of Proposition 2.7, set $M^{\Box}:=M\otimes_{\underline{A}}A$ for $M\in{\rm Mod}^{\rm cond}_{\underline{A}}$, and $M^{\operatorname{L}_{\Box}}:=M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\underline{A}}A$ for $M\in{\mathcal{D}}({\rm Mod}^{\rm cond}_{\underline{A}})$.

## Lemma 2.20

The canonical forgetful functor ${\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid})\to{\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm CondAb})$ is (topologically) fully faithful and has a (topological) left adjoint $\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\Box}$, given by the sheafification of the presheaf $\mathcal{F}^{\Box,\rm psh}:\{Y\mapsto\mathcal{F}(Y)^{\Box}\}$. It commutes with all colimits.

## Lemma 2.21

(1) For $\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in{\rm PSh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid})$, there is a canonical isomorphism in ${\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid})$:
$$\mathcal{F}^{\sharp}\otimes^{\Box}\mathcal{G}^{\sharp}\simeq(\mathcal{F}\otimes^{\Box,\rm psh}\mathcal{G})^{\sharp}.$$
(2) For $\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in{\rm PSh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm CondAb})$, there is a canonical isomorphism in ${\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid})$:
$$(\mathcal{F}^{\sharp}\otimes\mathcal{G}^{\sharp})^{\Box}\simeq\big((\mathcal{F}\otimes^{\rm psh}\mathcal{G})^{\Box,\rm psh}\big)^{\sharp}.$$

## Lemma 2.22

If $\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3}\in{\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid})$, there is a canonical functorial isomorphism in ${\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid})$:
$$\mathcal{H}om^{\Box}_{S}(\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes^{\Box}\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{H}om^{\Box}_{S}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{H}om^{\Box}_{S}(\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3})).$$
In particular, there is a canonical functorial isomorphism in ${\rm Solid}$:
$${\rm Hom}^{\Box}_{S}(\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes^{\Box}\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3})\simeq{\rm Hom}^{\Box}_{S}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{H}om^{\Box}_{S}(\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3})).$$

## Lemma 2.23

## Lemma 2.25

If $\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3}\in{\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p,\Box})$, there is a canonical functorial isomorphism in ${\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p,\Box})$:
$$\mathcal{H}om^{\Box}_{S,\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}(\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes^{\Box}_{\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{H}om^{\Box}_{S,\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{H}om^{\Box}_{S,\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}(\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3})).$$

$$\underline{{\rm Hom}}^{\Box}_{S,\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}(\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes^{\Box}_{\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3})\simeq\underline{{\rm Hom}}^{\Box}_{S,\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{H}om^{\Box}_{S,\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}(\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3})).$$

## Lemma 2.27

Let $W\in{\bf Q}_{p,\Box}$ be a Fréchet space or of compact type, and let $\mathcal{F}\in{\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p,\Box})$.

(1) There is a canonical quasi-isomorphism in ${\mathcal{D}}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\bf Q}_{p,\Box})$:
$$W\otimes^{\operatorname{L}_{\Box}}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline{W}\otimes^{\operatorname{L}_{\Box}}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}\mathcal{F}.\tag{2.28}$$

(2) There is a natural isomorphism in ${\bf Q}_{p,\Box}$:
$$\underline{{\rm Hom}}^{\Box}_{S,{\bf Q}_{p}}(\underline{W},\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline{{\rm Hom}}^{\Box}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}(W,\mathcal{F}(S)).$$

(3) There is a natural quasi-isomorphism in ${\mathcal{D}}({\bf Q}_{p,\Box})$:
$$\mathrm{R}\underline{{\rm Hom}}^{\Box}_{S,{\bf Q}_{p}}(\underline{W},\mathcal{F})\simeq\mathrm{R}\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{{\bf Q}_{p,\Box}}(W,\mathrm{R}\Gamma(S^{\rm top}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{F})).$$

(4) If $W$ is of compact type, there is a natural quasi-isomorphism in ${\mathcal{D}}({\bf Q}_{p,\Box})$:
$$\mathrm{R}\underline{{\rm Hom}}^{\Box}_{S,{\bf Q}_{p}}(\underline{W},\mathcal{F})\simeq W^{*}\otimes^{\operatorname{L}_{\Box}}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(S^{\rm top}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{F}).$$

## Proposition 2.29

(1) The forgetful functor
$${\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid})\to{\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm CondAb})\tag{2.30}$$
is (topologically) fully faithful. Its essential image is stable under all limits, colimits, and extensions. The inclusion (2.30) admits a (topological) left adjoint, the solidification functor
$${\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm CondAb})\to{\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid}):\ \mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\Box},$$
which preserves all colimits and is symmetric monoidal. Analogous claims hold for the forgetful functor ${\rm Sh}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\bf Q}_{p,\Box})\to{\rm Sh}(S^{\rm top}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\bf Q}_{p})$.

(2) The forgetful functor
$${\mathcal{D}}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid})\to{\mathcal{D}}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm CondAb})\tag{2.32}$$
is (topologically) fully faithful. It preserves all limits and colimits.

(3) The functor (2.32) admits a left adjoint $\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\operatorname{L}_{\Box}}$, which is the (topological) left derived functor of the solidification functor $(-)^{\Box}$. It is symmetric monoidal. Analogous claims hold for the forgetful functor ${\mathcal{D}}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p,\Box})\to{\mathcal{D}}(S^{\rm top}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p})$.

(4) For $\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\in{\mathcal{D}}(S_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},{\rm Solid})$, the natural map $\mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Box}_{S}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})\to\mathrm{R}{\mathcal{H}om}_{S^{\rm top}}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2})$ is a quasi-isomorphism. An analogous claim holds for the $\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}$-sheaves.

## Lemma 2.33

If $W$ is a prodiscrete condensed set (a cofiltered limit of discrete condensed sets), then ${\bf Z}[W]^{\operatorname{L}_{\Box}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\bf Z}[W]^{\Box}$. Similarly with $\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}(S)$-coefficients, for $S\in{\rm Perf}_C$.

## Remark 2.36

Sheaves of $\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p}$-modules on ${\rm Spa}(C)_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}$ are called Vector Spaces (${\rm VS}$ for short). Sheaves from ${\rm Sh}({\rm Spa}(C)_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\underline{{\bf Q}}_{p,\Box})$ are called Naive Topological Vector Spaces (${\rm NTVS}$ for short).
