# Restatement

## Abstract

Random noncommutative geometry (NCG) can be viewed as a Euclidean path-integral quantization of the theory defined by the Spectral Action in NCG. To investigate phase transitions in random NCG of arbitrary dimension, the paper studies the nonperturbative Functional Renormalization Group (FRG) for multimatrix models whose action consists of noncommutative polynomials in Hermitian and anti-Hermitian matrices. This structure is dictated by the Spectral Action for the Dirac operator in Barrett's spectral triple formulation of fuzzy spaces. The treatment is mathematically rigorous and uses a "coordinate-free" language, which is stated to hold for general multimatrix models. The toolkit is a noncommutative calculus on the free algebra. The generator of the RG flow, a noncommutative Laplacian introduced in the paper, is described in terms of Voiculescu's cyclic gradient and the Rota–Sagan–Stein noncommutative derivative. The paper explores the algebraic structure of the FRG equation and applies the formalism to find the $\beta$-functions, identify fixed points in the large-$N$ limit, and obtain critical exponents of two-dimensional geometries in two different signatures.

## 1. Introduction

**Background.** Random noncommutative geometry was initiated by Barrett and Glaser [BG16] as a path-integral approach to the quantization of noncommutative geometries. The problem has been addressed by other methods in [MvS14], [CCvS19] and [DKvS19], and is treated in [CM07, §18.4]. The classical theory of the Standard Model [CCM07, Bar07] and some theories beyond it [CvS19] are formulated in this NCG language.

**Matter and gravity.** The paper notes that the asymptotically safe approach to quantum gravity [DEP14] (see also [RS19]) states that matter is relevant. Based on the Renormalization Group (RG) argument of [Eic14], gravity and matter influence each other: gravity loops affect matter, and matter modifies the gravity sector in the RG flow. The paper proposes to develop the Functional Renormalization Group (FRG) in random NCG as a first step.

**FRG.** The FRG equation (FRGE), reviewed in [DCE+20], describes the Wilsonian RG flow [Wil75]. It was derived by Wetterich and Morris [Wet93, Mor94]. It determines the effective action by a differential equation in place of path integration.

**Fuzzy geometries.** The space(time) model is an abstraction of fuzzy spaces [DO03, DHMO08, SchSt13], later assembled into a spectral triple called fuzzy geometry [Bar15, BG19]. The partition function is
$$\mathcal{Z}=\int \mathrm{e}^{-\Tr f(D)}\,\mathrm{d}D,$$
which corresponds to the Spectral Action $\Tr f(D)$, where $f$ is a polynomial. This contrasts with the bump function $f$ used originally by Connes–Chamseddine [CC97]. It was shown [Bar15, BG16] that this quantization of fuzzy geometries leads to a class of multimatrix models characterized in [Pér19].

**Phase transitions.** Finite dimensionality is related to a minimal length, as in the fuzzy sphere [GP95]. The paper relates this discrete picture to pre-geometric phases in approaches such as Group Field Theory [BCO+14], Matrix Models [Eyn16, DFGZJ95], Tensor Models [Gur16] and Causal Dynamical Triangulations [AJL00]. Phase transitions to a manifold-like phase are studied in [AJJL12, DG15, EKP19, EPP20, PT21], and condensation of fuzzy geometries to a continuum is addressed in [Gla17] and, in dimension 1, by [KP21]. The paper aims to estimate candidates for such transitions.

**Ingredients.** The non-standard ingredients are:
- The models originate in Random NCG [BG16]. Barrett's characterization of Dirac operators relates them to certain multimatrix models [Bar15]. Their Spectral Action was computed in [Pér19] using chord diagrams.
- The RG-flow parameter is the logarithm of the matrix size, as introduced in [BZJ92]. Here an $N+1$ square matrix $\varphi$ is reduced to an effective $N\times N$ matrix field by integrating out the entries $\varphi_{a,N+1},\varphi_{N+1,a}$ $(a=1,\ldots,N)$. Eichhorn and Koslowski gave a nonperturbative formulation for Hermitian matrix models in [EK13], following [SK11] and extended to tensor models in [EKP19]. The paper proves the FRGE for multimatrix models, since this determines the algebraic structure needed for the $FP^{-1}$-expansion [BGMS11, Sec. 2.2.2].
- The language is abstract noncommutative algebra, using Voiculescu's cyclic derivative [Voi00] and the Rota–Sagan–Stein noncommutative derivative [RSS80].

**Organization and results.**
- *Section 2* introduces a noncommutative (NC) Hessian and an NC-Laplacian on the free algebra, defined through the noncommutative differential operators of [RSS80] and [Voi00, Voi08]. A graphical method for computing this second-order operator is given.
- *Section 3* reviews fuzzy geometries and their Spectral Action. For 2-dimensional fuzzy geometries, it characterizes the allowed terms in the action.
- *Section 4* proves that the FRGE is governed by the NC-Hessian.
- *Section 5* introduces truncations and projections to compute $\beta$-functions. It develops the $FP^{-1}$-expansion in the large-$N$ limit. The tadpole approximation, the order-one term of that expansion, is restated as a heat equation whose Laplacian is the noncommutative Laplacian of Section 2. (Footnote 1: that a Laplacian appears in the FRG is expected [Sal99, Sec. 3.3].)
- *Section 6* reconsiders the FRGE for Hermitian matrix models. This serves as a bridge from index computations to index-free ones. It calibrates the infrared (IR) regulator: with a quadratic instead of the linear IR regulator, the fixed point is closer to the exact value $-1/12$ for gravity coupled with conformal matter.
- *Section 7* treats a class of two-matrix models. These differ from the Ising two-matrix model [Kaz86, Sta93, Eyn03], where $\Tr(AB)$ is the only mixing interaction. NCG models forbid $AB$ and instead include words such as $ABAB$, $A^2B^2$, $A^3BAB$, $\ldots$ and tensor products such as $A\otimes A$, $A^2\otimes B^2$, $A\otimes A^2BAB$, $\ldots$. Their exact form is investigated in [Pér19], including higher dimensions.
- The RG flow does not stay in the space of Dirac operators. The IR regulator breaks symmetry and moves the flow to couplings indexed by a larger subspace of tensor products of the free algebra.
- Two errors arise: projection errors from considering only operators with unbroken symmetries, and truncation errors from keeping a finite number of parameters. The paper looks for critical exponents at fixed points obeying the duality $A\leftrightarrow B$, when the signature allows it, inside the hypercube $|g_i|\leq 1$.
- Footnote 3: at sixth order the NCG matrix model includes up to 48 operators in a double-trace even-degree truncation. The Ising two-matrix model would include at most 19 operators in the same truncation.
- Footnote 4: Appendices B, C, D and E correspond to Supplementary Material I, II, III and IV in the Annales Henri Poincaré version. Appendix A is a glossary and notation guide.

## 2. Algebraic framework

**Example 2.1.** In the free algebra generated by $\mathtt{A},\ldots,\mathtt{Z}$:
$$\partial^{\mathtt{E}}(\mathtt{FREENESS})=\mathtt{FR}\otimes\mathtt{ENESS}+\mathtt{FRE}\otimes\mathtt{NESS}+\mathtt{FREEN}\otimes\mathtt{SS}.$$
If $\mathbf{1}$ is the empty word,
$$\partial^{\mathtt{S}}(\mathtt{FREENESS})=\mathtt{FREENE}\otimes\mathtt{S}+\mathtt{FREENES}\otimes\mathbf{1}.$$
For the cyclic derivative,
$$\mathrm{D}^{\mathtt{E}}(\mathtt{FREENESS})=\tilde{m}\big(\mathtt{FR}\otimes\mathtt{ENESS}+\mathtt{FRE}\otimes\mathtt{NESS}+\mathtt{FREEN}\otimes\mathtt{SS}\big)=\mathtt{ENESSFR}+\mathtt{NESSFRE}+\mathtt{SSFREEN}.$$

**Proposition 2.2.** Let $Y=X_i$ be any generator of $\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}$. For any $Q\in\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}$, the derivatives $\partial^Y$ and $\mathrm{D}^Y$ satisfy:

- Abstract derivative as a matrix derivative:
$$\partial^{Y}_{ab}=\frac{\delta}{\delta Y_{ba}}\tag{2.8}$$
That is, with $(U\otimes V)_{ab;cd}=U_{ab}V_{cd}$ for $U,V\in\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}$,
$$[(\partial^{Y}Q)(X)]_{ab;cd}=\frac{\delta}{\delta Y_{bc}}[Q(X)]_{ad}\quad\text{for }X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\in\mathcal{M}_N.$$

- Cyclic derivative as the noncommutative derivative of the trace:
$$\partial^{Y}\Tr Q=\mathrm{D}^{Y}Q.\tag{2.9}$$

**Definition 2.3 (NC-Hessian).** The NC-Hessian is the operator
$$\Hess:\mathrm{im}\,\Tr_N\to M_n(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}^{\otimes 2},\tag{2.10}$$
whose $(ij)$-entry ($1\le i,j\le n$) in the first tensor factor is
$$(\Hess\Tr_N P)_{ij}:=(\partial^{X_i}\circ\partial^{X_j}\Tr_N P)\in\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}^{\otimes 2}.\tag{2.11}$$
Here $\Tr_N:\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}\to\mathbb{C}$ is the ordinary trace of $M_N(\mathbb{C})\supset\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}$. The NC-Hessian is the block matrix whose $(i,j)$ entry is $\partial^{X_i}\circ\partial^{X_j}$.

The modified Hessian $\Hess_g$, for $g=\diag(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ (the "signature"), is
$$(\Hess_g\Tr_N P)_{ij}:=(e_i)^{\delta_{ij}}(\partial^{X_i}\circ\partial^{X_j}\Tr_N P)\in\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}^{\otimes 2},\tag{2.12}$$
so its diagonal entries are $e_i\,\partial^{X_i}\circ\partial^{X_i}$ and its off-diagonal entries are unchanged.

The noncommutative Laplacian $\nabla^2$ is the trace of $\Hess_g$ with the signature:
$$\nabla^2:\mathrm{im}\,\Tr_N\to\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}^{\otimes 2},\qquad \nabla^2:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_i\,(\partial^{X_i}\circ\partial^{X_i}).\tag{2.13}$$
Abbreviating $\partial^{X_j}\circ\partial^{X_j}=\nabla_j^2$, this reads $\nabla^2=\sum_{j=1}^n e_j\nabla_j^2$.

**Proposition 2.4.** Let $Q=X_{\ell_1}X_{\ell_2}\cdots X_{\ell_k}$ be a monomial in $\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}$ with $k\ge 2$. Then for $i,j=1,\ldots,n$,
$$(\partial^{X_i}\circ\partial^{X_j})\Tr_N Q=\sum_{\pi=(uv)}\delta^{j}_{\ell_u}\delta^{i}_{\ell_v}\,\pi_1(Q)\otimes\pi_2(Q),\tag{2.14}$$
where the sum runs over all (directed) pairings $\pi=(uv)$ between the letters of the word $Q$, distributed on a circle. The words $\pi_1(Q)$ and $\pi_2(Q)$ are the words between $X_{\ell_u}$ and $X_{\ell_u}$ [as printed]. They satisfy: $\pi_2(Q)\,X_{\ell_u}\,\pi_1(Q)\,X_{\ell_v}$ matches $Q$ up to cyclic reordering.

**Proposition 2.5.** The NC-Hessian of a product of traces is
$$\Hess(\Tr P\,\Tr Q)=\Tr Q\,\Hess(\Tr P)+\Tr P\,\Hess\Tr Q+\Delta(P,Q),\tag{2.19}$$
where $\Delta(P,Q)$ is the matrix with entries
$$[\Delta(P,Q)]_{ij}=\mathrm{D}^{X_i}P\otimes_\tau\mathrm{D}^{X_j}Q+\mathrm{D}^{X_i}Q\otimes_\tau\mathrm{D}^{X_j}P\in\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle}^{\otimes 2}.$$

**Example 2.6.**
- On $\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle,N}$,
$$\nabla^2\Tr(X_iX_j)=\sum_{k=1}^n 2e_i\delta_i^k\delta_j^k\,1_N\otimes 1_N=2e_i\delta_j^i\,1_N\otimes 1_N,$$
since only the empty word lies between the two letters.
- On $\mathbb{C}_{\langle 1\rangle,N}=\mathbb{C}\langle X\rangle$ with $X^*=X$, $\nabla^2=(\partial^X)^2$, and for $m\ge 2$,
$$\nabla^2\Tr_N\Big(\frac{X^m}{m}\Big)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-2}X^{\ell}\otimes X^{m-2-\ell}.\tag{2.22}$$
Even though $\mathbb{C}_{\langle 1\rangle,N}$ consists of ordinary polynomials, its NC-derivatives are not ordinary.

**Example 2.7.** On $\mathbb{C}_{\langle 2\rangle}=\mathbb{C}\langle A,B\rangle$, with $g=\diag(e_1,e_2)=\diag(e_{\mathsf a},e_{\mathsf b})$:
$$\Hess_g\{\Tr(ABAB)\}=\begin{pmatrix}e_{\mathsf a}\partial^A\circ\partial^A&\partial^A\circ\partial^B\\ \partial^B\circ\partial^A&e_{\mathsf b}\partial^B\circ\partial^B\end{pmatrix}\Tr(ABAB)$$
$$=2\begin{pmatrix}e_{\mathsf a}\,B\otimes B&AB\otimes 1+1\otimes BA\\ BA\otimes 1+1\otimes AB&e_{\mathsf b}\,A\otimes A\end{pmatrix}.$$
This shows the asymmetry of the Hessian. The Laplacian is the trace of the block matrix:
$$\nabla^2\Tr(ABAB)=\Tr_2\big(\Hess_g\Tr(ABAB)\big)=2e_{\mathsf a}\,B\otimes B+2e_{\mathsf b}\,A\otimes A.$$

**Proposition 2.8.** For monomials $U,W,P,Q\in\mathbb{C}_{\langle n\rangle}$:
$$(U\otimes W)\times(P\otimes Q)=UP\otimes WQ,\tag{2.26a}$$
$$(U\otimes W)\times(P\otimes_\tau Q)=WP\otimes_\tau UQ,\tag{2.26b}$$
$$(U\otimes_\tau W)\times(P\otimes Q)=UP\otimes_\tau WQ,\tag{2.26c}$$
$$(U\otimes_\tau W)\times(P\otimes_\tau Q)=WP\otimes UQ.\tag{2.26d}$$

**Proposition 2.9.** It follows that:
$$(U\otimes_\tau W)\star(P\otimes_\tau Q)=PU\otimes_\tau WQ,\tag{2.27a}$$
$$(U\otimes W)\star(P\otimes_\tau Q)=U\otimes PWQ,\tag{2.27b}$$
$$(U\otimes_\tau W)\star(P\otimes Q)=WPU\otimes Q,\tag{2.27c}$$
$$(U\otimes W)\star(P\otimes Q)=\Tr(WP)\,U\otimes Q.\tag{2.27d}$$

**Proposition 2.10.** The product $\star$ is associative on $\mathcal{A}_n$.

**Definition 2.11.** The twisted NC-Hessian is
$$\Hess^{\tau}_{\sigma}:=(1\otimes_\tau 1)\times\Hess_{\sigma}.$$

**Example 2.12.** The twisted Hessian of $AABB$ is
$$\Hess_\sigma^\tau(AABB)=\begin{pmatrix}e_{\mathsf a}(1\otimes_\tau BB+BB\otimes_\tau 1)& 1\otimes_\tau AB+BA\otimes_\tau 1+A\otimes_\tau B+B\otimes_\tau A\\ 1\otimes_\tau BA+AB\otimes_\tau 1+A\otimes_\tau B+B\otimes_\tau A& e_{\mathsf b}(1\otimes_\tau AA+AA\otimes_\tau 1)\end{pmatrix}.$$
The twisted Hessian of $\Tr A\,\Tr(ABB)$ is
$$\Hess_\sigma^\tau[\Tr A\,\Tr(ABB)]=\begin{pmatrix}e_{\mathsf a}(1\otimes BB+BB\otimes 1)&\Tr A(B\otimes_\tau 1+1\otimes_\tau B)+1\otimes AB+1\otimes BA\\ \Tr A(B\otimes_\tau 1+1\otimes_\tau B)+AB\otimes 1+BA\otimes 1&e_{\mathsf b}\Tr A(A\otimes_\tau 1+1\otimes_\tau A)\end{pmatrix}.$$
The diagonal entries of the product $\Hess_\sigma^\tau[\Tr A\,\Tr(ABB)]\star\Hess_\sigma^\tau(AABB)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}&*\\ *&\mathcal{Q}\end{pmatrix}$ are, using $e_{\mathsf a}^2=e_{\mathsf b}^2=1$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{P}={}&\Tr A\{1\otimes BBA+ABB\otimes 1+A\otimes BB+2B\otimes BA+2AB\otimes B+BB\otimes A\}\\
&+1\otimes_\tau AABB+2\cdot 1\otimes_\tau ABAB+2\cdot 1\otimes_\tau ABBA+2\cdot 1\otimes_\tau BABA\\
&+1\otimes_\tau BBAA+2\cdot 1\otimes_\tau BBBB+2BB\otimes_\tau BB,
\end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{Q}={}&\Tr A\{1\otimes BAB+BAB\otimes 1+A\otimes BB+B\otimes AB+B\otimes BA+AB\otimes B\\
&\quad+BA\otimes B+BB\otimes A+1\otimes AAA+AAA\otimes 1+A\otimes AA+AA\otimes A\}\\
&+2AB\otimes_\tau AB+2AB\otimes_\tau BA+2BA\otimes_\tau AB+2BA\otimes_\tau BA.
\end{aligned}$$
The $M_n(\mathbb{C})$-trace of products of Hessians, or of their anticommutators, is later shown to be fundamental for the RG flow. (Footnote 8: the $*$ entries of products of two Hessians are not used in this paper, except for third-order RG-flow computations.) The order of the letters in the Hessians matters, and cyclic permutation is allowed only after taking traces.

## 3. Spectral Action and admissible terms

**Lemma 3.1.** Let $w\in\mathbb{C}_{\langle 2\rangle}=\mathbb{C}\langle A,B\rangle$ be a word of even degree in each generator, $\deg_A(w),\deg_B(w)\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$. Then
$$[\Tr_N w]\,(\Tr D^{m})\neq 0,\qquad\text{for }m=\deg_A(w)+\deg_B(w).$$
That is, $\Tr_N w$ has nonzero coefficient in the Spectral Action for a suitable power of the Dirac operator $D$.

**Lemma 3.2.** Let $\mathbb{C}_{\langle 2\rangle}=\mathbb{C}\langle A,B\rangle$. The coefficient of the double-trace $\Tr_N(w_1)\Tr_N(w_2)$ in the Spectral Action is nontrivial for any word $w=w_1\otimes w_2\in\mathbb{C}_{\langle 2\rangle}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\langle 2\rangle}$ satisfying
$$\deg_A(w_1)+\deg_A(w_2),\ \deg_B(w_1)+\deg_B(w_2)\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}:$$
$$[\Tr_N^{\otimes 2}w]\,(\Tr D^{m})\neq 0,\qquad\text{for }m=\sum_{r=1,2}\big(\deg_A(w_r)+\deg_B(w_r)\big).$$

## 4. Functional Renormalization Group equation

**Proposition 4.1.** The interpolating effective action $\Gamma_N$ of a matrix model with $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\in\mathcal{M}_N^{p,q}$ satisfies, for each $N\le\Lambda$, the Wetterich–Morris equation
$$\partial_t\Gamma_N[X]=\frac12\STr\bigg(\frac{\partial_t R_N^\tau}{\Hess_\sigma^\tau\Gamma_N[X]+R_N^\tau}\bigg),\tag{FRGE}$$
where $t=\log N$ is the RG-flow parameter and $\sigma=\diag(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$, with $X_i^*=\pm X_i$ if and only if $e_i=\pm 1$. The signs are determined by the signature $(p,q)$ of the fuzzy geometry that originates the matrix model. For dimensions $p+q\le 2$, this coincides with $g=\diag(e_1,\ldots,e_{p+q})$; otherwise the signs are given by Eq. (3.3). The quotient of operators is taken with respect to the $\times$ product.

**Corollary 4.2 (FRGE for Hermitian multimatrix models).** The Wetterich–Morris equation for Hermitian $n$-matrix models is
$$\partial_t\Gamma_N[X]=\frac12\STr\bigg(\frac{\partial_t R_N^\tau}{\Hess^\tau\Gamma_N[X]+R_N^\tau}\bigg).\tag{4.17}$$

## 5. Truncations and the tadpole approximation

**Remark 5.1.** It would be interesting to determine whether the vanishing of $\STr_N(L)$, which appears in the physics literature as part of the definition, constrains the IR regulator. Specifically, one can explore the conditions that
$$\STr_N(P^{-1}1_N\otimes 1_N)=0$$
imposes on $R_N$, if the condition (5.7) is not included in the definition automatically.

**Proposition 5.2.** The RG flow is generated by the noncommutative Laplacian scaled by
$$\varrho:=\sum_{a,b,c,d}(\partial_t R_N\cdot P^{-2})_{ab;cd}.$$
In the tadpole approximation, the FRGE is
$$\partial_t\Gamma_N[X]=-\frac12\,\varrho\,\Tr_N\otimes\Tr_N\big(\nabla^2\Gamma_N\big).\tag{5.8}$$

Then, at any order in the $FP^{-1}$-expansion, the RG flow generates exclusively operators that appear again in the Spectral Action $\Tr f(D)$, generally with a different nonzero coefficient. In the worst case, this requires a suitable (generally higher-degree) polynomial $f$.
