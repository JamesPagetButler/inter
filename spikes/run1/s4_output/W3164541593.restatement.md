# Restatement

## Abstract

For every affinoid analytic adic space $X$, the pseudocoherent complexes, perfect complexes, and finite projective modules over $\mathcal{O}_X(X)$ form a stack with respect to the analytic topology on $X$. In particular, the category of vector bundles on $X$ is equivalent to the category of finite projective modules over $\mathcal{O}_X(X)$.

The paper constructs a fully faithful functor from the category of complete Huber pairs to the category of analytic rings in the sense of Clausen and Scholze, and studies its basic properties. It gives an explicit description of the functor of measures of the analytic ring associated to a complete Huber pair.

Following Kedlaya and Liu, the paper introduces pseudocoherent modules and pseudocoherent sheaves on adic spaces, and shows that there is a similar equivalence of the corresponding categories.

## Introduction

**Setting and notation.** Throughout, $(A,A^{+})$ is a sheafy analytic complete Huber pair unless otherwise specified. The adic space $\mathrm{Spa}(A,A^{+})$ is denoted $X$. For every affinoid open $U\subset X$, the rings $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$ and $\mathcal{O}_X^{+}(U)$ are denoted $A_U$ and $A_U^{+}$.

**Definition 1.1** ([Kdl], Definition 1.1.2). Let $A$ be a complete Huber ring. It is called *analytic* if its topologically nilpotent elements generate the unit ideal. A complete Huber pair $(A,A^{+})$ is said to be *analytic* if $A$ is.

**Proposition 1.2** ([Kdl], Lemma 1.1.3). Let $(A,A^{+})$ be a complete Huber pair. The following are equivalent:

- The ring $A$ is analytic.
- Any ideal of definition in any ring of definition of $A$ generates the unit ideal in $A$.
- Every open ideal of $A$ is equal to the unit ideal of $A$.
- For every nontrivial ideal $I$ of $A$, the quotient topology on $A/I$ is not discrete.
- The only discrete topological $A$-module is the zero module.
- The set $\mathrm{Spa}(A,A^{+})$ contains no point on whose residue field the induced valuation is trivial, i.e., the pre-adic space $\mathrm{Spa}(A,A^{+})$ is analytic.
- The set $\mathrm{Spa}(A,A^{+})$ admits a rational open cover whose elements are the adic spectra of Tate Huber pairs.

**Footnote 1.** An adic space is said to be *analytic* if all of its points are analytic.

**Definition 1.3.** An object $M$ of $\mathcal{D}(A)$ is a *pseudocoherent complex* if it is quasi-isomorphic to a complex of the form
$$\cdots \rightarrow P_{n+m} \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow P_{n+1} \rightarrow P_{n} \rightarrow 0, \tag{$\dagger$}$$
where the $P_i$ are finite projective modules over $A$. An object $M$ is a *perfect complex* if it is quasi-isomorphic to a finite complex of the form $(\dagger)$. A perfect complex $M$ has *tor-amplitude in $[a,b]$*, where $a\le b$ are integers, if it is quasi-isomorphic to a complex of the form $(\dagger)$ all of whose terms in cohomological degrees outside $[a,b]$ are trivial.

Notation: $\mathrm{PCoh}_A$ denotes the full $\infty$-subcategory of pseudocoherent complexes in $\mathcal{D}(A)$; $\mathrm{Perf}_A$ denotes the $\infty$-subcategory of perfect complexes; $\mathrm{FP}_A$ denotes the subcategory of finite projective modules. For $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathrm{PCoh}_A^{\le n}$ denotes the full $\infty$-subcategory of pseudocoherent complexes in $\mathcal{D}(A)^{\le n}$. For $a\le b$, $\mathrm{Perf}_A^{[a,b]}$ denotes the $\infty$-subcategory of perfect complexes with tor-amplitude in $[a,b]$. Note that $\mathrm{FP}_A = \mathrm{Perf}_A^{[0,0]}$.

**Theorem 1.4** (see Theorem 5.3 below). The functors sending each affinoid subspace $U\subset X$ to the $\infty$-categories $\mathrm{PCoh}_{A_U}$, $\mathrm{PCoh}_{A_U}^{\le n}$, $\mathrm{Perf}_{A_U}$, $\mathrm{Perf}_{A_U}^{[a,b]}$, and $\mathrm{FP}_{A_U}$, respectively, define sheaves of $\infty$-categories on $X$.

For finite projective modules, this was already known: the first proof is due to Kedlaya and Liu ([Kdl], Theorem 1.4.2), and it was originally proved in [KLI], Theorem 2.7.7, in the case where $A$ is Tate. The descent results for pseudocoherent and perfect complexes are new.

In [KLII] (see also [Kdl], §1), Kedlaya and Liu introduce pseudocoherent modules. They prove in [Kdl], Theorem 1.4.14, that these satisfy descent for the analytic topology (see [KLII], Theorem 2.4.15, for the original proof when $A$ is Tate). A new proof of [Kdl], Theorem 1.4.14, is given as Theorem 5.24 below.

**Footnote 2.** The paper's definition of pseudocoherent modules is slightly different from that of Kedlaya–Liu, so the result established is slightly different; see the remark after 5.19.

**Theorem 1.5** (see Theorem 3.27 below). For every profinite set $S$,
$$(A,A^{+})_{\blacksquare}[S] \cong \operatorname*{colim}_{B\rightarrow A^{+},\ M} \prod_{J} \underline{M} \subset \prod_{J} \underline{A},$$
where $J$ is a set such that $\mathbb{Z}_{\blacksquare}[S]\cong\prod_{J}\mathbb{Z}$, and the colimit is taken over all finitely generated subrings $B\subset A^{+}$ and all quasi-finitely generated $B$-submodules $M$ of $A$.

**Theorem 1.6** (see Theorem 4.1 below). Let $X$ be an analytic adic space and let $U$ be an arbitrary affinoid subspace of $X$. Then the association
$$U\mapsto \mathcal{D}\big((\mathcal{O}_X(U),\mathcal{O}_X^{+}(U))_{\blacksquare}\big)$$
defines a sheaf of $\infty$-categories on $X$. Explicitly, let $\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ be a family of open affinoid subspaces of $X$ covering $U$. Let $\mathcal{C}_I$ denote the category of finite subsets of $I$, and for $J\in\mathcal{C}_I$ set $U_J \overset{\mathrm{def}}{=} \bigcap_{i\in J} U_i$. Then the natural functor
$$\mathcal{D}\big((\mathcal{O}_X(U),\mathcal{O}_X^{+}(U))_{\blacksquare}\big) \rightarrow \lim_{J\in\mathcal{C}_I} \mathcal{D}\big((\mathcal{O}_X(U_J),\mathcal{O}_X^{+}(U_J))_{\blacksquare}\big)$$
is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories.

In what follows, the derived $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}(A)$ embeds fully faithfully into $\mathcal{D}(X)$ via the condensification functor. Its essential image consists of the *(relatively) discrete* objects. Hence $\mathrm{PCoh}_{A_U}$, $\mathrm{PCoh}_{A_U}^{\le n}$, $\mathrm{Perf}_{A_U}$, $\mathrm{Perf}_{A_U}^{[a,b]}$, and $\mathrm{FP}_{A_U}$ can be viewed as full $\infty$-subcategories of $\mathcal{D}(X)$.

Subsection 5.3 introduces the notions of *pseudocoherent*, *dualizable*, *nuclear*, and *compact* objects in $\mathcal{D}(X)$. Every pseudocoherent complex in $\mathcal{D}(A)$ is pseudocoherent and nuclear as an object of $\mathcal{D}(X)$. A discrete object of $\mathcal{D}(X)$ is pseudocoherent if and only if it comes from a pseudocoherent complex in $\mathcal{D}(A)$.

## Section 2: Recollections from condensed mathematics

**Definition 2.1** ([CM], Definition 1.2). Let $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$ be the pro-étale site of the point $\ast$, i.e., the category $\mathrm{ProFin}\cong\mathrm{Pro}(\mathrm{Fin})$ of profinite sets with covers given by finite families of jointly surjective maps. A *condensed set* is a sheaf on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$. Similarly, a *condensed abelian group*, *ring*, etc., is a sheaf of abelian groups, rings, etc., on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$.

Footnote 3: This is not quite the official definition; see [CM] for the correct version and a discussion of set-theoretic issues. The paper ignores such issues. The category of condensed sets is denoted $\mathrm{Cond}$, and the category of condensed modules over a condensed ring $R$ is denoted $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_R$.

**Definition 2.2.** Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an abelian category that admits all filtered colimits. An object $M$ is *compact* if $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,-)$ commutes with all filtered colimits. An object $X$ of $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ is *compact* if $\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,-)$ commutes with all filtered colimits, or equivalently, with all direct sums.

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category that admits all coproducts. A family of objects $S$ in $\mathcal{C}$ *generates* $\mathcal{C}$ if the following equivalent conditions hold:

- If $g_1,g_2\colon c\rightarrow d$ are morphisms in $\mathcal{C}$ such that $g_1\circ f = g_2\circ f$ for all morphisms $f\colon s\rightarrow c$ with $s\in S$, then $g_1=g_2$.
- For every object $c$ of $\mathcal{C}$, there exists an epimorphism $\coprod_{i\in I} s_i \rightarrow c$, where each $s_i$ is an object of $S$.

**Theorem 2.3** ([AG], Proposition 1.2). Consider the functor from compactly generated weakly Hausdorff topological spaces to condensed sets, $T\mapsto \underline{T}$.

1. It has a left adjoint $X\mapsto X(\ast)_{\mathrm{top}}$, which sends a condensed set $X$ to its underlying set $X(\ast)$, endowed with the quotient topology arising from the map $\coprod_{S,\,a\in X(S)} S \rightarrow X(\ast)$, where $S$ ranges over all profinite sets.
2. It is fully faithful and induces an equivalence between compact Hausdorff spaces and quasi-compact quasi-separated condensed sets.
3. It induces an equivalence between ind-compact Hausdorff spaces of the form $\varinjlim_i T_i$, where all transition maps $T_i\rightarrow T_j$ are closed immersions, and quasi-separated condensed sets.

Footnote 5: A condensed set $X$ is *quasi-compact* if there is a profinite set $S$ with a surjective map $\underline{S}\rightarrow X$. It is *quasi-separated* if for every pair of profinite sets $S_1,S_2$ with maps to $X$, the fiber product $S_1\times_X S_2$ is quasi-compact.

**Theorem 2.4** ([CM], Theorem 1.10). The category of condensed abelian groups is an abelian category satisfying Grothendieck's axioms (AB3), (AB4), (AB5), (AB6), (AB3*), and (AB4*). Furthermore, it is generated by compact projective objects.

**Definition 2.5** ([CM], Definitions 7.1 & 7.4, and [AG], Definition 6.4). A *pre-analytic ring* $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ is a condensed ring $\mathcal{A}$ (the underlying condensed ring) equipped with a functor
$$\{\text{extremally disconnected sets}\}\rightarrow \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}},\quad S\mapsto \mathcal{M}[S],$$
called the *functor of measures*, which sends finite disjoint unions to products, together with a natural transformation
$$\Phi_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}\colon \underline{S}\rightarrow \mathcal{M}[S]$$
of functors $\{\text{extremally disconnected sets}\}\rightarrow \mathrm{Cond}$.

A pre-analytic ring $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ is *analytic* if for every complex $C\colon \cdots\rightarrow C_i\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow C_1\rightarrow C_0\rightarrow 0$ of $\mathcal{A}$-modules, such that each $C_i$ is a direct sum of objects of the form $\mathcal{M}[T]$ for extremally disconnected sets $T$, the map
$$\mathrm{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M}[S],C)\rightarrow \mathrm{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}[S],C)$$
is an isomorphism for all extremally disconnected sets $S$. Equivalently, $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ is analytic if and only if for any families $\{S_i\}_{i\in I}$, $\{S_j\}_{j\in J}$ of extremally disconnected sets and any map $f\colon \bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{M}[S_i]\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathcal{M}[S_j]$ with kernel $K$, the map
$$\mathrm{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M}[S],K)\rightarrow \mathrm{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}[S],K)$$
is an isomorphism for all extremally disconnected $S$.

An analytic ring $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ is *complete* if the map $\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}[\ast]$ is an isomorphism.

Footnote 7: The paper denotes both the analytic ring associated to $(A,A^{+})$ and its functor of measures by $(A,A^{+})_{\blacksquare}$. If $A^{+}=A^{\circ}$, both are denoted $A_{\blacksquare}$.

**Example 2.6.**

1. The most important example for this paper is $\mathbb{Z}_{\blacksquare}$. Its underlying condensed ring is $\mathbb{Z}$, and its functor of measures is $S\mapsto \mathbb{Z}_{\blacksquare}[S]$. (Footnote 8: $\mathbb{Z}_{\blacksquare}[S]$ is defined for all profinite sets $S$, while the functor of measures of an analytic ring is a priori defined only on extremally disconnected sets. This discrepancy is addressed later; the same comment applies to Examples 2.6.2 and 2.6.3.) The analyticity of $\mathbb{Z}_{\blacksquare}$ is nontrivial; see [CM], Lectures V and VI.

2. Let $A$ be a discrete finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra and $S$ a profinite set. Write $S=\varprojlim_i S_i$ as a limit of finite sets and define $A_{\blacksquare}[S]\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\varprojlim_i A[S_i]$. Then the condensed ring $A$ and the functor $S\mapsto A_{\blacksquare}[S]$ form an analytic ring, denoted $A_{\blacksquare}$. For a proof, see [CM], Appendix to Lecture VIII.

3. Let $S=\varprojlim_i S_i$ be a profinite set written as a limit of finite sets. Define $\mathbb{Z}_{p,\blacksquare}[S]\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\varprojlim_i \underline{\mathbb{Z}}_p[S_i]$. Then the condensed ring $\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_p$ and the functor $S\mapsto\mathbb{Z}_{p,\blacksquare}[S]$ form an analytic ring, denoted $\mathbb{Z}_{p,\blacksquare}$. For a proof, see [CM], Proposition 7.9. (Footnote 9: The analytic ring constructed in Section 3 for every complete Huber pair is isomorphic to this one; this is easy to check.)

4. The main objective of [AG] is to construct analytic rings with underlying condensed rings $\underline{\mathbb{R}}$ and $\underline{\mathbb{C}}$. Clausen and Scholze construct such rings for each real $0<p\le 1$. This gives a class of complete $\underline{\mathbb{R}}$- and $\underline{\mathbb{C}}$-condensed modules for each $0<p\le 1$, called *$p$-liquid $\mathbb{R}$- and $\mathbb{C}$-vector spaces*. The verification that they are analytic is complicated; see [AG], Lecture VI.

**Theorem 2.7** ([CM], Proposition 7.5). Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ be an analytic ring.

1. The full subcategory $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}\subset \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ of all $\mathcal{A}$-modules $M$ such that $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M}[S],M)\rightarrow M(S)$ is an isomorphism for every extremally disconnected set $S$, is an abelian subcategory stable under all limits, colimits, and extensions. Objects of the form $\mathcal{M}[S]$, where $S$ is an extremally disconnected profinite set, constitute a family of compact projective generators of $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$. The inclusion admits a left adjoint
$$\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})},\quad M\mapsto M\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}),$$
which is the unique colimit-preserving extension of $\mathcal{A}[S]\mapsto\mathcal{M}[S]$. There is a unique symmetric monoidal tensor product $\otimes_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$ on $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$ making $-\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ symmetric monoidal.

2. The functor $\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}})$ is fully faithful. Its essential image is stable under all limits and colimits and consists of those $C\in\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}})$ for which
$$\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M}[S],C)\rightarrow \mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}[S],C)$$
is an isomorphism for all extremally disconnected $S$. For such $C$, the map $\mathrm{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M}[S],C)\rightarrow \mathrm{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}[S],C)$ is also an isomorphism. An object $C\in\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}})$ lies in $\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})})$ if and only if, for each $i\in\mathbb{Z}$, the cohomology group $H^i(C)$ lies in $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$. The inclusion admits a left adjoint
$$\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow \mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}),\quad C\mapsto C\overset{L}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}),$$
the left derived functor of $M\mapsto M\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$. There is a unique symmetric monoidal tensor product $\overset{L}{\otimes}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$ on $\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})})$ making $-\overset{L}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ symmetric monoidal.

**Definition 2.8.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ and $(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ be pre-analytic rings. A map $f\colon(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})\rightarrow(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ is a map of condensed rings $\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}$ together with a natural transformation $\Phi^{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}_{(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})}\colon \mathcal{M}[S]\rightarrow\mathcal{N}[S]$ of condensed $\mathcal{A}$-modules.

If $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ and $(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ are analytic rings, a map $f\colon(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})\rightarrow(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ is a map of condensed rings $\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}$ such that, for every extremally disconnected set $S$, the condensed module $\mathcal{N}[S]$ is $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete.

**Proposition 2.9** ([CM], Proposition 7.7). Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ and $(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ be analytic rings and $f\colon(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})\rightarrow(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ a morphism of analytic rings.

1. There exists a unique functor $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})}$, denoted $-\otimes_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$, such that the composite
$$\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{-\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}}\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{B}}\xrightarrow{-\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})}\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})}$$
factors as $\big(-\otimes_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})\big)\circ\big(-\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})\big)$.

2. The composite
$$\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{-\overset{L}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B})\xrightarrow{-\overset{L}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$$
factors as $\big(-\overset{L}{\otimes}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})\big)\circ\big(-\overset{L}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})\big)$, where $-\overset{L}{\otimes}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ denotes the left derived functor of $-\otimes_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$.

**Definition 2.10.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ be an analytic ring. For every profinite set $S$, $\mathcal{M}[S]\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ is defined as $\mathcal{A}[S]\overset{L}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$.

**Proposition 2.11.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ be an analytic ring over $\mathbb{Z}_{\blacksquare}$ (e.g., $A_{\blacksquare}$ with $A$ discrete). Then for every profinite set $S$, the object $\mathcal{M}[S] := \mathcal{A}[S]\overset{L}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ is concentrated in degree $0$, compact, and projective. Moreover, for any profinite sets $S_1$ and $S_2$, the derived tensor product $\mathcal{M}[S_1]\overset{L}{\otimes}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{M}[S_2]$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal{M}[S_1\times S_2]$.

**Corollary 2.11.1.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ be an analytic ring over $\mathbb{Z}_{\blacksquare}$. Then the tensor product of two projective condensed $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-modules is projective.

**Corollary 2.11.2.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ be an analytic ring over $\mathbb{Z}_{\blacksquare}$. Then the symmetric monoidal tensor product $\overset{L}{\otimes}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$ of $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ is the left derived functor of $\otimes_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$.

**Definition 2.12** ([AG], Definition 11.4). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category that admits all small colimits and is generated under small colimits by the full subcategory $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{cp}}$ of compact projective objects. The *animation* of $\mathcal{C}$ is the $\infty$-category $\mathrm{Ani}(\mathcal{C})$ freely generated under sifted colimits by $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{cp}}$.

**Definition 2.13.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the category of condensed sets or of condensed abelian groups, respectively. Then $\mathrm{Ani}(\mathcal{C})$ is called the $\infty$-category of *animated condensed sets* or *animated condensed abelian groups*, respectively.
