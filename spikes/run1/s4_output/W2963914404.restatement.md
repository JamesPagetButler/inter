# Restatement

## Abstract

By a quantum metric space we mean a $C^*$-algebra (or more generally an order-unit space) equipped with a generalization of the Lipschitz seminorm on functions which is defined by an ordinary metric. We develop for compact quantum metric spaces a version of Gromov–Hausdorff distance. We show that the basic theorems of the classical theory have natural quantum analogues. Our main example involves the quantum tori $A_\theta$. We show, for consistently defined "metrics", that if a sequence $\{\theta_n\}$ of parameters converges to a parameter $\theta$, then the sequence $\{A_{\theta_n}\}$ of quantum tori converges in quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance to $A_\theta$.

## Introduction

**Quantum metric spaces and the Kantorovich metric.** Quantum spaces are unital $C^*$-algebras, or more generally order-unit spaces. A generalization of the usual Lipschitz seminorm on functions, defined by an ordinary metric, plays the role of the metric. Such a "Lipschitz seminorm" defines an ordinary metric on the state-space of the quantum space, generalizing the Kantorovich metric [39] [40] on probability measures on an ordinary metric space [61], [62].

**Compact quantum metric spaces.** The notion of compact quantum metric space was introduced in [61], [62] (without that terminology), following glimpses given by Connes [11], [12] in connection with quantum Riemannian geometry defined by Dirac operators. This article deals only with compact quantum metric spaces.

**Quantum tori.** The main example is the quantum tori [58] [60]. For a consistent choice of "metrics":

> If a sequence of parameters $\{\theta_n\}$ converges to a parameter $\theta$, then the corresponding sequence of quantum tori $\{A_{\theta_n}\}$ converges in quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance to $A_\theta$.

**Matrix algebras and spheres.** One class of examples [63] involves sequences of matrix algebras of increasing dimension, equipped with consistent "metrics", which converge to ordinary compact metric spaces. The case of the 2-sphere is discussed in [27], [5], Section 2.2 of [70], and references therein; the matrix algebras there are often called "fuzzy spheres". The quantum fuzzy sphere is discussed in [28]. The fuzzy 4-sphere appears in [33]. In [63], using the present material together with Berezin quantization, matrix algebras are shown to converge to the sphere for quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance, with analogous results for any integral coadjoint orbit of any compact Lie group.

**Tori.** Matrix algebras converging to 2-tori (and tori of any dimension) are discussed in [20], [2], and references therein.

**Higher genus and Berezin–Toeplitz quantization.** The case of higher-genus surfaces is mentioned in [15] and [8]. Berezin–Toeplitz quantization for compact Kähler manifolds is treated in [65] and references therein.

**Li's result.** Hanfeng Li has shown [46], for the Connes–Landi–Dubois-Violette spheres $\{S_\theta\}$ (and related quantum manifolds) [17] [16] with their Dirac operators, that they form compact quantum metric spaces. Moreover, if a sequence $\theta_n$ of parameters converges to a parameter $\theta$, then $S_{\theta_n}$ converges to $S_\theta$ for quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

**Kerr's result.** David Kerr has developed a matricial version of quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance [41].

**Section outline and stated results.**

- Section 3 recalls the ordinary Gromov–Hausdorff distance and develops material on quotients ("subsets") needed in the quantum setting.
- Section 4 gives the definition of quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance and proves that it satisfies the triangle inequality. When applied to ordinary metric spaces, the quantum distance does not in general equal the ordinary Gromov–Hausdorff distance, but it is never greater than the ordinary distance. A specific failure, found by Hanfeng Li, is presented in Appendix 1.
- Section 5 develops a technique for estimating quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distances using "bridges", with simple examples; bridges are used extensively in later sections.
- In the classical setting, if the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between two compact metric spaces is $0$, the spaces need not be equal as sets but are isometric. Section 7 shows that the analogous fact holds for quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance, with preparation in Section 6 on isometries between compact quantum metric spaces.
- In [61], several ways of constructing "metrics" on $C^*$-algebras were described, given an action of a compact group on a $C^*$-algebra together with a length function on the group. Section 8 uses this construction to approximate the $C^*$-algebra with metric, for Gromov–Hausdorff distance, by finite-dimensional compact quantum metric spaces.
- Sections 9–11 treat quantum tori, including continuous fields of quantum metric spaces.
- Section 12 shows that the space of isometry classes of compact quantum metric spaces, with quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance, is complete. The classical analogue is a basic fact of the classical theory.
- Section 13 formulates and proves the quantum analogue of Gromov's compactness criterion for subsets of the complete space.
- Appendix 2 answers question 11.1 of [62] by showing that every "metric" for a compact quantum metric space can be obtained by the Dirac operator construction.
