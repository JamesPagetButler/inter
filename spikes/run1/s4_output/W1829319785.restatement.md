# Restatement

## Introduction

### 1.1 Setting

Fix a perfect field $k$ of characteristic $p$, and let $W(k)$ be the ring of Witt vectors of $k$. The paper concerns the set
$$\mathrm{GL}_n\big(W(k)[\tfrac{1}{p}]\big)/\mathrm{GL}_n(W(k)),$$
equivalently the set of $W(k)$-lattices in $W(k)[\tfrac{1}{p}]^n$. This is a mixed-characteristic analogue of the affine Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff}}$, which for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ parametrizes $k[[t]]$-lattices in $k((t))^n$. The Witt vector affine Grassmannian is denoted $\mathrm{Gr}^{W\mathrm{aff}}$.

**Footnote 1.** For example, if $\beta: G\to G_0$ is an isogeny of $p$-divisible groups over $k$, and a trivialization $W(k)[\tfrac{1}{p}]^n\simeq D(G_0)[\tfrac{1}{p}]$ of the Dieudonné module of $G_0$ has been fixed, then the induced map $D(\beta)$ on Dieudonné modules defines a point of $\mathrm{Gr}^{W\mathrm{aff}}$. In the equal characteristic case, this relation was obtained by Hartl and Viehmann, [HV11, Theorem 6.3].

Affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties in $\mathrm{Gr}^{W\mathrm{aff}}$ were previously defined only ad hoc, cf. [CKV13], [Ham13, §10].

### 1.2 Results

**Classical case.** For integers $a\le b$, there are closed subfunctors $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}\subset\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff}}$ parametrizing lattices $M\subset k((t))^n$ lying between $t^a k[[t]]^n$ and $t^b k[[t]]^n$. These exhaust $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff}}$:
$$\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff}}\simeq\varinjlim \mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}.$$
Each $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}$ admits a closed embedding into a finite disjoint union of classical Grassmannians $\mathrm{Gr}(d,t^a k[[t]]^n/t^b k[[t]]^n)$, as the $k$-subspaces $V\subset t^a k[[t]]^n/t^b k[[t]]^n$ stable under multiplication by $t$. All transition maps are closed embeddings. The line bundle
$$\mathcal{L}=\det_k\big(t^a k[[t]]^n/M\big)$$
on $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}$ is ample.

Results for $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff}}$ were first obtained by Beauville–Laszlo, [BL94]; earlier work includes [SS83] and [SW85].

**Witt vector case.** Previous work on this question includes [Hab05], [Kre14], and [Zhu14]. For a general $\mathbf{F}_p$-algebra $R$, $W(R)$ may contain $p$-torsion, and $W(R)/p\to R$ need not be an isomorphism. If $R$ is perfect, then
$$W(R)=\Big\{\sum_{n\ge 0}[a_n]p^n \,\Big|\, a_n\in R\Big\}.$$
$W(R)$ is the unique (up to unique isomorphism) $p$-adically complete flat $\mathbf{Z}_p$-algebra lifting $R$. By [Kre14, Theorem 5], for perfect $R$ the set of $W(R)$-lattices in $W(R)[\tfrac{1}{p}]^n$ is a reasonable object; for example, faithfully flat descent holds. Kreidl's work also indicates that representability is difficult without the perfectness restriction. Zhu, [Zhu14], proved representability of $\mathrm{Gr}^{W\mathrm{aff}}$ on perfect rings. Zhu's result is that each $\mathrm{Gr}^{W\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}$, parametrizing lattices $M$ between $p^aW(R)^n$ and $p^bW(R)^n$, is represented by the perfection of a proper algebraic space over $\mathbf{F}_p$.

**Theorem 1.1.** The functor $\mathrm{Gr}^{W\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}$ on perfect rings $R$, parametrizing $W(R)$-lattices $M\subset W(R)[\tfrac{1}{p}]^n$ lying between $p^aW(R)^n$ and $p^bW(R)^n$, is representable by the perfection of a projective algebraic variety over $\mathbf{F}_p$.

Consequently, $\mathrm{Gr}^{W\mathrm{aff}}$ is representable by an inductive limit of perfections of projective varieties.

The key step is the construction of a line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ on $\mathrm{Gr}^{W\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}$, analogous to $\mathcal{L}=\det_R(t^aR[[t]]^n/M)$ on $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}$.

**Footnote 2.** The existence of this line bundle was already conjectured by Zhu. Since $p^aW(R)^n/M$ does not carry the structure of an $R$-module, $\det_R(p^aW(R)^n/M)$ is not a priori defined. Two constructions are given.

**Theorem 1.2.** Any vector bundle $\mathcal{E}$ on a perfect $\mathbf{F}_p$-scheme $X$ gives a sheaf for the $h$-topology on perfect schemes over $X$ via pullback, and one has $H^i_h(X,\mathcal{E})\simeq H^i(X,\mathcal{E})$ for all $i$. Moreover, one has effective descent for vector bundles along $h$-covers of perfect schemes.

The first part is due to Gabber, cf. [BST13, §3]. The second part extends to the full derived category (see §11).

**K-theoretic construction.** If $R$ is a field, or after an $h$-cover, one can filter $p^aW(R)^n/M$ so that all graded pieces $Q_i$ are finite projective $R$-modules. Define
$$\mathcal{L}=\bigotimes_i\det_R(Q_i).$$
The construction must be shown independent of the filtration; then Theorem 1.2 defines $\mathcal{L}$ in general. Equivalently, one constructs a natural map
$$\widetilde{\mathrm{det}}: K(W(R)\text{ on }R)\to \mathcal{P}\mathrm{ic}^{\mathbf{Z}}(R)$$
from the $K$-theory spectrum of perfect complexes on $W(R)$ that are acyclic after inverting $p$, to the groupoid of graded line bundles.

**Footnote 3.** The statement is ultimately needed only at the level of $\pi_0$, but the language of spectra or Picard groupoids is needed to carry out descent.

Taking determinants of projective modules gives a natural map $\mathrm{det}: K(R)\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ic}^{\mathbf{Z}}(R)$. Let $\alpha: K(R)\to K(W(R)\text{ on }R)$ be the forgetful map. The problem is to extend $\mathrm{det}$ along $\alpha$. When $R$ is the perfection of a regular $\mathbf{F}_p$-algebra, $\alpha$ is an equivalence, by Quillen's dévissage. In general, de Jong's alterations and $h$-descent for line bundles reduce to this case.

**Geometric construction.** On a Demazure resolution $\widetilde{\mathrm{Gr}}^{W\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}\to\mathrm{Gr}^{W\mathrm{aff},[a,b]}$, parametrizing filtrations of $p^aW(R)^n/M$ with graded pieces finite projective over $R$, there is a line bundle $\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}$ by definition. The problem is to descend $\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}$.

**Theorem 1.3.** Let $f: X\to Y$ be the perfection of a proper surjective map of $\mathbf{F}_p$-schemes. Assume that all geometric fibres of $f$ are connected. Then a vector bundle $\mathcal{E}$ on $X$ descends (necessarily uniquely) to a vector bundle on $Y$ if and only if $\mathcal{E}$ is trivial on all geometric fibres of $f$.

(This is Theorem 6.8 and Remark 6.12.)

**Remark 1.4.** In a previous version, this theorem was stated under the stronger hypothesis $Rf_*\mathcal{O}_X=\mathcal{O}_Y$.

Ampleness of $\mathcal{L}$ is proved using a result of Keel, [Kee99], on semiample line bundles in positive characteristic.

## 2. The $h$-topology

**Definition 2.1.** A morphism $f: X\to Y$ of qcqs schemes is called *universally subtrusive* or a *$v$-cover* if for any map $\mathrm{Spec}(V)\to Y$, with $V$ a valuation ring, there is an extension of valuation rings $V\hookrightarrow W$ and a commutative diagram
$$\mathrm{Spec}(W)\to X,\qquad \mathrm{Spec}(V)\to Y,$$
compatible with $f$, $\mathrm{Spec}(W)\to\mathrm{Spec}(V)$, and the given map $\mathrm{Spec}(V)\to Y$.

A map $f': X'\to Y$ is a *refinement* of $f$ if $f'$ is still universally subtrusive and factors through $f$.

**Remark 2.2.** The name $v$-cover suggests surjectivity at the level of valuations. Recall that there is a fully faithful functor $X\mapsto X^{\mathrm{ad}}$ from schemes to adic spaces, sending $\mathrm{Spec}(R)$ to $\mathrm{Spa}(R,R)$, the space of equivalence classes of valuations on $R$ taking values $\le 1$, cf. [Hub94]. Then $f: X\to Y$ is a $v$-cover if and only if $|f^{\mathrm{ad}}|:|X^{\mathrm{ad}}|\to|Y^{\mathrm{ad}}|$ is surjective.

**Example 2.3** ([Ryd10, Remark 2.5]). Let $f: X\to Y$ be a map of qcqs schemes. Then $f$ is a $v$-cover in each of the following cases:
- (i) $f$ is faithfully flat;
- (ii) $f$ is proper and surjective;
- (iii) $f$ is an $h$-cover in the sense of Voevodsky.

Case (iii) follows from (i) and (ii), since $h$-covers are generated by proper surjective maps and fppf maps.

*Counterexample (Voevodsky).* Let $Y=\mathbb{A}^2_k$ over a field $k$, let $\tilde X\to Y$ be the blow-up at $(0,0)$, and let $X\subset\tilde X$ be the complement of one point in the exceptional locus. Then $X\to Y$ is surjective but not a $v$-cover: a valuation specializing to $(0,0)$ in the direction of the missing point does not lift to $\tilde X$.

**Theorem 2.4** ([Ryd10, Theorem 3.12]). Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finitely presented $v$-cover, where $Y$ is affine. Then there is a refinement $f': X'\to Y$ of $f$ that factors as a quasi-compact open covering $X'\to Y'$ followed by a proper surjective map $Y'\to Y$ of finite presentation.

**Definition 2.5.** Let $f: X\to Y$ be a proper surjective map of finite presentation between qcqs schemes. Then $f$ is of *inductive level $0$* if it can be refined into a composition of a proper fppf map $f': X'\to Y'$ and a finitely presented nilimmersion $Y'\hookrightarrow Y$.

For $n>0$, $f$ is of *inductive level $\le n$* if $f$ admits a refinement $f': X'\to Y$ that factors as $X'\to X_0\to Y_0\to Y$, where:
- (o) $Y_0\to Y$ is a finitely presented nilimmersion;
- (i) $X_0\to Y_0$ is proper surjective of finite presentation, and is an isomorphism outside a finitely presented closed subset $Z\subset Y_0$, such that $X_0\times_{Y_0}Z\to Z$ is of inductive level $\le n-1$;
- (ii) $X'\to X_0$ is a proper fppf cover.

**Lemma 2.6.** Let $f: X\to Y$ be a proper surjective map of finite presentation between qcqs schemes. Then $f$ is of inductive level $\le n$ for some $n\ge 0$.

**Definition 2.7.** The $h$-topology on $\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$ is generated by finitely presented $v$-covers.

**Proposition 2.8.** Let $F$ be a presheaf of sets on $\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$. Then $F$ is an $h$-sheaf if and only if:
- (i) $F$ is a sheaf for the fppf topology;
- (ii) for every affine $Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$ and every proper surjective map $X\to Y$ of finite presentation which is an isomorphism outside a finitely presented closed subset $Z\subset Y$ with preimage $E\subset X$, the square
$$\begin{array}{ccc} F(Y) & \to & F(X)\\ \downarrow && \downarrow\\ F(Z) & \to & F(E)\end{array}$$
is a pullback square.

**Theorem 2.9.** Let $F$ be a presheaf of spaces on $\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$. Then $F$ is an $h$-sheaf if and only if:
- (i) $F$ is a sheaf for the fppf topology;
- (ii) for every affine $Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$ of finite presentation over $S$, and every proper surjective map $X\to Y$ of finite presentation which is an isomorphism outside a finitely presented closed subset $Z\subset Y$ with preimage $E\subset X$, the same square as in Proposition 2.8 is a (homotopy) pullback square.

**Lemma 2.10** ([LZ12, Lemma 3.1.2]). Let $f: X\to Y$ and $g: Y\to Z$ be morphisms in $\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$, with composition $h=g\circ f$.
- (a) If $h$ is of universal $F$-descent, then $g$ is of universal $F$-descent.
- (b) If $f$ is of universal $F$-descent and $g$ is of $F$-descent, then $h$ is of $F$-descent.

In particular, if $f$ and $g$ are of universal $F$-descent, then $h$ is of universal $F$-descent.

**Corollary 2.11.** Let $F$ be a sheaf of abelian groups on $\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$. Assume that for every affine $Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$ of finite presentation over $S$, and every proper surjective map $X\to Y$ of finite presentation which is an isomorphism outside a finitely presented closed $Z\subset Y$ with preimage $E\subset X$, the triangle
$$R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(Y,F)\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(X,F)\oplus R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(Z,F)\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(E,F)$$
is distinguished in the derived category of abelian groups. Then for all $X\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$, $R\Gamma_h(X,F)=R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(X,F)$.

**Lemma 2.12.** Let $f: X=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\to Y=\mathrm{Spec}(B)$ be a $v$-cover in $\mathrm{Sch}_{/S}$. Then $f$ can be written as a cofiltered limit of $h$-covers $f_i: X_i=\mathrm{Spec}(A_i)\to Y_i=\mathrm{Spec}(B_i)$ with $X_i,Y_i\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$.

**Definition 2.13.** For a qcqs base $S$, the *$v$-topology* on $\mathrm{Sch}_{/S}$, the category of qcqs schemes over $S$, is the topology generated by $v$-covers.

**Corollary 2.14.** Let $F$ be a presheaf of $n$-truncated spaces on $\mathrm{Sch}_{/S}$ for some $n\ge 0$. Assume:
- (i) $F$ is a sheaf for the fppf topology;
- (ii) for every affine $Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\in\mathrm{Sch}_{/S}$ of finite presentation over $S$, and every proper surjective map $X\to Y$ of finite presentation which is an isomorphism outside a finitely presented closed $Z\subset Y$ with preimage $E\subset X$, the square of Proposition 2.8 is a homotopy pullback square;
- (iii) if $Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\in\mathrm{Sch}_{/S}$ is a cofiltered limit of finitely presented $Y_i=\mathrm{Spec}(A_i)\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$, then $\mathrm{colim}\,F(Y_i)\to F(Y)$ is a weak equivalence.

Then $F$ is a sheaf for the $v$-topology.

**Corollary 2.15.** Let $F$ be a sheaf of abelian groups on $\mathrm{Sch}_{/S}$. Assume:
- for every affine $Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$ of finite presentation over $S$, and every proper surjective map $X\to Y$ of finite presentation which is an isomorphism outside a finitely presented closed $Z\subset Y$ with preimage $E\subset X$, the triangle $R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(Y,F)\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(X,F)\oplus R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(Z,F)\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(E,F)$ is distinguished in the derived category of abelian groups;
- if $Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\in\mathrm{Sch}_{/S}$ is a cofiltered limit of finitely presented $Y_i=\mathrm{Spec}(A_i)\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/S}$, then $F(Y)=\mathrm{colim}\,F(Y_i)$.

Then for all $X\in\mathrm{Sch}_{/S}$, $R\Gamma_v(X,F)=R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(X,F)$.

## 3. Perfect schemes

**Definition 3.1.** A scheme $X$ over $\mathbf{F}_p$ is *perfect* if the Frobenius $\mathrm{Frob}_X: X\to X$ is an isomorphism.

**Definition 3.2.** Let $\mathrm{Perf}$ be the category of perfect qcqs schemes over $\mathbf{F}_p$, endowed with the $v$-topology generated by $v$-covers.

**Remark 3.3.** Choose an uncountable strong limit cardinal $\kappa$, i.e. for all cardinals $\lambda<\kappa$ also $2^\lambda<\kappa$. Replace $\mathrm{Perf}$ by the category of perfect qcqs schemes covered by $\mathrm{Spec}(A)$ with $|A|<\kappa$. Then $\mathrm{Perf}$ is essentially small, and all arguments go through in this truncated version.

**Footnote 5.** In Lemma 6.2 below, a "big" construction is used; it works in the truncated version because $\kappa$ is a strong limit cardinal. A more careful construction in Lemma 6.2 keeps the rings smaller and allows more general $\kappa$.

**Lemma 3.4.** Let $f: X\to Y$ be a morphism of (not necessarily qcqs) schemes over $\mathbf{F}_p$. The following properties hold for $f$ if and only if they hold for $f_{\mathrm{perf}}$:
- (i) quasicompact;
- (ii) quasiseparated;
- (iii) affine;
- (iv) separated;
- (v) integral;
- (vi) universally closed;
- (vii) a universal homeomorphism.

Moreover, if one of the following holds for $f$, then it also holds for $f_{\mathrm{perf}}$:
- (viii) a closed immersion;
- (ix) an open immersion;
- (x) an immersion;
- (xi) étale;
- (xii) (faithfully) flat.

**Lemma 3.5.** For any qcqs $\mathbf{F}_p$-scheme $X$, pullback along $X_{\mathrm{perf}}\to X$ induces $\mathrm{Pic}(X)[\tfrac{1}{p}]\simeq\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\mathrm{perf}})$.

**Lemma 3.6.** If $X\in\mathrm{Sch}_{/\mathbf{F}_p}$ and $\mathcal{L}$ is a line bundle on $X$, then $\mathcal{L}$ is ample if and only if its pullback to $X_{\mathrm{perf}}$ is ample.

**Theorem 3.7.** Let $X$ be any $\mathbf{F}_p$-scheme with perfection $X_{\mathrm{perf}}$. Then $Y\in X_{\text{ét}}\mapsto Y_{\mathrm{perf}}\in(X_{\mathrm{perf}})_{\text{ét}}$ is an equivalence of sites.

**Lemma 3.8.** If $f: X\to Y$ is a universal homeomorphism of perfect schemes, then $f$ is an isomorphism. In particular, if $f: X\to Y$ is a morphism of $\mathbf{F}_p$-schemes, then $f$ is a universal homeomorphism if and only if $f_{\mathrm{perf}}$ is an isomorphism.

**Remark 3.9.** A different proof of Lemma 3.8 is as follows. Let $A\to B$ be a map of perfect rings inducing a universal homeomorphism on spectra. By $v$-descent (Theorem 4.1), it suffices to show $A\to B$ is an isomorphism after $v$-localization. By Lemma 6.2, one may reduce to $A$ being w-local with all local rings valuation rings. Checking on local rings reduces to a valuation ring. There is then a unique section over the generic point, which by integrality extends to the valuation ring. The section $B\to A$ is surjective and still a universal homeomorphism on spectra, hence injective since $B$ is reduced. So $A=B$.

**Definition 3.10.** A map $g: B\to A$ of perfect rings is *perfectly finitely presented* if $A=(A_0)_{\mathrm{perf}}$ for some finitely presented $B$-algebra $A_0$.

**Proposition 3.11.** Let $f: X\to Y$ be a morphism in $\mathrm{Perf}$ (recall that $X$ and $Y$ are qcqs). The following are equivalent:
- (i) there is a covering of $X$ by open affines $\mathrm{Spec}(A_i)\subset X$ mapping into open affines $\mathrm{Spec}(B_i)\subset Y$ such that $B_i\to A_i$ is perfectly finitely presented;
- (ii) for any open affine $\mathrm{Spec}(A)\subset X$ mapping into an open affine $\mathrm{Spec}(B)\to Y$, the map $B\to A$ is perfectly finitely presented;
- (iii) for any cofiltered system $\{Z_i\}\in\mathrm{Perf}_{/Y}$ with affine transition maps and limit $Z=\lim Z_i\in\mathrm{Perf}_{/Y}$, the natural map $\mathrm{colim}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{/Y}(Z_i,X)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{/Y}(Z,X)$ is a bijection.

If these equivalent conditions hold, $f$ is called *perfectly finitely presented*.

**Footnote 6.** This includes the assumption that $X$ and $Y$ are qcqs.

**Proposition 3.12.** Let $X_i$ be a cofiltered inverse system in $\mathrm{Perf}$ with affine transition maps. Then the limit $X=\lim X_i$ exists in $\mathrm{Perf}$ and agrees with the limit taken in schemes. The natural pullback functor
$$2\text{-}\varinjlim_i \mathrm{Perf}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/X_i}\to\mathrm{Perf}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{/X}$$
is an equivalence.

**Footnote 7.** All objects of $\mathrm{Perf}$ are assumed qcqs.

**Proposition 3.13.** Let $f: X\to Y$ be a perfectly finitely presented morphism in $\mathrm{Perf}$. Then there exists a finitely presented morphism of schemes $f_0: X_0\to Y$ such that $f=(f_0)_{\mathrm{perf}}$.

**Definition 3.14.** A perfectly finitely presented map $f: X\to Y$ in $\mathrm{Perf}$ is called *proper* if it is separated and universally closed.

**Corollary 3.15.** Let $f: X\to Y$ be a perfectly finitely presented map in $\mathrm{Perf}$, with a model $f_0: X_0\to Y$. Then $f$ is proper if and only if $f_0$ is proper.

**Lemma 3.16.** Let $C\leftarrow A\to B$ be a diagram of perfect rings. Then $\mathrm{Tor}_i^A(B,C)=0$ for all $i>0$.

**Remark 3.17.** The proof of Lemma 3.16 shows that any quotient $R/I$ of a perfect ring $R$ by the radical $I$ of a finitely generated ideal $I_0$ has finite $\mathrm{Tor}$-dimension as an $R$-module. Proposition 11.31 extends this to non-reduced quotients under a mild finite presentation constraint.

**Lemma 3.18.** Let
$$\begin{array}{ccc} X' & \xrightarrow{g'} & X\\ \downarrow{\scriptstyle f'} && \downarrow{\scriptstyle f}\\ Y' & \xrightarrow{g} & Y\end{array}$$
be a pullback diagram in $\mathrm{Perf}$. Then for any $K\in D_{qc}(X)$, the base-change morphism $Lg^*Rf_*K\to Rf'_*Lg'^*K$ is a quasi-isomorphism.

## 4. Descent for vector bundles

**Theorem 4.1.**
- (i) If $X=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\in\mathrm{Perf}$ is affine and $\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(W_n(X))$ (resp. $\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(W(X))$) corresponds to a finite projective $W_n(A)$-module (resp. $W(A)$-module) $M$, then $M=R\Gamma_v(X,\mathcal{E})$.
- (ii) For each $n$, the functor $X\mapsto\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(W_n(X))$ on $\mathrm{Perf}$ is a $v$-stack. Consequently, $X\mapsto\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(W(X))$ is a $v$-stack.

**Remark 4.2.** Theorem 4.1 implies that the $v$-topology on $\mathrm{Perf}$ is subcanonical. Lemma 6.3 provides covers in the canonical topology that are not $v$-covers.

**Remark 4.3.** Theorem 4.1 does not imply descent for standard properties of morphisms in the $h$-topology. For example, the inclusion
$$j: U:=(\mathbf{A}^2-\{0\})_{\mathrm{perf}}\hookrightarrow X:=\mathbf{A}^2_{\mathrm{perf}}$$
is not affine, but becomes affine after base change to the blow-up $\pi: Y:=(\mathrm{Bl}_0 X)_{\mathrm{perf}}\to X$ at $0$. Pullback along $\pi$ is not exact. Likewise, $h$-descent data for flat quasi-coherent sheaves need not be effective: the pullback of $Rj_*\mathcal{O}_U$ along $\pi$ is a flat quasi-coherent sheaf on $Y$ with descent data along $\pi$ that does not come from a flat quasi-coherent sheaf on $X$.

**Corollary 4.4.** Fix a perfect qcqs scheme $X$ and $\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2\in\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(W(X))$. Then the functor $(f:Y\to X)\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}(f^*\mathcal{E}_1,f^*\mathcal{E}_2)$ is a $v$-sheaf on $\mathrm{Perf}_{/X}$.

**Proposition 4.5.** Let $F$ be a presheaf of spaces on $\mathrm{Perf}$, and let $F'$ be the presheaf of spaces on $\mathrm{Sch}_{/\mathbf{F}_p}$ defined by $F'(X)=F(X_{\mathrm{perf}})$. Then $F$ is a $v$-sheaf if and only if $F'$ is a $v$-sheaf.

**Lemma 4.6.** Let $f: X\to Y$ be a proper map of noetherian $\mathbf{F}_p$-schemes that is an isomorphism outside a closed subset $Z\subset Y$ with preimage $E\subset X$.
- (i) If $\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(Y_{\mathrm{perf}})$, then the triangle
$$R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(Y,\mathcal{E})\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(X,\mathcal{E})\oplus R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(Z,\mathcal{E})\to R\Gamma_{\mathrm{fppf}}(E,\mathcal{E})$$
is distinguished.
- (ii) Pullback gives an equivalence of groupoids
$$\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(Y_{\mathrm{perf}})\simeq\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(X_{\mathrm{perf}})\times_{\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(E_{\mathrm{perf}})}\mathcal{V}\mathrm{ect}(Z_{\mathrm{perf}}).$$

## 5. Graded line bundles

**Construction 5.1.** For any scheme $X$, let $\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ic}^{\mathbf{Z}}(X)$ be the groupoid of graded line bundles on $X$. An object is a pair $(L,f)$, where $L$ is a line bundle on $X$ and $f: X\to\mathbf{Z}$ is locally constant. The morphisms satisfy $\mathrm{Isom}((L,f),(M,g))=\emptyset$ if $f\ne g$, and $\mathrm{Isom}((L,f),(M,g))=\mathrm{Isom}(L,M)$ otherwise. This groupoid is symmetric monoidal, with
$$(L,f)\otimes(M,g):=(L\otimes M,\ f+g),$$
and commutativity constraint $(L\otimes M,f+g)\simeq(M\otimes L,g+f)$.
