# Restatement

## Abstract

The paper develops the deformation theory of instantons on asymptotically conical $G_2$-manifolds, in which an asymptotic connection at infinity is fixed. A spinorial approach relates the space of deformations to the kernel of a twisted Dirac operator on the $G_2$-manifold and to the eigenvalues of a twisted Dirac operator on the nearly Kähler link. The framework is used to calculate the virtual dimension of the moduli spaces of $G_2$-instantons on several known examples, including the Günaydin–Nicolai $G_2$-instanton on $\mathbb{R}^7$. Knowledge of the virtual dimension is used to show that unobstructed connections in the moduli space are $G_2$-invariant. By classifying such connections, the paper proves a uniqueness result for unobstructed $G_2$-instantons on the principal $G_2$-bundle over $\mathbb{R}^7$.

## Introduction (mathematical content)

- Instantons are connections whose curvature satisfies an algebraic equation. On a $G_2$-manifold, $G_2$-instantons automatically satisfy the Yang–Mills equation. On a compact manifold, $G_2$-instantons are absolute minima of the Yang–Mills energy functional.
- The first example of a $G_2$-instanton, on the principal $G_2$-bundle over $\mathbb{R}^7$, was constructed by Günaydin–Nicolai [24]. It is referred to as the standard $G_2$-instanton.
- Further examples: Clarke [11] found a family of instanton connections on $\not{S}(S^3)$, which carries a $G_2$-metric by Bryant–Salamon [8]. Oliveira [35] constructed instantons on $\Lambda^2_-(S^4)$ and $\Lambda^2_-(\mathbb{C}P^2)$. Lotay–Oliveira [31] studied moduli spaces of $G_2$-invariant instantons on non-compact $G_2$-manifolds.
- The instantons in these examples are defined on asymptotically conical manifolds and converge to instanton connections on the nearly Kähler 6-manifold at infinity.
- The deformation space is the kernel of an elliptic operator. The analytic framework of Lockhart–McOwen [28] and Marshall [32] applies, and determines when elliptic operators on non-compact manifolds are Fredholm, so that a Kuranishi model can be used.
- Nakajima [34] used a similar analysis for ASD-instantons on asymptotically locally Euclidean manifolds with a fixed flat connection at infinity.
- The deformation theory of nearly Kähler instantons was developed by Charbonneau–Harland [9].
- The paper proves that the dimension of the space of solutions to the linearised $G_2$-instanton equation is determined by the spectrum of a twisted Dirac operator on a nearly Kähler 6-manifold (Section 4). The implicit function theorem then shows that the moduli space is smooth when the deformation theory is unobstructed.
- Section 5 develops methods for eigenvalues of Dirac operators on homogeneous nearly Kähler manifolds with twisting by the canonical connection, and gives a Lichnerowicz-type formula reducing the virtual dimension to eigenvalues of Hermitian matrices.
- Sections 6 and 7 compute the virtual dimension for Clarke's instantons, the Lotay–Oliveira instanton (on $\mathbb{R}^4\times S^3$), Oliveira's instantons, and the Günaydin–Nicolai instanton (on $\mathbb{R}^7$). A formula for the twisted Dirac operator on $S^6$ is presented.
- Section 8 proves that unobstructed connections are invariant under an action of $G_2$, and classifies them using the algebraic framework of Wang's theorem [37].

## Section 2: Spinors, $G_2$- and nearly Kähler structures

**Definition 2.1.** A $G_2$-manifold is a 7-manifold $M$ together with a $G_2$-structure $\varphi$ such that $\nabla\varphi=0$, where $\nabla$ is the Levi-Civita connection of the metric determined by $\varphi$. Such a $G_2$-structure is called *torsion free*.

**Theorem 2.2.** Let $(M,\varphi)$ be a $G_2$-structure manifold. Then the following are equivalent:
1. $\nabla\varphi=0$
2. $\mathrm{Hol}(g_\varphi)\subseteq G_2$
3. $d\varphi=d\psi=0$.

**Lemma 2.3.** The 3-form $\varphi$ and 4-form $\psi$ act on the subspaces of $\not{S}(M)$ isomorphic to $\Lambda^0$ and $\Lambda^1$ with the following eigenvalues:

| | $\Lambda^0$ | $\Lambda^1$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\varphi$ | $-7$ | $1$ |
| $\psi$ | $-7$ | $1$ |

**Remark 2.4.** If $\mathrm{Vol}_7$ is instead chosen to act as $-1$, the eigenvalues of $\varphi$ differ from those in Lemma 2.3 by a minus sign. The eigenvalues of $\psi$ are independent of this choice.

**Corollary 2.5.** For $\alpha\in\Omega^2(M)$,
$$\alpha\cdot s_7=*(\alpha\wedge\psi)\cdot s_7.$$

**Corollary 2.6.** Let $f\in\Omega^0(M)$ and $u,v\in\Omega^1(M)$. Clifford multiplication of the spinor $(f+v)\cdot s_7$ by $u$ is
$$\mathrm{cl}(u)(f+v)\cdot s_7=\big(-u\lrcorner v+fu+*(u\wedge v\wedge\psi)\big)\cdot s_7.$$

**Definition 2.7.** A 6-manifold $(\Sigma,g)$ together with a real Killing spinor $s_6\in\Gamma(\not{S}(\Sigma))$ is called a *nearly Kähler 6-manifold*.

**Lemma 2.8 ([9, Lemma 2]).** The subspaces of $\not{S}(\Sigma)$ isomorphic to $\Lambda^0$, $\Lambda^1$ and $\Lambda^6$ are eigenspaces of Clifford multiplication by $\mathrm{Re}\,\Omega$ and $*\omega$, with eigenvalues:

| | $\Lambda^0$ | $\Lambda^1$ | $\Lambda^6$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathrm{Re}\,\Omega$ | $4$ | $0$ | $-4$ |
| $*\omega$ | $-3$ | $1$ | $-3$ |

**Lemma 2.9.** For any $\alpha\in\Omega^2(\Sigma)$,
$$\alpha\cdot s_6=\big(-(\alpha\lrcorner\omega)\mathrm{Vol}_6-\alpha\lrcorner\mathrm{Re}\,\Omega\big)\cdot s_6. \tag{23}$$

**Corollary 2.10.** Clifford multiplication of a spinor $(f+v+h\,\mathrm{Vol}_6)\cdot s_6$ by a 1-form $u\in\Omega^1(\Sigma)$ is
$$(u)(f+v+h\,\mathrm{Vol}_6)\cdot s_6=\big(-u\lrcorner v+fu-hJu-(u\wedge v)\lrcorner\mathrm{Re}\,\Omega-(u\lrcorner Jv)\mathrm{Vol}_6\big)\cdot s_6. \tag{24}$$

**Corollary 2.11.** Under the splitting $\not{S}(\Sigma)\cong\Lambda^0\oplus\Lambda^1\oplus\Lambda^6$, the Dirac operator is
$$D^t=\begin{pmatrix}-3+3t&d^*&0\\ d&2-(d\cdot)\lrcorner\mathrm{Re}\,\Omega&Jd*\\ 0&*\langle d\cdot,\omega\rangle&3-3t\end{pmatrix}. \tag{26}$$

## Section 3: Asymptotically conical $G_2$-manifolds

**Definition 3.1.** Let $(\Sigma^6,g_6)$ be nearly Kähler. The $G_2$ cone of $\Sigma$ is $C(\Sigma)=(0,\infty)\times\Sigma$ with the torsion-free $G_2$-structure $(C(\Sigma),\varphi_C)$ defined by
$$\varphi_C=r^2\,\omega\wedge dr+r^3\,\mathrm{Im}\,\Omega,$$
where $r$ is the coordinate on $(0,\infty)$. The metric determined by $\varphi_C$ is the cone metric $g_C=dr^2+r^2g_6$. The orientation is chosen so that $dr\wedge r^6\mathrm{Vol}_6$ is the volume form on $C$. $C$ is called a $G_2$ cone and $\Sigma$ the *link* of the cone. The natural projection is $\pi\colon C(\Sigma)\to\Sigma$.

**Definition 3.2.** Let $(M,g,\varphi)$ be a non-compact $G_2$-manifold. $M$ is an *asymptotically conical (AC) $G_2$-manifold with rate $\mu<0$* if there exist a compact subset $K\subset M$, a compact connected nearly Kähler 6-manifold $\Sigma$, a constant $R>1$, and a diffeomorphism $h\colon(R,\infty)\times\Sigma\to M\setminus K$ such that
$$\big|\nabla_C^j\big(h^*(\varphi|_{M\setminus K})-\varphi_C\big)\big|(r,\sigma)=O(r^{\mu-j})\quad\text{as } r\to\infty \tag{27}$$
for each $\sigma\in\Sigma$ and $j=0,1,2,\ldots$, where $\nabla_C$ is the Levi-Civita connection of $g_C$, $\varphi_C$ is the $G_2$-structure on the cone, and $|\cdot|$ is computed using $g_C$. $M\setminus K$ is called the *end* of $M$ and $\Sigma$ the *asymptotic link* of $M$.

**Remark 3.3.** The norm in (27) is always understood pointwise in $\Sigma$, and the dependence on a point of $\Sigma$ is often dropped.

**Example 3.4.**
1. $(\mathbb{R}^7,\varphi_0)$ is an AC $G_2$-manifold with any rate $\mu<0$, since $C(S^6)=\mathbb{R}^7\setminus\{0\}$. In this convention $\varphi_C=\varphi_0$ and the induced metric is Euclidean.
2. The total space of $\not{S}(S^3)$, the spin bundle of $S^3$, carries a metric of $G_2$ holonomy, due to Bryant and Salamon [6]. It has cohomogeneity one, and the $G_2$-structure is AC with asymptotic link $\Sigma=S^3\times S^3$ and rate $-3$. Bryant–Salamon also give $G_2$-holonomy metrics on $\Lambda^2_-S^4$ and $\Lambda^2_-\mathbb{CP}^2$, with asymptotic links $\mathbb{CP}^3$ and $F_{1,2,3}$ respectively, and rate $-4$ in both cases.
3. Foscolo, Haskins and Nordström [20] find infinitely many new diffeomorphism types of AC $G_2$-manifolds with asymptotic link (a quotient of) $S^3\times S^3$.

**Definition 3.5.** A *radius function* $\rho$ on $M$ is a smooth function satisfying: on the compact set $K$, $\rho=1$. For $x\in M_{2R}$ with $h^{-1}(x)=(r,y)$, where $r\in(2R,\infty)$, $\rho(x)=r$. On $h((R,2R)\times\Sigma)$, $\rho$ interpolates smoothly and decreasingly between these two definitions.

**Definition 3.6.** Let $p\ge1$, $k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$ and $\mu\in\mathbb{R}$. Let $(M,\varphi)$ be an AC $G_2$-manifold with a fixed radius function $\rho$. Let $P\to M$ be a principal bundle with connection $A$, and let $T$ be a tensor or spinor bundle with induced metric and Levi-Civita connection. For $L^k_{\text{loc}}$ sections $\eta$ of $T\otimes\mathrm{Ad}P$, define
$$\|\eta\|_{L^p_{k,\mu}}=\left(\sum_{j=0}^k\int_M\big|\rho^{j-\mu}\nabla^j\eta\big|^p\rho^{-7}\,dV_g\right)^{1/p},$$
where $|\cdot|$ is computed using $g$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}$, and $\nabla=\nabla^{LC}\otimes\nabla^A$. $L^p_{k,\mu}(T\otimes\mathrm{Ad}P)$ denotes the completion under this norm.

**Definition 3.7.** Let $\mu\in\mathbb{R}$ and $k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$. The weighted $C^k$ space $C^k_\mu(E)$ is the subspace of $C^k_{\text{loc}}(E)$ for which
$$\|\eta\|_{C^k_\mu}=\sum_{j=0}^k\sup_M|\rho^{j-\mu}\nabla^j\eta|$$
is finite. Also $C^\infty_\mu(E)=\bigcap_{k\ge0}C^k_\mu(E)$. The spaces $C^k_\mu$ are Banach spaces, but $C^\infty_\mu$ need not be.

**Definition 3.8.** Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$, $k\in\mathbb{N}$, $\mu\in\mathbb{R}$. Let $d(x,y)$ be the geodesic distance, let $0<c_1<1<c_2$ be constants, and let
$$H=\{(x,y)\in M\times M;\ x\ne y,\ c_1\rho(x)\le\rho(y)\le c_2\rho(x),\ \text{and there exists a geodesic in $M$ of length $d(x,y)$ from $x$ to $y$}\}.$$
A section $\eta$ of a vector bundle $E$ with fibre metric and metric connection $\nabla$ is *Hölder continuous with exponent $\alpha$* if
$$[\eta]^\alpha=\sup_{(x,y)\in H}\frac{|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|_E}{d(x,y)^\alpha}<\infty,$$
where $\nabla$ identifies fibres by parallel transport along a geodesic joining $x$ and $y$. The weighted Hölder space $C^{k,\alpha}_\mu(E)$ is the subspace of $C^{k,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}(E)$ for which
$$\|\eta\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_\mu}=\|\eta\|_{C^k_\mu}+[\eta]^{k,\alpha}_\mu$$
is finite, where $[\eta]^{k,\alpha}_\mu=[\rho^{k+\alpha-\mu}\nabla^k\eta]^\alpha$.

**Theorem 3.9 (Weighted Sobolev Embedding).** Let $k,l\ge0$ and $\alpha\in(0,1)$.
1. If $k-\frac{7}{p}\ge l+\alpha$, there is a continuous embedding $L^p_{k,\mu}(E)\hookrightarrow C^{l,\alpha}_\mu(E)$.
2. If $k\ge l\ge0$, $k-\frac{7}{p}\ge l-\frac{7}{q}$, $p\le q$ and $\mu\le\nu$, there is a continuous embedding $L^p_{k,\mu}(E)\hookrightarrow L^q_{l,\nu}(E)$.

**Theorem 3.10 (Weighted Sobolev Multiplication).** Let $\xi\in L^2_{k,\mu}(E)$, $\eta\in L^2_{l,\nu}(F)$, with $l\ge k$. If $k>\frac72$, then multiplication
$$L^2_{k,\mu}(E)\times L^2_{l,\nu}(F)\hookrightarrow L^2_{k,\mu+\nu}(E\otimes F)$$
is bounded: there is $C>0$ with $\|\xi\otimes\eta\|_{k,\mu+\nu}\le C\|\xi\|_{k,\mu}\|\eta\|_{l,\nu}$.

## Section 4: Instantons and deformation theory

**Example 4.1.**
- In [24] an instanton $A_{\text{std}}$ on the trivial $G_2$-bundle over $\mathbb{R}^7$ is constructed. It decays to the canonical connection on the trivial $G_2$-bundle over $S^6$. It is called the "standard" instanton and is treated in Section 7.
- Nearly Kähler instantons have been constructed on the Bryant–Salamon manifolds by Clarke [11], Oliveira [35] and Lotay–Oliveira [31]. All such examples decay to the canonical connection on an appropriate bundle. A limiting connection of Clarke's family was constructed in [31], and it decays faster than Clarke's examples.

**Definition 4.2.** Let $M$ be an AC $G_2$-manifold with asymptotic cone $\Sigma$, and $P\to M$ a principal bundle. $P$ is *asymptotically framed* if there is a principal bundle $Q\to\Sigma$ with $h^*P\cong\pi^*Q$.

**Remark 4.3.** Such a framing always exists [31], so fixing a framing loses no generality. This condition is slightly more general than the setup of Taubes [36], where $Q$ is assumed trivial. In that case Taubes notes that if $G$ is simple and simply connected, $P$ must also be trivial.

**Definition 4.4.** Let $M$ be an asymptotically conical manifold. A connection $A$ on an asymptotically framed bundle $P\to M$ is *asymptotically conical with rate $\mu$* if there is a connection $A_\infty$ on $Q\to\Sigma$ such that, with $A_C=\pi^*(A_\infty)$,
$$\big|\nabla_C^j\big(h^*(A|_{M_\infty})-A_C\big)\big|_C=O(r^{\mu-1-j}) \tag{35}$$
for all non-negative integers $j$, for some $\mu<0$, where $\nabla_C$ is a combination of the Levi-Civita connection of the cone metric and $A_C$, and $|\cdot|_C$ is the norm induced by the cone metric and the metric on $\mathfrak{g}$. The *fastest rate of convergence* of $A$ is
$$\mu_0=\inf\{\mu;\ A\text{ is asymptotically conical with rate }\mu\}.$$

**Lemma 4.5.** The sets $\mathcal{G}_{k+1,\mu}$ are Hilbert Lie groups with Lie algebra $\Omega^0_{k+1,\mu}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)$ for $k\ge3$. The group $\mathcal{G}_{k+1,\mu}$ acts smoothly on $\mathcal{A}_{k,\mu-1}$ by gauge transformations when $k\ge4$.

**Definition 4.6.** Let $M$ be an AC $G_2$-manifold with asymptotic link $\Sigma$, $P\to M$ a bundle asymptotically framed by $Q\to\Sigma$, and $A_\infty$ a nearly Kähler instanton on $Q$. The moduli space of $G_2$-instantons asymptotic to $A_\infty$ with rate $\mu$ is
$$\mathcal{M}(A_\infty,\mu)=\{G_2\text{-instantons } A \text{ on } P \text{ satisfying (35)}\}/\mathcal{G}_\mu. \tag{37}$$

**Remark 4.7.** By [35, Proposition 3], an asymptotically conical $G_2$-instanton $A$ has connection at infinity $A_\infty$ a nearly Kähler instanton.

**Theorem 4.8.** Let $P\colon C^\infty_c(E)\to C^\infty_c(F)$ be a smooth uniformly elliptic, asymptotically conical operator of rate $\gamma$ and order $l\ge1$. Suppose $\eta\in L^1_{\text{loc}}(F)$ and $\xi\in L^1_{\text{loc}}(E)$ is a weak solution of $P\xi=\eta$. If $\xi\in L^p_{0,\beta+\gamma}(E)$ and $\eta\in L^p_{k,\beta}(F)$, then $\xi\in L^p_{k+l,\beta+\gamma}(E)$ and
$$\|\xi\|_{L^p_{k+l,\beta+\gamma}(E)}\le C\left(\|\eta\|_{L^p_{k,\beta}(F)}+\|\xi\|_{L^p_{0,\beta+\gamma}(E)}\right),$$
where $C>0$ is independent of $\xi$.

**Definition 4.9.** Let $C$ be a $G_2$ cone with asymptotic link $\Sigma$ and $A_C=\pi^*(A_\infty)$. Let $P_C$ be either the twisted Dirac operator $D_{A_C}$ or the coupled Laplacian $d^*_{A_C}d_{A_C}$, acting on sections of $E=\not{S}(C)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}(\pi^*Q)$ and $E=\mathrm{Ad}(\pi^*Q)$ respectively. The set of critical weights of $P_C$ is
$$W_{\text{crit}}(P_C)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R};\ \exists\text{ a non-zero homogeneous order $\lambda$ section $\eta$ of $E$ with } P_C(\eta)=0\}. \tag{41}$$

**Theorem 4.10.** Let $E$ and $P$ be as above. The extension $P\colon L^p_{k+l,\mu}(E)\to L^p_{k,\mu-\nu}(F)$ is Fredholm whenever $\mu\in\mathbb{R}\setminus W_{\text{crit}}(P_C)$. If $[\mu,\mu']\cap W_{\text{crit}}(P_C)=\emptyset$, then $\text{Ker}P_\mu=\text{Ker}P_{\mu'}$.

**Theorem 4.11.** Let $P$ be an AC uniformly elliptic operator of order $l$ and rate $\nu$, and suppose $\mu\notin W_{\text{crit}}(P_C)$, so that $P\colon L^2_{k+l,\mu}(E)\to L^2_{k,\mu-\nu}(F)$ is Fredholm. Then:
1. There exists a finite-dimensional subspace $\mathcal{O}_{\mu-\nu}$ of $L^2_{k,\mu-\nu}$ such that
$$L^2_{k,\mu-\nu}(F)=P(L^2_{k+l,\mu}(E))\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mu-\nu} \tag{44}$$
and
$$\mathcal{O}_{\mu-\nu}\cong\text{Ker}P^*_{-7-\mu+\nu}. \tag{45}$$
2. If $\mu>-\frac72+\nu$, then $\mathcal{O}_{\mu-\nu}=\text{Ker}P^*_{-7-\mu+\nu}$.
3. The image of $P$ is
$$P(L^2_{k+l,\mu}(E))=\left\{\eta\in L^2_{k,\mu-\nu}(F);\ \langle\eta,\kappa\rangle_{L^2(F)}=0\text{ for all }\kappa\in\text{Ker}(P^*)_{-7-\mu+\nu}\right\}. \tag{46}$$

**Proposition 4.12.** Let $A$ be an asymptotically conical $G_2$-instanton. If $\mu\in(-5,0)$, then
$$d_A^*d_A\colon\Omega^0_{k+2,\mu}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\to\Omega^0_{k,\mu-2}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)$$
is Fredholm.

**Remark 4.13.** All known examples of AC $G_2$-instantons converge at a rate in this interval.

**Lemma 4.14.** Let $\xi\in\Omega^{m-1}_{k,\mu}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)$ and $\eta\in\Omega^m_{l,\nu}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)$. If $k,l\ge4$ and $\mu+\nu<-6$, then
$$\langle d_A\xi,\eta\rangle_{L^2}=\langle\xi,d_A^*\eta\rangle_{L^2}.$$

**Corollary 4.15.** Let $f\in\text{Ker}(d_A^*d_A)_\mu$. If $\mu<0$, then $d_Af=0$.

**Lemma 4.16.** Let $(M,g)$ be an asymptotically conical manifold. If $\mu<0$, then $\text{Ker}(\Delta)_\mu=\{0\}$.

**Corollary 4.17.** Let $f\in\text{Ker}(d_A^*d_A)_\mu$. If $\mu<0$, then $f=0$.

**Corollary 4.18.** If $\mu<0$ and $(f,A)\in\Omega^0_{k+1,\mu}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\oplus\mathcal{A}$ satisfies the $G_2$-monopole equation $d_Af+*(\psi\wedge F_A)=0$, then $f=0$.

**Corollary 4.19.** Let $A$ be an asymptotically conical $G_2$-instanton. If $\mu<0$ and $(f,a)\in L^2_{k+1,\mu}((\Lambda^0\oplus\Lambda^1)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}P)$ satisfies the linearised $G_2$-monopole equation $d_Af+*(\psi\wedge d_Aa)=0$, then $f=0$.

**Lemma 4.20.** Let $(M,g)$ be an asymptotically conical $G_2$-manifold and $A$ an asymptotically conical connection on a principal bundle $P\to M$. If $-5<\mu<0$, then
$$d_A^*d_A\colon\Omega^0_{k+2,\mu}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\to\Omega^0_{k,\mu-2}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)$$
is an isomorphism of topological vector spaces.

**Lemma 4.21.** Let $T\colon X\to Y$ be a bounded linear operator between Banach spaces, so that $\text{Ker}T$ is closed and admits a closed complement. A closed subspace $X_0\subset X$ is a complement to $\text{Ker}T$ if and only if
1. $T|_{X_0}$ is injective, and
2. $T(X)=T(X_0)$.

**Lemma 4.22.** The operator
$$d_A\colon\Omega^0_{k+2,\mu}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\to\Omega^1_{k+1,\mu-1}(M,\mathrm{Ad})$$
has closed image.

**Theorem 4.23 (Slice Theorem).** Let $-5<\mu<0$. Then
$$\Omega^1_{k+1,\mu-1}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)=\text{Ker}\big(d_A^*\colon\Omega^1_{k+1,\mu-1}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\to\Omega^0_{k,\mu-2}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\big)\oplus d_A\big(\Omega^0_{k+2,\mu}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\big). \tag{47}$$

**Corollary 4.24.** Let $P\to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle with $G$ semisimple. If $-5<\mu<0$, then the moduli space $\mathcal{B}_{k+1,\mu}=\mathcal{A}_{k+1,\mu-1}/\mathcal{G}_{k+2,\mu}$ is a smooth manifold, and the sets $T_{A,\mu,\epsilon}$ provide charts near $[A]$.

**Proposition 4.25.** Let $P\to M$ be a principal $G$-bundle with $G$ a compact Lie group. Let $A$ be an $L^2_{k+1,\mu-1}$ connection on $P$ for $\mu\in(-5,0)$. There is a constant $c(A)>0$ such that if $B$ is another $L^2_{k+1,\mu-1}$ connection on $P$ and $a=B-A$ satisfies $\|a\|_{L^2_{4,\mu-1}}<c(A)$, then there is a gauge transformation $g\in\mathcal{G}_{k+2,\mu}$ such that $u(B)$ is in Coulomb gauge relative to $A$.

**Proposition 4.26.** Let $k\ge4$ and $-5<\mu<0$. Then the natural inclusion $\mathcal{M}(A_\infty,\mu)_{k+1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}(A_\infty,\mu)_k$ is a homeomorphism.

**Corollary 4.27.** Let $A$ be an AC $G_2$-instanton asymptotic to $A_\infty$ with rate $\mu$, where $-5<\mu<0$. The zero set of the operator
$$\mathcal{D}_A\colon L^2_{k+1,\mu-1}(\not{S}(M)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}P)\to L^2_{k,\mu-2}(\not{S}(M)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}P)$$
is independent of $k\ge3$, and a neighbourhood of $A$ in $\mathcal{M}(A_\infty,\mu)$ is homeomorphic to a neighbourhood of $0$ in $(\mathcal{D}_A)^{-1}_{\mu-1}(0)$.

**Remark 4.28.** The above calculation generalises easily to an $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold $(X^n,g)$ and its $(n+1)$-dimensional cone $(C(X),dr^2+r^2g)$.

**Proposition 4.29.** The set of critical weights of the twisted Dirac operator $D_A$ is
$$W_{\text{crit}}(D_{A_C})=\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}\colon\mu+3\in\mathrm{Spec}\,D^0_{A_\infty}\},$$
where $D^0_{A_\infty}$ is the Dirac operator on the link twisted by $A_\infty$. This set is discrete, unbounded and symmetric about $-3$. The operator $(D_A)_\mu$ is Fredholm whenever $\mu+3\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathrm{Spec}\,D^0_{A_\infty}$.

**Remark 4.30.** In [9] Charbonneau and Harland show that the linearised nearly Kähler instanton equation on the link $\Sigma$ is solved by one-forms $a$ satisfying $D_{A_\infty}(a\cdot s)=2\,a\cdot s$. Thus rate-0 deformations on the cone $C(\Sigma)$ may be regarded as deformations of the nearly Kähler instanton $A_\infty$.

**Definition 4.31.** For $\mu\in\mathbb{R}$, define
$$\mathcal{K}(\mu)=\left\{\eta_C\in\text{Ker}D_{A_C};\ \eta_C(r,\sigma)=r^{\mu-1}\sum_{j=0}^m(\log r)^j\eta_j(\sigma),\ \text{each }\eta_j\text{ a section of }\not{S}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}Q\right\}.$$
That is, $\mathcal{K}(\mu)$ consists of sections in the kernel of $D_{A_C}$ that are polynomials in $\log r$ whose coefficients are homogeneous of order $\mu$ spinors.

**Theorem 4.32.** Let $\mathrm{ind}_\mu$ denote the index of
$$D_A\colon L^2_{k+1,\mu-1}(\not{S}(M)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}P)\to L^2_{k,\mu-2}(\not{S}(M)\otimes\mathrm{Ad}P).$$
If $\mu,\mu'\in\mathbb{R}\setminus W$ satisfy $\mu\le\mu'$, then
$$\mathrm{ind}_{\mu'}D_A-\mathrm{ind}_\mu D_A=\sum_{\nu\in W\cap(\mu,\mu')}k(\nu).$$

**Remark 4.33.** Being self-adjoint, the operator $(D_A)_{-2}$ has index zero, since the dual of the target space has the same weight as the domain. Hence, when $D^0_{A_\infty}$ has non-trivial kernel,
$$\mathrm{ind}(D_A)_{-2+\epsilon}=\tfrac12\dim\text{Ker}D^0_{A_\infty}$$
for $\epsilon$ sufficiently small that $[-2,-2+\epsilon)$ contains no other critical weights. In this situation the virtual dimension is negative for all rates $\mu<-2$.

**Proposition 4.34.** Suppose $D_{A_C}\left(r^{\mu-1}\sum_{j=0}^m(\log r)^jv_j(\sigma)\right)=0$. Then $m=0$.

**Definition 4.35.** For a weight $\mu<0$, the rate-$\mu$ infinitesimal deformation space is
$$\mathcal{I}(A,\mu)\coloneqq\left\{(f,a)\in\Omega^0_{k+1,\mu-1}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\oplus\Omega^1_{k+1,\mu-1}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P);\ D_A(f,a)=0\right\}.$$
By AC uniform elliptic regularity this is independent of $k$ and is finite-dimensional.

**Remark 4.36.** Deformations of $G_2$-instantons can also be described by an elliptic complex. In the weighted setting it is
$$\Omega^0_\mu(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\xrightarrow{\ d_A\ }\Omega^1_{\mu-1}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\xrightarrow{\ d_A\ }\Omega^6_{\mu-2}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\xrightarrow{\ \psi\wedge d_A\ }\Omega^7_{\mu-3}(M,\mathrm{Ad}P)\xrightarrow{\ d_A\ }\cdots$$
Denote the cohomology groups of this complex by $H^k_{A,\mu}$. The zeroth cohomology group is trivial, $H^0_{A,\mu}=\{0\}$, while $H^1_{A,\mu}\cong\mathcal{I}(A,\mu)$ and $H^2_{A,\mu}\cong\mathcal{O}(A,\mu)$ provided $-5<\mu<0$.

**Theorem 4.37.** Let $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$ be Banach spaces and $\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{X}$ an open neighbourhood of $0$. Let $\mathcal{F}\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{Y}$ be a $C^k$ map, $k\ge1$, with $\mathcal{F}(0)=0$. Suppose $d\mathcal{F}|_0\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}$ is surjective with kernel $\mathcal{K}$ such that $\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{K}\oplus\mathcal{Z}$ for some closed subspace $\mathcal{Z}$. Then there exist open sets $\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{K}$ and $\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{Z}$, both containing $0$, with $\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{U}$, and a unique $C^k$ map $\mathcal{G}\colon\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{W}$ such that
$$\mathcal{F}^{-1}(0)\cap(\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{W})=\{(x,\mathcal{G}(x))\colon x\in\mathcal{V}\}.$$

**Theorem 4.38.** Let $A_\infty$ be a nearly Kähler instanton and $A$ an AC $G_2$-instanton converging to $A_\infty$. Suppose $\mu\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus W)\cap(-5,0)$. There exists a smooth manifold $\hat{\mathcal{M}}(A,\mu)$, which is an open neighbourhood of $0$ in $\mathcal{I}(A,\mu)$, and a smooth map $\pi\colon\hat{\mathcal{M}}(A,\mu)\to\mathcal{O}(A,\mu)$ with $\pi(0)=0$, such that an open neighbourhood of $0$ in $\pi^{-1}(0)$ is homeomorphic to a neighbourhood of $A$ in $\mathcal{M}(A_\infty,\mu)$. Thus the virtual dimension of the moduli space is
$$\dim\mathcal{I}(A,\mu)-\dim\mathcal{O}(A,\mu),$$
and $\mathcal{M}(A_\infty,\mu)$ is smooth if $\mathcal{O}(A,\mu)=\{0\}$.

**Remark 4.39.** When $\mu\in W$, or when $\mathcal{O}(A,\mu)\ne\{0\}$, the moduli space may not be smooth, or may have larger than expected dimension.
