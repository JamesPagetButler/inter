# Restatement

## Abstract

Spectrum of a certain class of first order conformally invariant operators on the sphere is explicitly computed. The class contains the (elliptic versions of) Rarita-Schwinger operator and its higher spin analogues.

## 1. Introduction

Let $M$ be a smooth oriented compact $n$-dimensional manifold, $n \geq 3$, endowed with a Riemannian metric and a spin structure.

A large body of work has computed spectral properties of basic invariant differential operators on $M$. Spectra of the Laplace and Dirac operators have been computed explicitly on many examples of homogeneous spaces ([B1, B2, BGM, CaH, CFG, Mi, MS, Sa]). Estimates concerning first eigenvalues and their relation to the geometry of $M$ have been studied in many papers ([Fri1, Hi, Kir, KSW, Li2, Lo, Su]). In the general case, exact formulae for eigenvalues are not available, but their asymptotic behaviour is a classical subject studied for a long time ([DF, Ga]).

Recently, more complicated invariant first order differential operators on $M$ have attracted growing interest. A prototype is the Rarita-Schwinger operator (see [Fra1, Fra2, FraS, MP, N, NGRN, Pe1, Pe2, Pe3, Pe4, Pe5, RaS, US, Wa]). It acts on sections of the bundle associated to a more complicated representation of the group $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$.

In the paper, spectral properties are studied for a certain class of differential operators on $M$ which has been used intensively in Clifford analysis in connection with monogenic differential forms (see [DSS, Ry1, So1, So2, SoS, So3]). The aim of the paper is to compute explicitly the spectra of this class of conformally invariant operators on the flat model, i.e. on spheres.

The main tool is the general results of Branson, Ólafsson and Ørsted ([BOO]) describing the construction of intertwining operators between principal series representations of semisimple Lie groups. These results compute spectra of a wide class of invariant operators up to normalisation, i.e. they give explicit formulae for ratios of eigenvalues. These formulae can be used directly in odd dimensions. In even dimensions, the differential operators studied here are not covered by the results in [BOO], but the methods used there can be adapted (see Sect. 3).

The symbol of the Dirac operator is given by Clifford multiplication, so the normalisation question is settled by a choice of Clifford action. For higher spin representations and the associated invariant operators, the normalisation of the operators studied must first be settled (see Sect. 2). To compute exact formulae for spectra, it is then sufficient to find explicitly one eigenvalue. This is done in Sect. 4 using methods developed in [VSe].

## 2. Definitions and statements

**Definition 1.** Let $\mathbf{S}$ (for $n = 2k+1$), resp. $\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{S}^+ \oplus \mathbf{S}^-$ (for $n = 2k$), denote the basic spinor representations with highest weights
$$\sigma = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}, \ldots, \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2}\right), \quad\text{resp.}\quad \sigma^{\pm} = \left(\tfrac{1}{2}, \ldots, \tfrac{1}{2}, \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\right).$$
Let $\lambda \in \Lambda^+$ (for $n = 2k+1$), resp. $\lambda^{\pm} \in \Lambda^+$ (for $n = 2k$), be dominant weights with
$$\lambda = \left(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_{k-1}, \tfrac{1}{2}\right), \quad\text{resp.}\quad \lambda^{\pm} = \left(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_{k-1}, \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\right).$$
Denote further
$$\lambda' = \lambda - \sigma \in \Lambda^+, \quad\text{resp.}\quad \lambda' = \lambda^+ - \sigma^+ \in \Lambda^+.$$
In even dimensions, we use the notation $\mathbf{V}_\lambda = \mathbf{V}_{\lambda^+} \oplus \mathbf{V}_{\lambda^-}$.

The representation $\mathbf{V}_\lambda$ appears with multiplicity one in the decomposition of the tensor product $\mathbf{S} \otimes \mathbf{V}_{\lambda'}$ (it is the Cartan product of both representations). Hence we can write
$$\mathbf{S} \otimes \mathbf{V}_{\lambda'} = \mathbf{V}_\lambda \oplus \mathbf{W},$$
where $\mathbf{W}$ is the sum of all other irreducible components in the decomposition. Let $D^T_{\lambda'}$ be the twisted Dirac operator on $S \otimes V_{\lambda'}$. If we write $D^T_{\lambda'}$ in block form, we obtain 4 new invariant operators, one of them being
$$D_\lambda: \Gamma(V_\lambda) \to \Gamma(V_\lambda).$$
Operators $D_\lambda$ defined in this way are called higher spin Dirac operators.

**Lemma 1.** The eigenvalues of the Dirac operator on the sphere $S_n$ with standard metric are
$$\mu_l = \pm\left(\tfrac{n}{2} + l\right), \quad l = 0, 1, 2, \ldots,$$
with multiplicity $2^{[n/2]} \binom{l+n-1}{l}$.

**Theorem 1.** Let $D_{\lambda_j} = -\tilde{D}_j$, $0 < j < n/2$, be the higher spin Dirac operators defined above, considered on the sphere $S_n$ with the standard metric. Then their eigenvalues are:

- $\mu^1_l = \pm\left(\tfrac{n}{2} + l\right)$, $l = 1, 2, \ldots$, with multiplicity
$$2^{[n/2]} \binom{n+1}{j+1} \binom{l+n}{l-1} \frac{(n-2j)(j+1)}{(l+j)(l+n-j)};$$

- $\mu^2_l = \pm\left[\frac{n-2j}{n-2j+2}\left(\tfrac{n}{2} + l\right)\right]$, $l = 1, 2, \ldots$, with multiplicity
$$2^{[n/2]} \binom{n+1}{j} \binom{l+n}{l-1} \frac{(n-2j+2)j}{(l+j-1)(l+n-j+1)}.$$

**Theorem 2.** Let $D_\lambda$, $\lambda \in \Lambda^+$, $|\lambda_k| = \tfrac{1}{2}$, be a higher spin Dirac operator (see Def. 1), and let $\mu, \mu'$ be two different eigenvalues of it, having the same sign. Let $W$, resp. $W'$, be the corresponding spaces of eigenvectors. Then there exist isotypic components $W_\alpha$, $W_{\alpha'}$ with highest weights $\alpha, \alpha' \in \Lambda^+(K)$ such that $W \subset W_\alpha$, $W' \subset W_{\alpha'}$, and
$$\frac{\mu}{\mu'} = \prod_{a=1}^{[(n+1)/2]} \frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(n+3) - a + \alpha_a\right)\,\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(n+1) - a + \alpha'_a\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(n+3) - a + \alpha'_a\right)\,\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(n+1) - a + \alpha_a\right)}.$$

**Theorem 3.** Let $M$ be an Einstein spin manifold. Define a first order differential operator $T_j$ by
$$T_j = \pi_{j+1,j+1} \circ \nabla^S\big|_{E^{j,j}}, \quad 0 \le j \le [n/2] - 1.$$
If $s$ is an eigenvector of the operator $\tilde{D}_j$ corresponding to an eigenvalue $\mu$, and if $T_j(s) \neq 0$, then $s' = T_j(s)$ is an eigenvector of the operator $\tilde{D}_{j+1}$ corresponding to the eigenvalue
$$\mu' = -\frac{n-2}{n}\mu.$$

**Theorem 4.** The operators $T_j$, $0 \le j \le \frac{n}{2} - 1$, have nontrivial symbol; hence their kernel is a proper subset of $\Gamma(V_{\lambda_j})$.
