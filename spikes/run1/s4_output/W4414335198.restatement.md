# Restatement

## Abstract

Non-abelian generalized cohomology is recognized in algebraic topology as the non-abelian Poincaré dual of factorization homology, and in theoretical physics as providing flux quantization for non-linear Gauss laws. Unstable Cohomotopy, first studied by Pontrjagin, has been underappreciated as a cohomology theory and has only recently been considered as a flux-quantization law ("Hypothesis H").

The paper gives a general construction of an analogue of the Chern character map on twisted equivariant non-abelian cohomology theories whose classifying spaces are equivariantly simply connected. The construction is illustrated by a twisted equivariant form of Cohomotopy, obtained essentially by computing its equivariant Sullivan model. The abstract closes with an outlook on applying this result to the deduction of anyonic quantum states on M5-branes wrapped over Seifert 3-orbifolds.

## 1 Introduction & Overview

### Cohomology via classifying spaces

**Ordinary cohomology.** For reduced singular cohomology with coefficients in a discrete abelian group $A$, the cohomology in degree $n$ is classified by Eilenberg–MacLane spaces $K(A,n)$. On well-behaved spaces $X$, notably smooth manifolds, there are natural isomorphisms

$$H^{n}(X;A)\simeq \pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}\big(X,K(A,n)\big),\qquad \widetilde H^{n}(X;A)\simeq \pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}^{*/}\big(X,K(A,n)\big). \tag{1}$$

The Eilenberg–MacLane spaces are successive loop spaces of each other, via weak homotopy equivalences

$$\sigma_n:\;K(A,n)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\Omega K(A,n+1). \tag{2}$$

These maps give the suspension isomorphisms

$$\widetilde H^{n}(X;A)\simeq \mathrm{Maps}^{*/}\big(X,K(A,n)\big)\xrightarrow{(\sigma_n)_*}\mathrm{Maps}^{*/}\big(X,\Omega K(A,n+1)\big)\simeq_{\text{adjunction}}\mathrm{Maps}^{*/}\big(\Sigma X,K(A,n+1)\big)\simeq \widetilde H^{n+1}(\Sigma X;A).$$

Here the first and last isomorphisms are (1). The stated properties of cohomology (homotopy invariance, exactness and wedge property) follow from general properties of mapping spaces.

**Ordinary non-abelian cohomology.** For a non-abelian group $G$, the classifying space $K(G,1)\simeq BG$ exists but is not a loop space. The suspension isomorphism is therefore lost. The assignment

$$X\longmapsto H^{1}(X;G):=\pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}(X,BG)\in \mathrm{Set}^{*/}\tag{3}$$

still satisfies homotopy invariance, exactness and wedge property, but is not abelian. This is known as non-abelian cohomology [cf. Chern–Weil theory].

**Whitehead-generalized cohomology.** If abelian groups and suspension isomorphisms are retained, one may use any sequence of spaces $(E_n)_{n=0}^{\infty}$ with weak equivalences

$$\sigma_n:\;E_n\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\Omega E_{n+1},$$

called a sequential $\Omega$-spectrum (or spectrum). By the Brown representability theorem, the assignments $X\mapsto E^n(X):=\pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}(X;E_n)$ are equivalently the generalized cohomology theories in the sense of Whitehead, including K-theory, elliptic cohomology and cobordism cohomology.

**Non-abelian generalized cohomology.** Suspension isomorphisms may be dropped. Any pointed space $\mathscr{A}$ (which may be assumed connected) then classifies a non-abelian generalized cohomology theory, defined by

$$H^{1}(X;\Omega\mathscr{A}):=\pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}(X,\mathscr{A}).\tag{4}$$

Any loop space $\Omega\mathscr{A}$ carries the structure of a homotopy-coherent group (a groupal $A_\infty$-space, or $\infty$-group), and its delooping is the connected component of the original space:

$$\mathscr{A}\simeq B\,\Omega\mathscr{A}.\tag{5}$$

For $\mathscr{A}\equiv S^n$, the $n$-sphere, the classified theory is unstable Cohomotopy:

$$\widetilde H^{1}\big(X;\Omega S^{n}\big)\equiv \pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}^{*/}(X,S^n)\equiv \pi^{n}(X),\tag{6}$$

in dual reference to the homotopy groups $\pi_n(X)\simeq\pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}^{*/}(S^n,X)$.

Another example is unstable topological K-theory [HK04], whose classifying spaces are the finite stages $\mathrm{U}(n)$ of the sequential colimits that construct the classifying spaces of topological K-theory.

### Developing non-abelian cohomology

Cohomotopy (6) was studied by Pontrjagin [Bo36] [Po38] [Sp49], but these early works did not treat it as a cohomology theory. The early development addressed the fact that cohomotopy sets need not carry group structure. Non-abelian gerbes were introduced in [Gi71]. Explicit treatment of non-abelian generalized cohomology in the form (4) appears in a lecture [To02], possibly following [Si02].

Two developments in 2009 placed non-abelian generalized cohomology in context:

- Non-abelian Poincaré duality [Lu09b, §3.8] relates non-abelian cohomology of manifolds to "factorization homology", a non-abelian homology theory that requires more work to define. The duality was later made explicit in [Lu14, Def. 6].
- In theoretical physics [SS08] [Sc09] [SSS12], charge/flux-quantization laws [SS25a] for higher gauge fields were observed to lie in non-abelian cohomology.

Considering the right-hand side of (4) for sheaves of spaces (higher stacks) yields non-abelian generalized sheaf cohomology. This includes non-abelian generalized versions of twisted and equivariant cohomology. Differential cohomology is also covered by this framework but is not treated here.

**Equivariant non-abelian cohomology.** Let $G$ be a finite group acting on a space $\mathscr{A}$, and consider $G$-equivariant maps [cf. SS25b]. The corresponding equivariant (also called proper equivariant) cohomology is

$$H^{1}_{G}\big(X;\Omega\mathscr{A}\big)=\pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}\big(G\curvearrowright X,\;G\curvearrowright\mathscr{A}\big)^{G}.\tag{7}$$

Elmendorf's theorem (Prop. 2.26 below) states that $G$-homotopy equivalences between $G$-cell complexes are systems of ordinary weak homotopy equivalences between the fixed spaces $\mathscr{A}^H$ for all subgroups $H\subset G$. These systems may be repackaged as presheaves on the orbit category $\mathrm{Orb}(G)$. Thus $G$-equivariant homotopy theory is equivalent to the homotopy theory of presheaves of spaces on $\mathrm{Orb}(G)$.

**Twisted non-abelian cohomology.** For a connected space $\mathscr{B}\simeq B\mathscr{G}$ as in (5), fibrations over $\mathscr{B}$ are equivalent to Borel constructions $\mathscr{A}/\!/\mathscr{G}$ of homotopy-coherent actions $\mathscr{G}\curvearrowright\mathscr{A}$. A domain object $\tau: X\to B\mathscr{G}$ is a twist, and the codomain $\mathscr{A}/\!/\mathscr{G}\to B\mathscr{G}$ is a local coefficient bundle. The corresponding non-abelian cohomology is the set of homotopy classes of sections of the $\tau$-associated $\mathscr{A}$-fiber bundle, namely the $\tau$-twisted $\mathscr{A}$-cohomology [FSS23, §3]:

$$H^{1+\tau}(X,\Omega\mathscr{A}):=\pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}\big(X,\mathscr{A}/\!/\mathscr{G}\big)_{/B\mathscr{G}}\tag{8}$$

(relative homotopy classes over $B\mathscr{G}$). If all spaces carry $G$-actions as in (7), this is twisted equivariant non-abelian cohomology.

**The non-abelian character.** The Chern–Dold character map to de Rham cohomology is, in the case of K-cohomology, the familiar Chern character; on ordinary cohomology it is essentially the de Rham theorem. It is the cohomology operation induced by rationalization of the classifying space,

$$\eta^{\mathbb{Q}}:\;\mathscr{A}\longrightarrow L^{\mathbb{Q}}\mathscr{A}.$$

This makes sense for non-abelian classifying spaces, immediately under mild assumptions such as nilpotency, and more generally with additional work. [FSS23] developed this generalization. The fundamental theorem of dg-algebraic rational homotopy theory can be restated as a non-abelian de Rham theorem. Over smooth manifolds $X$, it identifies non-abelian rational cohomology with concordance classes of flat differential forms with coefficients in the real Whitehead-bracket $L_\infty$-algebra $\mathfrak{l}\mathscr{A}$:

$$H^{1}\big(X;\mathscr{A}\big)\equiv\pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}\big(X,\mathscr{A}\big)\xrightarrow{(\eta^{\mathbb{Q}})_*}\pi_0\,\mathrm{Maps}\big(X,L^{\mathbb{Q}}\mathscr{A}\big)\cong H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}\big(X;\mathfrak{l}\mathscr{A}\big).\tag{9}$$

Here the three terms are non-abelian generalized cohomology, non-abelian rational cohomology, and non-abelian de Rham cohomology. The left map is the character map, induced by rationalization of the classifying space. The right isomorphism is the non-abelian de Rham theorem.

Since generalized cohomology is typically hard to analyze, the character map extracts the first non-trivial stage of more tractable invariants. The character of a non-abelian class is the first obstruction to its trivialization.

*Footnote 2.* In the physics application, flux densities of a higher gauge field are sourced by charges, which are classes in non-abelian de Rham cohomology. A flux-quantization law lifts these charges through the character map to classes in a chosen non-abelian cohomology theory.

The non-abelian character (9) generalizes to twisted non-abelian cohomology (8) using relative minimal Sullivan models. The following table lists examples of twisted non-abelian character maps, several computed previously, with the last one being the case treated here.

The identification of the character map on non-abelian cohomology with passage of classifying spaces to their minimal dgc-algebraic models yields two consequences:

- The twisted equivariant non-abelian character can be defined by constructing equivariant relative Sullivan models, using the theory of [Tr82] [Scu02, §11] [Scu08].
- The equivariant theory supplies motivation and applications for the Sullivan model theory itself.

### Main result

The construction is carried out for classifying spaces that are equivariantly simply connected, meaning simply connected on each fixed locus.[^3] The main result, whose proof culminates in Rem. 3.79 below, is:

> **Theorem 1.1.** (i) The character map (Def. 3.78) in $\mathbb{Z}_2$-equivariant twistorial Cohomotopy (Def. 2.48), on $\mathbb{Z}_2$-orbifolds (Def. 2.36) with $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$-structure $\tau$ and $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$-connection $\omega$ (Example 3.70), is of the following form (3.79):
>
> $$\left\{\begin{array}{l} H_3,\ F_2,\ 2G_7,\ \widetilde G_4\in\Omega^\bullet_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\quad\text{(fluxes)}\\[4pt] \text{with twisted Bianchi identities}\\ \quad dH_3=\widetilde G_4-\tfrac12 p_1(\omega)-F_2\wedge F_2\\ \quad dF_2=0\\ \quad d\,2G_7=-\widetilde G_4\wedge\big(\widetilde G_4-\tfrac12 p_1(\omega)\big)\\ \quad d\,\widetilde G_4=0 \end{array}\right.\ \Bigg|\ \begin{array}{l} \text{bulk}\\[4pt] \text{fixed locus:}\\ dH_3|_{X^{\mathbb{Z}_2}}=-\tfrac12 p_1\big(\omega|_{X^{\mathbb{Z}_2}}\big)-F_2\wedge F_2|_{X^{\mathbb{Z}_2}}\\ dF_2|_{X^{\mathbb{Z}_2}}=0\\ G_7|_{X^{\mathbb{Z}_2}}=0\\ \widetilde G_4|_{X^{\mathbb{Z}_2}}=0 \end{array}\right\}\Big/\!\sim$$
>
> This is identified via $\widetilde G_4\mapsto G_4+\tfrac14 p_1(\omega)$ with
>
> $$\left\{\begin{array}{l} 2G_7,\ G_4\in\Omega^\bullet_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\quad\text{(fluxes)}\\ d\,2G_7=-G_4\wedge G_4+\big(\tfrac14 p_1(\omega)\big)^2\\ dG_4=0\quad\text{(twisted Bianchi identities)} \end{array}\right\}\Big/\!\sim$$
>
> which is the twisted character map $\mathrm{ch}_\pi^\tau$ of $J$-twisted Cohomotopy $\pi^\tau_{\mathbb{Z}_2}(X_{\text{spacetime}})$. Here $\pi^\tau$ is classified by $S^4$ parametrized over $B\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ with $\mathrm{Sp}(1)$-action.

*Footnote 3.* Dropping the simple-connectedness assumption makes the discussion considerably more involved. One must rationalize the fixed-locus-wise covering spaces while retaining the actions of their fundamental groups by deck transformations over each fixed locus, in addition to the action of the equivariance group $G$ and the twisting group $\mathscr{G}$.
