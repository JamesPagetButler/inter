# Restatement

## Abstract

Let $X$ be a scheme. The exodromy correspondence of Barwick, Glasman, and Haine computes constructible sheaves of spaces on $X$ as an $\infty$-category of continuous functors from the profinite category $\operatorname{Gal}(X)$. Viewing $\operatorname{Gal}(X)$ as a condensed category, Wolf extended this to an exodromy correspondence for pro-étale sheaves. This paper proves the pro-étale exodromy theorem directly in the condensed setting. It then extracts an exodromy theorem for Postnikov complete étale sheaves, and uses it to give a new proof of the constructible étale exodromy correspondence of Barwick, Glasman, and Haine. The qcqs hypotheses are removed, and versions are given for coefficients in more general $\infty$-categories. The methods also give a $\kappa$-condensed statement for any uncountable cardinal $\kappa$ with $\kappa > \lvert \mathcal{O}_X(U) \rvert$ for every affine open $U \subseteq X$.

## Introduction

### Background

For a topological space $X$, the monodromy correspondence identifies locally constant sheaves on $X$ with values in a presentable $\infty$-category $\mathscr{E}$ with $\operatorname{Fun}(\Pi_{\infty}(X), \mathscr{E})$. For a scheme $X$, the analogous statement holds for locally constant sheaves of finite sets (or $\pi$-finite spaces) using the étale fundamental group $\pi_1^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X)$ [15, Exp. V], [12, Exp. X, §6], [9, §7] (resp. the étale homotopy type $\Pi_\infty^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(X)$ [5, 13, 22]). MacPherson (unpublished, first presented in Treumann's thesis [45, 46]) extended this to constructible sheaves, and Lurie obtained an $\infty$-categorical version [29, Thm. A.9.3]. Barwick, Glasman, and Haine proved an étale version [7, Thm. 12.1.6] and coined the term *exodromy correspondence*.

**Theorem 0 (Barwick–Glasman–Haine).** Let $X$ be a qcqs scheme. Then there is an exodromy correspondence
$$\Sh_{\operatorname{cons}}(X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}, \mathcal{S}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Fun^{\operatorname{cts}}(\Gal(X), \mathcal{S}_{\pi})$$
between the $\infty$-category of constructible sheaves of spaces on $X$ and the $\infty$-category of continuous functors from the Galois category of $X$ to $\pi$-finite spaces.

Here $\Gal(X)$ is a pro-object in $\pi$-finite layered $\infty$-categories whose materialisation (limit in $\Cat_\infty$) is the 1-category $\Pts^*(X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}) = \Fun^*(\Sh(X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}), \Set)$ of points of the étale topos. Continuous functors are functors in $\Pro(\Cat_\infty)$, where $\mathcal{S}_\pi$ is treated as the constant pro-system. A profinite $\infty$-category such as $\Gal(X)$ can be viewed as a condensed $\infty$-category by taking the limit in $\Cond(\Cat_\infty)$ of the diagram of discrete $\infty$-categories [7, §13.5]. Writing $\mathbf{Gal}(X)$ for the resulting condensed $\infty$-category, Wolf [49, Cor. 1.2] proved:

**Theorem 0 (Wolf).** Let $X$ be a qcqs scheme. Then there is an exodromy correspondence
$$\Sh^{\operatorname{hyp}}(X_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}, \mathcal{S}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Fun^{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbf{Gal}(X), \mathbf{Cond}(\mathcal{S})).$$
Here $\mathbf{Cond}(\mathcal{S})$ sends a profinite set $S$ to $\Cond(\mathcal{S})_{/S}$ (equivalently, $\Sh(\ProFin_{/S}, \mathcal{S})$), and continuous functors are functors of condensed $\infty$-categories.

### Main results

**Theorem 1 (Subsection 3.1).** Let $X$ be a scheme, and let $\mathscr{E}$ be an $\infty$-category that has small limits. Then there is an exodromy correspondence
$$\mathcal{Ex}\colon \Sh^{\operatorname{hyp}}(X_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}}, \mathscr{E}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Fun^{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbf{Gal}(X), \mathbf{Cond}(\mathscr{E})),$$
where $\mathbf{Cond}(\mathscr{E})$ sends a profinite set $S$ to $\Sh(\ProFin_{/S}, \mathscr{E})$.

**Theorem 2 (Subsection 3.2).** Let $X$ be a scheme, and let $\mathscr{E}$ be a compactly assembled presentable $\infty$-category. Then the pro-étale exodromy correspondence restricts to an étale exodromy correspondence
$$\mathcal{Ex}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}\colon \Sh^{\post}(X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}, \mathscr{E}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Fun^{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbf{Gal}(X), \underline{\mathscr{E}}),$$
where $\underline{\mathscr{E}}$ is the condensed $\infty$-category $S \mapsto \Sh(S, \mathscr{E})$.

The paper notes that $\underline{\mathscr{E}}$ is a condensed $\infty$-category by [17, Cor. 2.8], which uses that $\mathscr{E}$ is compactly assembled. The paper states that the case $\mathscr{E} = \mathcal{S}$ does not appear in the literature. When $\mathscr{E} = \Set$ and $X$ is qcqs, the statement is almost the same as Lurie's version of Makkai's strong conceptual completeness theorem [32, Thm. 0.0.6], with a small technical difference discussed in Subsection 3.2.

**Theorem 3 (Subsection 3.3).** Let $X$ be a scheme. Then the étale exodromy correspondence restricts to a constructible étale exodromy correspondence
$$\mathcal{Ex}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{\operatorname{cons}}\colon \Sh_{\operatorname{cons}}(X_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}, \mathcal{S}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Fun^{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbf{Gal}(X), \underline{\mathcal{S}}_{\operatorname{cons}}),$$
where $\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{\operatorname{cons}}$ is the condensed $\infty$-category $S \mapsto \Sh_{\operatorname{cons}}(S, \mathcal{S})$.

The paper states there are no qcqs hypotheses in Theorems 1, 2, and 3.

**Theorem 4 (Subsection 4.1).** The functor $\mathbf{Gal}\colon \Sch \to \Cond(\Cat_\infty)$ is a pro-étale hypercosheaf.

**Theorem 5.** Let $X$ be a scheme, and let $\Stone_{/X}^{\operatorname{loc\ \!coh}} \to \ProFin$ be the Grothendieck construction of the functor $\mathbf{Gal}(X)\colon \ProFin^{\operatorname{op}} \to \Cat$. Then $\Stone_{/X}^{\operatorname{loc\ \!coh}}$ is canonically equivalent to the category $\AffPro\Et_{/X}^{\operatorname{wsl}}$ of w-strictly local schemes that are limits of affine schemes in $\Et_{/X}$.

## Cardinality conventions

**Remark 1.1.1.** Let $\kappa$ be an infinite cardinal, and let $\mathcal{I}$ be a small category with $\lvert \ob \mathcal{I} \rvert \leq \kappa$. Let $\mathscr{C}$ be either $\Set$ or $\CRing$, let $A \in \mathscr{C}$, and let $D\colon \mathcal{I} \to \mathscr{C}_{A/}$ be a diagram such that $\lvert D(i) \rvert \leq \kappa$ for all $i \in \mathcal{I}$. Then $\lvert \colim D \rvert \leq \kappa$.

**1.1.2.** Let $\mathscr{C}$ be a small $\infty$-category and $\kappa$ an infinite cardinal. $\Ind^{\kappa}(\mathscr{C})$ denotes the full subcategory of $\Ind(\mathscr{C})$ on the $\kappa$-small filtered colimits of objects of $\mathscr{C}$. Dually, $\Pro^{\kappa}(\mathscr{C})$ denotes the full subcategory of $\Pro(\mathscr{C})$ on $\kappa$-small cofiltered limits of objects of $\mathscr{C}$. An object of $\Ind(\mathscr{C})$ (resp. $\Pro(\mathscr{C})$) is called $\kappa$-small if it lies in $\Ind^{\kappa}(\mathscr{C})$ (resp. $\Pro^{\kappa}(\mathscr{C})$). When $\kappa$ is regular, these agree with other common notations: $\Ind^{\kappa}(\mathscr{C}) = \Ind^{\kappa}_{\omega}(\mathscr{C})$ by [30, Tags 0664 and 063R (a)]. If $\kappa$ is uncountable, this coincides with $\Ind(\mathscr{C})^{\kappa} \subseteq \Ind(\mathscr{C})$, the full subcategory of $\kappa$-compact objects [30, Tags 0692 and 061Q]. For $\kappa = \omega$, $\Ind^{\omega}(\mathscr{C}) \subseteq \Ind(\mathscr{C})^{\omega}$. Moreover, $\Ind(\mathscr{C})^{\omega}$ is an idempotent completion of $\mathscr{C}$ [30, Tag 0693], so $\mathscr{C} \hookrightarrow \Ind^{\omega}(\mathscr{C}) \hookrightarrow \Ind(\mathscr{C})^{\omega}$ are equivalences when $\mathscr{C}$ is idempotent complete. When $\mathscr{C} \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ is the full subcategory of finite CW complexes, Wall's finiteness obstruction [48, Thm. F] measures the difference between $\mathscr{C} = \Ind^{\omega}(\mathscr{C})$ and $\Ind(\mathscr{C})^{\omega} = \mathcal{S}^{\omega}$. See also [35, Rmk. 2.1.6 and Prop. 2.3.11] in the 1-categorical case.

**1.1.3.** For the category $\Pro(\Fin)$ of Stone spaces, write $\ProFin_\kappa$ for $\Pro^\kappa(\Fin)$. For instance, $\ProFin_{\aleph_1}$ is the category of light profinite sets.

**Lemma 1.1.4.** Let $\kappa$ be an infinite cardinal, and let $X \in \ProFin$. The following are equivalent:
(a) $X \in \ProFin_\kappa$.
(b) $\lvert C(X, \mathbf{F}_2) \rvert < \kappa$.
(c) There exists a directed set $I$ with $\lvert I \rvert < \kappa$ and a diagram $I^{\operatorname{op}} \to \Fin$ such that $X \cong \lim X_i$.
(d) There exists a category $\mathscr{C}$ with $\lvert \ob \mathscr{C} \rvert < \kappa$ and $\lvert \mor \mathscr{C} \rvert < \kappa$ and a diagram $\mathscr{C} \to \Fin$ such that $X \cong \lim X_i$.

**1.1.5.** For schemes, the paper uses a notion of size such that every $\kappa$-small scheme has a pro-étale cover by a $\kappa$-small w-strictly local scheme. Since the algebraic closure of a finite field is countably infinite, the notion of size does not distinguish finite from countably infinite sets.

**Definition 1.1.6.** If $X$ is a profinite set, $\size(X)$ denotes the supremum of $\aleph_0$ and $\lvert C(X, \mathbf{F}_2) \rvert$.

**Definition 1.1.7.** Let $X$ be a scheme. $\size(X)$ denotes the supremum of $\aleph_0$ and the cardinalities $\lvert \mathscr{O}_X(U) \rvert$ over all affine opens $U \subseteq X$. For a cardinal $\kappa$, $X$ is *$\kappa$-small* if $\size(X) < \kappa$, and $\Sch^\kappa$ denotes the full subcategory of $\Sch$ on $\kappa$-small schemes. For $A \in \CRing$, $\size(A) = \max(\aleph_0, \lvert A \rvert)$, and $\CRing^\kappa$ denotes the full subcategory of $\CRing$ on $\kappa$-small commutative rings.

**1.1.8.** If $X$ is qcqs, the quotient topology on $\pi_0(X)$ for the map $X \to \pi_0(X)$ is profinite [44, Tags 0906 and 094L]. The paper always regards $\pi_0(X)$ as a profinite set.

**Lemma 1.1.9.** Let $X$ be a qcqs scheme, and let $\pi_0(X) \in \ProFin$ be its profinite set of connected components. Then $\size(\pi_0(X)) \leq \size(X)$.

**Remark 1.1.10.** A scheme $X$ is $\kappa$-small if and only if $\size(X) < \kappa$. The same does not quite hold for profinite sets: by 1.1.4 and Definition 1.1.6, a profinite set $X$ is $\kappa$-small if and only if $\lvert C(X, \mathbf{F}_2) \rvert < \kappa$, but $\size(X) = \max(\aleph_0, \lvert C(X, \mathbf{F}_2) \rvert)$, not $\lvert C(X, \mathbf{F}_2) \rvert$. The main theorems start with a $\kappa$-small scheme $X$, which forces $\kappa$ to be uncountable since $\kappa > \size(X) \geq \aleph_0$.

## Acyclic and w-strictly local schemes

**Definition 1.2.1.** A scheme $W$ is *acyclic* if it is quasi-compact and every étale surjection $U \twoheadrightarrow W$ has a section. This is the definition of [6, §3] with quasi-compactness always assumed. A scheme $W$ is *w-strictly local* if it is acyclic and the set of closed points $W^{\operatorname{cl}} \subseteq W$ is closed. This coincides with the definition of [9, Def. 2.1.1 and Def. 2.2.1], since $W$ is automatically affine by Lemma 1.2.3(a).

**Remark 1.2.2.** A scheme $W$ is *w-projective* if it is w-strictly local and $\pi_0(W)$ is projective in compact Hausdorff spaces. Such schemes are called w-contractible in [9, Def. 2.4.1]. This notion is stronger than the weakly projective objects of [32, Def. 6.2.2] in $\AffPro\Et_{/X} = \Pro(\AffEt_{/X})$, which correspond to acyclic objects in the paper's definition.
