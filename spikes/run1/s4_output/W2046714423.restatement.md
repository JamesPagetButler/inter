# Restatement

## 1. Introduction

Let $G = \mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$ (Eq. (1)).

A finite geometry $F$ is given by the following data:

- A finite-dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$.
- An antilinear isometry $J$ of $\mathcal{H}$ with $J^{2}=\epsilon$.
- An involutive algebra $\mathcal{A}$ (over $\mathbb{R}$) acting in $\mathcal{H}$, satisfying the order zero condition
$$[a,b^{0}]=0,\quad \forall\,a,b\in\mathcal{A},\quad b^{0}=Jb^{*}J^{-1}. \tag{1.1}$$
- A $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading $\gamma$ of $\mathcal{H}$, such that $J\gamma=\epsilon''\gamma J$.
- A self-adjoint operator $D$ in $\mathcal{H}$, such that $JD=\epsilon' DJ$.

The signs $(\epsilon,\epsilon',\epsilon'')\in\{\pm1\}^3$ are taken to be $(\epsilon,\epsilon',\epsilon'')=(1,1,-1)$. This choice gives $K$-theoretic dimension $6$ modulo $8$. The product $M\times F$ then has $K$-theoretic dimension $10$ modulo $8$.

The order one condition is
$$[[D,a],b^{0}]=0\qquad \forall\,a,b\in\mathcal{A}_F. \tag{1.2}$$

The classification of irreducible triplets $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},J)$ yields two classes:

- **First case:** an integer $k$ and a real form of $M_k(\mathbb{C})$, acting by left multiplication on $\mathcal{H}=M_k(\mathbb{C})$, with $J(x)=x^*$. The real forms are unitary $M_k(\mathbb{C})$, orthogonal $M_k(\mathbb{R})$, and symplectic $M_a(\mathbb{H})$ with $2a=k$.
- **Second case:** a real form of $M_k(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_k(\mathbb{C})$, acting by left multiplication on $\mathcal{H}=M_k(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_k(\mathbb{C})$, with $J(x,y)=(y^*,x^*)$.

The grading analysis shows that $J\gamma=-\gamma J$ excludes the first case. The resulting algebra is $\mathcal{A}=M_2(\mathbb{H})\oplus M_4(\mathbb{C})$. Equivalently, $\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V,W)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(W,V)$, where $V$ is a 4-dimensional complex vector space and $W$ is a two-dimensional graded right vector space over $\mathbb{H}$. The left action is $\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{H}}(W)\oplus\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)$.

The main result is that there exists, up to isomorphism, a unique involutive subalgebra $\mathcal{A}_F$ of maximal dimension of $\mathcal{A}^{\rm ev}$ (the even part of $\mathcal{A}$) satisfying the order one condition (3). It is isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus M_3(\mathbb{C})$. Together with its representation in $(\mathcal{H},J,\gamma)$, it gives the noncommutative geometry $F$ of [7]. By the results of [7], after introducing multiplicity $3$ as the number of generations, the spectral action on $M\times F$ with inner fluctuations gives the Standard Model minimally coupled to gravity.

1. The unimodular subgroup of the unitary group acting by the adjoint representation $\mathrm{Ad}(u)$ in $\mathcal{H}$ is the group of gauge transformations of the Standard Model.
2. The unimodular inner fluctuations of the metric give the gauge bosons of the Standard Model.
3. The full Standard Model (with neutrino mixing and seesaw mechanism) minimally coupled to Einstein gravity is given in Euclidean form by the action functional
$$S=\mathrm{Tr}\big(f(D_A/\Lambda)\big)+\tfrac12\langle J\tilde\xi, D_A\tilde\xi\rangle,\quad \tilde\xi\in\mathcal{H}^+_{cl}, \tag{4.8}$$

$$\mathrm{Int}(\mathcal{A})\sim\mathrm{SU}(\mathcal{A})=\{u\in\mathcal{A}\;|\;uu^*=u^*u=1,\ \det(u)=1\}. \tag{4.9}$$
This applies also to the product geometry by the manifold $M$.

## 5. Conclusion

The fermion doubling problem requires (cf. [2], [11]) crossing the ordinary 4-dimensional continuum by a space of $K$-theoretic dimension $6$.

The classification of finite noncommutative geometries of $K$-theoretic dimension $6$ singles out the algebras which are real forms of $M_k(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_k(\mathbb{C})$ acting in a Hilbert space of dimension $2k^2$ by left multiplication, together with a specific antilinear isometry. This predicts the number of fermions per generation to be a square. The simplest case is $k=4$.

The number of generations is taken as the input $N=3$, which gives multiplicity $3$. The condition that $D$ commutes with the subalgebra $\{(\lambda,\lambda,0);\ \lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}$ (as in the classification of [7]) is an additional assumption.
