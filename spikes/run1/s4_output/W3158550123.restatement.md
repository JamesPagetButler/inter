# Restatement of the Source Material

## Abstract

We continue the study of fuzzy geometries inside Connes' spectral formalism and their relation to multimatrix models. In this companion paper to Pérez-Sánchez (Ann Henri Poincaré 22:3095-3148, 2021, arXiv:2007.10914), we propose a gauge theory setting based on noncommutative geometry, which has the ability to also accommodate a Higgs field, as does the traditional formulation in terms of almost-commutative manifolds. In contrast to almost-commutative manifolds, the present framework, called gauge matrix spectral triples, employs only finite-dimensional algebras. In a path-integral quantization approach to the Spectral Action, this allows one to state Yang-Mills-Higgs theory (on four-dimensional Euclidean fuzzy space) as an explicit random multimatrix model, obtained here, whose matrix fields exactly mirror those of the Yang-Mills-Higgs theory on a smooth manifold.

## 1. Introduction

The introduction is summarised below, retaining the statements and citations that carry mathematical content.

- Finite geometries (finite-dimensional algebras) approximating smooth manifolds are studied in noncommutative geometry (NCG) [LLS01], and arise as truncations of space [DLM14, CvS21].
- The paper treats gauge theories from the Spectral Formalism of NCG using exclusively finite-dimensional algebras, including for the description of space(time) [CCvS19, DKvS21 for quantization via von Neumann entropy and second quantization].
- In the Spectral Action [CC97], the finite geometry $F$ is determined by a finite-dimensional algebra; this yields the Higgs field.
- Other non-associative approaches [BH10, Fur12, BF20, Tod19] are noted but not used here; the paper restricts to associative NCG structures.
- Almost-commutative geometries $M\times F$ [Ste06, CCM07, vS15] use $F$ as discrete extra dimensions.
- The paper replaces smooth spin geometries $M$ by finite spectral triples known as matrix or fuzzy geometry [Bar15].
- Fuzzy spaces [Mad92, DHMO08, SS13, SS18, ŠT20, Ste21] are noted; other discretisations or truncations [DLM14, GS20, GS21, BSZ20] are noted as closer to spectral triples.

**Organisation.** Figure 1 describes three axes: the $F$-direction (finite geometries, classified by Paschke–Sitarz [PS98] and Krajewski [Kra98]); the $1/N>0$ direction (matrix geometry, in the setting of [Bar15]); and the $\hbar$ direction (quantization, with partition function $Z=\int \mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}S(\xi)/\hbar}$). The planes are:

- $(\hbar,1/N,0)$: base geometries.
- $(\hbar,0,F)=\lim_{N\to\infty}(\hbar,1/N,F)$: quantum smooth geometries, with Gromov–Hausdorff convergence for the sphere [Rie04, Rie07, Rie15, Rie17].
- $(0,1/N,F)=\lim_{\hbar\to 0}(\hbar,1/N,F)$: classical geometries.

The paper's choices are:

- $\mathcal{A}_F = M_n(\mathbb{C})$ with $n\geq 2$, giving $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ Yang–Mills theory.
- $M_N(\mathbb{C})$ in place of a function algebra, with $N$ large and $n$ not necessarily large.
- Random geometries, via the Wick rotation $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}S(\xi)/\hbar}\to \mathrm{e}^{-S(\xi)/\hbar}$ [Gla17, BDG19].

## 2. Spectral Triples and Fuzzy Geometries

**Definition 2.1 (Fuzzy geometry).** A fuzzy geometry is determined by:

- A signature $(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, equivalently $\eta=\mathrm{diag}(+_p,-_q)$.
- Three signs $\epsilon,\epsilon',\epsilon''\in\{-1,+1\}$ determined by $s\equiv q-p \pmod 8$ according to the table:

| $s$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\epsilon$ | + | + | − | − | − | − | + | + |
| $\epsilon'$ | + | − | + | + | + | − | + | + |
| $\epsilon''$ | + | + | − | − | + | + | − | + |

*(The table above is transcribed from the source; entries for $\epsilon'$ and $\epsilon''$ are reproduced as they appear in the extracted text and may be garbled.)*

- A matrix algebra $\mathcal{A}_F = M_N(\mathbb{C})$.
- A Clifford $\mathcal{C}\ell(p,q)$-module $V$ (spinor space). The vector space is $\mathcal{H}_F = V\otimes M_N(\mathbb{C})$, with inner product $\langle v\otimes T, w\otimes W\rangle = (v,w)\,\mathrm{Tr}_N(T^*W)$ for $T,W\in M_N(\mathbb{C})$, $v,w\in V$. Here $(v,w)=\sum_a \bar v_a w_a$ on $V\cong\mathbb{C}^k$. The integer $k$ is chosen so that $V$ is irreducible for even $s$; for odd $s$ only the $\pm1$-eigenspaces of the grading are required to be irreducible.
- A chirality $\gamma_F=\gamma\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}}$, where $\gamma$ is self-adjoint on $V$ and $\gamma^2=1$.
- A left representation $\varrho(a)(v\otimes W)=v\otimes(aW)$.
- A real structure $J_F := C\otimes *$, where $C:V\to V$ is antilinear and satisfies
$$C^2=\epsilon \quad\text{and}\quad \gamma^\mu C=\epsilon' C\gamma^\mu. \tag{2.1}$$
- A self-adjoint operator $D$ on $\mathcal{H}$ satisfying the order-one condition
$$\big[[D_F,\varrho(a)],\,J_F\varrho(b)J_F^{-1}\big]=0\quad\text{for all } a,b\in\mathcal{A}. \tag{2.2}$$
- The condition $D\gamma_F=-\gamma_F D$ for even $s$.
- The sign relations
$$J_F^2=\epsilon,\qquad J_F D_F=\epsilon' D_F J_F,\qquad J_F\gamma_F=\epsilon''\gamma_F J_F. \tag{2.3a–c}$$

The number $d:=p+q$ is the dimension, and $s:=q-p \pmod 8$ is the KO-dimension.

**Remark 2.2.** The commutant property
$$[a, Jb^*J^{-1}]=0\quad\text{for all } a,b\in\mathcal{A} \tag{2.4}$$
is not assumed in this setting, but follows from Definition 2.1. Explicitly, for $v\in V$ and $m\in M_N(\mathbb{C})$,
$$ab^{\mathrm{o}}(v\otimes m)=aJ(v\otimes b^*m^*)=v\otimes(amb)=b^{\mathrm{o}}a(v\otimes m). \tag{2.5}$$

**Example 2.3.** Dirac operators of fuzzy $d$-dimensional geometries, $d=2,3,4$:

- **Type (0,2)**: $s=d=2$, $\epsilon'=1$, $(\gamma^i)^2=-1$, and
$D^{(0,2)}_F=\gamma^1\otimes[L_1,\bullet]+\gamma^2\otimes[L_2,\bullet]$.
- **Type (0,3)**: $s=3$. With quaternion units $\mathrm{i},\mathrm{j},\mathrm{k}$ in place of the gamma matrices,
$D^{(0,3)}_F=\{H,\bullet\}+\mathrm{i}[L_1,\bullet]+\mathrm{j}[L_2,\bullet]+\mathrm{k}[L_3,\bullet]$.
- **Type (0,4)**: $s=4$, Riemannian. The Dirac operator is
$$D^{(0,4)}_F=\sum_\rho \gamma^\rho\otimes[L_\rho,\bullet]+\gamma^{\hat\rho}\otimes\{H_{\hat\rho},\bullet\},\qquad \{\rho,\kappa,\lambda,\mu\}=\{0,1,2,3\}.$$
- **Type (1,3)**: $s=2$, Lorentzian. With $\gamma^0$ the time-like gamma matrix,
$$D^{(1,3)}_F=\gamma^0\otimes\{H_0,\bullet\}+\sum_i\gamma^i\otimes[L_i,\bullet]+\sum_{i<j}\gamma^0\gamma^i\gamma^j\otimes[L_{ij},\bullet]+\gamma^{\hat 0}\otimes\{H_{\hat 0},\bullet\}. \tag{2.8}$$

## 3. Towards a 'Matrix Spin Geometry'

**Remark 3.1.** Most results are proved for general signature, although the geometric meaning is identified only in Riemannian signature.

**Lemma 3.2.** For $\mu,\nu\in\Delta_4=\{0,1,2,3\}$ and any signature $\eta=\mathrm{diag}(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)$ in four dimensions:
$$\gamma^\mu\gamma^{\hat\nu}=(-1)^\mu\Big(\delta^\mu_\nu\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3+\mathrm{sgn}(\nu-\mu)\sum_{\alpha<\sigma}\delta_{\mu\nu\alpha\sigma}e_\mu\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\sigma\Big), \tag{3.3a}$$
$$\gamma^{\hat\mu}\gamma^\mu=-\gamma^\mu\gamma^{\hat\mu}, \tag{3.3b}$$
$$\gamma^{\hat\nu}\gamma^\mu=+\gamma^\mu\gamma^{\hat\nu}\qquad(\nu\neq\mu), \tag{3.3c}$$
$$\gamma^{\hat\mu}\gamma^{\hat\nu}=(-1)^{1+|\mu-\nu|}\sum_{\rho,\lambda}\tfrac12\delta_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}e_\lambda e_\rho\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu-1_V\delta^\mu_\nu\cdot e_\mu\cdot\det(\eta). \tag{3.3d}$$

**Lemma 3.3.** The square of the Dirac operator of a fuzzy geometry $G_f$ of signature $\eta=\mathrm{diag}(e_0,\dots,e_3)$ is
$$D_f^2=\sum_{\mu,\nu}1_V\otimes\eta^{\mu\nu}k_\mu\circ k_\nu+\tfrac12\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\otimes[k_\mu,k_\nu]_\circ-\sum_\mu\det(\eta)e_\mu1_V\otimes x_\mu\circ x_\mu \tag{3.5}$$
$$+\sum_{\mu<\nu}t_{\mu\nu}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\otimes[x_\mu,x_\nu]_\circ+\tfrac12\sum_{\mu,\nu,\sigma,\alpha}s_{\mu\nu\alpha\sigma}\cdot\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\sigma\otimes\{x_\nu,k_\mu\}_\circ+\tfrac{1}{\sigma(\eta)}\sum_\mu(-1)^\mu\gamma\otimes[x_\mu,k_\mu]_\circ,$$
where $[f,g]_\circ=f\circ g-g\circ f$ and
$$k_\mu:=\{K_\mu,\bullet\}_{e_\mu},\qquad x_\mu:=\{K_{\hat\mu},\bullet\}_{e_{\hat\mu}}. \tag{3.6}$$
The signs, defined when non-vanishing, are
$$s_{\mu\nu\alpha\sigma}:=e_\mu(-1)^\mu\cdot\mathrm{sgn}(\nu-\mu)\cdot\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma-\alpha)\cdot\delta_{\mu\nu\alpha\sigma}\in\{-1,0,+1\}, \tag{3.7}$$
$$t_{\mu\nu}:=\sum_{\lambda<\rho}(-1)^{1+|\mu-\nu|}\delta_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}e_\lambda e_\rho\in\{-1,0,+1\}. \tag{3.8}$$

## 4. Gauge Matrix Spectral Triples

**Definition 4.1.** A gauge matrix spectral triple is the spectral triple product $G_f\times F$ of a fuzzy geometry $G_f$ with a finite geometry $F=(\mathcal{A}_F,\mathcal{H}_F,D_F,J_F,\gamma_F)$, with $\dim\mathcal{A}_F<\infty$. If $\mathcal{A}_F=M_n(\mathbb{C})$ and $\mathcal{H}_F=M_n(\mathbb{C})$ with $2\leq n$, then $G_f\times F$ is a **Yang–Mills–Higgs matrix spectral triple**. If moreover $D_F=0$, it is a **Yang–Mills matrix spectral triple**.

**Theorem 4.2.** On the Yang–Mills matrix spectral triple over a four-dimensional fuzzy geometry of type $(p,q)$, i.e. of signature $\eta=\mathrm{diag}(+_p,-_q)$, the fluctuated Dirac operator $D=D_f\otimes 1_F$ reads
$$D_{\text{gauge}}:=D_{\omega_f}=\sum_\mu\gamma^\mu\otimes\{\mathsf{K}_\mu+\mathsf{A}_\mu,\bullet\}_{e_\mu}+\gamma^{\hat\mu}\otimes\{\mathsf{X}_\mu+\mathsf{S}_\mu,\bullet\}_{e_{\hat\mu}}, \tag{4.4}$$
where $\mathsf{A}_\mu,\mathsf{S}_\mu\in\Omega^1_D(\mathcal{A}_f\otimes\mathcal{A}_F)$ satisfy
$$(\mathsf{A}_\mu)^*=e_\mu\mathsf{A}_\mu,\qquad (\mathsf{S}_\mu)^*=(-1)^{q+1}e_\mu\mathsf{S}_\mu. \tag{4.5}$$
Here $\{A,B\}_\pm=AB\pm BA$, with the sign depending on $e_\mu,e_{\hat\mu}\in\{+1,-1\}$.

**Lemma 4.3 (Fluctuations with respect to $\mathsf{K}_\mu$).** With $X_\mu=K_{\hat\mu}=0$, the fluctuated Dirac operator is
$$D_{\text{gauge}}|_{X=0}=\sum_\mu\gamma^\mu\otimes\big\{\mathsf{K}_\mu+\mathsf{A}_\mu,\bullet\big\}_{e_\mu},\quad\text{where } e_\mu(\mathsf{A}_\mu)^*=\mathsf{A}_\mu\in\Omega^1_D(\mathcal{A}). \tag{4.6}$$

**Lemma 4.4 (Fluctuations with respect to $\mathsf{X}_\mu$).** With additionally $K_\mu=0$,
$$D_{\text{gauge}}|_{K=0}=\sum_\mu\gamma^{\hat\mu}\otimes\big\{\mathsf{X}_\mu+\mathsf{S}_\mu,\bullet\big\}_{e_{\hat\mu}},\qquad (-1)^{q+1}e_\mu(\mathsf{S}_\mu)^*=\mathsf{S}_\mu\in\Omega^1_D(\mathcal{A}). \tag{4.11}$$

**Definition 4.5.** The (anti)commutators are abbreviated as
$$\text{k}_\mu:=\{\mathsf{K}_\mu,\bullet\}_{e_\mu},\quad \text{x}_\mu:=\{\mathsf{X}_\mu,\bullet\}_{e_{\hat\mu}}=\{\mathsf{K}_{\hat\mu},\bullet\}_{e_{\hat\mu}},\quad \text{a}_\mu:=\{\mathsf{A}_\mu,\bullet\}_{e_\mu},\quad \text{s}_\mu:=\{\mathsf{S}_\mu,\bullet\}_{e_{\hat\mu}}. \tag{4.12a–b}$$
One has $k_\mu\otimes 1_F=\text{k}_\mu$. The field strength $\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{A}_f\otimes\mathcal{A}_F)$ is
$$\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}:=[\text{d}_\mu,\text{d}_\nu]=[\text{k}_\mu+\text{a}_\mu,\text{k}_\nu+\text{a}_\nu]_\circ,\qquad \text{where } \text{d}_\mu:=\text{k}_\mu+\text{a}_\mu. \tag{4.13}$$

**Proposition 4.6.** The square of the fluctuated Dirac operator of a flat Yang–Mills gauge matrix spectral triple ($X=0$, $x=s=0$) is
$$D_{\text{gauge}}^2|_{X=0}=\tfrac12\sum_{\mu,\nu}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\otimes\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}+1_V\otimes\vartheta, \tag{4.14}$$
where
$$\vartheta:=\sum_{\mu,\nu}\eta^{\mu\nu}(\text{a}_\mu+\text{k}_\mu)\circ(\text{a}_\nu+\text{k}_\nu). \tag{4.15}$$

**Proposition 4.7.** The fluctuated Dirac operator of a finite Yang–Mills geometry satisfies
$$D_{\text{gauge}}^2=\sum_{\mu,\nu}1_V\otimes\eta^{\mu\nu}(\text{k}_\mu+\text{a}_\mu)\circ(\text{k}_\nu+\text{a}_\nu)+\tfrac12\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\otimes[\text{k}_\mu+\text{a}_\mu,\text{k}_\nu+\text{a}_\nu]_\circ$$
$$+\sum_\mu\det(\eta)(-e_\mu)1_V\otimes(\text{x}_\mu+\text{s}_\mu)\circ(\text{x}_\mu+\text{s}_\mu)$$
$$+\sum_{\mu<\nu}t_{\mu\nu}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\otimes[\text{x}_\mu+\text{s}_\mu,\text{x}_\nu+\text{s}_\nu]_\circ+\tfrac12\sum_{\mu,\nu,\sigma,\alpha}s_{\mu\nu\alpha\sigma}\cdot\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\sigma\otimes\{\text{x}_\nu+\text{s}_\nu,\text{k}_\mu+\text{a}_\mu\}_\circ$$
$$+\sum_\mu(-1)^\mu\gamma\otimes[\text{x}_\mu+\text{s}_\mu,\text{k}_\mu+\text{a}_\mu]_\circ. \tag{4.20}$$

**Proposition 4.8.** Let $G_1\times G_2=G_f\times F$ be a gauge matrix geometry, with algebra $\mathcal{A}=A_1\otimes A_2$, $A_1=M_{n_1}(\mathbb{C})$, $A_2=M_{n_2}(\mathbb{C})$, and reality $J=J_1\otimes J_2$. The gauge group is
$$\mathsf{G}(\mathcal{A},J)=\mathrm{PU}(n_1)\times\mathrm{PU}(n_2).$$

**Lemma 4.9.** For $\mathcal{A}$ and $J$ as in Proposition 4.8, $\mathcal{U}(Z(\mathcal{A}))=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A}_J)$.

**Lemma 4.10.** The following is a short exact sequence of groups:
$$1\to\mathbb{C}^\times\to\{\mathbb{R}^+\times\mathrm{U}(n_1)\}\times_{|\det|}\{\mathbb{R}^+\times\mathrm{U}(n_2)\}\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathcal{U}(A_1\otimes A_2)\to 1,$$
where
$$\{\mathbb{R}^+\times\mathrm{U}(n_1)\}\times_{|\det|}\{\mathbb{R}^+\times\mathrm{U}(n_2)\}:=\{(\rho_1,u_1,\rho_2,u_2)\in\mathbb{R}^+\times\mathrm{U}(n_1)\times\mathbb{R}^+\times\mathrm{U}(n_2)\mid\rho_1\rho_2=1\}. \tag{4.28}$$
*(Footnote 9: this group is isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^+\times\mathrm{U}(n_1)\times\mathrm{U}(n_2)$; the full notation and embedding are kept for later convenience.)*

**Lemma 4.11.** The following group sequence is exact:
$$1\to\mathbb{C}^\times\to\mathbb{C}^\times\times_{|\det|}\mathbb{C}^\times\to\mathcal{U}\{Z(A_1\otimes A_2)\}\to 1.$$
The middle group is the subgroup of $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^2$ whose entries $(z_1,z_2)$ satisfy $|z_1|=|z_2|^{-1}$.

**Lemma 4.12.** The special unitary group $\mathcal{SU}(A_1\otimes A_2)=\{u_1\otimes u_2\in\mathcal{U}(A_1\otimes A_2)\mid \det(u_1\otimes u_2)=1\}$ fits in a short exact sequence
$$1\to\mathrm{U}(1)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{U}(n_1)\times_{\det}\mathrm{U}(n_2)\xrightarrow{\kappa}\mathcal{SU}(A_1\otimes A_2)\to 1,$$
where $\mathrm{U}(n_1)\times_{\det}\mathrm{U}(n_2)$ is the categorical pullback of either morphism in (4.29) along the other.

**Lemma 4.13.** The following sequence of groups is exact:
$$1\to\mu_{\mathrm{mcd}(n_1,n_2)}\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(n_1)\times\mathrm{SU}(n_2)\xrightarrow{\xi}\mathrm{U}(n_1)\times_{\det}\mathrm{U}(n_2)\xrightarrow{\zeta}\mu_{n_1\cdot n_2}\to 1,$$
where $\mathrm{mcd}(n_1,n_2)$ is the maximum common divisor of $n_1$ and $n_2$.

## 5. Yang–Mills–Higgs Theory with Finite-Dimensional Algebras

**Proposition 5.1.** The inner fluctuations of the Dirac operator along the finite geometry $F$ are
$$(\omega_F+J\omega_FJ^{-1})(\Psi)=(\gamma\otimes\phi)(\Psi)+\epsilon''(\gamma\otimes 1_{M_N(\mathbb{C})}\otimes 1_{M_n(\mathbb{C})})\Psi(1_V\otimes\phi), \tag{5.2}$$
for each $\Psi\in\mathcal{H}=V\otimes M_N(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{A}_F$. These are parametrised by $\phi\in M_N(\mathbb{C})\otimes\Omega^1_{D_F}(M_n(\mathbb{C}))$, and $\phi^*=\phi$.

**Corollary 5.2.** On the flat ($x=0$) fuzzy space factor $G_f$ of a gauge matrix geometry $G_f\times F$, the fluctuated Dirac operator satisfies
$$D_\omega^2|_{X=0}=\tfrac12\sum_{\mu,\nu}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\otimes\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}+1_V\otimes(\vartheta+\Phi^2)+\sum_\mu\gamma^\mu\gamma\otimes[\text{d}_\mu,\Phi]. \tag{5.6}$$

**Proposition 5.3.** In Riemannian signature, the field strength of a Yang–Mills(–Higgs) finite geometry transforms under the gauge group as
$$\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}=[\mathsf{F}_{\mu\nu},\bullet]\mapsto\mathscr{F}^u_{\mu\nu}=[\mathsf{F}^u_{\mu\nu},\bullet],\qquad u\in\mathsf{G}(\mathcal{A};J), \tag{5.10}$$
which is determined by
$$\mathsf{F}_{\mu\nu}\mapsto\mathsf{F}^u_{\mu\nu}=u\mathsf{F}_{\mu\nu}u^*=\mathrm{Ad}_u(\mathsf{F}_{\mu\nu}). \tag{5.11}$$

**Remark 5.4.** Since $\mathsf{L}_\mu$ is the fuzzy analogue of the derivatives, the commutator $[\mathsf{L}_\mu,\mathsf{L}_\nu]$ is the analogue of $[\partial_\mu,\partial_\nu]$, which is identically zero on $C^\infty(M)$. (The precise statement is that $\mathrm{ad}_{[\mathsf{L}_\mu,\mathsf{L}_\nu]}$ is the analogue of $[\partial_\mu,\partial_\nu]$; the Jacobi identity used as in Eq. (5.7) lets one state this in terms of $[\mathsf{L}_\mu,\mathsf{L}_\nu]$ alone.) This seems to imply freedom of choice in defining the field strength matrix; as the text notes, it does not.
