# Restatement

## Introduction

### Quillen's theorem (Section 1.1)

Let $G$ be a finite group and $k$ a field of characteristic $p>0$. Let $E_p(G)$ denote the family of elementary abelian $p$-subgroups of $G$. A class $x\in H^r(BG;k)$ determines, for each $A\in E_p(G)$, a class $x_A\in H^r(BA;k)$ by restriction. These tuples satisfy:

1. If $A,A'\leqslant G$ are elementary abelian $p$-subgroups conjugate by $g\in G$, then $x_A$ maps to $x_{A'}$ under the isomorphism $H^*(BA;k)\cong H^*(BA';k)$ induced by conjugation by $g$.
2. If $A\leqslant A'$ are elementary abelian subgroups of $G$, then $x_{A'}$ maps to $x_A$ under the restriction $H^*(BA';k)\to H^*(BA;k)$.

Let $R\subseteq\prod_{A\in E_p(G)}H^*(BA;k)$ be the subring of all tuples $\{x_A\}$ satisfying 1 and 2. The product of the restriction maps gives a ring homomorphism

$$H^*(BG;k)\xrightarrow{\ \psi\ }R,\qquad x\mapsto\{x_A\}_{A\in E_p(G)}. \tag{1.1}$$

**Theorem 1.2** ([Qui71b, Theorem 7.1]). The map $\psi$ is a uniform $\mathcal{F}_p$-isomorphism: there exist integers $m$ and $n$ such that

1. For every $x\in\ker\psi$, $x^m=0$.
2. For every $x\in R$, $x^{p^n}$ belongs to the image of $\psi$.

### Descent up to nilpotence (Section 1.2)

In the homotopy theory $\mathrm{Fun}(BG,\mathrm{Mod}(k))$, the commutative algebra objects $\{k^{G/A}\}_{A\in E_p(G)}$ generate, as a thick $\otimes$-ideal, all of $\mathrm{Fun}(BG,\mathrm{Mod}(k))$. Theorem 1.2 is cited as following from this statement (compare [Mat16a, §4.2]).

### $\mathscr{F}$-nilpotence (Section 1.3)

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a presentable stable $\infty$-category with a symmetric monoidal structure such that $\otimes$ preserves colimits in each variable. An object is $A$-nilpotent if it lies in the thick $\otimes$-ideal generated by the algebra object $A$.

**Definition** (See Definition 6.36 below). Let $G$ be a finite group, and let $\mathrm{Sp}_G$ denote the $\infty$-category of $G$-spectra (see Definition 5.10). Let $\mathscr{F}$ be a family of subgroups of $G$. We say that $M\in\mathrm{Sp}_G$ is $\mathscr{F}$-nilpotent if it is nilpotent with respect to the algebra object $\prod_{H\in\mathscr{F}}F(G/H_+,S^0_G)\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_G)$.

For a ring $G$-spectrum $R$, the text states (6.41) that $R$ is $\mathscr{F}$-nilpotent if and only if the geometric fixed points $\Phi^H R$ are contractible for every subgroup $H\leq G$ not belonging to $\mathscr{F}$.

**Theorem** (in Section 1.3). Suppose $R\in\mathrm{Alg}(\mathrm{Sp}_G)$ is $\mathscr{F}$-nilpotent. Let $\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{F}}(G)$ be the category of $G$-sets of the form $G/H$, $H\in\mathscr{F}$. Then there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories

$$\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{Sp}_G}(R)\simeq\varprojlim_{G/H\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{F}}(G)^{op}}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{Sp}_H}(\mathrm{Res}^G_H R).$$

If $R$ is an $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-algebra object, the equivalence is (canonically) symmetric monoidal.

### Equivariant module spectra (Section 1.4)

**Theorem 1.3.** Let $R$ be an even periodic $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring. Then there is an equivalence of symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories

$$\mathrm{Fun}(BU(n),\mathrm{Mod}(R))\simeq\mathrm{Mod}\big(F(BU(n)_+,R)\big)_{\mathrm{cpl}},$$

where on the right we consider modules complete with respect to the augmentation ideal in $\pi_0\big(F(BU(n)_+,R)\big)\simeq\pi_0(R)[[c_1,\dots,c_n]]$.

**Theorem 1.4.** Let $R$ be an $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring and let $G$ be a compact, connected Lie group. Suppose $H^*(BG;\pi_0R)\simeq H^*(BG;\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_0R$, and that this is a polynomial ring over $\pi_0R$. Suppose furthermore that the cohomological $R$-based Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence (AHSS) for $BG$ degenerates (e.g. $\pi_*(R)$ is torsion-free). Then there is an equivalence of symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories between $\mathrm{Fun}(BG,\mathrm{Mod}(R))$ and the symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category of $R$-complete $F(BG_+,R)$-modules.

**Theorem 1.5.** Let $R$ be a complex-orientable $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-ring. Then, if $G$ is a finite group, the Borel-equivariant $G$-spectrum associated to $R$ is $\mathscr{F}$-nilpotent for $\mathscr{F}$ the family of abelian subgroups.

**Theorem 1.6.** Let $G$ be a compact, connected Lie group with $\pi_1(G)$ torsion-free. Then the symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories $\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{Sp}_G}(KU_G)$ and $\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{Sp}_G}(KO_G)$ are equivalent to the symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories of modules (in spectra) over the categorical fixed points of $KU_G$ and $KO_G$ respectively.

**Theorem 1.7.** Let $G$ be any compact Lie group. Then there is an equivalence of $KO_G$-modules
$$KO_G\wedge\Sigma^{-2}\mathbb{CP}^2\simeq KU_G,$$
where $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is a pointed space with trivial $G$-action.

## Section 2: Adams towers, cobar constructions and completion

**Construction 2.2 (The Adams tower).** Let $M\in\mathcal{C}$. Form a tower
$$\dots\to T_2(A,M)\to T_1(A,M)\to T_0(A,M)\simeq M \tag{2.3}$$
as follows.

1. $T_1(A,M)$ is the fiber of the map $M\to A\otimes M$ induced by the unit $\mathbf{1}\to A$, so that $T_1(A,M)$ maps naturally to $M$.
2. More generally, $T_i(A,M):=T_1(A,T_{i-1}(A,M))$ with its natural map to $T_{i-1}(A,M)$.

This defines the functors $T_i$ and the tower inductively. It is called the $A$-Adams tower of $M$. The $A$-Adams tower of $M$ is the tensor product of $M$ with the $A$-Adams tower of $\mathbf{1}$.

**Example 2.4.** Take $\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbb{Z})$ and $A=\mathbb{Z}/p$. The Adams tower $\{T_i(\mathbb{Z}/p,M)\}$ of $M\in\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbb{Z})$ is
$$\dots\to M\xrightarrow{\,p\,}M\xrightarrow{\,p\,}M.$$

**Proposition 2.5.**
1. For each $i$, the cofiber of $T_i(A,M)\to T_{i-1}(A,M)$ admits the structure of an $A$-module (internal to $\mathcal{C}$).
2. Each map $T_i(A,M)\to T_{i-1}(A,M)$ becomes nullhomotopic after tensoring with $A$.

**Corollary 2.6.** Suppose $M\in\mathcal{C}$ is an $A$-module up to homotopy. Then the successive maps $T_i(A,M)\to T_{i-1}(A,M)$ in the Adams tower are nullhomotopic.

**Construction 2.7.** Given $A\in\mathrm{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$, form the cosimplicial object
$$\mathrm{CB}^\bullet(A)=\left\{A\rightrightarrows A\otimes A\begin{smallmatrix}\to\\ \to\\ \to\end{smallmatrix}\dots\right\}\in\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta,\mathcal{C}),$$
called the cobar construction on $A$. It extends to an augmented cosimplicial object $\mathrm{CB}^\bullet_{\mathrm{aug}}(A):N(\Delta_+)\to\mathcal{C}$, where the augmentation is from $\mathbf{1}$. The augmented cosimplicial object $\mathrm{CB}^\bullet_{\mathrm{aug}}(A)$ admits a splitting [Lur16, §4.7.3] after tensoring with $A$; that is, $\mathrm{CB}^\bullet_{\mathrm{aug}}(A)\otimes A$ is split.

**Theorem 2.8** (Lurie [Lur16, Th. 1.2.4.1]; $\infty$-categorical Dold–Kan correspondence). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a stable $\infty$-category. Then the functor
$$\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta,\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}^{op},\mathcal{C}),\qquad X^\bullet\mapsto\{\mathrm{Tot}_n(X^\bullet)\}_{n\geq0}$$
establishes an equivalence between cosimplicial objects in $\mathcal{C}$ and towers in $\mathcal{C}$.

**Definition 2.9.** For a finite nonempty set $S$, let $\mathcal{P}(S)$ denote the partially ordered set of nonempty subsets of $S$ ordered by inclusion. Let $\mathcal{P}^+(S)$ denote the partially ordered set of all subsets of $S$ ordered by inclusion.

**Construction 2.10.** Suppose given morphisms $f_s:X_s\to Y_s$ in $\mathcal{C}$ for each $s\in S$. This gives a functor $F^+(\{f_s\}):\mathcal{P}^+(S)\to\mathcal{C}$ with value on $S'\subset S$
$$F^+(\{f_s\})(S')=\bigotimes_{s_1\notin S'}X_{s_1}\otimes\bigotimes_{s_2\in S'}Y_{s_2}.$$
Let $F(\{f_s\}):\mathcal{P}(S)\to\mathcal{C}$ denote the restriction of $F^+(\{f_s\})$.

**Proposition 2.11.** Let $S$ be a finite nonempty set and let $f_s:X_s\to Y_s$ be morphisms in $\mathcal{C}$ for each $s\in S$. Form $F(\{f_s\})$ as in 2.10. Then there is an identification, functorial in $\prod_{s\in S}\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^1,\mathcal{C})$,
$$\varprojlim_{\mathcal{P}(S)}F(\{f_s\})\simeq\mathrm{cofib}\left(\bigotimes_{s\in S}\mathrm{fib}(X_s\to Y_s)\to\bigotimes_{s\in S}X_s\right).$$

**Proposition 2.14.** The tower associated (via Dold–Kan) to the cosimplicial object $\mathrm{CB}^\bullet(A)$ is precisely
$$\{\mathrm{cofib}(T_{n+1}(A,\mathbf{1})\to\mathbf{1})\}.$$
In other words, $\mathrm{Tot}_n(\mathrm{CB}^\bullet(A))\simeq\mathrm{cofib}(I^{\otimes(n+1)}\to\mathbf{1})$.

**Definition 2.15.** An object $X\in\mathcal{C}$ is $A$-complete (or $A$-local) if, for any $Y\in\mathcal{C}$ with $Y\otimes A\simeq0$, the space $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,X)$ is contractible. The $A$-complete objects span a full subcategory $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}\subset\mathcal{C}$.

**Example 2.16.** For $\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbb{Z})$ and $A=\mathbb{Z}/p$, the $A$-complete objects are the $p$-adically complete objects.

**Example 2.17.** Suppose tensoring with $A$ is conservative on $\mathcal{C}$. For instance, this holds if $A$ is dualizable (cf. [Lur16, §4.6.1]) and $\mathbb{D}A$ generates $\mathcal{C}$ as a localizing subcategory. Then every object of $\mathcal{C}$ is $A$-complete.

**Example 2.18.** Suppose $M\in\mathcal{C}$ admits the structure of an $A$-module. Then $M$ is $A$-complete. Indeed, if $X\in\mathcal{C}$ is such that $A\otimes X$ is contractible, then
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,M)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)}(A\otimes X,M)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)}(0,M)\simeq0.$$

**Definition 2.19.** Let $L_A:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}$ denote the left adjoint to the inclusion $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}\subset\mathcal{C}$. It is called $A$-completion. $L_A$ is also used for the composite $\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}\subset\mathcal{C}$.

**Remark 2.20.** Colimits in $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}$ are computed by taking the colimit in $\mathcal{C}$ and then applying $L_A$. The composite $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}\subset\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\otimes A}\mathcal{C}$ preserves colimits.

**Proposition 2.21.** Suppose $A$ is dualizable. For any $M\in\mathcal{C}$, the map $M\to\mathrm{Tot}(M\otimes\mathrm{CB}^\bullet(A))$ exhibits the target as the $A$-completion of $M$.

**Proposition 2.23.** The following are equivalent for $M\in\mathcal{C}$ and $A\in\mathrm{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$, assumed dualizable in $\mathcal{C}$:
1. $M$ is $A$-complete.
2. The homotopy limit of the Adams tower $\{T_i(A,M)\}_{i\geq0}$ is contractible.
3. The augmented cosimplicial object $\mathrm{CB}^\bullet_{\mathrm{aug}}(A)\otimes M$ is a limit diagram.

**Corollary 2.24.** If $X\in\mathcal{C}$ is $A$-complete and $Y\in\mathcal{C}$ is dualizable, then $X\otimes Y$ is $A$-complete.

**Construction 2.25.** The object $\mathbb{D}A$ admits the structure of an $A$-module. There is a map $A\otimes\mathbb{D}A\to\mathbb{D}A$ which is (doubly) adjoint to the multiplication $A\otimes A\to A$. Alternatively, the module structure comes from applying the right adjoint $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,\cdot)$ of the forgetful functor $\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)\to\mathcal{C}$ to $\mathbf{1}$.

**Hypotheses 2.26.** $(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathbf{1})$ is a presentable, symmetric monoidal stable $\infty$-category where $\otimes$ commutes with colimits in each variable. Furthermore:
1. The unit $\mathbf{1}$ is compact.
2. The object $A$ is dualizable.
3. $\mathcal{C}$ is generated as a localizing subcategory by dualizable objects.

**Proposition 2.27.** Let $\mathcal{D}$ be a family of dualizable generators for $\mathcal{C}$. Then $\{\mathbb{D}A\otimes X\}_{X\in\mathcal{D}}$ forms a system of compact generators for $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}$.

**Proposition 2.28.** Suppose $A,B\in\mathrm{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$ are dualizable in $\mathcal{C}$. Then $X\in\mathcal{C}$ is $(A\otimes B)$-complete if and only if $X$ is both $A$-complete and $B$-complete.

**Theorem 2.30.** If $A\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})$ is dualizable in $\mathcal{C}$, then $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}$ can be recovered as the homotopy limit, in the $\infty$-category of symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories,
$$\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}\simeq\mathrm{Tot}\left(\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)\rightrightarrows\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A\otimes A)\begin{smallmatrix}\to\\ \to\\ \to\end{smallmatrix}\dots\right).$$

**Example 2.33.** Suppose tensoring with $A$ is conservative on $\mathcal{C}$. Then $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}}=\mathcal{C}$, and 2.30 is a descent theorem for $\mathcal{C}$ as a homotopy limit of modules over $\{A^{\otimes(n+1)}\}_{n\geq0}$. By [Mat16a, Th. 3.36], $A$ is descendable in $\mathcal{C}$, i.e. the thick $\otimes$-ideal it generates is all of $\mathcal{C}$. This descent theorem is [Mat16a, Prop. 3.21]. The decomposition of $\mathcal{C}$ as a homotopy limit does not require compactness of the unit, but descendability of $A$ does.

## Section 3: Torsion objects, acyclization and localization

**Definition 3.1.** The subcategory $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}$ of $A$-torsion objects is the smallest localizing subcategory of $\mathcal{C}$ containing $A\otimes X$ for $X\in\mathcal{C}$ dualizable.

**Construction 3.2.** By [Lur16, Cor. 1.4.4.2], $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}$ is presentable. By [Lur09b, Cor. 5.5.2.9], the inclusion $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}\subset\mathcal{C}$ is a left adjoint with right adjoint $\mathrm{AC}_A:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}$, called the $A$-acyclization functor. For any $X\in\mathcal{C}$ there is a natural counit map $\mathrm{AC}_A(X)\to X$.

**Proposition 3.3.** If $Y\in\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}$ and $X\in\mathcal{C}$, then $X\otimes Y\in\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}$.

**Construction 3.4.** Recall the Adams tower of $\mathbf{1}$: $\dots\to T_2(A,\mathbf{1})\to T_1(A,\mathbf{1})\to T_0(A,\mathbf{1})\simeq\mathbf{1}$. Since $A$ is dualizable, its objects are dualizable, so the dual tower $\mathbf{1}\to U_1\to U_2\to\dots$ exists, where $U_i:=\mathbb{D}(T_i(A,\mathbf{1}))$. Define $U_A=\varinjlim U_i$ and let $V_A$ be the fiber of $\mathbf{1}\to U_A$. Equivalently, $V_A=|\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{CB}^\bullet(A))|$.

**Proposition 3.5.** For any $X\in\mathcal{C}$ there is a natural equivalence $\mathrm{AC}_A(X)\simeq V_A\otimes X$. Therefore $X\in\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}$ if and only if the natural map $V_A\otimes X\to X$ is an equivalence.

**Example 3.6.** For $\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbb{Z})$ and $A=\mathbb{Z}/p$, the sequence $1\to U_1\to U_2\to\dots$ becomes $\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{p}\dots$, so $U_{\mathbb{Z}/p}=\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]$ and $V_{\mathbb{Z}/p}=\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]/\mathbb{Z})$.

**Proposition 3.7.** Let $\mathcal{D}$ be a collection of dualizable generators for $\mathcal{C}$. Then $\{\mathbb{D}A\otimes X\}_{X\in\mathcal{D}}$ forms a system of compact generators for $\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}$.

**Proposition 3.8.** Let $A,B\in\mathrm{Alg}(\mathcal{C})$ be dualizable. Then $X\in\mathcal{C}$ is $(A\otimes B)$-torsion if and only if it is both $A$-torsion and $B$-torsion.

**Theorem 3.9** (Cf. [HPS97, Th. 3.3.5]). $A$-completion gives an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$L_A:\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-cpl}},$$
whose inverse is $\mathrm{AC}_A$.

**Definition 3.10.** An object $X\in\mathcal{C}$ is $A^{-1}$-local if $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,X)\simeq0$ for every $Y\in\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}$. Let $\mathcal{C}[A^{-1}]\subset\mathcal{C}$ denote the full subcategory of $A^{-1}$-local objects.

**Proposition 3.11.** An object $X\in\mathcal{C}$ is $A^{-1}$-local if and only if $A\otimes X$ is contractible.

**Construction 3.12.** Recall the sequence $\mathbf{1}\to U_1\to U_2\to\dots$ and the cofiber sequence $V_A\to\mathbf{1}\to U_A$. The cofiber of each $U_i\to U_{i+1}$ admits the structure of an $A$-module. For $X\in\mathcal{C}$, consider $X\to X[A^{-1}]:=X\otimes U_A$. The object $X[A^{-1}]$ is $A^{-1}$-local. If $Y$ is $A^{-1}$-local, the natural map $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X[A^{-1}],Y)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)$ is an equivalence, since its fiber is $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_A\otimes X,Y)$ and $V_A\otimes X\in\mathcal{C}_{A\text{-tors}}$. Hence $X\to X[A^{-1}]$ is $A^{-1}$-localization, the left adjoint to $\mathcal{C}[A^{-1}]\subset\mathcal{C}$. $A^{-1}$-localization is smashing: it is tensoring with $\mathbf{1}[A^{-1}]\simeq U_A$.

**Example 3.13.** If $\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbb{Z})$ and $A=\mathbb{Z}/p$, then $A^{-1}$-localization is $p^{-1}$-localization, i.e. tensoring with $\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]$.

**Remark 3.14.** These localizations are called finite localizations in [Mil92]. They localize away from the compact objects $\mathbb{D}A\otimes X$ for $X$ dualizable. See also [HPS97, §3.3].

**Proposition 3.15.** Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism in $\mathcal{C}$. Then $f$ is an equivalence if and only if both $1_A\otimes f:A\otimes X\to A\otimes Y$ and $f[A^{-1}]:X[A^{-1}]\to Y[A^{-1}]$ are equivalences.

**Construction 3.16.** For any $X\in\mathcal{C}$ there is a commutative square (3.17) with corners $X$, $L_AX$, $X[A^{-1}]$, … (the text is cut off here, so the remainder of the square is not given).
