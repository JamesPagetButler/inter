# Restatement

## Abstract

The article gives a formula for the scalar curvature and Ricci curvature of a $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure in terms of its torsion. When the fundamental 3-form $\sigma$ is closed, this formula implies that the scalar curvature of the underlying metric $g_\sigma$ is nonpositive, and that it vanishes if and only if the structure is torsion-free. The article also contains results on the pinching of the Ricci curvature of metrics associated to closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures. It derives formulae for closed solutions of the Laplacian flow that specify how the torsion and the metric evolve along the flow.

## 1. Introduction

**Definition 1 (The group $\mathrm{G}_2$):** (2.2)
$$\mathrm{G}_2=\left\{\, g\in\operatorname{GL}(7,\mathbb{R}) \;\middle|\; g^*(\phi)=\phi \,\right\}.$$

## Remarks and definitions in order of appearance

**Remark 1 (The vector cross product definition of $\mathrm{G}_2$):** Let $V$ be a real vector space with a positive definite inner product $\langle\,,\rangle$. A (2-fold) vector cross product on $(V,\langle\,,\rangle)$ is a skew-symmetric bilinear map $\times:V\times V\to V$ such that, for all $v_1,v_2\in V$, (2.34)
$$\langle v_1\times v_2,\,v_1\rangle=0 \quad\text{and}\quad |v_1\times v_2|^2=|v_1|^2|v_2|^2-\langle v_1,v_2\rangle^2.$$
It can be shown that the $\operatorname{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})$-stabilizer of the vector cross product $\times_\phi$ is equal to $\mathrm{G}_2$. Hence $\times_\phi:\mathbb{R}^7\times\mathbb{R}^7\to\mathbb{R}^7$ can be taken as the algebraic structure defining $\mathrm{G}_2$. The article does not use vector cross products in any significant way; it uses the 3-form formulation.

**Definition 2 (Definite 3-forms on manifolds):** A 3-form $\sigma$ on $M$ that takes values in $\Lambda^3_+(T^*M)$ is called a *definite 3-form* on $M$. The set of definite 3-forms on $M$ is denoted $\Omega^3_+(M)$.

**Remark 2 (Alternative terminologies):** Some authors use "almost $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure" for what the article calls a $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure. In the article, for a subgroup $G\subset\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$, a $G$-structure on an $n$-manifold $M$ is a $G$-subbundle of the frame (or coframe) bundle of $M$. The article also states that the modifiers "integrable", "almost", "nearly" and similar words will not be used when referring to $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures. Some authors use "integrable $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure" for a $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure $\sigma\in\Omega^3_+(M)$ satisfying differential equations such as $\mathrm{d}\sigma=0$. The article states that no first-order condition on a $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure implies that it is locally equivalent to the flat model. The term "$\mathrm{G}_2$-manifold" is used to denote a manifold with a $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure that is flat to first order (i.e. "torsion-free" in the usual terminology).

**Remark 3 (Existence of $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures):** A connected 7-manifold $M$ admits a $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure if and only if it is orientable and spinnable, i.e. its first two Stiefel–Whitney classes vanish. The necessity follows because $\mathrm{G}_2$ is connected and simply connected. For sufficiency (due to Gray [9]): $\mathrm{G}_2$ is the image of a unique subgroup of $\operatorname{Spin}(7)$ under the double covering $\rho:\operatorname{Spin}(7)\to\operatorname{SO}(7)$, also denoted $\mathrm{G}_2$. The restriction of the faithful $\operatorname{Spin}(7)$-representation on $\mathbb{R}^8$ to $\mathrm{G}_2$ is isomorphic to $\mathsf{V}_{0,0}\oplus\mathsf{V}_{1,0}$. Hence $\operatorname{Spin}(7)$ acts transitively on the unit 7-sphere in $\mathbb{R}^8$ with stabilizer $\mathrm{G}_2$. If $M^7$ is orientable and spinnable, choose a Riemannian metric $g$, an orientation, and a spin structure $\tilde{\mathsf{F}}\to M$. The spinor bundle $\mathbb{S}=\tilde{\mathsf{F}}\times_{\operatorname{Spin}(7)}\mathbb{R}^8$ has rank 8 and therefore has a nowhere-vanishing unit section $s$. This reduces the structure group of $\tilde{\mathsf{F}}$, and hence of $\mathsf{F}$, from $\operatorname{Spin}(7)$ to $\mathrm{G}_2$. Thus $M$ admits a $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure whose associated metric and orientation are the chosen ones.

**Definition 3 (Associated metric, orientation, and vector cross product):** For $\sigma\in\Omega^3_+(M)$, $g_\sigma$, $*_\sigma$, and $\times_\sigma$ denote the metric, Hodge star operator, and vector cross product on $M$ canonically associated to $\sigma$. The oriented orthonormal frame bundle of $g_\sigma$, with this orientation, is denoted $\mathsf{F}_\sigma=F_\sigma\cdot\operatorname{SO}(7)$.

**Remark 4 ($\mathrm{G}_2$-structures with the same associated metric and orientation):** Let $a$ be a function and $\alpha$ a 1-form on $M$ with $a^2+|\alpha|^2_\sigma=1$. Then the 3-form (3.6)
$$\tilde\sigma=\bigl(a^2-|\alpha|^2_\sigma\bigr)\,\sigma+2a\,*_\sigma(\alpha\wedge\sigma)+\mathsf{i}(\alpha\circ\alpha)$$
is definite and has the same associated metric and orientation as $\sigma$. (The article notes that this is most easily checked for $\sigma=\phi$ and $(a,\alpha)=(c,s\,e^1)$ with $c^2+s^2=1$, using the transitivity of $\mathrm{G}_2$ on the unit 6-sphere in $\mathbb{R}^7$.) Conversely, any definite 3-form on $M$ with associated metric $g_\sigma$ and orientation $*_\sigma$ is of the form (3.6) for some pair $(a,\alpha)$ with $a^2+|\alpha|^2_\sigma=1$, unique up to replacement by $(-a,-\alpha)$. If $H^1(M,\mathbb{Z}_2)\neq0$, the pair $(a,\alpha)$ might only be defined up to sign. Since $\operatorname{SO}(7)/\mathrm{G}_2\simeq\mathbb{RP}^7$, the formula (3.6) displays a concrete isomorphism between the bundle $\mathsf{F}_\sigma/\mathrm{G}_2$ and the $\mathbb{RP}^7$-bundle $\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{R}\oplus T^*M)$ over $M$.

**Proposition 1 (The torsion forms):** For any $\sigma\in\Omega^3_+(M)$ there exist unique forms $\tau_0\in\Omega^0(M)$, $\tau_1\in\Omega^1(M)$, $\tau_2\in\Omega^2_{14}(M,\sigma)$, and $\tau_3\in\Omega^3_{27}(M,\sigma)$ such that (3.7)
$$\mathrm{d}\sigma=\tau_0\,*_\sigma\sigma+3\,\tau_1\wedge\sigma+*_\sigma\tau_3,$$
$$\mathrm{d}(*_\sigma\sigma)=4\,\tau_1\wedge *_\sigma\sigma+\tau_2\wedge\sigma.$$
(The second identity is referred to as (3.8) in Remark 5.)

**Definition 4 (The torsion forms):** For $\sigma\in\Omega^3_+(M)$, the quadruple $(\tau_0,\tau_1,\tau_2,\tau_3)$ defined by (3.7) is called the *intrinsic torsion forms* of $\sigma$.

**Remark 5 (General intrinsic torsion):** For any subgroup $G\subset\operatorname{SO}(n)$, the first-order invariants (the "intrinsic torsion") of a $G$-structure $F$ on an $n$-manifold $M$ take values in a bundle associated to the natural $G$-representation on $(\mathfrak{so}(n)/\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathbb{R}^n$. (See §4.2 for further explanation.) If these first-order invariants vanish, the $G$-structure is called "1-flat" or "flat to first order". The existence of identity (3.8) can be understood from these general considerations. See [2] for more discussion.

**Proposition 2 (1-flatness of $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures):** A $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure $\sigma\in\Omega^3_+(M)$ is flat to first order if and only if all its torsion forms vanish, i.e. if and only if
$$\mathrm{d}\sigma=\mathrm{d}(*_\sigma\sigma)=0.$$

**Remark 6 (Fernández and Gray's theorem on vector cross products):** Proposition 2 implies the 1982 result of Fernández and Gray [6]: a vector cross product $\times:TM\times TM\to TM$ compatible with a Riemannian metric $g$ on $M$ is $g$-parallel if and only if the corresponding 3-form is closed and coclosed (with respect to $g$). Unlike Proposition 2, their result assumes a specific metric $g$ and vector cross product are given, whereas Proposition 2 starts with a definite 3-form $\sigma$ and constructs the associated metric.

**Remark 7 (Canonical connections):** The connection $\nabla$ on a $G$-structure $F$ called "natural" in the article is not the only canonical connection compatible with $F$. If the $G$-modules $V_1(\mathfrak{g})$ and $\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{R}^n$ have common constituents, so that $\dim\operatorname{Hom}^G\bigl(V_1(\mathfrak{g}),\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{R}^n\bigr)=r>0$, then there is an $r$-parameter family of ways to modify $\nabla$ by adding a $\mathfrak{g}$-valued 1-form whose coefficients are linear in the torsion functions. Each such modification defines a connection compatible with $F$. Equivalence of $G$-structures induces isomorphisms between the corresponding connections. Connection modifications that are not linear in the torsion functions are also possible, for example via any $G$-equivariant polynomial map $V_1(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{R}^n$.

**Remark 8 (Canonical $\mathrm{G}_2$-connections):** Since $\mathfrak{g}_2\otimes\mathsf{V}_{1,0}=\mathsf{V}_{1,0}\oplus\mathsf{V}_{2,0}\oplus\mathsf{V}_{1,1}$ shares two irreducible modules with $V_1(\mathfrak{g})$, there is a 2-parameter family of canonical connections associated to any $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure $\sigma$. Each is compatible with $\sigma$. The common constituents $\mathsf{V}_{1,0}$ and $\mathsf{V}_{2,0}$ correspond to $\tau_1$ and $\tau_3$. Hence the two-parameter family collapses to a single connection if and only if $\tau_1=\tau_3=0$. In that case, differentiating (3.7) shows that $\tau_0\tau_2=0$ and $\mathrm{d}\tau_0=0$. For connected $M$, $\tau_0$ is constant. If $\tau_0=0$, the structure is closed. If $\tau_0\neq0$, then $\tau_2=0$ and $\mathrm{d}\sigma=\tau_0\,*_\sigma\sigma$, which is the defining equation of nearly $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds. Thus the family of canonical $\mathrm{G}_2$-connections collapses to a single connection if and only if $\sigma$ is closed or defines a nearly $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifold.

**Remark 9 (Covariant differentials):** The forms $D\varphi$ and $D\theta$ [symbols as rendered in the source] are used to express the curvature tensor. They do not contain all the information about the second-order invariants of $\sigma$, but they contain enough to recover the Riemann curvature tensor of the underlying metric.

**Remark 10 (General identities):** The scalar curvature, as a $\mathrm{G}_2$-invariant, is a sum of a linear expression in the second-order invariants (a section of a bundle modelled on $V_2(\mathfrak{g}_2)$) and an expression in the first-order invariants (the torsion forms) that is at most quadratic. The representation $V_2(\mathfrak{g}_2)$ has only one trivial summand, so there is essentially one possible second-order term up to a constant multiple. Since $\delta\tau_1$ is a scalar second-order invariant, it represents this copy of $\mathsf{V}_{0,0}$. Since $V_1(\mathfrak{g}_2)$ consists of four mutually inequivalent $\mathrm{G}_2$-modules, the space of $\mathrm{G}_2$-invariant quadratic forms on it has dimension 4, and it is represented by the squared norms of the four torsion forms. Thus a formula of the form (4.28) is forced, and its numerical coefficients were determined with the aid of Maple. The trace-free Ricci formula (4.30) has an analogous form: it uses linear terms in $V_2(\mathfrak{g}_2)$ and quadratic terms in $V_1(\mathfrak{g}_2)$ taking values in $\mathsf{V}_{2,0}$. There are at most three second-order $\mathsf{V}_{2,0}$-terms, obtained from derivatives of $\tau_1$, $\tau_2$, and $\tau_3$, and these are independent. The second symmetric power of $V_1(\mathfrak{g}_2)$ contains eight copies of $\mathsf{V}_{2,0}$. Five of these are computable via the wedge product and appear in the Ricci formula. Of the remaining three, one is bilinear in $\tau_2$ and $\tau_3$, and of the other two, which are quadratic in $\tau_3$, only one term appears.

**Remark 11 (Differential invariants of closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures):** Let $V'_k(\mathfrak{g}_2)\subset V_k(\mathfrak{g}_2)$ denote the module of $k$-th order differential invariants of closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures. Then (4.38)
$$V'_1(\mathfrak{g}_2)\simeq\mathsf{V}_{0,1},\qquad V'_2(\mathfrak{g}_2)\simeq\mathsf{V}_{2,0}\oplus\mathsf{V}_{1,1}\oplus\mathsf{V}_{0,2}.$$
Since $V'_2(\mathfrak{g}_2)$ has no $\mathsf{V}_{0,0}$ component, the scalar curvature of a closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure must be expressed in terms of the first-order invariants. The full Ricci tensor can be expressed in terms of $\tau_2$ and $\mathrm{d}\tau_2$. Locally, if $\beta\in\Omega^2(\mathbb{R}^7)$ vanishes to second order at $0$, then $\sigma=\phi+\mathrm{d}\beta$ is a closed definite 3-form on a neighbourhood of $0$. Conversely, if $\sigma$ is a closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure on $M^7$, every $p\in M$ has a neighbourhood $U$ and a $p$-centred chart $x:U\to\mathbb{R}^7$ with $\sigma_U=x^*(\phi+\mathrm{d}\beta)$, where $\beta$ vanishes to second order at $0$. In Cartan's sense of the generality of solutions of a system of PDE, the general closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure modulo diffeomorphism depends on 8 functions of 7 variables.

**Corollary 1:** For any closed $\sigma\in\Omega^3_+(M)$, the scalar curvature of $g_\sigma$ is non-positive, and it vanishes identically if and only if the entire Ricci tensor of $g_\sigma$ vanishes. Equivalently, the scalar curvature vanishes identically if and only if $\mathrm{d}\sigma=\mathrm{d}(*_\sigma\sigma)=0$.

**Corollary 2:** A closed $\sigma\in\Omega^3_+(M)$ has an Einstein underlying metric if and only if
$$\mathrm{d}(*_\sigma\sigma)=\tau_2\wedge\sigma,$$
where
$$\mathrm{d}\tau_2=\tfrac{3}{14}\,|\tau_2|^2\,\sigma+\tfrac12\,*_\sigma(\tau_2\wedge\tau_2).$$

**Remark 12 (Nonexistence of compact Einstein examples):** The article does not know whether there exist closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures that are Einstein but not Ricci-flat, even local (incomplete) ones. After Version 1.0 of the article was posted to the arXiv, Cleyton and Ivanov [3] showed that no compact 7-manifold can carry a closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure $\sigma$ with Einstein $g_\sigma$ unless $\sigma$ is also coclosed, i.e. $\mathrm{d}(*_\sigma\sigma)=0$. Corollary 2 gives a simple proof. Suppose $\sigma$ is closed, $g_\sigma$ is Einstein, and $M$ is compact. By Corollary 2, $\mathrm{d}(*_\sigma\sigma)=\tau_2\wedge\sigma$ with $\mathrm{d}\tau_2=\tfrac{3}{14}|\tau_2|^2\sigma+\tfrac12*_\sigma(\tau_2\wedge\tau_2)$. Using this and (2.21), (4.40)
$$\mathrm{d}\bigl(\tfrac13\tau_2^3\bigr)=\tau_2^2\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau_2=\tau_2^2\wedge\Bigl(\tfrac{3}{14}|\tau_2|^2\sigma+\tfrac12*_\sigma(\tau_2\wedge\tau_2)\Bigr)=-\tfrac{3}{14}|\tau_2|^4*_\sigma1+\tfrac12|\tau_2\wedge\tau_2|^2*_\sigma1=\tfrac27|\tau_2|^4*_\sigma1.$$
Integrating over the compact $M$ and applying Stokes' theorem gives (4.41)
$$0=\int_M\mathrm{d}\bigl(\tfrac13\tau_2^3\bigr)=\int_M\tfrac27|\tau_2|^4*_\sigma1,$$
so $\tau_2\equiv0$.

**Corollary 3:** Let $\sigma\in\Omega^3_+(M)$ be a closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure on a compact 7-manifold $M$ satisfying the pinching condition (4.42)
$$\bigl|\operatorname{Ric}^0(g_\sigma)\bigr|^2\le\tfrac{4}{21}\,C\,\operatorname{Scal}(g_\sigma)^2$$
for some constant $C\le1$. If $C<1$, then $\sigma$ is also coclosed. If $C=1$, equality must hold in (4.42) everywhere on $M$, and moreover
(4.43)
$$\mathsf{i}\bigl(\operatorname{Ric}^0(g_\sigma)\bigr)=\tfrac23\Bigl(*_\sigma(\tau_2\wedge\tau_2)+\tfrac17|\tau_2|^2\sigma\Bigr),$$
or equivalently (4.44)
$$\mathrm{d}\tau_2=\tfrac16\Bigl(|\tau_2|^2\sigma+*_\sigma(\tau_2\wedge\tau_2)\Bigr)$$
must hold everywhere on $M$.

**Remark 13 (Extremally Ricci-pinched closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures):** Corollary 3 can be restated as follows. Using (4.45), the inequality (4.48)
$$\int_M\bigl|\operatorname{Ric}^0(g_\sigma)\bigr|^2\,*_\sigma1\ \ge\ \tfrac{4}{21}\int_M\operatorname{Scal}(g_\sigma)^2\,*_\sigma1$$
holds for any closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure $\sigma$ on a compact $M^7$, and equality holds if and only if (4.49)
$$\mathrm{d}\sigma=0,\qquad \mathrm{d}(*_\sigma\sigma)=\tau\wedge\sigma,\qquad \mathrm{d}\tau=\tfrac16\Bigl(|\tau|^2\sigma+*_\sigma(\tau\wedge\tau)\Bigr).$$
Under (4.49), the following hold. First, (4.50)
$$\mathrm{d}(\tau^3)=3\,\tau^2\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau=\tau^2\wedge\Bigl(\tfrac12|\tau|^2\sigma+\tfrac12*_\sigma(\tau\wedge\tau)\Bigr)=-\tfrac12|\tau|^4*_\sigma1+\tfrac12|\tau\wedge\tau|^2*_\sigma1=0.$$
Second, (4.51)
$$0=\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{d}\tau)=\mathrm{d}\Bigl(\tfrac16\bigl(|\tau|^2\sigma+*_\sigma(\tau\wedge\tau)\bigr)\Bigr)=\alpha\wedge\sigma+*_\sigma\gamma,$$
where $\gamma\in\Omega^3_{27}(M,\sigma)$ and (4.52)
$$\alpha=\tfrac18\Bigl(\mathrm{d}\bigl(|\tau|^2\bigr)-\tfrac29\,*_\sigma(\tau^3)\Bigr).$$
(The vanishing of $\gamma$ imposes 27 further equations on the covariant derivative of $\tau$, which are not as easily stated.) Consequently, every solution of (4.49) satisfies (4.53)
$$\mathrm{d}\bigl(|\tau|^2\bigr)=\tfrac29\,*_\sigma(\tau^3).$$
Combining (4.53) with (4.50) gives (4.54)
$$\Delta_\sigma\bigl(|\tau|^2\bigr)=0.$$
If $M$ is compact and connected, (4.54) implies that $|\tau|^2$ is constant. If $|\tau|^2=0$, then $\tau=0$, and $\sigma$ is coclosed and hence $g_\sigma$-parallel. Assume now $|\tau|^2>0$. Then (4.53) implies $\tau^3=0$. Since $|\tau\wedge\tau|^2=|\tau|^4\neq0$, the form $\tau$ has constant rank 4 (rather than the a priori maximum of 6), and $\tau\wedge\tau$ is a nonzero simple 4-form of constant norm. Using (2.23) and $\tau^3=0$,
(4.55)
$$\mathrm{d}(\tau^2)=2\,\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau=\tfrac13\,\tau\wedge\Bigl(|\tau|^2\sigma+*_\sigma(\tau\wedge\tau)\Bigr)=-\tfrac13|\tau|^2*_\sigma\tau+\tfrac13|\tau|^2*_\sigma\tau=0,$$
so $\tau\wedge\tau$ is closed. Since $\tau\wedge\tau$ is simple with constant norm, $*_\sigma(\tau\wedge\tau)$ is also nonzero, simple, and of constant norm. Expanding $\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{d}\tau)=0$ and using (4.49) and the constancy of $|\tau|^2$ shows that $*_\sigma(\tau\wedge\tau)$ is also closed. Consequently, $TM$ splits as an orthogonal direct sum of two integrable subbundles (4.56)
$$TM=P\oplus Q,\qquad P=\{\,v\in TM\mid v\lrcorner(\tau\wedge\tau)=0\,\}\ \text{of rank 3},\qquad Q=\{\,v\in TM\mid v\lrcorner *_\sigma(\tau\wedge\tau)=0\,\}\ \text{of rank 4}.$$
The $P$-leaves are calibrated by $-|\tau|^{-2}*_\sigma(\tau\wedge\tau)$, and the $Q$-leaves are calibrated by $-|\tau|^{-2}(\tau\wedge\tau)$.
