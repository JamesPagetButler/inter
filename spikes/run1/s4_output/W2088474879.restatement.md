# Restatement

## Introduction

The basic ideas of non-commutative geometry were developed in [1, 2], and the matrix-geometry form of them in [3, 4]. Applications to physical models were presented in [2, 5]. In those models the non-commutativity is minimal: Minkowski space is extended not by a standard Kaluza–Klein manifold describing internal degrees of freedom, but by two discrete points. The algebra of functions on this manifold remains commutative, while the complex of differential forms does not. This gives a description of the $SU(2)_L \bigotimes U(1)_R$ symmetry of the standard model of electroweak interactions. Gravity was included in [6].

Such models do not lead to UV-regularization, because they do not modify the short-distance behaviour of space-time. UV-regularization requires a non-commutative deformation of the algebra of functions on a space-time manifold (Minkowski case) or on the space manifold (Euclidean version).

One of the simplest locally Euclidean manifolds is the sphere $S^2$. Its non-commutative (fuzzy) deformation was described in [7, 8] within matrix geometry. A more general construction of non-commutative homogeneous spaces was described in [9] using coherent-state techniques. The first attempts to construct fields on a truncated sphere were presented in [8, 10] within the matrix formulation. Fields on truncated $S^2$ were investigated in detail in [11–13]. In [11] the quantum scalar field on truncated $S^2$ was studied, and it was explicitly shown that UV-regularization occurs automatically upon non-commutative deformation of the algebra of functions.

This article extends the approach from the 2-dimensional sphere $S^2$ to the 4-dimensional sphere $S^4$. Since $S^4$ is not a (co)-adjoint orbit, this extension has new nontrivial features. The article introduces only the notions of non-commutative geometry that are needed for the approach.

The outline is as follows. Section 2 describes the standard (commutative) sphere $S^4$ as the Hopf fibration $S^7 \to S^4$, together with the scalar self-interacting field on it. Section 3 generalises the model to the non-commutative truncated sphere $S^4$, introducing the non-commutative analogue of the Hopf fibration, and quantises the model using Feynman (path) integrals. Section 4 contains a discussion and concluding remarks.
