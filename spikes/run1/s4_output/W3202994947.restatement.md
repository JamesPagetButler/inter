# Restatement

## Abstract

The paper introduces categories of quasi-measurable spaces, which generalise quasi-Borel spaces to general sample spaces and less restrictive random variables. Each such category is complete, cocomplete, cartesian closed, locally cartesian closed, a quasitopos and a Heyting category. The push-forward probability monad $\mathcal{P}$ is singled out, with two properties of the sample space: a Fubini property, which makes $\mathcal{P}$ commutative, and a product isomorphism $\Omega\times\Omega\cong\Omega$, which makes it strong. The monad is affine in all cases.

The main object of study is the category of quasi-universal spaces, where the sample space is the Hilbert cube $[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}$ with the $\sigma$-algebra of all universally measurable subsets. For this category:

- the induced $\sigma$-algebras are intersections of Lebesgue-complete $\sigma$-algebras;
- for countably separated spaces, the induced $\sigma$-algebra is the completion of any countable separating family;
- all push-forward probability monads coincide, and on standard quasi-universal spaces the Giry monad also coincides with them.

The paper proves a Fubini theorem, a disintegration theorem for Markov kernels, Kolmogorov extension theorems and a conditional de Finetti theorem, and translates these into properties of the associated Markov category. It also formalises causal Bayesian networks over quasi-universal spaces, proves a global Markov property with asymmetric separoid rules for transitional conditional independence, and introduces partially generic causal Bayesian networks using exponential objects.

## Introduction (mathematical content only)

**Contributions.** The following results are stated in the introduction, with references to the paper's own numbering.

1. The categories $\mathbf{QMS}$ have all small limits and colimits (Theorems 2.38 and 2.45). Arbitrary small products and coproducts distribute (Theorem 2.42).
2. $\mathbf{QMS}$ is cartesian closed (Theorem 2.32).
3. The adjunction between $\mathbf{Meas}$ and $\mathbf{QMS}$ holds (Theorem 2.16).
4. The patchable quasi-measurable spaces $\mathbf{PQMS}$ form a reflective subcategory of $\mathbf{QMS}$ and an exponential ideal (Theorem 4.10).
5. The strong, affine monad $\mathcal{Q}$ is defined on $\mathbf{QMS}$ (Theorem 3.16).
6. Under $\Omega\times\Omega\cong\Omega$, the push-forward monads $\mathcal{K}$, $\mathcal{P}$, $\mathcal{R}$, $\mathcal{S}$ are strong (Theorem 3.25).
7. The induced $\sigma$-algebras on $\mathbf{QUS}$ are intersections of Lebesgue-complete $\sigma$-algebras (Lemma 5.5). For countably separated spaces they are given by Theorem 5.16.
8. The monads $\mathcal{K}$, $\mathcal{P}$, $\mathcal{R}$, $\mathcal{S}$ agree on $\mathbf{QUS}$ (Theorem 5.29).
9. On $\mathbf{QUS}$ the following hold: a Fubini theorem (Theorem 5.33), a disintegration theorem (Theorem 5.35), Kolmogorov extension theorems (Theorems 5.40 and 5.41) and a conditional de Finetti theorem (Theorem 5.47).
10. Universal quasi-universal spaces are exactly the retracts of $\Omega$ in $\mathbf{QUS}$ (Lemma 5.22).
11. $(\mathbf{UQUS},\times,\mathbf{1})$ is a symmetric cartesian monoidal category with countable products and coproducts. $(\mathcal{P},\delta,\mathbb{M})$ is a strong affine symmetric monoidal commutative monad on it. Its Kleisli category is an a.s.-compatibly representable Markov category with conditionals and Kolmogorov powers (Theorem 5.46).
12. The transitional conditional independence of [For21] is translated to $\mathbf{QUS}$ (Definition 6.5), and its asymmetric separoid rules hold (Theorem 6.7).
13. The global Markov property holds for causal Bayesian networks, relating the graph to transitional conditional independencies via d-separation (Theorem 6.19).
14. Partially generic causal Bayesian networks are defined (Definition 6.21).

## Notation and definitions

**Notation 2.1.** For two sets $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$,
$$[\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}]:=\mathbf{Sets}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}):=\{f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}\}$$
denotes the set of all set-theoretic maps from $\mathcal{X}$ to $\mathcal{Y}$.

**Notation 2.2 (The sample space).** Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega,\Omega^\Omega)$ be a triple consisting of:²

1. a measurable space $(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)$, where $\mathcal{B}_\Omega$ is a $\sigma$-algebra of subsets of $\Omega$;
2. a set of functions $\Omega^\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{Meas}((\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega),(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega))$ that:
   3. is closed under composition: $\circ:\Omega^\Omega\times\Omega^\Omega\to\Omega^\Omega$, $(\varphi_1,\varphi_2)\mapsto\varphi_1\circ\varphi_2$;
   4. contains the constant maps: $\Omega^{\mathbf{1}}:=[\mathbf{1}\to\Omega]\circ[\Omega\to\mathbf{1}]\subseteq\Omega^\Omega$;
   5. contains the identity: $\mathrm{id}_\Omega\in\Omega^\Omega$.

² *We will later further assume that $\Omega\cong\Omega\times\Omega$. This will be needed to get well-defined and strong probability monads.*

**Remark 2.3.**

1. If $(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)$ is a measurable space, a valid choice for $\Omega^\Omega$ in Notation 2.2 is
$$\Omega^\Omega:=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\Omega):=\mathbf{Meas}((\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega),(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)).$$
2. If $\Omega$ is a set and $\Omega^\Omega$ is a set of maps $\Omega\to\Omega$ that is closed under composition, contains $\Omega^{\mathbf{1}}$ and $\mathrm{id}_\Omega$, then a valid choice for $\mathcal{B}_\Omega$ in Notation 2.2 is
$$\mathcal{B}_\Omega:=\sigma\left(\left\{\varphi_2^{-1}(\varphi_1(\Omega))\;\middle|\;\varphi_1,\varphi_2\in\Omega^\Omega\right\}\right),$$
or its universal completion. In either case,
$$\Omega^\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{Meas}((\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega),(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)).$$

**Example 2.4.** Let $\Omega=\mathbb{R}$ and $\Omega^\Omega=\mathbf{Top}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})$, the set of continuous maps $\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$. This set is closed under composition and contains the identity and all constant maps. Then
$$\mathcal{B}_\Omega:=\sigma\left(\left\{\varphi_2^{-1}(\varphi_1(\Omega))\;\middle|\;\varphi_1,\varphi_2\in\Omega^\Omega\right\}\right)$$
is the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of $\mathbb{R}$. Also
$$\mathcal{B}_\Omega:=\left(\left\{\varphi_2^{-1}(\varphi_1(\Omega))\;\middle|\;\varphi_1,\varphi_2\in\Omega^\Omega\right\}\right)_{\mathcal{G}}$$
is the $\sigma$-algebra of all universally measurable subsets of $\mathbb{R}$. In either case,
$$\Omega^\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{Meas}((\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega),(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)).$$

**Definition 2.5 (Quasi-measurable spaces).** A quasi-measurable space $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega)$ with respect to $(\Omega,\Omega^\Omega)$ is a set $\mathcal{X}$ together with a set of maps $\mathcal{X}^\Omega\subseteq[\Omega\to\mathcal{X}]$ such that:

1. for all $\alpha\in\mathcal{X}^\Omega$ and $\varphi\in\Omega^\Omega$, $\alpha\circ\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^\Omega$, i.e. $\mathcal{X}^\Omega\circ\Omega^\Omega\subseteq\mathcal{X}^\Omega$;
2. all constant maps $\Omega\to\mathcal{X}$ lie in $\mathcal{X}^\Omega$, i.e. $\mathcal{X}^{\mathbf{1}}:=[\mathbf{1}\to\mathcal{X}]\circ[\Omega\to\mathbf{1}]\subseteq\mathcal{X}^\Omega$.

The space $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega)$ is usually referred to as $\mathcal{X}$.

**Example 2.6.** If $\mathcal{X}$ is a set and $\mathcal{X}^\Omega:=[\Omega\to\mathcal{X}]$, then $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega)$ is a quasi-measurable space.

**Example 2.7 (Measurable spaces as quasi-measurable spaces).**

1. If $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X})$ is a measurable space and
$$\mathcal{X}^\Omega:=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}):=\mathbf{Meas}((\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega),(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X})),$$
then $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega)$ is a quasi-measurable space with respect to $\Omega$.
2. $(\Omega,\Omega^\Omega)$ and $(\Omega,\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\Omega))$ may be different quasi-measurable spaces with respect to $(\Omega,\Omega^\Omega)$.
3. If $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{T}_\mathcal{X})$ is a topological space, it is usually endowed with
$$\mathcal{X}^\Omega:=\mathcal{F}(\sigma(\mathcal{T}_\mathcal{X}))=\{\alpha:\Omega\to\mathcal{X}\mid\forall A\in\mathcal{T}_\mathcal{X}.\ \alpha^{-1}(A)\in\mathcal{B}_\Omega\}.$$
4. If $\mathcal{X}$ is a countable set, it is usually endowed with the discrete topology and power-set $\sigma$-algebra. The induced structure is
$$\mathcal{X}^\Omega:=\{\alpha:\Omega\to\mathcal{X}\mid\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.\ \alpha^{-1}(x)\in\mathcal{B}_\Omega\}.$$

**Definition 2.8 (Quasi-measurable maps).** Let $(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Y}^\Omega)$ and $(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Z}^\Omega)$ be quasi-measurable spaces with respect to $\Omega$. A map $h:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Z}$ is called $\mathcal{Y}^\Omega$-$\mathcal{Z}^\Omega$-quasi-measurable, or just quasi-measurable, if
$$\forall\alpha\in\mathcal{Y}^\Omega.\quad h\circ\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^\Omega,$$
i.e. $h\circ\mathcal{Y}^\Omega\subseteq\mathcal{Z}^\Omega$. The set of all quasi-measurable maps is denoted $\mathbf{QMS}((\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Y}^\Omega),(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Z}^\Omega))$, or $\mathbf{QMS}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})$, with the dependence on $(\Omega,\Omega^\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)$ kept implicit.

**Remark 2.9.** The composition of quasi-measurable maps is quasi-measurable.

**Remark 2.10.** For a quasi-measurable space $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega)$,
$$\mathbf{QMS}((\Omega,\Omega^\Omega),(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega))=\mathcal{X}^\Omega.$$

**Notation 2.11.**

1. The category of all quasi-measurable spaces with quasi-measurable maps, with respect to $(\Omega,\Omega^\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)$, is denoted $\mathbf{QMS}$.
2. The category of all patchable (see Definition 4.2) quasi-measurable spaces with respect to $(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)=(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}_\mathbb{R})$, the real numbers with their Borel $\sigma$-algebra, is called the category of quasi-Borel spaces, denoted $\mathbf{QBS}$.
3. The category of all quasi-measurable spaces with respect to $(\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)=(\mathbb{R},(\mathcal{B}_\mathbb{R})_\mathcal{G})$, the real numbers with the $\sigma$-algebra of all universally measurable subsets, is called the category of quasi-universal (measurable) spaces, denoted $\mathbf{QUS}$.

**Notation 2.12 ($\sigma$-algebra induced by functions).** For any set $\mathcal{X}$ and any subset $\mathcal{X}^\Omega\subseteq[\Omega\to\mathcal{X}]$, define
$$\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^\Omega):=\left\{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}\;\middle|\;\forall\alpha\in\mathcal{X}^\Omega.\ \alpha^{-1}(A)\in\mathcal{B}_\Omega\right\}.$$
This is the largest $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}$ on $\mathcal{X}$ such that all $\alpha\in\mathcal{X}^\Omega$ are $\mathcal{B}_\Omega$-$\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}$-measurable.

**Notation 2.13 (Functions induced by $\sigma$-algebras).** Let $\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}$ be a $\sigma$-algebra of subsets of $\mathcal{X}$. Then
$$\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}):=\mathbf{Meas}((\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega),(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}))\subseteq[\Omega\to\mathcal{X}].$$
This is the largest set $\mathcal{X}^\Omega$ of $\mathcal{B}_\Omega$-$\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}$-measurable functions such that $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega)$ is still a quasi-measurable space.

**Lemma 2.14.** Let $\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}$ be a $\sigma$-algebra of subsets of a set $\mathcal{X}$, and let $\mathcal{X}^\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{Meas}((\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega),(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}))$ be any subset of measurable maps. Then
$$\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}))\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^\Omega),$$
and
$$\mathcal{X}^\Omega\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^\Omega))\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}).$$
In particular,
$$\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^\Omega)))=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^\Omega),\qquad \mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X})))=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{X}).$$

**Remark 2.15.** For the sample space $(\Omega,\Omega^\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega)$,
$$\mathcal{B}(\Omega^\Omega)=\mathcal{B}_\Omega.$$
So there is no ambiguity in the notation $\mathcal{B}_\Omega$. In general, $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\Omega)\supsetneq\Omega^\Omega$.

## Theorem 2.16 (Adjunction)

The functor
$$\mathcal{B}:\mathbf{QMS}\to\mathbf{Meas},\quad(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega)\mapsto(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^\Omega)),\quad \mathcal{B}(f)=f,$$
where
$$\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^\Omega):=\left\{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}\;\middle|\;\forall\alpha\in\mathcal{X}^\Omega.\ \alpha^{-1}(A)\in\mathcal{B}_\Omega\right\},$$
is left adjoint to the functor
$$\mathcal{F}:\mathbf{Meas}\to\mathbf{QMS},\quad(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{Y})\mapsto(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{Y})),\quad \mathcal{F}(g)=g,$$
where
$$\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{Y}):=\mathbf{Meas}((\Omega,\mathcal{B}_\Omega),(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{Y})).$$

Hence, for a quasi-measurable space $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega)$ and a measurable space $(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{Y})$, there are canonical identifications
$$\mathbf{Meas}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^\Omega)),(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{Y}))=\mathbf{QMS}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^\Omega),(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{Y}))).$$

Furthermore,
$$\mathcal{B}\circ\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B},\qquad \mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{B}\circ\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F},$$
and
$$\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B}_\mathcal{Y})$$
