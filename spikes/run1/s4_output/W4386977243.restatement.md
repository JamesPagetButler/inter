# Restatement

## Abstract

- The paper considers two variants of Mann's $\infty$-categorical definition of abstract six-functor formalisms:
  - **Nagata six-functor formalisms**, which additionally have Grothendieck and Wirthmüller contexts.
  - **Local six-functor formalisms**, which additionally take values in presentable stable $\infty$-categories and have recollements.
- Using Nagata's compactification theorem, Nagata six-functor formalisms on varieties are specified by adjoint triples for open immersions and for proper morphisms, satisfying certain compatibilities.
- The existence of recollements is (almost) equivalent to a hypersheaf condition for a Grothendieck topology on the category of varieties and spans consisting of an open immersion and a proper map.
- The category of local six-functor formalisms embeds faithfully into the category of lax symmetric monoidal functors from smooth and complete varieties to presentable stable $\infty$-categories and adjoint triples.
- Lax symmetric monoidal functors on complete varieties, with values in presentable stable $\infty$-categories and adjoint triples, are characterized by which of them extend to local six-functor formalisms.

## Introduction: relations used in the statements

**Recollement diagram (1.0.1).** For an open immersion $i:U\to X$ with closed complement $j:X\setminus U\to X$, the associated diagram is
$$\mathcal{D}(X\setminus U)\ \rightleftarrows\ \mathcal{D}(X)\ \rightleftarrows\ \mathcal{D}(U),$$
with adjoint triples
$$j^{*}\dashv(j_{*}\cong j_{!})\dashv j^{!},\qquad i_{!}\dashv(i^{!}\cong i^{*})\dashv i_{*}.$$
Such a diagram is a **recollement** if it satisfies a list of properties, including $j^{*}i_{*}=0$, and $i_{!}$, $j_{!}$ and $j_{*}$ are embeddings. A six-functor formalism $\mathcal{D}$ **satisfies localization** if, for every open immersion $i:U\to X$, the associated diagram is a recollement.

**Correspondences.** For a category with products $\mathcal{C}$, $\textup{Corr}(\mathcal{C})$ is the $(\infty,1)$-category of correspondences in $\mathcal{C}$, whose morphisms are spans $X\xleftarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z$ (see [Man22, Definition A.5.2]). The image of such a span under a functor $\mathcal{D}$ is $\mathcal{D}(X)\xrightarrow{f^{*}}\mathcal{D}(Y)\xrightarrow{g_{!}}\mathcal{D}(Z)$.

**Lax symmetric monoidal description (1.1.1).** A six-functor formalism on $\mathcal{C}$ is a lax symmetric monoidal functor
$$\mathcal{D}:\textup{Corr}(\mathcal{C})\to\textup{Cat}_{\infty},$$
such that each $\mathcal{D}(X)$ is a closed symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category and $\mathcal{D}(f)$ is a left adjoint for every morphism $f$.

**Nagata setup.** A Nagata setup $(\mathcal{C},I,P)$ is an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$ with fiber products and a terminal object, together with classes of morphisms $I$ and $P$ such that every morphism factors as a morphism in $I$ followed by a morphism in $P$. Both $I$ and $P$ are stable under pullback along arbitrary morphisms. The example relevant here is qcqs schemes over $S$ with $I$ the open immersions and $P$ the proper morphisms.

**Nagata three- and six-functor formalisms.** $\mathbf{3FF}$ and $\mathbf{6FF}$ denote the $\infty$-categories of three- and six-functor formalisms on $\mathcal{C}$ with values in closed symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories, having Grothendieck contexts for $p\in P$ and Wirthmüller contexts for $i\in I$ (localization is not required).

**Local six-functor formalisms.** $\mathbf{6FF}^{\textup{loc}}\subseteq\mathbf{6FF}$ is the subcategory where $\mathcal{D}(X)$ is a presentable stable $\infty$-category for all $X$, and each sequence (1.2.3) is sent to a recollement.

**Sequence (1.2.3).** For $\mathbf{Var}$ and composable morphisms
$$U\xleftarrow{=}U\xhookrightarrow{i}X\xleftarrow{j}X\setminus U\xhookrightarrow{=}X\setminus U,$$
where $i$ is an open immersion and $j$ is proper.

**Compactly supported cohomology.** For $F:\mathbf{Span}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{S}\textup{pectra}$, set $H^{n}_{c}(X)=\pi_{-n}(F(X))$. If $F$ sends sequences (1.2.3) to fiber sequences, there are long exact sequences
$$\dots\to H^{n}_{c}(U)\to H^{n}_{c}(X)\to H^{n}_{c}(X\setminus U)\to H^{n+1}_{c}(U)\to\dots$$

## Definitions and statements

**Definition 1.2.1.** We denote by $\delta^{*}_{+}\mathcal{C}(P^{\mathrm{op}},I)\subseteq\textup{Corr}(\mathcal{C})$ the subcategory spanned by all objects of $\mathcal{C}$, and by morphisms of the form
$$X\xleftarrow{p}U\xhookrightarrow{i}Y,\qquad i\in I,\ p\in P.$$

**Proposition A (Proposition 3.2.15).** The $\infty$-category $\mathbf{6FF}$ is equivalent to the $\infty$-category of lax symmetric monoidal functors
$$F:\delta^{*}_{+}\mathcal{C}(P^{\mathrm{op}},I)\to\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}$$
such that:
1. for $X\in\mathcal{C}$, $F(X)$ with the induced symmetric monoidal structure is a closed symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category;
2. for every morphism $f$ in $\delta^{*}_{+}\mathcal{C}(P^{\mathrm{op}},I)$, $F(f)$ is a left-left adjoint;
3. $F$ sends certain squares to adjointable squares (the BC property, see (B) in Definition 3.2.1).

The equivalence is on symmetric natural transformations compatible with the right adjoints of the functors $F(f)$.

**Remark 1.2.2.** Proposition A is, in a sense, a dual of the conjecture stated on [Sch22, page 47] and of [Khа23, Theorem 2.51]. Scholze conjectures that Mann's construction [Man22, Proposition A.5.10] induces an equivalence between the $\infty$-category of "three-functor formalisms" with Grothendieck and Wirthmüller contexts, and the $\infty$-category of functors $D^{*}:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}$ that encode $f^{*}$ for all morphisms. These functors satisfy conditions involving a left adjoint $i_{\natural}\dashv i^{*}$ for $i\in I$ and a right adjoint $p^{*}\dashv p_{*}$ for $p\in P$. The conjecture can be refined to a statement about six-functor formalisms.

**Proposition B (Proposition 4.1.15).** The equivalence in Proposition A restricts to an equivalence between $\mathbf{6FF}^{\textup{loc}}$ and the $\infty$-category of lax symmetric monoidal functors
$$F:\delta^{*}_{+}\mathbf{Var}(P^{\mathrm{op}},I)\to\textup{PrSt}_{\infty}$$
which take values in presentable stable $\infty$-categories and left-left adjoints, have the BC-property, and additionally:
- send sequences (1.0.1) to recollements;
- send abstract blowups to pullbacks;
- satisfy $F(\emptyset)=\mathbb{1}$.

The equivalence is on symmetric monoidal natural transformations compatible with the right adjoints of the functors $F(f)$.

The condition that $F$ sends sequences (1.2.3) to recollements and abstract blowups to pullbacks is equivalent to $F$ being a hypersheaf for a certain topology on $\delta^{*}_{+}\mathbf{Var}(P^{\mathrm{op}},I)^{\mathrm{op}}$; together with $F(\emptyset)=\mathbb{1}$, this gives the hypersheaf condition.

**Theorem C (Theorem 4.3.13).** Restriction to $\mathbf{Comp}^{\mathrm{op}}$ induces an equivalence between $\mathbf{6FF}^{\textup{loc}}$ and the $\infty$-category of lax symmetric monoidal functors
$$F:\mathbf{Comp}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\textup{PrSt}_{\infty}$$
which take values in presentable stable $\infty$-categories and left-left adjoints, satisfy a BC-property (see (2) in Definition 4.3.4), send abstract blowups to pullbacks, and satisfy $F(\emptyset)=\mathbb{1}$. The equivalence is on symmetric monoidal natural transformations compatible with the right adjoints of the functors $F(f)$.

The condition "sends abstract blowups to pullback squares and $F(\emptyset)=\mathbb{1}$" is equivalent to $F$ being a hypersheaf for a certain topology on $\mathbf{Comp}$.

**Theorem D (Theorem 4.3.14).** Over a field of characteristic zero, restriction to smooth and complete varieties induces a fully faithful embedding of $\mathbf{6FF}^{\textup{loc}}$ into the $\infty$-category of $\infty$-operad maps
$$F:\mathbf{SmComp}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\textup{PrSt}_{\infty}^{\otimes}$$
that satisfy a BC-condition (see (3) in Remark 4.3.15), satisfy descent for blowups, and satisfy $F(\emptyset)=\mathbb{1}$. The embedding is on symmetric monoidal natural transformations compatible with the right adjoints of the functors $F(f)$.

Sending blowups to pullbacks and $F(\emptyset)=\mathbb{1}$ is equivalent to a hypersheaf condition for a certain topology on $\mathbf{SmComp}$.

**Proposition E (Proposition 4.3.1).** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a pointed, complete, presentable symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category. Then there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\textup{Alg}^{\tau^{c}_{A\cup L}}_{\mathbf{Span}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\textup{Alg}^{\tau_{AC}}_{\mathbf{Comp}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathcal{C}).$$
Over a field of characteristic zero, there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\textup{Alg}^{\tau^{c}_{A\cup L}}_{\mathbf{Span}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\textup{Alg}^{\tau_{B}}_{\mathbf{SmComp}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathcal{C}).$$
Here $\textup{Alg}^{\tau}_{\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathcal{C})$ denotes the category of lax symmetric monoidal functors $\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}$ whose underlying functor is a $\tau$-hypersheaf.

## Other displayed relations

- **Adjoint triples for Nagata formalisms.** For $i\in I$, $i_{!}\dashv(i^{!}\cong i^{*})\dashv i_{*}$; for $p\in P$, $p^{*}\dashv(p_{*}\cong p_{!})\dashv p^{!}$.
- **Equivalence (1.2.4).** For $\mathcal{C}$ complete, cocomplete and pointed,
$$\textup{HSh}_{\tau^{c}_{A\cup L}}(\mathbf{Span};\mathcal{C})_{\emptyset}\simeq\textup{HSh}_{\tau_{AC}}(\mathbf{Comp};\mathcal{C}).$$
- **Equivalence (1.2.5).** Over a field of characteristic zero,
$$\textup{HSh}_{\tau_{AC}}(\mathbf{Comp};\mathcal{C})\simeq\textup{HSh}_{\tau_{B}}(\mathbf{SmComp};\mathcal{C}).$$
- **Notation.** $\mathbf{Span}$ denotes the 1-category $\delta^{*}_{+}\mathbf{Var}(I^{\mathrm{op}},P)=\delta^{*}_{+}\mathbf{Var}(P^{\mathrm{op}},I)^{\mathrm{op}}$. $\mathbf{Comp}$ denotes the 1-category of complete varieties, and $\mathbf{SmComp}$ the smooth complete varieties.
