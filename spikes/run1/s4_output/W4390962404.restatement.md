# Restatement

## Abstract

Applying the theory of analytic stacks of Clausen and Scholze, we introduce a general notion of derived Tate adic spaces. We use this formalism to define the analytic de Rham stack in rigid geometry, extending the theory of $\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}$-modules of Ardakov and Wadsley to the theory of analytic $D$-modules. We prove foundational results, including the existence of a six-functor formalism and Poincaré duality for analytic $D$-modules, generalizing previous work of Bode. We relate the theory of analytic $D$-modules to previous work of the author with Rodrigues Jacinto on solid locally analytic representations of $p$-adic Lie groups.

## 1. Introduction

### Classical $D$-modules

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$ and $X$ a smooth scheme over $K$. The category of $D$-modules over $X$ is constructed by first defining the ring of algebraic differential operators $D_X$, and then taking the category of $D_X$-modules whose underlying $\mathscr{O}_X$-module is quasi-coherent. If $X$ admits an étale map $X\to\mathbb{A}^d_K$, then
$$D_X=\mathscr{O}_X[\partial_{T_1},\ldots,\partial_{T_d}],$$
where $\mathbb{A}^d_K=\Spec K[T_1,\ldots,T_d]$ and $\partial_{T_i}$ corresponds to the partial derivative along $T_i$.

Simpson [Sim96, ST97] defines, for a smooth variety $X$ over $K$, a stack $X_{dR}^{alg}$ from commutative rings over $K$ to sets whose quasi-coherent sheaves are naturally isomorphic to $D$-modules over $X$. Let $\mathrm{Ring}_K$ be the category of commutative rings of finite type over $K$. The de Rham stack is the stack in the étale topology given by
$$X_{dR}^{alg}(R)=X(R^{\red}),$$
where $R^{\red}$ is the reduction of $R$. This formula extends to any stack over $K$. The work of Gaitsgory and Rozenblyum [GR14] studies $D$-modules via the de Rham stack.

### Analytic setting

Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be a non-archimedean extension and $X$ a smooth rigid space over $K$. Ardakov and Wadsley [AW18, AW19] developed the theory of coadmissible $\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}$-modules over rigid spaces. Suppose $X=\Spa A$ is affinoid and $f:X\to\mathbb{D}^d_K=\Spa K\langle T_1,\ldots,T_d\rangle$ is étale. Set
$$D_X=A[\partial_{T_1},\ldots,\partial_{T_d}].$$
The action of $\partial_{T_i}$ on $A$ is continuous, so there is $N>0$ such that for all $n\geq N$, the left $A^\circ$-submodule $A^\circ[p^n\partial_{T_1},\ldots,p^n\partial_{T_d}]\subset D_X$ is stable under multiplication. Taking $p$-adic completions gives
$$\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_X^{(n)}:=A\langle p^n\partial_{T_1},\ldots,p^n\partial_{T_d}\rangle,\qquad \wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_X=\varprojlim_n \wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_X^{(n)}.$$
The algebra $\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_X$ is a Fréchet–Stein algebra, motivated by the algebra of analytic distributions of $p$-adic Lie groups of Schneider–Teitelbaum [ST03]. Coadmissible $\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_X$-modules are defined as a limit of the categories of finite type $\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_X^{(n)}$-modules along pullbacks. Extensions using bornological vector spaces exist, and Bode [Bod21] constructed a six-functor formalism for $\wideparen{\mathcal{D}}_X$-modules.

### Example

Let $\mathbb{G}_a=\Spec K[T]$ with its additive group structure. The algebraic de Rham stack satisfies $\mathbb{G}_{a,dR}^{alg}=\mathbb{G}_a/\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_a$, where $\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_a$ is the formal completion at $0\in\mathbb{G}_a$, acting by translations. The Cartier dual of $*/\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_a$ is $\mathbb{G}_a$.

In the analytic setting, let $\mathbb{G}_a=\mathbb{D}^1_K=\Spa K\langle T\rangle$. The continuous dual of $\mathscr{O}(\mathbb{A}^{1,\an}_K)$ is, as a Hopf algebra, the ring of germs at $0$:
$$K\{T\}^\dagger=\varinjlim_n K\left\langle\frac{T}{p^n}\right\rangle.$$
The candidate subgroup is $\mathbb{G}_a^\dagger=\Spa K\{T\}^\dagger$, and one sets $\mathbb{G}_{a,dR}=\mathbb{G}_a/\mathbb{G}_a^\dagger$.

### Foundational issues

Three issues arise:

1. One must work with topological rings and topological modules. This is handled by the analytic geometry and condensed mathematics of Clausen and Scholze [CS19, CS20, CS22].
2. A six-functor formalism requires analytic stacks with descent. This is handled by the abstract six-functor formalisms and $\mathscr{D}$-stacks of [Man22b, Man22a] and [Sch23].
3. The dagger-nilradical $\Nil^\dagger$ must be defined for suitable analytic rings. Elements of $\mathbb{G}_a^\dagger$ are those of spectral norm zero, meaning $|a|\leq|p^n|$ for all $n\geq 0$.

The paper restricts to *bounded affinoid rings*, rings admitting a pseudo-uniformizer. The analytic de Rham stack of a rigid space $X$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is a sheafification of the prestack
$$X_{dR}(\mathcal{A})=X(\mathcal{A}^{\dagger-\red}),\qquad \mathcal{A}^{\dagger-\red}:=\mathcal{A}/\Nil^\dagger(\mathcal{A}).$$

### Overview of the paper

- **§2** develops derived Tate adic spaces, starting from the $\infty$-category of bounded affinoid rings (Definition 2.6.10) and spectra $\Spa\mathcal{A}$ (Definition 2.7.3).
- **§3** develops Tate stacks, a six-functor formalism on analytic $\mathscr{D}$-stacks (Definitions 3.2.7 and 3.2.10), finite presentation (§3.3), cotangent complexes (§3.4), and solid smooth and étale maps (§3.5, Theorem 3.5.6, Corollary 3.5.16).
- **§4** studies Cartier duality for vector bundles (Construction 4.3.9).
- **§5** defines the algebraic and analytic de Rham stacks.
- **§6** relates the analytic de Rham stack to locally analytic representations of $p$-adic Lie groups.

## 2. Labelled statements

**Theorem 1.0.1.** Let $R=\mathbb{Z}((\pi))$ be the Huber ring parametrizing pseudo-uniformizers in Tate Huber pairs. There is a full subcategory $\AffRing^b_R\subset\AnRing_R$ of the $\infty$-category of analytic $R$-algebras, called the category of bounded affinoid rings, containing fully faithfully the $1$-category of Tate Huber pairs over $R$. The category $\AffRing^b_R$ is stable under small colimits in $\AnRing_R$. Furthermore, let $\mathcal{A}\in\AffRing^b_R$. The following hold.

1. There is an animated subring $\mathcal{A}^+\subset\underline{\mathcal{A}}(*)$ such that an $\underline{\mathcal{A}}$-module is $\mathcal{A}$-complete if and only if it is $\mathbb{Z}[a]_\square$-complete for all $a\in\mathcal{A}^+$.
2. For any map $\mathbb{Z}[T]\to\mathcal{A}$ of analytic rings, there is some $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and an extension $R\langle\pi^nT\rangle\to\mathcal{A}$. In other words, any element $a\in\mathcal{A}$ is bounded.
3. The ring $\mathcal{A}$ has a condensed nil-radical $\Nil(\mathcal{A})$ whose $S$-points (for $S$ profinite) are the maps $S\to\mathcal{A}$ which are uniformly nilpotent. The analytic ring structure on $\mathcal{A}$ is determined by that of $\mathcal{A}^{\red}:=\mathcal{A}/\Nil(\mathcal{A})$, and $\Nil(\mathcal{A}^{\red})=0$.
4. The ring $\mathcal{A}$ has a $\dagger$-nil-radical $\Nil^\dagger(\mathcal{A})$ whose $S$-points (for $S$ profinite) are the maps $S\to\mathcal{A}$ which are overconvergently close to zero. The analytic ring structure on $\mathcal{A}$ is determined by that of $\mathcal{A}^{\dagger-\red}:=\mathcal{A}/\Nil^\dagger(\mathcal{A})$, and $\Nil^\dagger(\mathcal{A}^{\dagger-\red})=0$.
5. There is a spectral space $\Spa\mathcal{A}$ with an analytic topology whose basis is given by quasi-compact rational subspaces, generalizing Huber's $\Spa(A,A^+)$. Any map $\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}$ of bounded affinoid rings gives a spectral morphism $\Spa\mathcal{B}\to\Spa\mathcal{A}$ preserving rational localizations.
6. There are homeomorphisms of spectral spaces $\Spa\mathcal{A}=\Spa\mathcal{A}^{\red}=\Spa\mathcal{A}^{\dagger-\red}$.
7. The $\infty$-category $\Mod(\mathcal{A})$ of solid $\mathcal{A}$-modules satisfies descent for the analytic topology of $\Spa\mathcal{A}$.

**Definition 1.0.2.** Let $\Aff^b_R$ be the opposite category of bounded affinoid rings. An object of $\Aff^b_R$ is called a bounded affinoid space. For $\mathcal{A}$ a bounded affinoid ring, $\AnSpec\mathcal{A}\in\Aff^b_R$ is its analytic spectrum. A *derived Tate adic space* is a sheaf $X:\Aff^{b,\op}_R\to\Ani$ for the analytic topology of $\Aff^b_R$, such that $X$ admits an open analytic cover by bounded affinoid spaces. For $X$ a derived Tate adic space, $|X|=\varinjlim_{\AnSpec\mathcal{A}\to X}|X|$ is its underlying topological space. $\AdicSp_R$ denotes the $\infty$-category of derived Tate adic spaces over $R$.

**Theorem 1.0.3.** Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of solid finite presentation of derived Tate adic spaces over $R=\mathbb{Z}((\pi))$. The following are equivalent.

1. The map $f$ is formally smooth (resp. étale).
2. The map $f$ is solid smooth (resp. étale), namely, locally in the analytic topology of $X$ and $Y$, $f$ is standard solid smooth (resp. standard solid étale).

**Theorem 1.0.4.** Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of solid finite presentation of derived Tate adic spaces. The following hold.

1. The map $f$ admits $!$-functors in the six-functor formalism of solid quasi-coherent sheaves.
2. If $f$ is solid smooth (resp. étale), then $f$ is cohomologically smooth (resp. cohomologically étale) for the six-functor formalism of quasi-coherent sheaves.

Furthermore, there is a natural identification $f^!1_Y=\Omega^d_{X/Y}[d]$, where $d$ is the relative dimension of $f$, and $1_Y$ is the unit in the category of solid quasi-coherent sheaves on $Y$ (the structure sheaf).

**Proposition 1.0.5.** Let $f:X\to Y$ be a solid smooth (resp. étale) map of derived Tate adic spaces over $R$. Then $f$ is $\dagger$-formally smooth (resp. étale) locally in the analytic topology of $X$ and $Y$.

**Definition 1.0.6.** Let $X$ be a derived Tate adic space over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathscr{F}$ a vector bundle over $X$ of rank $d$. Let $\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^{\an}$ be its geometric realization as a derived Tate adic space over $X$. Let $\iota:X\to\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^{\an}$ be the zero section, and let $\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^\dagger$ denote the overconvergent neighbourhood of zero.

If $X=\AnSpec\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathscr{E}$ is free of rank $1$, then $\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^{\an}\cong\mathbb{A}^{1,\an}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$. Similarly, $\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^\dagger$ is the space $\mathbb{G}^\dagger_{a,\mathbb{Q}_p}$, the analytic spectrum of
$$\mathbb{Q}_p\{T\}^\dagger=\varinjlim_n\mathbb{Q}_p\left\langle\frac{T}{p^n}\right\rangle,$$
the ring of functions overconverging at $0\in\mathbb{A}^{1,\an}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$.

**Theorem 1.0.7.** Let $X$ be a derived Tate adic space over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and let $\mathscr{E}$ be a vector bundle over $X$ of rank $d$.

1. Let $f:\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^{\an}\to X$. Then $f$ is cohomologically smooth, with natural equivalences $f^!1_X=f^*\bigwedge^d\mathscr{F}^\vee[d]$ and $f_!f^!1_X=\mathrm{Sym}^\dagger_X(\mathscr{F})$, where $\mathrm{Sym}^\dagger_X(\mathscr{F})$ is the sheaf of functions on $\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F}^\vee)^\dagger$.
2. The map $f:\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^\dagger\to X$ satisfies $f_*=f_!$.
3. Let $g:X/\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^{\an}\to X$. Then $g$ is cohomologically smooth with $g^!1_X=g^*\bigwedge^d\mathscr{F}[-d]$, and there is a natural equivalence $g_!\cong g_*[-2d]$.
4. Let $g:X/\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^\dagger\to X$. Then $g$ is cohomologically smooth with $g^!1_X=g^*\bigwedge^d\mathscr{F}[d]$, and there is a natural equivalence $g_!\cong g_*$.

**Theorem 1.0.8 (analytic Cartier duality).** Let $X$ be a derived Tate adic space over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and let $\mathscr{F}$ be a vector bundle over $X$ of rank $d$.

1. There is a bilinear morphism $F:\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^\dagger\times(X/\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F}^\vee)^{\an})\to B\mathbb{G}_m$ such that $F^*(\mathscr{O}(1))$ is an isomorphism $\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^\dagger\xrightarrow{\sim}X/\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F}^\vee)^{\an}$ in the category of Fourier–Mukai kernels for the six-functor formalism of solid quasi-coherent sheaves. Its inverse is $F^*(\mathscr{O}(-1))\otimes\bigwedge^d\mathscr{F}^\vee[-d]$.
2. There is a bilinear morphism $G:\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^{\an}\times(X/\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F}^\vee)^\dagger)\to B\mathbb{G}_m$ such that $G^*(\mathscr{O}(1))$ is an isomorphism $\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^{\an}\xrightarrow{\sim}X/\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F}^\vee)^\dagger$ in the category of Fourier–Mukai kernels for the six-functor formalism of solid quasi-coherent sheaves. Its inverse is $G^*(\mathscr{O}(-1))\otimes\bigwedge^d\mathscr{F}^\vee[d]$.

**Corollary 1.0.9.** In the notation of Theorem 1.0.8, there are natural equivalences of stable $\infty$-categories given by Fourier–Mukai transforms
$$FM_1:\Mod_\square(\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^\dagger)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Mod_\square(X/\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F}^\vee)^{\an}),$$
$$FM_2:\Mod_\square(\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F})^{\an})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Mod_\square(X/\mathbb{V}(\mathscr{F}^\vee)^\dagger).$$

**Definition 1.0.10 (Algebraic de Rham stack).** Let $R=\mathbb{Z}((\pi))$ and let $X$ be a derived Tate adic space over $R\otimes\mathbb{Q}$. The algebraic de Rham prestack of $X$ is the presheaf on $\Aff^b_{R\otimes\mathbb{Q}}$ given by
$$X_{dR}^{\alg}(\mathcal{A})=\varinjlim_{I\to\mathcal{A}}X(\cone(I\to\mathcal{A})),$$
where $I$ runs over all uniformly nilpotent ideals of $\mathcal{A}$. The de Rham stack of $X$ is the sheafification of this prestack in the $\mathscr{D}$-topology, also denoted $X_{dR}^{\alg}$. For a morphism $X\to Y$ of derived Tate adic spaces, the relative algebraic de Rham stack $X^{\alg}_{dR,Y}$ is the pullback of $Y\to Y^{\alg}_{dR}\leftarrow X^{\alg}_{dR}$. The category $\Mod_\square(X^{\alg}_{dR,Y})$ is called the category of algebraic $D_{X/Y}$-modules.

**Definition 1.0.11 (Analytic de Rham stack).** Let $X$ be a derived Tate adic space over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. The analytic de Rham prestack is the presheaf on $\Aff^b_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ defined by
$$X_{dR}(\mathcal{A}):=X(\mathcal{A}^{\dagger-\red}).$$
The analytic de Rham stack is the $\mathscr{D}$-sheafification of this prestack, also denoted $X_{dR}$. For a map $f:X\to Y$ of derived Tate adic spaces, the relative de Rham stack $X_{dR,Y}$ is the pullback of $Y\to Y_{dR}\leftarrow X_{dR}$. The category $\Mod_\square(X_{dR,Y})$ is called the category of analytic $D_{X/Y}$-modules.

**Theorem 1.0.12.** Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of derived Tate adic spaces locally of solid finite presentation. Write $f^{\alg}_{dR}:X^{\alg}_{dR}\to Y^{\alg}_{dR}$ and $f_{dR}:X_{dR}\to Y_{dR}$ for the associated maps of stacks.

1. The formation of $X\mapsto X_{dR}$ and $X\mapsto X^{\alg}_{dR}$ commutes with colimits and finite limits at the level of prestacks.
2. The maps $f^{\alg}_{dR}$ and $f_{dR}$ admit $!$-functors.
3. Suppose $X$ is a rigid space over a non-archimedean extension $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$. Then the map $h:X\to X_{dR,K}$ is a $\mathscr{D}$-cover. In particular, quasi-coherent sheaves on $X_{dR,K}$ descend along $h$.
4. (Kashiwara equivalence) Let $X\to Y$ be a Zariski closed immersion of derived Tate adic spaces, and let $Y^{\dagger/X}$ be the overconvergent neighbourhood of $X$ in $Y$. Then there is an equivalence of analytic de Rham stacks $X_{dR}=Y^{\dagger/X}_{dR}$. In particular, analytic $D$-modules on $Y$ supported on $X$ are equivalent to analytic $D$-modules on $X$.
5. Suppose $f$ is solid smooth (resp. étale). Then $f^{\alg}_{dR}$ and $f_{dR}$ are cohomologically smooth (resp. étale).
6. Suppose $f$ is solid smooth. Then there are natural equivalences $f^{\alg,!}_{dR}1_{Y^{\alg}_{dR}}=1_{X^{\alg}_{dR}}$ and $f^!_{dR}1_{Y_{dR}}=1_{X_{dR}}[2d]$, where $d$ is the relative dimension of $f$.
7. Suppose $f$ is solid smooth, and consider $g:X^{\alg}_{dR,Y}\to X_{dR,Y}$. Then $g$ admits $!$-functors, $g_*$ satisfies the projection formula, and there is a natural equivalence $1_{X_{dR,Y}}\xrightarrow{\sim}g_*1_{X^{\alg}_{dR,Y}}$. In particular, the pullback $g^*$ is fully faithful and induces an embedding of analytic $D_{X/Y}$-modules into algebraic $D_{X/Y}$-modules.
8. Suppose $f$ is solid smooth, and denote by $f^{\alg}_{dR,Y}:X^{\alg}_{dR,Y}\to Y$ and $f_{dR,Y}:X_{dR,Y}\to Y$ the natural maps. Then there are natural equivalences of de Rham cohomology
$$DR(X/Y):=f^{\alg}_{dR,Y,*}1_{X^{\alg}_{dR,Y}}=f_{dR,Y,*}1_{X_{dR,Y}}.$$
Furthermore, $DR(X/Y)$ can be promoted to a filtered object given by the Hodge filtration, with graded pieces $\gr^iDR(X/Y)=\Omega^i_{X/Y}$, extending the Hodge filtration for smooth maps of rigid spaces.

**Remark 1.0.13.** To prove Theorem 1.0.12 one considers the filtered de Rham stacks $X^{\alg}_{dR^+}$ and $X_{dR^+}$, see Definitions 5.1.1 and 5.2.2.

**Proposition 1.0.14.** Let $G$ be a $p$-adic Lie group. There is a natural equivalence $G^{la}_{dR}=G^{sm}$. In particular, there is an equivalence of classifying stacks $(*/G^{la})_{dR}=*/G^{sm}$.

**Remark 1.0.15.** Let $f:X\to Y$ be a solid smooth map of derived Tate adic spaces. By Proposition 1.0.5, the map $h:X\to X_{dR,Y}$ is an epimorphism of $\mathscr{D}$-stacks. The Čech nerve of $h$ is the analytic de Rham groupoid, whose $n$-th term is the overconvergent neighbourhood $(\Delta^{n+1}_YX)^\dagger$ of the diagonal map $\Delta^{n+1}_YX\to X^{\times_Y n+1}$. This is the analytic space whose functions are those of $X^{\times_Y n+1}$ that overconverge the locally closed subspace $|\Delta^{n+1}_Y|\subset|X^{\times_Y n+1}|$. This gives an equivalence of $\mathscr{D}$-stacks
$$X_{dR,Y}:=\varinjlim_{[n]\in\Delta^{\op}}(\Delta^{n+1}_YX)^\dagger.$$
The analytic de Rham groupoid is extended to $\dagger$-smooth groupoids in Definition 6.1.3. Examples arise from Lie algebroids over rigid spaces, see Example 6.1.8 (3).

**Definition 1.0.16.** Let $X$ be a derived Tate adic space over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $G$ a $p$-adic Lie group acting locally analytically on $X$. Let $\mathbb{H}^\dagger$ be a $\dagger$-smooth group over $X$ and let $\mathbb{H}^\dagger\to G^{la}\times X$ be a map of groupoids with given normal quotient $G^{la}/\mathbb{H}^{la}$. The category of analytic equivariant $D(G^{la}/\mathbb{H}^\dagger)$-modules is defined to be $\Mod(X/(G^{la}/\mathbb{H}^\dagger))$. (Definition 6.2.5.)

**Theorem 1.0.17.** Let $X\to Y$ be a solid smooth morphism of derived Tate adic spaces over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of relative dimension $d$. Let $G$ be a $p$-adic Lie group of dimension $g$ acting locally analytically on $X$ over $Y$. Let $\mathbb{H}^\dagger$ be a $\dagger$-smooth group over $X$ of relative dimension $e$, and let $\mathbb{H}^\dagger\to G^{la}\times X$ be a map of groupoids over $X$ with given normal quotient $G^{la}/\mathbb{H}^\dagger$. Then $g:X/(G^{la}/\mathbb{H}^\dagger)\to Y$ is cohomologically smooth, and its underlying $G^{la}$-equivariant invertible sheaf is equivalent to
$$\Omega^d_{X/Y}[d]\otimes\bigwedge^g\Lie G[g]\otimes\bigwedge^e\ell_{\mathbb{H}^\dagger/X}[-e].$$

## 3. Conventions

Throughout, the paper uses the higher category theory and higher algebra of [Lur09] and [Lur17], the condensed mathematics of [CS19, CS20, CS22], and the abstract six-functor formalisms of [Man22b, Man22a] and [Sch23]. $(\infty,1)$-categories are called $\infty$-categories, and classical categories are called $1$-categories.

- $\Cat_\infty$ denotes the large $\infty$-category of $\infty$-categories.
- $\Cat_\infty^{\colim}$ is the subcategory of $\infty$-categories admitting small colimits, with colimit-preserving functors as morphisms.
- $\Cat^{\ex}_\infty$ is the subcategory of stable $\infty$-categories with exact functors.
- $\mathcal{P}r^L$ (resp. $\mathcal{P}r^R$) is the $\infty$-category of presentable $\infty$-categories with colimit-preserving (resp. accessible limit-preserving) functors.
- $\mathcal{P}r^{L,\ex}$ is the $\infty$-category of presentable stable $\infty$-categories.

Following Lurie, these categories carry cartesian symmetric monoidal structures, and:

- $\CAlg(\Cat_\infty)$ is equivalent to the $\infty$-category of symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories $\Cat^\otimes_\infty$.
- $\CAlg(\Cat_\infty^{\colim})$ is the $\infty$-category of colimit-preserving symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories, i.e. $\infty$-categories admitting small colimits with a symmetric monoidal structure commuting with colimits in each variable, and symmetric monoidal colimit-preserving functors.
- $\CAlg(\Cat_\infty^{\ex})$ is the $\infty$-category of stable symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories, i.e. stable $\infty$-categories with a symmetric monoidal structure exact in each variable, and symmetric monoidal exact functors.
- $\CAlg(\Cat_\infty^{\colim,\ex})$ is the $\infty$-category of colimit-preserving symmetric monoidal stable $\infty$-categories, the full subcategory of $\CAlg(\Cat_\infty^{\colim})$ with stable underlying $\infty$-category.
