# Restatement

## Abstract

We present an effective unified theory based on noncommutative geometry for the standard model with neutrino mixing, minimally coupled to gravity. The unification is based on the symplectic unitary group in Hilbert space and on the spectral action. It yields the detailed structure of the standard model together with several predictions at the unification scale. Besides the predictions for the gauge couplings that are familiar from GUT theories, it predicts the Higgs scattering parameter and the sum of the squares of the Yukawa couplings. From these relations, predictions at low energy are extracted, in particular a Higgs mass around 170 GeV and a top mass compatible with the present experimental value. The geometric picture that emerges is that space-time is the product of an ordinary spin manifold (for which the theory delivers Einstein gravity) by a finite noncommutative geometry $F$. ## 1. Introduction

The model is based on noncommutative geometry and describes the standard model with massive neutrinos, minimally coupled to gravity. The unification is based on the symplectic unitary group in Hilbert space rather than on finite-dimensional Lie groups. The parameters of the model are set at the unification scale, and physical predictions are obtained by running them down through the renormalization group using the Wilsonian approach. For the renormalizability of the gravity part of the model one can follow the renormalization analysis of higher-derivative gravity as in [12] and [21].

The input of the model is the choice of a finite-dimensional algebra, which is natural in the context of left–right symmetric models:

$$
\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C}), \tag{1.1}
$$

where $\mathbb{H}$ is the involutive algebra of quaternions. This algebra has a natural representation $\mathcal{M}$, which is the sum of the irreducible bimodules of odd spin. The fermions of the standard model can be identified with a basis for a sum of $N$ copies of $\mathcal{M}$, where $N$ is the number of generations. The text restricts to $N=3$ generations.

The representation $\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{M}^{\oplus 3}$ of the algebra (1.1) gives, through geometric considerations on the form of a Dirac operator, a subalgebra of (1.1) of the form

$$
\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C}). \tag{1.2}
$$

This gives a model for neutrino mixing with Majorana mass terms and a see-saw mechanism. For this algebra a classification of all possible Dirac operators giving a real spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$ is given. The resulting Dirac operators depend on 31 real parameters, which physically correspond to the masses of leptons and quarks (including neutrino Yukawa masses), the angles of the CKM and PMNS matrices, and the Majorana mass matrix. The resulting family of geometries $F=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$ is metrically zero-dimensional, but has dimension $6 \bmod 8$ from the point of view of real $K$-theory.

The product of this finite spectral triple with the spectral triple of a 4-dimensional compact Riemannian spin manifold is considered. The bosons of the standard model, including the Higgs, are obtained as inner fluctuations of the Dirac operator of this product geometry. The corresponding mass scale specifies the inverse size of the discrete geometry $F$. The symmetry is broken by the choice of the finite geometry.

The spectral action principle is applied to this product geometry. It is the universal action functional

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\big(f(D/\Lambda)\big), \tag{1.3}
$$

where $\Lambda$ fixes the energy scale and $f$ is a test function. The function $f$ enters only through its momenta $f_0$, $f_2$, $f_4$, where

$$
f_{k}=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(v)\,v^{k-1}\,dv \quad (k>0), \qquad f_0=f(0).
$$

(See Remark 6.5 below for the relation with the notation of [8].) These give three additional real parameters in the model.

For classical Riemannian manifolds, where no inner fluctuations are present, (1.3) gives the Einstein–Hilbert action of pure gravity. The coupling with fermions is obtained by adding the term

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\big(f(D/\Lambda)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\langle J\psi,D\psi\rangle, \tag{1.4}
$$

where $J$ is the real structure on the spectral triple and $\psi$ is an element of $\mathcal{H}$, viewed as a classical fermion (a Grassmann variable). The fermionic part of the Euclidean functional integral is the Pfaffian of the antisymmetric bilinear form $\langle J\psi',D\psi\rangle$. This gives a substitute for Majorana fermions in Euclidean signature (cf. e.g. [29], [38]).

The gauge symmetries of the standard model, with the correct hypercharge assignment, are obtained as a subgroup of the symplectic unitary group of Hilbert space, given by the adjoint representation of the unimodular unitary group of the algebra. The full Euclidean Lagrangian of the standard model minimally coupled to gravity, with neutrino mixing and Majorana mass terms, is the result of the computation of the asymptotic expansion of the spectral action (1.4). Positivity of the test function $f$ in (1.3) gives positivity of the momenta $f_0$, $f_2$, $f_4$, and the explicit calculation shows that all terms have the expected physical signs.

The resulting action contains the Einstein–Hilbert action with a cosmological term, the square of the Weyl curvature, and a pairing of the scalar curvature with the square of the Higgs field. The Weyl curvature term does not affect gravity at low energies, due to the smallness of the Planck length. The coupling of the Higgs to the scalar curvature was discussed by Feynman in [23]. The general form of the Dirac operator for the finite geometry gives a see-saw mechanism for the neutrinos (cf. [37]). The large masses in the Majorana mass matrix arise as a consequence of the equations of motion.

Under the assumption of the "big desert", the model makes three predictions when running down from the unification scale.

1. The relation $g_{2}=g_{3}=\sqrt{5/3}\,g_{1}$ between the coupling constants at the unification scale, as in GUT models (cf. e.g. [37] §9.1 for $SU(5)$ and [11] for $SO(10)$). This follows directly from the asymptotic expansion of the spectral action and holds for any model that unifies the gauge interactions without altering the fermionic content.
2. The Higgs scattering parameter $\alpha_{h}$ at the unification scale. From this condition one obtains a prediction for the Higgs mass as a function of the $W$ mass, after running down through the renormalization group equations. This gives a Higgs mass of the order of 170 GeV, in agreement with the "big desert" prediction of the minimal standard model (cf. [45]).
3. A mass relation between the Yukawa masses of fermions and the $W$ boson mass, valid at the unification scale:

$$
\sum_{\text{generations}}m_{e}^{2}+m_{\nu}^{2}+3m_{d}^{2}+3m_{u}^{2}=8M_{W}^{2}. \tag{1.5}
$$

After applying the renormalization group to the Yukawa couplings, assuming that the Yukawa coupling for $\nu_\tau$ is comparable to that of the top quark, the text reports good agreement with the measured value.

The model also gives predictions for the gravitational constant in terms of the parameter $f_2/f_0$. The assumption that $f_0$ and $f_2$ are of the same order of magnitude yields a realistic value for the Newton constant.

The properties of the finite geometries suggest further approaches. There are examples of spectral triples of metric dimension zero with a different $KO$-homology dimension, realized by homogeneous spaces over quantum groups [20]. The data parameterizing the Dirac operators can be described in terms of classical moduli spaces related to double coset spaces of the form $K\backslash(G\times G)/(K\times K)$, where $G$ is a reductive group and $K$ the maximal compact subgroup acting diagonally on the left. The renormalization group defines a flow on this moduli space. The product geometry is 10-dimensional from the $KO$-homology point of view and may be realized as a low-energy truncation, using the type of compact fibers considered in string theory models (cf. e.g. [25]).

The "big desert" hypothesis is not expected to hold exactly. The fact that experimental values show the coupling constants do not exactly meet at the unification scale indicates the presence of new physics.

The modification of the standard model required by neutrino mixing gives several improvements on previous descriptions of the standard model via noncommutative geometry. The intricate Lagrangian of the standard model coupled with gravity is obtained from a modification of space-time geometry within the formalism of noncommutative geometry. Section 5 contains the predictions and can be read directly. Section 4 compares the spectral action with the standard model. Sections 2 and 3 are more mathematical. The results develop the preliminary announcement of [17]. The independent preprint of John Barrett [4] gives a solution of the fermion doubling problem.

## 2. Finite noncommutative geometry

**Definition 2.1.** Let $\mathcal{M}$ be an $\mathcal{A}_{LR}$-bimodule. Then $\mathcal{M}$ is *odd* iff the adjoint action of $s=(1,-1,-1,1)$ satisfies $\operatorname{Ad}(s)=-1$.

**Lemma 2.2.** An odd bimodule $\mathcal{M}$ is a representation of the reduction
$$\mathcal{B}=(\mathcal{A}_{LR}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}_{LR}^{0})_{p}$$
of $\mathcal{A}_{LR}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}_{LR}^{0}$ by the projection $p=\tfrac12(1-s\otimes s^{0})$. This subalgebra is an algebra over $\mathbb{C}$.

**Definition 2.3.** The contragredient bimodule of a bimodule $\mathcal{M}$ is the complex conjugate space
$$\mathcal{M}^{0}=\{\bar\xi\;;\;\xi\in\mathcal{M}\},\qquad a\,\bar\xi\,b=\overline{b^{*}\xi\,a^{*}},\qquad\forall\,a,b\in\mathcal{A}_{LR}. \tag{2.3}$$

**Proposition 2.4.** Let $\mathcal{M}_F$ be the direct sum of all inequivalent irreducible odd $\mathcal{A}_{LR}$-bimodules.
- The dimension of the complex vector space $\mathcal{M}_F$ is $32$.
- The $\mathcal{A}_{LR}$-bimodule $\mathcal{M}_F=\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^0$ is the direct sum of the bimodule
$$\mathcal{E}=\mathbf{2}_L\otimes\mathbf{1}^0\oplus\mathbf{2}_R\otimes\mathbf{1}^0\oplus\mathbf{2}_L\otimes\mathbf{3}^0\oplus\mathbf{2}_R\otimes\mathbf{3}^0 \tag{2.4}$$
with its contragredient $\mathcal{E}^0$.
- The bimodule $\mathcal{M}_F$ is isomorphic to the contragredient bimodule $\mathcal{M}_F^0$ by the antilinear isometry $J_F$ given by
$$J_F(\xi,\bar\eta)=(\eta,\bar\xi),\qquad\forall\,\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{E}. \tag{2.5}$$
- One has
$$J^{2}=1,\qquad \xi\,b=Jb^{*}J\,\xi,\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathcal{M}_F,\;b\in\mathcal{A}_{LR}. \tag{2.6}$$

**Lemma 2.5.** The algebra $\mathcal{B}=(\mathcal{A}_{LR}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}_{LR}^{0})_{p}$ is the direct sum of 4 copies of $M_2(\mathbb{C})\oplus M_6(\mathbb{C})$. The sum of irreducible representations of $\mathcal{B}$ has dimension $32$ and is given by
$$\mathbf{2}_L\otimes\mathbf{1}^0\oplus\mathbf{2}_R\otimes\mathbf{1}^0\oplus\mathbf{2}_L\otimes\mathbf{3}^0\oplus\mathbf{2}_R\otimes\mathbf{3}^0\oplus\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{2}_L^0\oplus\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{2}_R^0\oplus\mathbf{3}\otimes\mathbf{2}_L^0\oplus\mathbf{3}\otimes\mathbf{2}_R^0. \tag{2.7}$$

**Definition 2.6.** A spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$ is given by an involutive unital algebra $\mathcal{A}$ represented as operators in a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ and a self-adjoint operator $D$ with compact resolvent such that all commutators $[D,a]$ are bounded for $a\in\mathcal{A}$. A spectral triple is *even* if $\mathcal{H}$ is endowed with a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading $\gamma$ which commutes with any $a\in\mathcal{A}$ and anticommutes with $D$.

**Definition 2.7.** A real structure of $KO$-dimension $n\in\mathbb{Z}/8$ on a spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$ is an antilinear isometry $J:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}$ such that
$$J^{2}=\varepsilon,\qquad JD=\varepsilon'DJ,\qquad\text{and}\qquad J\gamma=\varepsilon''\gamma J\ \text{(even case)}. \tag{2.8}$$
The signs $\varepsilon,\varepsilon',\varepsilon''\in\{-1,1\}$ are functions of $n \bmod 8$:

| $n$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\varepsilon$ | 1 | 1 | −1 | −1 | −1 | −1 | 1 | 1 |
| $\varepsilon'$ | 1 | −1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | −1 | 1 | 1 |

The values of $\varepsilon''$ listed in the source are $1,\,-1,\,1,\,-1$. The action of $\mathcal{A}$ satisfies
$$[a,b^{0}]=0\qquad\forall\,a,b\in\mathcal{A}, \tag{2.9}$$
where
$$b^{0}=Jb^{*}J^{-1}\qquad\forall\,b\in\mathcal{A}, \tag{2.10}$$
and $D$ satisfies the order-one condition
$$[[D,a],b^{0}]=0\qquad\forall\,a,b\in\mathcal{A}. \tag{2.11}$$
A spectral triple endowed with a real structure is called a *real spectral triple*.

**Definition 2.8.** Let $\Omega^1_D$ denote the $\mathcal{A}$-bimodule
$$\Omega_{D}^{1}=\Big\{\sum_{j}a_{j}[D,b_{j}]\;\Big|\;a_{j},b_{j}\in\mathcal{A}\Big\}. \tag{2.14}$$

**Definition 2.9.** The inner fluctuations of the metric are
$$D\to D_{A}=D+A+\varepsilon'\,J\,A\,J^{-1}, \tag{2.15}$$
where $A\in\Omega^1_D$ is self-adjoint, $A=A^*$, of the form
$$A=\sum_{j}a_{j}[D,b_{j}],\qquad a_{j},b_{j}\in\mathcal{A}. \tag{2.16}$$

**Remark 2.10.** The multiplicity $N=3$ is an input and corresponds to the number of particle generations in the standard model. The number of generations is not predicted by the model in its present form.

**Proposition 2.11.** Up to an automorphism of $\mathcal{A}_{LR}$, there exists a unique subalgebra $\mathcal{A}_F\subset\mathcal{A}_{LR}$ of maximal dimension admitting off-diagonal Dirac operators, namely operators that intertwine the subspaces $\mathcal{H}_f$ and $\mathcal{H}_{\bar f}$ of $\mathcal{H}_F$. ## 3. Spectral action and inner fluctuations

**Proposition 3.1.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$ be a real spectral triple in the sense of Definition 2.7. Then:
1. $\mathcal{A}_J=\{x\in\mathcal{A};\;xJ=Jx\}$ defines an involutive commutative real subalgebra of the centre of $\mathcal{A}$.

**Lemma 3.2.** For the finite noncommutative geometry $F$:
- The real part of $\mathcal{A}_F$ is $\mathbb{R}=\{(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda),\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathcal{A}_F$.
- The real part of $C^\infty(M,\mathcal{A}_F)$ for the product geometry $M\times F$ is $C^\infty(M,\mathbb{R})$.

**Remark 3.3.** The real part $\mathcal{A}_J$ can be regarded as a refinement of the centre of the algebra. Although the centre of $\mathcal{A}_F$ is non-trivial, the geometry is "central" in this sense, since the real part of $\mathcal{A}_F$ reduces to the scalars $\mathbb{R}$.

**Definition 3.8.** A gauge potential $A$ is *unimodular* iff $\operatorname{Tr}(A)=0$.

**Proposition 3.9.**
1. The unimodular gauge potentials are parameterized by a $U(1)$ gauge field $B$, an $SU(2)$ gauge field $W$ and an $SU(3)$ gauge field $V$.
2. The adjoint action $A+JAJ^{-1}$ on $\mathcal{H}_f$ is obtained by replacing $\partial_\mu$ by $\partial_\mu+\mathbb{A}_\mu$, where $\mathbb{A}_\mu=(\mathbb{A}^q_\mu\oplus\mathbb{A}^\ell_\mu)\otimes 1_3$ (the $1_3$ accounts for the three generations), and

$$
\mathbb{A}^q_\mu=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{2i}{3}g_1B_\mu\otimes1_3&0&0\\0&\frac{i}{3}g_1B_\mu\otimes1_3&0\\0&0&\big(-\frac{i}{2}g_2W^\alpha_\mu\sigma^\alpha-\frac{i}{6}g_1B_\mu\otimes1_2\big)\otimes1_3\end{bmatrix}+1_4\otimes\Big(-\frac{i}{2}g_3V^i_\mu\lambda^i\Big),
$$

$$
\mathbb{A}^\ell_\mu=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\0&i\,g_1B_\mu&0\\0&0&\big(-\frac{i}{2}g_2W^\alpha_\mu\sigma^\alpha+\frac{i}{2}g_1B_\mu\otimes1_2\big)\end{bmatrix}.
$$

**Remark 3.10.** This gives exactly the gauge bosons of the standard model, with the hypercharges $Y_L$ and $Y_R$. The electromagnetic charge $Q_{em}$ satisfies $2Q_{em}=Y_R$ for right-handed particles and $2Q_{em}=Y_L+2I_3$, where $I_3$ is the third generator of the weak isospin group $SU(2)$. The charge $Q_{em}$ is the same for the left and right components of each particle. It equals $\tfrac23$ and $-\tfrac13$ for the $u$ and $d$ quarks, and $0$ and $-1$ for the $\nu$ and $e$ leptons.

**Proposition 3.11.** The unimodular inner fluctuations of the metric are parameterized by independent fields $\varphi_1,\varphi_2,B,W,V$, as in Propositions 3.5 and 3.9.

**Lemma 3.12.** The square of $D_A$ is
$$D_A^2=\nabla^*\nabla-\mathcal{E}, \tag{3.33}$$
where $\nabla^*\nabla$ is the connection Laplacian for the connection

$$-\mathcal{E}=\frac14 s\otimes\mathrm{id}+\sum_{\mu<\nu}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\mu\nu}-i\gamma_5\gamma^\mu\otimes\mathbb{M}(D_\mu\varphi)+1_4\otimes(D^{0,1})^2, \tag{3.35}$$
