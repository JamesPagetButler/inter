# Restatement

## Abstract

A fuzzy geometry is a type of spectral triple whose Dirac operator is a finite matrix. The class includes fuzzy spheres and fuzzy tori. In the framework of random noncommutative geometry, Barrett's characterization of Dirac operators of fuzzy geometries is used to compute the spectral action $S(D)=\operatorname{Tr} f(D)$ for $2n$-dimensional fuzzy geometries. The polynomial $f$ satisfies $f(x)\to\infty$ as $|x|\to\infty$. The resulting path integral is a random matrix model with action

$$S(D)=N\cdot\operatorname{tr}F+\sum_i \operatorname{tr}A_i\cdot\operatorname{tr}B_i,$$

where $F$, $A_i$ and $B_i$ are noncommutative polynomials in $2^{2n-1}$ complex $N\times N$ matrices that parametrize the Dirac operator $D$.

- For arbitrary signature (any admissible KO-dimension), formulas are given for 2-dimensional fuzzy geometries up to a sextic polynomial in $f$, and for 4-dimensional fuzzy geometries up to a quartic polynomial.
- The noncommutative polynomials $F$, $A_i$, $B_i$ are obtained via chord diagrams and satisfy:
  - independence of $N$;
  - self-adjointness of $F$ modulo cyclic reordering of each monomial;
  - for fixed $i$, up to cyclicity, $A_i$ and $B_i$ are either both self-adjoint or both anti-self-adjoint.

## 1. Introduction

**Path integral.** The partition function is

$$\mathcal{Z}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}e^{-\operatorname{Tr} f(D)}\,dD,\tag{1.1}$$

where $S(D)=\operatorname{Tr} f(D)$ is the (bosonic) spectral action. The integration runs over the space $\mathcal{M}$ of Dirac operators $D$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$. The statement that this is well defined is a simplified version of an open problem stated in [CM07, Ch. 18.4].

**Heat kernel expansion (commutative case).** For a manifold $M$ or an almost commutative space $M\times F$ with $F$ a finite geometry [vS15, Sec. 8],

$$\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tD^2})\sim\sum_{n\ge0}t^{\frac{n-\dim(M)}{2}}a_n(D^2)\qquad(t\to0^+).$$

For $f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^+}e^{-tx^2}\,d\nu(t)$, this determines $\operatorname{Tr} f(D/\Lambda)$ in terms of the Seeley–DeWitt coefficients $a_{2n}(D^2)$ [Gil95], with $t=\Lambda^{-1}$ the inverse cutoff; see also [EI19].

**Polynomial spectral action.** In this paper $f$ is a polynomial with $f(x)\to\infty$ as $|x|\to\infty$. This allows direct computation of traces of powers of $D$. The choice comes from a convergence requirement for the path integral (1.1), as initiated in [BG16]. A polynomial spectral action also appears in [MvS14] for gauge networks arising from embedded quivers in a spin manifold.

**Results.**

- Section 4 gives formulas for the spectral action of 2-dimensional fuzzy geometries.
- Section 5 gives formulas for 4-dimensional fuzzy geometries, for Riemannian and Lorentzian signatures and for any admissible KO-dimension (see Proposition 5.4).
- Formulas for signature $(0,3)$ appear in [BG16, App. A.6]; they give a tetra-matrix model. Strictly speaking this is an octo-matrix model, but a simplification is allowed because the product of all gamma matrices is a scalar.
- Signatures $(1,3)$ (KO-dimension 2), $(1,1)$ (KO-dimension 0) and $(2,0)$ (KO-dimension 6) are treated in [Gla17] and [Gla].

**Main polynomial structure.** Write

$$\operatorname{Tr} f(D)=N\cdot\operatorname{Tr}_N P+\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}_N\Phi_i\,\operatorname{Tr}_N\Psi_i.$$

The main polynomial $P$ is cyclically self-adjoint. For fixed $i$, the polynomials $\Phi_i$ and $\Psi_i$ either satisfy this same condition, or both are cyclically anti-self-adjoint. The trace $\operatorname{Tr}_N$ cannot distinguish these conditions from genuine self-adjointness of a noncommutative polynomial.

**Chord diagrams.** Trace identities for gamma matrices are described by chord diagrams. These are used to compute $\operatorname{Tr}(D^m)$, $m\in\mathbb{N}$.

## 2. Spectral triples and fuzzy geometries

**Definition 2.1.** A real, even spectral triple of KO-dimension $s\in\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}$ consists of the following objects and relations.

- (i) An algebra $\mathcal{A}$ with involution $*$.
- (ii) A Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ and a faithful $*$-representation $\rho:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})$.
- (iii) An antilinear unitary $J:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}$ (the real structure) with $\langle Jv,Jw\rangle=\langle w,u\rangle$, where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the inner product of $\mathcal{H}$.
- (iv) A self-adjoint operator $\gamma:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}$ (the chirality) commuting with $\rho$, with $\gamma^2=1$.
- (v) For all $a,b\in\mathcal{A}$: $[\rho(a),J\rho(b)J^{-1}]=0$.
- (vi) A self-adjoint operator $D$ on $\mathcal{H}$ with $[[D,\rho(a)],J\rho(b)J^{-1}]=0$ for all $a,b\in\mathcal{A}$.
- (vii) The relations
$$J^2=\epsilon,\qquad JD=\epsilon' DJ,\qquad J\gamma=\epsilon''\gamma J,\tag{2.1}$$
with signs $\epsilon,\epsilon',\epsilon''$ determined by $s$ as follows.

| $s$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\epsilon$ | + | + | + | + | − | − | − | − |
| $\epsilon'$ | + | + | − | − | + | + | + | + |
| $\epsilon''$ | + | + | + | − | − | + | + | + |

**Definition 2.2** (paraphrased from [Bar15]). A fuzzy geometry of signature $(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^2_{\ge0}$ is given by the following data.

- A simple matrix algebra $\mathcal{A}$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}$. If $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{H}$, then $M_{N/2}(\mathbb{H})\subset M_N(\mathbb{C})$. Otherwise $\mathcal{A}$ is $M_N(\mathbb{R})$ or $M_N(\mathbb{C})$. In this paper $\mathcal{A}=M_N(\mathbb{C})$ always.
- A Hermitian $\mathcal{C}\ell(p,q)$-module $V$ with chirality $\gamma:V\to V$, satisfying $\gamma^*=\gamma$ and $\gamma^2=1$.
- A Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}=V\otimes M_N(\mathbb{C})$ with inner product $\langle v\otimes R,w\otimes S\rangle=(v,w)\operatorname{Tr}(R^*S)$ for $R,S\in M_N(\mathbb{C})$, where $(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the inner product of $V$.
- A left $\mathcal{A}$-representation $\rho(a)(v\otimes R)=v\otimes(aR)$.
- Three signs $\epsilon,\epsilon',\epsilon''\in\{-1,+1\}$ determined through $s:=q-p$ by the same table as in Definition 2.1:

| $s\equiv q-p \pmod 8$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\epsilon$ | + | + | + | + | − | − | − | − |
| $\epsilon'$ | + | + | − | − | + | + | + | + |
| $\epsilon''$ | + | + | + | − | − | + | + | + |

*(As extracted.)*

- A real structure $J=C\otimes *$, where $*$ is complex conjugation and $C$ is an antiunitary on $V$ with $C^2=\epsilon$ and $C\gamma^\mu=\epsilon'\gamma^\mu C$ for all $\mu=1,\dots,p+q$.
- A self-adjoint operator $D$ on $\mathcal{H}$ satisfying the order-one condition $[[D,\rho(a)],J\rho(b)J^{-1}]=0$ for all $a,b\in\mathcal{A}$.
- A chirality $\Gamma=\gamma\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}}$ on $\mathcal{H}$.

These signs impose

$$J^2=\epsilon,\qquad JD=\epsilon' DJ,\qquad J\Gamma=\epsilon''\Gamma J.$$

For $s$ odd, $\Gamma$ can be taken to be the identity $1_{\mathcal{H}}$. The number $d=p+q$ is the dimension of the spectral triple, and $s=q-p$ is its KO-dimension.

**Example 2.3** (fuzzy $d=2$ geometries). These operators appear in [Bar15].

- **Type $(0,2)$.** Here $s=d=2$, so $\epsilon'=1$. The gamma matrices are anti-Hermitian and satisfy $(\gamma^i)^2=-1$. The Dirac operator is
$$D^{(0,2)}=\gamma^{\dot1}\otimes[L_1,\bullet\,]+\gamma^{\dot2}\otimes[L_2,\bullet\,].$$
- **Type $(1,1)$.** Here $d=2$, $s=0$, so $\epsilon'=1$. The Dirac operator is
$$D^{(1,1)}=\gamma^1\otimes\{H,\bullet\,\}+\gamma^{\dot2}\otimes[L,\bullet\,].$$
- **Type $(2,0)$.** Here $d=2$, $s=6$, so $\epsilon'=1$. The gamma matrices are Hermitian and satisfy $(\gamma^i)^2=+1$. The Dirac operator is
$$D^{(2,0)}=\gamma^1\otimes\{H_1,\bullet\,\}+\gamma^2\otimes\{H_2,\bullet\,\}.$$

**Example 2.4** (fuzzy $d=4$ geometries). The most important cases for realistic models are signatures $(0,4)$ and $(1,3)$, the Riemannian and Lorentzian cases. The Riemannian case is derived in detail in order to arrive at [Bar15, Ex. 10]. The other cases follow from equation (2.9).

- **Type $(0,4)$, $s=4$, Riemannian.** All gamma matrices are anti-Hermitian and square to $-1$. Products of three gamma matrices are self-adjoint:
$$(\gamma^{\dot a}\gamma^{\dot b}\gamma^{\dot c})^*=(-)^3\gamma^{\dot c}\gamma^{\dot b}\gamma^{\dot a}=\gamma^{\dot a}\gamma^{\dot b}\gamma^{\dot c}.$$
The Dirac operator is
$$D^{(0,4)}=\sum_{\dot a}\gamma^{\dot a}\otimes[L_{\dot a},\bullet\,]+\sum_{\dot a<\dot b<\dot c}\gamma^{\dot a}\gamma^{\dot b}\gamma^{\dot c}\otimes\{H_{\dot a\dot b\dot c},\bullet\,\},\tag{2.10}$$
with $H_{\dot a\dot b\dot c}$ self-adjoint.
- **Type $(1,3)$, $s=2$, Lorentzian.** Let $\gamma^0$ be the only gamma matrix squaring to $+1$, and let $\gamma^{\dot c}$, $\dot c=1,2,3$, be the others. Then
$$D^{(1,3)}=\gamma^0\otimes\{H,\bullet\,\}+\gamma^{\dot c}\otimes[L_{\dot c},\bullet\,]+\sum_{\dot a<\dot c}\gamma^0\gamma^{\dot a}\gamma^{\dot c}\otimes[L_{\dot a\dot c},\bullet\,]+\gamma^{\dot1}\gamma^{\dot2}\gamma^{\dot3}\otimes\{\tilde H,\bullet\,\}.\tag{2.11}$$
For Lorentzian signature the dotted-index convention is redundant with the spacetime indices $0,1,2,3$, and is dropped henceforth.
- **Type $(4,0)$, $s=-4=4 \pmod 8$.** This is the opposite case to Riemannian. All gamma matrices are Hermitian and square to $+1$. Triple products are skew-Hermitian. The Dirac operator is
$$D^{(4,0)}=\sum_a\gamma^a\otimes\{H_a,\bullet\,\}+\sum_{a<b<c}\gamma^a\gamma^b\gamma^c\otimes[L_{abc},\bullet\,].\tag{2.12}$$
- **Type $(2,2)$, $s=0$.** The first two gamma matrices square to $+1$, and $\gamma^{\dot3}$, $\gamma^{\dot4}$ square to $-1$. Then
$$D^{(2,2)}=\sum_{a=1,2}\gamma^a\otimes\{H_a,\bullet\,\}+\gamma^a\gamma^{\dot3}\gamma^{\dot4}\otimes[\tilde L_a,\bullet\,]+\sum_{\dot c=\dot3,\dot4}\gamma^{\dot c}\otimes[L_{\dot c},\bullet\,]+\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^{\dot c}\otimes\{\tilde H_{\dot c},\bullet\,\}.\tag{2.13}$$
Here $\tilde L_a=L_{\hat a}=L_{1\dots\hat a\dots4}$ and $\tilde H_{\dot c}=H_{1\dots\hat{\dot c}\dots4}$.
- **Type $(3,1)$, $s=6$.** Then
$$D^{(3,1)}=\sum_{a=1,2,3}\gamma^a\otimes\{H_a,\bullet\,\}+\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3\otimes[\tilde L,\bullet\,]+\gamma^{\dot4}\otimes[L,\bullet\,]+\sum_{a<c}\gamma^a\gamma^c\gamma^{\dot4}\otimes\{H_{ac\dot4},\bullet\,\}.\tag{2.14}$$

## 3. Spectral action in a general setting

**Proposition 3.1.** For any $r\in\mathbb{N}$,

$$\operatorname{Tr}_{M_N(\mathbb{C})}(k_{I_1}\cdots k_{I_r})=\sum_{\Upsilon\in\mathscr{P}_r}\operatorname{sgn}(I_\Upsilon)\cdot\operatorname{Tr}_N\big(K_{I_{\Upsilon^c}}\big)\cdot\operatorname{Tr}_N\big[(K^T)_{I_\Upsilon}\big],\tag{3.7}$$

where:

- $\operatorname{Tr}_N$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_{M_N(\mathbb{C})}$ are the traces on $\operatorname{End}(\mathbf N)$ and $\operatorname{End}(M_N(\mathbb{C}))$, respectively;
- $\mathscr{P}_r=2^{\{1,\dots,r\}}$ is the power set of $\{1,\dots,r\}$, and $\Upsilon^c=\{1,\dots,r\}\setminus\Upsilon$;
- $\operatorname{sgn}(I_\Upsilon)=(-1)^{\#\{\text{commutators appearing in all }k_{I_j},\,j\in\Upsilon\}}=\prod_{i\in\Upsilon}e_{I_i}\in\{-1,+1\}$;
- the cyclic order $(\dots\to r\to1\to2\to3\to\dots)$ on $\{1,\dots,r\}$, read off from the left-hand side of (3.7), induces a cyclic order on each subset $\Xi=\{b_1,\dots,b_\xi\}\in\mathscr{P}_r$. Respecting this order, define
$$K_{I_\Xi}=K_{I_{b_1}}K_{I_{b_2}}\cdots K_{I_{b_\xi}},\qquad (K^T)_{I_\Xi}=K^T_{I_{b_1}}K^T_{I_{b_2}}\cdots K^T_{I_{b_\xi}}=(K_{I_{b_\xi}}\cdots K_{I_{b_2}}K_{I_{b_1}})^T.$$

**Proposition 3.2.** Let $I_i\in\Lambda_d^-$ be multi-indices, and let $2n$ denote the total number of indices,
$$2n=2n(I_1,\dots,I_{2t}):=|I_1|+\dots+|I_{2t}|.$$
Then the even powers of the Dirac operator satisfy

$$\frac{1}{\dim V}\operatorname{Tr}(D^{2t})=\sum_{I_1,\dots,I_{2t}\in\Lambda_d}\bigg\{\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{CD}_{2n}}\chi^{I_1\dots I_{2t}}\times\Big[\sum_{\Upsilon\in\mathscr P_{2t}}\operatorname{sgn}(I_\Upsilon)\operatorname{Tr}_N(K_{I_{\Upsilon^c}})\operatorname{Tr}_N((K^T)_{I_\Upsilon})\Big]\bigg\}.\tag{3.8}$$

In terms of these, the spectral action $S(D)=\operatorname{Tr} f(D)=\sum_t f_{2t}\operatorname{Tr}(D^{2t})$ can be completely evaluated.

**Definition 3.3.** Let $t,n\in\mathbb{N}$, interpreted as in Proposition 3.2, and let $\chi\in\mathrm{CD}_{2n}$ be a chord diagram. Its action (functional) $\mathfrak a_n(\chi)$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-valued functional on $M_N(\mathbb{C})^{\oplus\kappa(d)}$, defined by

$$\mathfrak a_n(\chi)[\boldsymbol K]=\sum_{\substack{I_1,\dots,I_{2t}\in\Lambda_d^-\\ 2n=\sum_i|I_i|}}\chi^{I_1\dots I_{2t}}\Big[\sum_{\Upsilon\in\mathscr P_{2t}}\operatorname{sgn}(I_\Upsilon)\operatorname{Tr}_N(K_{I_{\Upsilon^c}})\operatorname{Tr}_N((K^T)_{I_\Upsilon})\Big]\tag{3.10}$$

for $\boldsymbol K=\{K_{I_i}\in M_N(\mathbb{C})\mid I_i\in\Lambda_d^-\}\in M_N(\mathbb{C})^{\oplus\kappa(d)}$. The dependence on the matrices is often omitted.

The bi-trace functional is the sum over non-trivial subsets:

$$\mathfrak b_n(\chi)[\boldsymbol K]=\sum_{\substack{I_1,\dots,I_{2t}\in\Lambda_d^-\\ 2n=\sum_i|I_i|}}\chi^{I_1\dots I_{2t}}\Big[\sum_{\substack{\Upsilon\in\mathscr P_{2t}\\ \Upsilon,\Upsilon^c\ne\emptyset}}\operatorname{sgn}(I_\Upsilon)\operatorname{Tr}_N(K_{I_{\Upsilon^c}})\operatorname{Tr}_N((K^T)_{I_\Upsilon})\Big].\tag{3.11}$$

The single-trace functional $\mathfrak s_n(\chi)$ is defined by

$$\mathfrak a_n(\chi)=N\cdot\mathfrak s_n(\chi)+\mathfrak b_n(\chi).$$

The factor $N$ ensures that $\mathfrak s_n$ does not depend on $N$ (cf. Sec. 3.4).

**Corollary 3.4.** Let $d=q+p$ (so $s$ is determined) be even. The spectral action for the fuzzy $(p,q)$-geometry with Dirac operator $D=D^{(p,q)}$ satisfies, for any polynomial $f(x)=\sum_{1\le r\le m}f_r x^r$,

$$\frac{1}{\dim V}\operatorname{Tr}f(D)=\frac{1}{\dim V}\sum_{1\le 2t\le m}f_{2t}\operatorname{Tr}\big([D^{(p,q)}]^{2t}\big)=N\sum_{1\le 2t\le m}f_{2t}\mathcal S_{2t}+\mathcal B,$$

where $2n(I_1,\dots,I_{2t})=\sum_i|I_i|$. Here $\mathcal B$ denotes products of two traces, whose coefficients are independent of $N$. The coefficient $\mathcal S_{2t}$ is given by (3.12), and for $\chi\in\mathrm{CD}_{2n}$,

$$\mathfrak s_n(\chi)=\sum_{\substack{I_1,\dots,I_{2t}\in\Lambda_d^-\\ 2n=\sum_i|I_i|}}\chi^{I_1\dots I_{2t}}\Big\{\operatorname{Tr}_N(K_{I_1}K_{I_2}\cdots K_{I_{2t}})+(e_{I_1}\cdots e_{I_{2t}})\operatorname{Tr}_N(K_{I_{2t}}K_{I_{2t-1}}\cdots K_{I_1})\Big\}.\tag{3.14}$$

**Proposition 3.5.** The Dirac operator of a fuzzy geometry of signature $(p,q)$ satisfies, for odd $d=p+q$,

$$\frac{1}{\dim V}\operatorname{Tr}\big([D^{(p,q)}]^2\big)=2\sum_{I\in\Lambda_d}(-1)^{u(I)+\binom{|I|}{2}}\big[N\cdot\operatorname{Tr}_N(K_I^2)+e_I(\operatorname{Tr}_N K_I)^2\big],$$

where $u(I)$ is the number of spatial indices in $I$.

If $d$ is even, the sum runs only over $I\in\Lambda_d^-$, and

$$\frac{1}{\dim V}\operatorname{Tr}\big([D^{(p,q)}]^2\big)=2\sum_{I\in\Lambda_d^-}(-1)^{u(I)+r(I)-1}\big[N\cdot\operatorname{Tr}_N(K_I^2)+e_I(\operatorname{Tr}_N K_I)^2\big],$$

with $|I|=2r(I)-1$.

## 4. Two-dimensional fuzzy geometries
