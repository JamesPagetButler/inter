# Restatement

## Abstract and Introduction

The text is an introduction to M-theory compactifications on manifolds with exceptional holonomy. It states, in part, the following.

M-theory is a candidate for a consistent quantum theory of gravity. At long distances it describes physics in spacetimes of eleven dimensions.

The main idea of Grand Unification is that three of the four fundamental forces combine into a single force at high energies. The unification condition is

$$\alpha_{1}(M_{GUT})=\alpha_{2}(M_{GUT})=\alpha_{3}(M_{GUT}) \tag{1.3}$$

at the scale

$$M_{GUT}\approx 10^{16}~GeV \tag{1.1}$$

The Standard Model gauge group is

$$U_{Y}(1)\otimes SU_{L}(2)\otimes SU_{c}(3) \tag{1.2}$$

A minimal supersymmetric generalization of the Standard Model (MSSM), with superpartner masses above the effective supersymmetry scale

$$M_{SUSY}\approx 1~TeV, \tag{1.4}$$

yields perfect unification (1.3) at the GUT scale (1.1) [ 3 ].

The review considers M-theory on a 7-manifold $X$ of $G_2$ holonomy, and on manifolds of $Spin(7)$ holonomy. The relevant duality is between M-theory on $K3$-fibered $G_2$-manifolds and heterotic string theory on $T^3$-fibered Calabi–Yau threefolds, as stated in the text. The text cites [ 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 ] for the singularities of $G_2$-manifolds that give non-Abelian gauge groups and chiral fermions, and refers to [ 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 ] for phenomenological applications.

The text ends by noting that the mechanisms of supersymmetry breaking and the smallness of the cosmological constant are not currently understood.
