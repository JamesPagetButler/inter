# Restatement

## Abstract (restated)

The paper develops basic concepts in the theory of categories internal to an $\infty$-topos $\mathcal{B}$. It treats adjunctions, limits and colimits, and Kan extensions for internal categories. These results are used to prove the universal property of internal presheaf categories. The paper also constructs the free cocompletion of an internal category by colimits indexed by an arbitrary class of diagram shapes.

## Introduction (mathematical content)

**Setting.** Fix an $\infty$-topos $\mathcal{B}$. A $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$ is a simplicial object in $\mathcal{B}$ satisfying the Segal conditions and univalence (see [Mar21, Definition 3.2.4]).

**Yoneda lemma for internal categories [Mar21].** For any $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$ there is a fully faithful functor of $\mathcal{B}$-categories
$$h\colon \mathsf{C}\rightarrow \underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega})$$
such that the functor
$$\operatorname{\Map}_{\underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega})}(h(-),-)\colon \mathsf{C}\times\underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega})\rightarrow \mathsf{\Omega}$$
is equivalent to the evaluation functor.

- $\mathsf{\Omega}$ is the universe in $\mathcal{B}$, defined as the $\mathcal{B}$-category corresponding to the sheaf $\mathcal{B}_{/-}\colon \mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\widehat{\Cat}_{\infty}$.
- $\underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}(-,-)$ denotes the internal hom of $\mathcal{B}$-categories.
- Notation: $\underline{\mathsf{PSh}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C}) = \underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega})$.

**Main theorem.** See Theorem 7.1.1 below. The Yoneda embedding $h\colon \mathsf{C}\hookrightarrow\underline{\mathsf{PSh}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C})$ exhibits the $\mathcal{B}$-category of presheaves on $\mathsf{C}$ as the free cocompletion of $\mathsf{C}$.

**Adjunctions.** Internal adjunctions of $\mathcal{B}$-categories form a theory parallel to that of adjunctions between $\infty$-categories. Adjunctions of $\mathcal{B}$-categories are also related to section-wise adjunctions of the corresponding sheaves on $\mathcal{B}$ that are compatible with étale base change. See Proposition 3.2.9.

**Cocompleteness.** A large $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$ is internally cocomplete if and only if the associated functor $\mathsf{C}\colon \mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\widehat{\Cat}_{\infty}$ factors through the subcategory of cocomplete $\infty$-categories and cocontinuous functors, such that the transition functors admit left adjoints that are compatible in a suitable sense.

**Kan extensions.** See Theorem 6.3.5 below. Its refined formulation, in which only colimits of certain comma $\mathcal{B}$-categories need to exist in $\mathsf{E}$, is given in § 6.3.

**Generalisation.** The results are generalised to the free $\mathsf{U}$-cocompletion of a small $\mathcal{B}$-category, for any internal class $\mathsf{U}$ of indexing $\mathcal{B}$-categories (see Definition 5.1.1).

**Relation to parametrised higher category theory.** When $\mathcal{B}=\PSh(\mathcal{C})$ is a presheaf $\infty$-topos, most results are contained in [Sha23] or [Sha21]. For general $\mathcal{B}$, the results also follow from parametrised theory, since $\mathcal{B}$ is a left exact localisation of a presheaf $\infty$-topos $\PSh(\mathcal{C})$.

**Universe.** The map $\Map_{\mathsf{\Omega}_{\mathcal{B}}}(1_{\mathsf{\Omega}},-)\colon \mathsf{\Omega}_{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow\mathsf{\Omega}_{\mathcal{B}}$ is equivalent to the identity.

## Statements

**Theorem 7.1.1.** For any small $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$ and any cocomplete large $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{E}$, the functor of left Kan extension $h_{!}$ along the Yoneda embedding $h\colon \mathsf{C}\hookrightarrow\underline{\mathsf{PSh}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{B})$ induces an equivalence
$$h_{!}\colon \underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}^{\text{cc}}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{E})\simeq \underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\underline{\mathsf{PSh}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C}),\mathsf{E}),$$
where $\underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}^{\text{cc}}(\underline{\mathsf{PSh}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C}),\mathsf{E})$ denotes the full subcategory of $\underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\underline{\mathsf{PSh}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C}),\mathsf{E})$ spanned by the cocontinuous functors. Equivalently, the Yoneda embedding $h\colon \mathsf{C}\hookrightarrow\underline{\mathsf{PSh}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C})$ exhibits the $\mathcal{B}$-category of presheaves on $\mathsf{C}$ as the free cocompletion of $\mathsf{C}$.

**Theorem 6.3.5.** Let $\mathsf{E}$ be a cocomplete (large) $\mathcal{B}$-category and let $f\colon \mathsf{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{D}$ be a functor of small $\mathcal{B}$-categories. Then the functor
$$f^{\ast}\colon \underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{D},\mathsf{E})\to \underline{\mathsf{Fun}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{E})$$
has a left adjoint $f_{!}$, which is fully faithful whenever $f$ is fully faithful.

**Definition 2.6.1** ([Mar21, Definitions 3.1.5 and 3.2.1]). A $\mathcal{B}$-category is a simplicial object $\mathsf{C}\in\mathcal{B}_{\Delta}$ that is internally local with respect to $I^{2}\hookrightarrow\Delta^{2}$ (Segal conditions) and $E^{1}\to 1$ (univalence). The full subcategory of $\mathcal{B}_{\Delta}$ spanned by the $\mathcal{B}$-categories is denoted $\Cat(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{\Delta}$. A $\mathcal{B}$-groupoid is a simplicial object $G\in\mathcal{B}_{\Delta}$ that is internally local with respect to $\Delta^{1}\to\Delta^{0}$. The full subcategory spanned by the $\mathcal{B}$-groupoids is denoted $\Grpd(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{\Delta}$.

**Remark 2.6.2** ([Mar21, Proposition 3.2.7]). A simplicial object $C$ is a $\mathcal{B}$-category if and only if, for all $n\geq 2$, the maps
$$C_{n}\to C_{1}\times_{C_{0}}\cdots\times_{C_{0}}C_{1}$$
and
$$C_{0}\to (C_{0}\times C_{0})\times_{C_{1}\times C_{1}}C_{3}$$
are equivalences.

**Remark 2.6.3.** There are several non-equivalent definitions of the walking equivalence. Charles Rezk [Rez01, § 6] defines it as the simplicial set $J$, the nerve of the category with two objects and a unique isomorphism between them. The model $E^{1}$ (adopted from [Lur09b, Notation 1.1.12]) consists of a map together with separate left and right inverses. There is a natural map $E^{1}\to J$ contained in the internal saturation of $I^{2}\hookrightarrow\Delta^{2}$, i.e. it becomes an equivalence when the Segal conditions are imposed. Either choice therefore gives the same notion of $\mathcal{B}$-categories. This is extracted from [Rez01, § 6]; see also [Ras18, § 2.4].

**Proposition 2.6.4** ([Mar21, Proposition 3.2.9, Remark 3.2.10 and Proposition 3.2.11]). The inclusion $\Cat(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{\Delta}$ preserves filtered colimits and admits a left adjoint which preserves finite products. Therefore $\Cat(\mathcal{B})$ is presentable and an exponential ideal in $\mathcal{B}_{\Delta}$, and in particular cartesian closed.

**Proposition 2.6.5** ([Mar21, after Corollary 3.2.12]). A simplicial object in $\mathcal{B}$ is a $\mathcal{B}$-groupoid if and only if it is constant, i.e. contained in the essential image of the diagonal embedding $\iota\colon\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{\Delta}$. Every $\mathcal{B}$-groupoid is a $\mathcal{B}$-category. The resulting embedding $\mathcal{B}\simeq\Grpd(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow\Cat(\mathcal{B})$ admits both a left adjoint $(-)^{\mathrm{gpd}}$ (the groupoidification functor) and a right adjoint $(-)^{\simeq}$ (the core $\mathcal{B}$-groupoid functor). Explicitly, for a $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$,
$$\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{gpd}}\simeq \colim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{C}\quad\text{and}\quad \mathsf{C}^{\simeq}\simeq \mathsf{C}_{0}.$$

**Definition 2.6.6.** If $\mathsf{C}$ is a $\mathcal{B}$-category, $\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ denotes the simplicial object obtained by precomposing $\mathsf{C}\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{B}$ with the involution $(-)^{\mathrm{op}}\colon\Delta\simeq\Delta$, which sends $\langle n\rangle$ (viewed as a $0$-category) to its opposite $\langle n\rangle^{\mathrm{op}}$. The simplicial object $\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ is again a $\mathcal{B}$-category, called the opposite $\mathcal{B}$-category of $\mathsf{C}$.

**Remark 2.6.7.** The equivalence $(-)^{\mathrm{op}}\colon\Cat(\mathcal{B})\simeq\Cat(\mathcal{B})$ from Definition 2.6.6 restricts to the identity on $\Grpd(\mathcal{B})$. This follows from the observation that $\mathcal{B}$-groupoids are constant simplicial objects (see Proposition 2.6.5).

**Remark 2.6.8** ([Mar21, § 3.3]). If $f_{\ast}\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}$ is a geometric morphism with associated algebraic morphism $f^{\ast}$, postcomposition induces an adjunction $f^{\ast}\dashv f_{\ast}\colon\Cat(\mathcal{A})\leftrightarrows\Cat(\mathcal{B})$. In particular there is an adjunction $\const_{\mathcal{B}}\dashv\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}}\colon\Cat_{\infty}\leftrightarrows\Cat(\mathcal{B})$. An $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$ is implicitly identified with the constant $\mathcal{B}$-category $\const_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{C})\in\Cat(\mathcal{B})$. If $f_{\ast}$ is étale, the further left adjoint $f_{!}$ of $f^{\ast}$ induces a functor $f_{!}\colon\Cat(\mathcal{B})\to\Cat(\mathcal{A})$ that identifies $\Cat(\mathcal{B})$ with $\Cat(\mathcal{A})_{/f_{!}1}$.

**Remark 2.6.9** ([Mar21, Proposition 3.1.2]). There is an equivalence of functors $\id_{\Cat(\mathcal{B})}\simeq((-)^{\Delta^{\bullet}})^{\simeq}$. For any $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$ and any integer $n\geq 0$, one may canonically identify $\mathsf{C}_{n}\simeq(\mathsf{C}^{\Delta^{n}})_{0}$.

**Proposition 2.7.1** ([Mar21, Proposition 3.5.1 and Remark 3.5.6]). There is a natural equivalence of $\infty$-categories $\Cat(\mathcal{B})\simeq\Shv_{\Cat_{\infty}}(\mathcal{B})$ which sends $\mathsf{C}\in\Cat(\mathcal{B})$ to the sheaf $\Fun_{\mathcal{B}}(\iota(-),\mathsf{C})$, where $\iota\colon\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\Cat(\mathcal{B})$ is the diagonal embedding. This equivalence restricts along $\iota$ to the equivalence $\mathcal{B}\simeq\Shv_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{B})$.

**Remark 2.7.2** (cf. [Mar21, Remark 3.1.1]). The $\infty$-category $\mathsf{C}(A)=\Fun_{\mathcal{B}}(\iota(A),\mathsf{C})$ is the complete Segal space whose space of $n$-morphisms is the $\infty$-groupoid $\Map_{\mathcal{B}}(A,\mathsf{C}_{n})$. The equivalence of Proposition 2.7.1 commutes with taking core $\mathcal{B}$-groupoids and opposite $\mathcal{B}$-categories, in the sense that there are equivalences of sheaves
$$\mathsf{C}^{\simeq}(-)\simeq \mathsf{C}(-)^{\simeq}\quad\text{and}\quad \mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}(-)\simeq \mathsf{C}(-)^{\mathrm{op}}.$$

**Remark 2.7.3.** Proposition 2.7.1 is a correspondence between internal and parametrised higher category theory. A statement about $\infty$-categories can be interpreted internally in $\mathcal{B}$ to obtain the corresponding statement for $\mathcal{B}$-categories. Conversely, a $\mathcal{B}$-category is often easier to construct via its associated sheaf of $\infty$-categories. Most practically relevant examples arise in this way.

**Remark 2.7.4** ([Mar21, § 3.5]). The equivalence $\Cat(\mathcal{B})\simeq\Shv_{\Cat_{\infty}}(\mathcal{B})$ is natural in $\mathcal{B}$. For a geometric morphism $f_{\ast}\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}$ with left adjoint $f^{\ast}$, the resulting squares commute. Explicitly, $f_{\ast}\colon\Shv_{\Cat_{\infty}}(\mathcal{B})\to\Shv_{\Cat_{\infty}}(\mathcal{A})$ is restriction along $f^{\ast}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}$, and $f^{\ast}\colon\Shv_{\Cat_{\infty}}(\mathcal{A})\to\Shv_{\Cat_{\infty}}(\mathcal{B})$ is left Kan extension along $f^{\ast}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}$. In particular, $\mathsf{C}(1)\simeq\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C})$ for every $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$. If the latter functor admits a further left adjoint $f_{!}$, then $f^{\ast}\colon\Shv_{\Cat_{\infty}}(\mathcal{A})\to\Shv_{\Cat_{\infty}}(\mathcal{B})$ is given by precomposition with $f_{!}$.

**Remark 2.7.5** ([Mar21, Proposition 3.5.1]). The equivalence between $\mathcal{B}$-categories and sheaves of $\infty$-categories respects universe enlargement. There is a commutative square in which the lower horizontal equivalence sends a large $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$ to $\Fun_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\iota(-),\mathsf{C})$, where $\iota\colon\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{B}}\hookrightarrow\Cat(\widehat{\mathcal{B}})$ is the inclusion.

**Definition 2.8.1.** Let $\mathsf{C}$ be a $\mathcal{B}$-category and let $A\in\mathcal{B}$. For an integer $n\geq 0$, an $n$-morphism in $\mathsf{C}$ in context $A$ is a map $A\to\mathsf{C}^{\Delta^{n}}$ in $\Cat(\mathcal{B})$. For $n=0$ this is an object in context $A$, and for $n=1$ a morphism in context $A$. Given objects $c,d\colon A\rightrightarrows\mathsf{C}$, the mapping $\mathcal{B}_{/A}$-groupoid $\Map_{\mathsf{C}}(c,d)$ is the pullback
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\Map_{\mathsf{C}}(c,d) & \to & \mathsf{C}_{1}\\
\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle (d_{1},d_{0})}\\
A & \xrightarrow{\scriptstyle (c,d)} & \mathsf{C}_{0}\times\mathsf{C}_{0}.
\end{array}$$
A section $f\colon A\to\Map_{\mathsf{C}}(c,d)$ is denoted $f\colon c\to d$.

**Remark 2.8.2** ([Mar21, § 3.6]). Equivalently, $\Map_{\mathsf{C}}(c,d)$ is the pullback of $(d_{1},d_{0})\colon\mathsf{C}^{\Delta^{1}}\to\mathsf{C}\times\mathsf{C}$ along $(c,d)\colon A\to\mathsf{C}\times\mathsf{C}$.

**Remark 2.8.3.** Viewed as an $\mathcal{S}$-valued sheaf on $\mathcal{B}_{/A}$, the object map $\Map_{\mathsf{C}}(c,d)$ of Definition 2.8.1 is given by
$$\mathcal{B}_{/A}\ni(s\colon B\to A)\mapsto \Map_{\mathsf{C}(B)}(s^{\ast}c,s^{\ast}d),$$
where $s^{\ast}c=cs$ and likewise for $d$.

**Remark 2.8.4.** A $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$ is determined by its associated sheaf of $\infty$-categories on $\mathcal{B}$, but not by the underlying $\infty$-category $\Gamma_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathsf{C})$ of global sections. It is therefore essential to allow objects and morphisms in $\mathsf{C}$ to have arbitrary context $A\in\mathcal{B}$. Objects and morphisms are thus only locally defined. The base $\infty$-topos $\mathcal{B}$ is regarded as a spatial object. In the view of simplicial homotopy type theory [RS17], the type-theoretic notion of contexts translates exactly into this notion of contexts.

**Remark 2.8.5.** Using the adjunction $(\pi_{A})_{!}\dashv\pi_{A}^{\ast}\colon\mathcal{B}_{/A}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{B}$ and Remark 2.6.8, an object $c\colon A\to\mathsf{C}$ corresponds exactly to an object $\bar{c}\colon 1_{\mathcal{B}_{/A}}\to\pi_{A}^{\ast}\mathsf{C}$, where $\pi_{A}^{\ast}\mathsf{C}\in\Cat(\mathcal{B}_{/A})$ is the image of $\mathsf{C}$ under the base change functor $\pi_{A}^{\ast}\colon\Cat(\mathcal{B})\to\Cat(\mathcal{B}_{/A})\simeq\Cat(\mathcal{B})_{/A}$. Replacing $\mathcal{B}$ by $\mathcal{B}_{/A}$ and $\mathsf{C}$ by $\pi_{A}^{\ast}\mathsf{C}$ turns objects in context $A$ into objects in global context.

**Remark 2.8.6.** For every $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$ there is a distinguished object $\tau\colon\mathsf{C}_{0}\to\mathsf{C}$, determined by the counit of the adjunction $\iota\dashv(-)_{0}\colon\Cat(\mathcal{B})\leftrightarrows\mathcal{B}$ from Proposition 2.6.5. It is called the tautological object of $\mathsf{C}$. Every object $c\colon A\to\mathsf{C}$ arises as a pullback of $\tau$, in the sense that $c\simeq c^{\ast}\tau$, where $c^{\ast}\colon\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{C}_{0})\to\mathsf{C}(A)$ is the restriction functor.

**Definition 2.8.7.** A morphism $f\colon c\to d$ in $\mathsf{C}$ is an equivalence if there are morphisms $g\colon c\to d$ and $h\colon c\to d$ (all in context $A$) such that $gf\simeq\id_{c}$ and $fh\simeq\id_{d}$.

**Proposition 2.8.8** ([Mar21, Corollary 3.6.3]). A map $f\colon A\to\mathsf{C}^{\Delta^{1}}$ in a $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$ is an equivalence if it factors through $s_{0}\colon\mathsf{C}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{C}^{\Delta^{1}}$.

**Proposition 2.9.1** ([Mar21, Propositions 3.8.6 and 3.8.7]). For a functor $f\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{D}$ of $\mathcal{B}$-categories, the following are equivalent:

1. The functor $f$ is fully faithful.
2. The square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathsf{C}_{1} & \xrightarrow{\scriptstyle f_{1}} & \mathsf{D}_{1}\\
\downarrow & & \downarrow\\
\mathsf{C}_{0}\times\mathsf{C}_{0} & \xrightarrow{\scriptstyle f_{0}\times f_{0}} & \mathsf{D}_{0}\times\mathsf{D}_{0}
\end{array}$$
is a pullback.
3. For every $A\in\mathcal{B}$ and any two objects $c_{0},c_{1}\colon A\to\mathsf{C}$ in context $A$, the morphism map $\Map_{\mathsf{C}}(c_{0},c_{1})\to\Map_{\mathsf{D}}(f(c_{0}),f(c_{1}))$ induced by $f$ is an equivalence in $\mathcal{B}_{/A}$.
4. For every $A\in\mathcal{B}$, the functor $f(A)\colon\mathsf{C}(A)\to\mathsf{D}(A)$ of $\infty$-categories is fully faithful.

**Proposition 2.9.2** ([Mar21, Corollary 3.8.12]). A functor $f\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{D}$ is essentially surjective if and only if $f_{0}\colon\mathsf{C}_{0}\to\mathsf{D}_{0}$ is a cover (i.e. an effective epimorphism) in $\mathcal{B}$.

**Proposition 2.9.3** ([Mar21, Proposition 3.9.3]). Taking core $\mathcal{B}$-groupoids determines an equivalence of posets
$$\Sub^{\mathrm{full}}(\mathsf{D})\simeq\Sub(\mathsf{D}_{0})$$
between the poset of full subcategories of $\mathsf{D}$ and the poset of subobjects of $\mathsf{D}_{0}\in\mathcal{B}$.

**Proposition 2.10.1** ([Mar21, Proposition 3.7.3]). For any two objects $g,h$ in $\mathsf{\Omega}$ in context $A\in\mathcal{B}$, corresponding to $\mathcal{B}_{/A}$-groupoids $\mathsf{G},\mathsf{H}\in\Grpd(\mathcal{B}_{/A})\simeq\mathcal{B}_{/A}$, there is an equivalence
$$\Map_{\mathsf{\Omega}}(g,h)\simeq\underline{\operatorname{hom}}_{\mathcal{B}_{/A}}(\mathsf{G},\mathsf{H})$$
in $\mathcal{B}_{/A}$, where $\underline{\operatorname{hom}}_{\mathcal{B}_{/A}}(-,-)$ denotes the internal hom in $\mathcal{B}_{/A}$.

**Remark 2.10.2.** The universe $\mathsf{\Omega}$ is the $\mathcal{B}$-categorical analogue of the $\infty$-category $\mathcal{S}$ of $\infty$-groupoids. The main result, Theorem 7.1.1, implies that $\mathsf{\Omega}$ is characterised among $\mathcal{B}$-categories by the same universal property that characterises $\mathcal{S}$ among $\infty$-categories, namely as the free cocompletion of the point.

**Proposition 2.10.3** ([Mar21, Proposition 3.9.12]). There is an equivalence between the partially ordered set of local classes in $\mathcal{B}$ and $\Sub^{\mathrm{full}}(\mathsf{\Omega})$.

**Example 2.10.4** (see the discussion towards the end of [Mar21, § 4.5]). Call a map $p\colon P\to A$ in $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}$ small if, for every map $A'\to A$ with $A'\in\mathcal{B}$, the pullback $A'\times_{A}P$ is contained in $\mathcal{B}$. This determines a local class of morphisms in $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}$. By Proposition 2.10.3 it determines a subuniverse of $\mathsf{\Omega}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}\in\Cat(\widehat{\mathcal{B}})$, which can be identified with $\mathsf{\Omega}_{\mathcal{B}}\in\Cat(\mathcal{B})$.
