## Sections 3–6 (definitions, lemmas, propositions)

**Definition 3.1.** Let $X$ be an object of $\mathcal{C}$. A presieve $S$ on $X$ is a set of morphisms with target $X$. If $f\in S$ is a morphism, $\operatorname{dom} f$ denotes the domain of $f$.

**Remark 3.2.** It is sometimes convenient to consider an indexed family of morphisms $(f_i\colon X_i\to X)_{i\in I}$. Any such family yields a presieve on $X$ containing exactly the $f_i$. Conversely, any presieve can be regarded as a family indexed by its elements. An indexed family may have duplicates while a presieve cannot, so the two notions are not exactly equivalent; the text allows free passage between them.

**Definition 3.3.** Let $F\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathsf{Set}$ be a presheaf and $S$ a presieve on $X$. A *family of elements* for $S$ is a collection $(x_f)_{f\in S}$ with $x_f\in F(\operatorname{dom} f)$. The family is *compatible* if for every commutative square with $f,f'\in S$ (i.e. $f\circ g = f'\circ g'$, with $g\colon Y\to\operatorname{dom} f$ and $g'\colon Y\to\operatorname{dom} f'$), we have $F(g)(x_f)=F(g')(x_{f'})$. An element $x\in F(X)$ is an *amalgamation* for $(x_f)_{f\in S}$ if $F(f)(x)=x_f$ for all $f\in S$.

**Definition 3.4.** A presheaf $F$ is a *sheaf for the presieve $S$* if for every compatible family of elements for $S$ there exists a unique amalgamation.

**Remark 3.5.** If $S$ is constructed from a family $(f_i\colon X_i\to X)_{i\in I}$ and all pullbacks $X_i\times_X X_j$ exist, the sheaf condition for $S$ is equivalent to the statement that
$$F(X)\to\prod_{i\in I}F(X_i)\rightrightarrows\prod_{i,j\in I}F(X_i\times_X X_j)$$
is an equalizer, where the first map is $(F(f_i))_{i}$ and the two parallel maps are induced by the projections.

**Definition 3.6.** A *coverage* on $\mathcal{C}$ is the datum of a set of presieves on each object $X$, called *covering presieves*, such that: for every morphism $f\colon X\to Y$ and every covering presieve $S$ on $Y$, there exists a covering presieve $T$ on $X$ such that for each $g\in T$, the composition $f\circ g$ factors through some $h\in S$.

**Definition 3.7.** A *sieve* $S$ on $X$ is a presieve on $X$ that is downward closed: for each $f\in S$ and every $g$ composable with $f$, $f\circ g\in S$. The sieve $\langle R\rangle$ generated by a presieve $R$ consists of all morphisms factoring through a morphism of $R$; it is the smallest sieve containing $R$. It is also called the sieve associated to $R$.

**Remark 3.8.** A sieve $S$ on $X$ is a full subcategory of $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$, hence comes with a forgetful functor $S\to\mathcal{C}$. $S$ induces a cocone over this functor with cocone point $X$ and coprojections the morphisms in $S$.

**Proposition 3.9.** Let $S$ be a presieve on $X$. A presheaf $F$ is a sheaf for $S$ if and only if it is a sheaf for $\langle S\rangle$.

**Definition 3.10.** The pullback of a sieve $S=(g_i\colon Y_i\to Y)_{i\in I}$ along $f\colon X\to Y$ is the sieve on $X$ consisting of all morphisms $g\colon Y_i\to X$ (for $i\in I$) such that $f\circ g\in S$. It is denoted $f^*S$.

**Definition 3.11.** A *Grothendieck topology* on $\mathcal{C}$ is a set of sieves on each object $X$, called *covering sieves*, satisfying:
GT-1) The top sieve (all morphisms with target $X$) is a covering sieve on $X$.
GT-2) For every covering sieve $S$ on $Y$ and every $f\colon X\to Y$, the pullback $f^*S$ is a covering sieve on $X$.
GT-3) If $S$ is a covering sieve on $Y$ and $R$ is a sieve on $Y$ such that $f^*R$ is a covering sieve on $X$ for every $f\colon X\to Y\in S$, then $R$ is a covering sieve on $Y$.

**Lemma 3.12.** Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a Grothendieck topology, $X$ an object, and $S\subseteq R$ sieves on $X$. If $S$ is a covering sieve, then $R$ is a covering sieve.

**Definition 3.13.** The coverage associated to a Grothendieck topology $\mathcal{T}$ is the coverage whose covering presieves are those whose associated sieve is a covering sieve in $\mathcal{T}$. The Grothendieck topology generated by a coverage $\mathcal{S}$ is the intersection of all Grothendieck topologies whose associated coverage contains $\mathcal{S}$.

**Definition 3.14.** A presheaf $F$ is a *sheaf for $\mathcal{T}$* if it is a sheaf for every covering sieve.

**Proposition 3.15.** If a Grothendieck topology $\mathcal{T}$ is generated by a coverage, then a presheaf is a sheaf if and only if it is a sheaf for every covering presieve in the coverage.

**Definition 4.1.** A morphism $f\colon X\to B$ is a *regular epimorphism* if it exhibits $B$ as a coequalizer of some pair of morphisms $g_1,g_2\colon Z\to X$.

**Remark 4.2.** If a regular epimorphism $f\colon X\to B$ has a kernel pair (the pullback $X\times_B X$ exists), then $B$ is the coequalizer of the two projections $X\times_B X\to X$.

**Definition 4.3.** A morphism $f\colon Y\to X$ is an *effective epimorphism* if for every morphism $e$ that coequalizes every pair of parallel morphisms that $f$ coequalizes, there exists a unique morphism $d$ with $d\circ f=e$.

**Remark 4.4.** If $f\colon Y\to X$ is an effective epimorphism, then it is an epimorphism. Indeed, if $h_1\circ f=h_2\circ f$, then $h_1\circ f$ coequalizes every pair $g_1,g_2$ coequalized by $f$; hence there is a unique $d$ with $d\circ f=h_1\circ f$, and since $h_1$ and $h_2$ both satisfy this, $h_1=h_2$.

**Definition 4.5.** Given a family $f=(f_i\colon X_i\to B)_{i\in I}$ and morphisms $g_{j_1}\colon Z\to X_{j_1}$, $g_{j_2}\colon Z\to X_{j_2}$, the family *coequalizes* $g_{j_1}$ and $g_{j_2}$ if $f_{j_1}\circ g_{j_1}=f_{j_2}\circ g_{j_2}$.

**Definition 4.6.** A family $(f_i\colon X_i\to B)_{i\in I}$ is *effective epimorphic* if for every family $(e_i\colon X_i\to W)_{i\in I}$ that coequalizes every pair $g_i, g_j$ which $f$ coequalizes, there exists a unique $d\colon B\to W$ with $d\circ f_i=e_i$ for all $i$.

**Lemma 4.7.** Let $(f_i\colon X_i\to B)$ be a family of morphisms and $S$ the sieve generated by $(f_i)_{i\in I}$, regarded as a presieve. The following are equivalent:
(i) The family $(f_i)_i$ is effective epimorphic.
(ii) For every object $W$, the presheaf $h_W$ is a sheaf for $S$.
(iii) The cocone corresponding to $S$ (Remark 3.8) is colimiting.

**Lemma 4.8.** Let $(\pi_i\colon X_i\to B)_{i\in I}$ be an effective epimorphic family such that the coproduct $\coprod_i X_i$ exists. The induced map $\pi\colon\coprod_i X_i\to B$ is an effective epimorphism.

**Lemma 4.9.** Let $(\pi_i\colon X_i\to B)_{i\in I}$ be a family of morphisms. Suppose:
1) all coproducts and pullbacks appearing in 2) exist;
2) for every object $Z$ and every morphism $g\colon Z\to\coprod_i X_i$, the induced map $i(g):=\coprod_i Z\times_{\coprod_i X_i}X_i\to Z$ is an epimorphism;
3) the induced map $\pi\colon\coprod_i X_i\to B$ is an effective epimorphism.
Then $(\pi_i)_i$ is an effective epimorphic family.

**Lemma 4.10.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be any of $\mathsf{Top}$, $\mathsf{CompHaus}$, $\mathsf{Profinite}$ or $\mathsf{Stonean}$. Then epimorphisms in $\mathcal{C}$ are surjective (continuous) maps.

**Lemma 4.11.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a full subcategory of $\mathsf{Top}$ and $f\colon Y\to X$ a morphism in $\mathcal{C}$ which is a quotient map. Then $f$ is an effective epimorphism in $\mathcal{C}$.

**Definition 5.1.** A category $\mathcal{C}$ is *preregular* if the collection of presieves consisting of single effective epimorphisms forms a coverage. That is, for every effective epimorphism $g\colon Z\to Y$ and every morphism $f\colon X\to Y$, there exist an effective epimorphism $h\colon W\to X$ and a morphism $i\colon W\to Z$ with $g\circ i=f\circ h$. This coverage is the *regular coverage*, and the Grothendieck topology it generates is the *regular topology*.

**Definition 5.2.** A category $\mathcal{C}$ is *finitary extensive* if:
1) $\mathcal{C}$ has finite coproducts;
2) $\mathcal{C}$ has pullbacks along coprojections of finite coproducts;
3) every commutative diagram in which $Z_1\to Z$ and $Z_2\to Z$ map to $X$ and $X\amalg Y\to Y$ are the coprojections, and the outer squares are pullbacks, consists of two pullback squares if and only if the top row is a coproduct diagram.

**Remark 5.3.** Our definition of finitary extensive category is precisely [5, Definition 2.1 and Proposition 2.2].

**Proposition 5.4.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be finitary extensive. The collection of finite families $(X_i\to X)_{i\in I}$ exhibiting $X$ as a coproduct of $(X_i)_{i\in I}$ forms a coverage.

**Definition 5.5.** For $\mathcal{C}$ finitary extensive, the coverage in Proposition 5.4 is the *extensive coverage*, and the topology it generates is the *extensive topology*.

**Definition 5.6.** A category $\mathcal{C}$ is *precoherent* if the collection of finite effective epimorphic families forms a coverage. That is, for any finite effective epimorphic family $(\pi_i\colon X_i\to B)_{i\in I}$ and any $f\colon B'\to B$, there exists a finite effective epimorphic family $(\pi'_j\colon X'_j\to B')_{j\in I'}$ such that for each $j$, the composition $f\circ\pi'_j$ factors through some $\pi_i$. This coverage is the *coherent coverage*, and the topology it generates is the *coherent topology*.

**Remark 5.7.** The notion of a precoherent category arose through formalization and is the minimal axiom needed for the coherent coverage to be a coverage. Similarly for preregular categories. We do not require the existence of pullbacks as in [10, A1.3] (regular) and [10, A1.4] (coherent). For example, [10, Example C.2.1.12 (d)] states that the coherent topology on a coherent category is subcanonical, which is extended to precoherent categories in Proposition 6.8. The analogous statement for regular topologies on regular categories is [11, Corollary B.3.6], extended to preregular categories in Proposition 6.1. Proposition 6.10 (respectively Lemma 6.2) characterizes covering sieves in the coherent (respectively regular) topology. Under stronger assumptions this is found in [11, Definition B.5.1 and Proposition B.5.2] (respectively [10, C.2.1.12 (c)]).

**Definition 6.16.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$ be categories with Grothendieck topologies and $F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}$ a functor. Fix an object $X$ of $\mathcal{C}$ and $Y$ of $\mathcal{D}$.
a) For a sieve $S$ on $X$, the *functor-pushforward* $F_*S$ is the sieve on $F(X)$ consisting of morphisms $f\colon Y\to F(X)$ that factor through $F(g)$ for some $g\colon Z\to X$ in $S$.
b) For a sieve $S$ on $F(X)$, the *functor-pullback* $F^*S$ is the sieve on $X$ consisting of arrows $f$ such that $F(f)\in S$.
c) The *$F$-image sieve* $S^F_Y$ on $Y$ consists of morphisms to $Y$ that factor through an object of the form $F(X)$.

**Definition 6.17.** In the setting of Definition 6.16, with topologies $\mathcal{T}$ on $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{T}'$ on $\mathcal{D}$:
a) $F$ is *continuous* if for every $\mathcal{T}'$-sheaf $P$ on $\mathcal{D}$, the presheaf $P\circ F^{\operatorname{op}}$ is a $\mathcal{T}$-sheaf. In that case $F$ induces $F^*\colon\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathcal{T}'}(\mathcal{D})\to\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{C})$.
b) $F$ is *cocontinuous* if for every object $U$ of $\mathcal{C}$ and every $\mathcal{T}'$-covering sieve $S$ on $F(U)$, the functor-pullback $F^*S$ is a $\mathcal{T}$-covering sieve of $U$.
c) $F$ is *cover-dense* if for every object $Y$ of $\mathcal{D}$, the $F$-image sieve $S^F_Y$ is a $\mathcal{T}'$-covering sieve.

**Remark 6.18.** In point c) of Definition 6.17 the topology $\mathcal{T}$ plays no role; cover-dense only requires a Grothendieck topology on the target.

**Proposition 6.19.** In the setting of Definition 6.17, suppose $F$ is continuous and cocontinuous. Then $F^*\dashv F_*$. If $F$ is also fully faithful and cover-dense, this adjunction is an adjoint equivalence.

**Definition 6.20.** Let $F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}$ be a fully faithful cover-dense functor and $\mathcal{T}'$ a Grothendieck topology on $\mathcal{D}$. A sieve $S$ on $X$ in $\mathcal{C}$ is a $\mathcal{T}$-covering sieve if and only if $F_*S$ is a $\mathcal{T}'$-covering sieve of $F(X)$. This is the *topology induced by $F$*.

**Lemma 6.21.** Let $F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}$ be a fully faithful cover-dense functor, $\mathcal{T}'$ a topology on $\mathcal{D}$, and equip $\mathcal{C}$ with the induced topology. Then $F$ is continuous and cocontinuous.

**Remark 6.22.** A finite-coproduct-preserving functor between finitary extensive categories preserves (respectively reflects) finite effective epimorphic families if and only if it preserves (respectively reflects) effective epimorphisms. This holds because finite effective epimorphic families in extensive categories are precisely those inducing effective epimorphisms on the coproduct (Lemmas 4.8 and 4.9). This yields variants (see for instance) of Proposition 6.15.
