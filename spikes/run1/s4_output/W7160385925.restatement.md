# Restatement

## Preface

These are lecture notes for a course on analytic geometry given in the winter term 2019/20 at the University of Bonn, based on joint work with Dustin Clausen. The notes are a stable citable version. The first half develops the theory of liquid real vector spaces, which is used in [CS22] to give a new approach to complex-analytic geometry. The second half gives a tentative definition of a category of analytic spaces containing adic spaces and complex-analytic spaces. That definition is an abandoned stepping stone, superseded by the analytic stacks of [CS26]. Peter Scholze, May 2026.

## Lecture I: Introduction (Section 0.1)

**Definition 0.1.1.** Consider the pro-étale site $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$ of the point, i.e. the category $\ProFin\cong\Pro(\Fin)$ of profinite sets $S$, with covers given by finite families of jointly surjective maps. A *condensed set* is a sheaf on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$. Similarly, a *condensed abelian group*, *condensed ring*, etc. is a sheaf of abelian groups, rings, etc. on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$.

Concretely, a condensed set is a functor $X:\ProFin^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Sets$ such that:

1. $X(\emptyset)=\ast$, and for all profinite sets $S_1,S_2$ the map $X(S_1\sqcup S_2)\to X(S_1)\times X(S_2)$ is bijective;
2. for any surjection $S'\to S$ of profinite sets, the map
$$X(S)\to\{x\in X(S')\mid p_1^\ast(x)=p_2^\ast(x)\in X(S'\times_S S')\}$$
is bijective, where $p_1,p_2:S'\times_S S'\to S'$ are the two projections.

**Proposition 0.1.2.** Consider the functor $T\mapsto\underline{T}$ from topological spaces to condensed sets, where $\underline{T}(S)=\Cont(S,T)$.

1. The functor has a left adjoint $X\mapsto X(\ast)_{\mathrm{top}}$, sending a condensed set $X$ to its underlying set $X(\ast)$ equipped with the quotient topology arising from the map
$$\bigsqcup_{S,\,a\in X(S)}S\to X(\ast).$$
2. Restricted to compactly generated (e.g. first-countable, e.g. metrizable) topological spaces, the functor is fully faithful.
3. The functor induces an equivalence between the category of compact Hausdorff spaces and the category of quasicompact quasiseparated condensed sets.
4. The functor induces a fully faithful functor from the category of compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces to quasiseparated condensed sets. The category of quasiseparated condensed sets is equivalent to the category of ind-compact Hausdorff spaces "$\varinjlim_i T_i$", where all transition maps $T_i\to T_j$ are closed immersions. If $X_0\hookrightarrow X_1\hookrightarrow\ldots$ is a sequence of compact Hausdorff spaces with closed immersions and $X=\varinjlim_n X_n$ as a topological space, then the map $\varinjlim_n\underline{X_n}\to\underline{X}$ is an isomorphism of condensed sets. In particular, $\varinjlim_n\underline{X_n}$ comes from a topological space. [BS15, Lemma 4.3.7]

Here quasicompactness and quasiseparatedness are general notions for sheaves on any (coherent) site. A condensed set $X$ is *quasicompact* if there is some profinite $S$ with a surjective map $S\to X$. A condensed set $X$ is *quasiseparated* if for any two profinite sets $S_1,S_2$ with maps to $X$, the fibre product $S_1\times_X S_2$ is quasicompact.

**Lemma 0.1.3.** Let $X_0\hookrightarrow X_1\hookrightarrow\ldots$ and $Y_0\hookrightarrow Y_1\hookrightarrow\ldots$ be two sequences of compact Hausdorff spaces with closed immersions. Then, inside the category of topological spaces, the natural map
$$\bigcup_n X_n\times Y_n\to\Big(\bigcup_n X_n\Big)\times\Big(\bigcup_n Y_n\Big)$$
is a homeomorphism; i.e. the product on the right is equipped with its compactly generated topology.

**Further statements in Section 0.1 (not separately labelled).**

- For a condensed set $X$, the map $X(S)\to\prod_{s\in S}X(\{s\})=\prod_{s\in S}X(\ast)=\Map(S,X(\ast))$ need not be injective. It can happen that $X(\ast)=\ast$ but $X(S)\neq\ast$ for large $S$. For quasiseparated condensed sets this map is always injective (as follows from Proposition 0.1.2 (4)). In particular, a quasiseparated condensed set $X$ is trivial as soon as $X(\ast)$ is trivial.
- Condensed abelian groups form an abelian category. The map $(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{discrete\ topology})\to(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{natural\ topology})$ gives a short exact sequence of condensed abelian groups
$$0\to\underline{(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{discrete\ topology})}\to\underline{(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{natural\ topology})}\to Q\to 0,$$
where $Q(S)=\Cont(S,\mathbb{R})/\{\text{locally constant maps } S\to\mathbb{R}\}$ for every profinite set $S$. This uses $H^1(S,M)=0$ for profinite $S$ and discrete abelian groups $M$ [Sch19].
- $Q(\ast)=0$, while $Q(S)\neq 0$ for general $S$. In particular, $Q$ is not quasiseparated.
- The free condensed ring $A=\mathbb{Z}[S]/([\infty]=0)$, for $S=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}$, has underlying ring the polynomial algebra $\mathbb{Z}[T]$, with a nonstandard condensed ring structure, in which the sequence $T,T^2,\ldots,T^n,\ldots$ converges to $0$.
- Over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the closed unit disc has algebra $\mathbb{Q}_p\langle T\rangle=\{\sum_{n\geq 0}a_nT^n\mid a_n\to 0\}$, and the affine line has algebra $\mathbb{Q}_p[T]$. The multiplication map $\mathbb{Q}_p\langle T\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p[T]}\mathbb{Q}_p\langle T\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}_p\langle T\rangle$ is not an isomorphism of condensed abelian groups.
- For $T=\tfrac12\in\mathbb{R}$, the powers of $T$ go to $0$, but it is not the case that every sum $\sum r_n(\tfrac12)^n$ with $r_n\in\mathbb{Z}$ converges in $\mathbb{R}$.

## Lecture II: Solid modules (Section 0.2)

**Proposition 0.2.1.** Let $S=\varprojlim_i S_i$ be a profinite set, written as an inverse limit of finite sets $S_i$. For any $n$, let $\mathbb{Z}[S_i]_{\leq n}\subset\mathbb{Z}[S_i]$ be the subset of formal sums $\sum_{s\in S_i}n_s[s]$ such that $\sum|n_s|\leq n$. This is a finite set, and the natural transition maps $\mathbb{Z}[S_j]\to\mathbb{Z}[S_i]$ preserve these subsets. There is a natural isomorphism of condensed abelian groups
$$\mathbb{Z}[S]\cong\bigcup_n\varprojlim_i\mathbb{Z}[S_i]_{\leq n}\subset\varprojlim_i\mathbb{Z}[S_i].$$
In particular, $\mathbb{Z}[S]$ is a countable union of the profinite sets $\mathbb{Z}[S]_{\leq n}:=\varprojlim_i\mathbb{Z}[S_i]_{\leq n}$.

*Remark (after 0.2.1).* The bound is an $\ell^1$-bound, but it is equivalent to an $\ell^0$-bound, as only finitely many $n_s$ (in fact $n$ of them) can be nonzero.

**Remark 0.2.2.** The condensed set $\mathbb{Z}[S]$ is quasiseparated, and comes from a compactly generated weak Hausdorff space $\mathbb{Z}[S]_{\mathrm{top}}$, by Proposition 0.1.2 (4). By Lemma 0.1.3, the addition $\mathbb{Z}[S]_{\mathrm{top}}\times\mathbb{Z}[S]_{\mathrm{top}}\to\mathbb{Z}[S]_{\mathrm{top}}$ is continuous (not only when the source carries its compactly generated topology). So $\mathbb{Z}[S]$ comes from a topological abelian group $\mathbb{Z}[S]_{\mathrm{top}}$. This is the free topological abelian group on $S$ [AT08].

**Exercise 0.2.3.** Prove that for any compact Hausdorff space $S$, the condensed abelian group $\mathbb{Z}[S]$ can naturally be written as a countable union $\bigcup_n\mathbb{Z}[S]_{\leq n}$ of compact Hausdorff spaces $\mathbb{Z}[S]_{\leq n}$, and comes from a topological abelian group $\mathbb{Z}[S]_{\mathrm{top}}$.

**Definition (before 0.2.4).** Set
$$\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\square}:=\varprojlim_i\mathbb{Z}[S_i].$$
In particular,
$$\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}]^{\square}/([\infty]=0)=\varprojlim_n\mathbb{Z}[\{0,1,\ldots,n-1,\infty\}]/([\infty]=0)=\varprojlim_n\mathbb{Z}[T]/T^n=\mathbb{Z}[[T]],$$
the power series algebra. Here $\mathbb{Z}[T]$ denotes the polynomial algebra in a variable $T$, not the free condensed module on a profinite set $T$.

**Definition 0.2.4.** A condensed abelian group $M$ is *solid* if for any profinite set $S$ with a map $f:S\to M$, there is a unique map of condensed abelian groups $\widetilde{f}:\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\square}\to M$ such that the composite $S\to\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\square}\to M$ is the given map $f$.

**Theorem 0.2.5** ([Sch19, Theorem 5.8 (i), Theorem 6.2 (i)]). 

1. The category $\Solid$ of solid abelian groups is, as a subcategory of the category $\Cond(\Ab)$ of condensed abelian groups, closed under all limits, colimits, and extensions.
2. The inclusion admits a left adjoint $M\mapsto M^{\square}$ (solidification), which, as an endofunctor of $\Cond(\Ab)$, commutes with all colimits and takes $\mathbb{Z}[S]$ to $\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\square}$.
3. The category of solid abelian groups admits compact projective generators, which are exactly the condensed abelian groups of the form $\prod_I\mathbb{Z}$ for some set $I$.
4. There is a unique symmetric monoidal tensor product $\otimes^{\square}$ on $\Solid$ making $\Cond(\Ab)\to\Solid:M\mapsto M^{\square}$ symmetric monoidal.

**Statements on $\mathbb{Z}$ being solid (Section 0.2, not separately labelled).**

- The following chain holds:
$$\Cont(S,\mathbb{Z})=\varinjlim_i\Cont(S_i,\mathbb{Z})=\varinjlim_i\Hom(\mathbb{Z}[S_i],\mathbb{Z})\to\Hom\Big(\varprojlim_i\mathbb{Z}[S_i],\mathbb{Z}\Big)=\Hom(\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\square},\mathbb{Z}).$$
The existence of $f$ is clear; uniqueness is not.
- It is not known whether the analogous result holds with $\mathbb{Z}$ replaced by a general ring $A$; the naive translation of the theorem fails for general rings.
- Measures: 
$$\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\square}=\varprojlim_i\mathbb{Z}[S_i]=\varprojlim_i\underline{\Hom}(C(S_i,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})=\underline{\Hom}\Big(\varinjlim_i C(S_i,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z}\Big)=\underline{\Hom}(C(S,\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z}),$$
which is the space dual to the discrete abelian group $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$ of continuous functions $S\to\mathbb{Z}$. Elements $\mu\in\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\square}$ are called measures. For a continuous $f:S\to M$ into a solid abelian group $M$ and a measure $\mu$, the integral is $\int_S f\mu:=\widetilde{f}(\mu)\in M$.
- For $S=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}$, with convergent sequence $m_0,m_1,\ldots,m_n,\ldots,m_\infty$ in $M$, a measure $\mu$ is determined by the masses $a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_n,\ldots$ it gives the (isolated) finite points, and the mass $a$ it gives the point $\infty$. Then formally
$$\int_S f\mu=a_0(m_0-m_\infty)+a_1(m_1-m_\infty)+\ldots+a_n(m_n-m_\infty)+\ldots+a\,m_\infty,$$
where the infinite sums on the right are defined in $M$. In particular, if $m_\infty=0$, then any sum $\sum a_im_i$ with $a_i\in\mathbb{Z}$ is defined in $M$.

**Setting for $\mathbb{F}_p$-modules.** Fix a prime $p$. Define
$$\mathbb{F}_p[S]^{\square}:=\varprojlim_i\mathbb{F}_p[S_i]=\mathbb{Z}[S]^{\square}/p.$$
The category of *solid $\mathbb{F}_p$-modules* $\Solid(\mathbb{F}_p)\subset\Cond(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is the full subcategory of condensed $\mathbb{F}_p$-modules $M$ such that for all profinite $S$ with a map $f:S\to M$, there is a unique extension to a map $\mathbb{F}_p[S]^{\square}\to M$ of condensed $\mathbb{F}_p$-modules. Equivalently, these are the $p$-torsion full subcategories inside $\Solid$, resp. $\Cond(\Ab)$.

Two simple facts about condensed sets are used:

1. If $M$ is a discrete set, viewed as a condensed set, then for any profinite $S=\varprojlim_i S_i$,
$$\underline{M}(S)=C(S,M)=\varinjlim_i C(S_i,M)=\varinjlim_i\underline{M}(S_i).$$
2. If $X_i$, $i\in I$, is a filtered system of condensed sets, then for any profinite $S$,
$$\Big(\varinjlim_i X_i\Big)(S)=\varinjlim_i X_i(S).$$

**Proposition 0.2.6.** The discrete $\mathbb{F}_p$-module $\mathbb{F}_p$ is solid.

**Corollary 0.2.7.** For any set $I$, the profinite $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space $\prod_I\mathbb{F}_p$ is solid.

A condensed $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space $V$ is *profinite* if its underlying condensed set is profinite. Any such $V$ can be written as a cofiltered limit of finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector spaces. The functor $V\mapsto V^\ast=\Hom(V,\mathbb{F}_p)$ defines an anti-equivalence between profinite $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector spaces and discrete $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector spaces. In particular, every profinite $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space is isomorphic to $\prod_I\mathbb{F}_p$ for some set $I$.

**Proposition 0.2.8.** The class of profinite $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector spaces forms an abelian subcategory of $\Cond(\mathbb{F}_p)$ stable under all limits, cokernels, and extensions.

**Theorem 0.2.9.** A condensed $\mathbb{F}_p$-module $M$ is solid if and only if it is a filtered colimit of profinite $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector spaces. These form an abelian category stable under all limits and colimits. The rest of Theorem 0.2.5 could also be proved in this setting.
