# Restatement

## Introduction (mathematical content)

**Setting (§0.1).** Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field of characteristic $0$ whose residue field $k$ is perfect of characteristic $p$. Let $K_\infty$ be obtained from $K$ by adjoining the $p$-power roots of unity. Then $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(K_\infty/K)$ is an open subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. The field of norms construction of Fontaine–Wintenberger [52] applied to $K_\infty$ produces a field $F$, isomorphic to the completed perfect closure of a formal power series field over a finite extension of $k$, with $\mathrm{Gal}(K_\infty) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(F)$. This isomorphism is induced by an equivalence between the categories of finite étale algebras over $K_\infty$ and over $F$.

- Discrete representations of $G_F$ on finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector spaces are equivalent to $\varphi$-modules over $F$ (finite-dimensional $F$-vector spaces with isomorphisms to their $\varphi$-pullbacks). This is a nonabelian form of the Artin–Schreier description of $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$-extensions [43, Exposé XXII, Proposition 1.1].
- Continuous representations of $G_F$ on finite free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules correspond to $\varphi$-modules over $W(F)$.
- Representations of $G_K$ correspond to $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-modules over $W(F)$, obtained by restricting to $G_{K_\infty}\cong G_F$ and adding descent data for $\Gamma_K = \mathrm{Gal}(K_\infty/K)$.
- $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-modules over the extended Robba ring which are semistable of degree $0$ are the ones arising from the descent process via $D_{\mathrm{crys}}$. Over the Robba ring, the whole category is described by vector bundles on the curve of Fargues and Fontaine [50].

**Relativization (§0.2).** For a perfectoid algebra $R$ corresponding to a perfect ring $S$ in characteristic $p$, the categories of étale $\mathbb{Z}_p$-local systems on $\mathrm{Spec}(R)$ and $\mathrm{Spec}(S)$ are equivalent, and are equivalent to $\varphi$-modules over $W(S)$. For adic spaces over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the pro-étale topology of Scholze [109] gives sheaves of perfect period rings: $W$, overconvergent $W$, and the extended Robba ring. $\varphi$-modules over $W$ correspond to étale $\mathbb{Z}_p$-local systems, and pointwise semistable $\varphi$-modules over the extended Robba sheaf correspond to étale $\mathbb{Q}_p$-local systems [91].

**Cherbonnier–Colmez (§0.3).** The field $F$ carries a complete discretely ramified subfield $L$. There is a subring $\mathfrak{o}_{\mathcal{E}} \subseteq W(F)$, stable under $\varphi$ and $\Gamma$, which is a Cohen ring for $L$. The overconvergent subring $\mathfrak{o}_{\mathcal{E}^\dagger} = \mathfrak{o}_{\mathcal{E}} \cap W^{\dagger}(F)$ consists of Laurent series converging on some open annulus in the open unit disc. Cherbonnier and Colmez [29] showed that $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-modules over $W^\dagger(F)$ descend uniquely to $\mathfrak{o}_{\mathcal{E}^\dagger}$. The analogous statement for $\varphi$-modules is false. The Fréchet completion of $\mathfrak{o}_{\mathcal{E}^\dagger}$ inside the extended Robba ring is the Robba ring $\mathcal{R}$.

**Imperfect period rings (§0.3–0.4).** $\psi$ denotes the reduced trace of Frobenius, and its kernel and cokernel are related to Iwasawa cohomology [105, 104]. The Lubin–Tate and Kummer towers are discussed in §6, and the toric towers used in §7 are those from the multiplication-by-$p$ maps on a torus.

**Pseudocoherent modules (§0.5).** Pseudocoherent $R$-modules are those admitting infinite projective resolutions by finitely generated projective modules [77, Chapter 2], [124, §2]. The class of pseudocoherent modules has the "two out of three" property. Under noetherian hypotheses, the theory reduces to Kiehl's theory of coherent sheaves.

**Applications (§0.6).** In §8, the following are established for a rigid analytic space: the category of pseudocoherent $\widehat{\mathcal{O}}$-modules on the pro-étale site is abelian, satisfies the ascending chain condition, and is stable under $\mathcal{H}om$, tensor product, and pullback together with their derived functors. The same holds for pseudocoherent $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-modules (§8.10).

## Definitions and Remarks (§1)

**Definition 1.1.1.** Let $R$ be a (commutative) ring. For $m \in \mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}$, an $R$-module $M$ is **$m$-pseudocoherent** if there exists a projective resolution
$$\cdots \to P_1 \to P_0 \to M \to 0$$
with $P_i$ finitely generated for all $i \leq m$. In particular, $M$ is $0$-pseudocoherent (resp. $1$-pseudocoherent) if and only if it is finitely generated (resp. finitely presented). We write *pseudocoherent* as shorthand for $\infty$-pseudocoherent.

We say that $M$ is **$m$-fpd** if there exists a finite projective resolution with $P_i = 0$ for all $i > m$. An equivalent condition is that $\mathrm{Ext}^i_R(M,N) = 0$ for all $R$-modules $N$ and all $i \geq m+1$, or even just for $i = m+1$ [119, Tag 065R]. We say $M$ **has finite projective dimension**, or is **fpd**, if it is $m$-fpd for some $m$. The minimum such $m$ is the **projective dimension** of $M$. By convention, the projective dimension of the zero module is $-\infty$.

**Remark 1.1.2.** An $R$-module $M$ is fpd if and only if the singleton complex $0 \to M \to 0$ is perfect, i.e. isomorphic in the derived category to a bounded complex of finite projective $R$-modules. Fpd modules could be called perfect modules, as in [119, Tag 0656], but this paper does not do so.

**Remark 1.1.3.** Given two projective resolutions
$$\cdots\to P_1 \to P_0 \to M \to 0, \qquad \cdots \to P'_1 \to P'_0 \to M' \to 0,$$
one obtains a projective resolution of $M \otimes_R M'$ by taking the totalization of the double complex $P_i \otimes_R P'_j$. Consequences:
- The tensor product of two $m$-pseudocoherent modules is $m$-pseudocoherent (compare [77, Corollaire 2.16.1(b)]).
- The tensor product of an $m$-fpd module with an $n$-fpd module is $(m+n)$-fpd.

**Remark 1.1.4.** The category of pseudocoherent (resp. fpd) modules over a ring $R$ is not guaranteed to admit internal Homs.

**Lemma 1.1.5.** Let $0 \to M_1 \to M \to M_2 \to 0$ be a short exact sequence of $R$-modules, and let $m \geq 0$ be an integer.

- (a) If $M_1$ and $M_2$ are $m$-pseudocoherent, then so is $M$.
- (b) If $M_1$ is $(m-1)$-pseudocoherent and $M$ is $m$-pseudocoherent, then $M_2$ is $m$-pseudocoherent.
- (c) If $M$ is $m$-pseudocoherent and $M_2$ is $(m+1)$-pseudocoherent, then $M_1$ is $m$-pseudocoherent.
- (d) If $M_1$ and $M_2$ are $m$-fpd, then so is $M$.
- (e) If $M_1$ is $m$-fpd and $M$ is $(m+1)$-fpd, then $M_2$ is $(m+1)$-fpd.
- (f) If $M$ is $m$-fpd and $M_2$ is $(m+1)$-fpd, then $M_1$ is $m$-fpd.

In particular, if any two of $M, M_1, M_2$ are pseudocoherent (resp. fpd), then so is the third. That is, the pseudocoherent (resp. fpd) $R$-modules form an exact tensor category.

**Remark 1.1.6.** Let $R$ be a ring and $f \in R$ a non-zero-divisor. Then $R/fR$ has projective resolution $\cdots \to 0 \to R \xrightarrow{\times f} R \to 0$, so $R/fR$ is $1$-fpd. Let $M$ be an $m$-pseudocoherent (resp. $m$-fpd) $R$-module. Then:
- $M/fM = M \otimes_R (R/fR)$ is $m$-pseudocoherent (resp. $(m+1)$-fpd) (Remark 1.1.3).
- $fM = \ker(M \to M/fM)$ is $(m-1)$-pseudocoherent (resp. $m$-fpd) (Lemma 1.1.5).
- $M[f] = \{\mathbf{v} \in M : f\mathbf{v} = 0\} = \ker(\times f: M \to fM)$ is $(m-2)$-pseudocoherent (resp. $(m+1)$-fpd).

Let $T = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty M[f^n]$. If $M$ is $m$-pseudocoherent, then $T$ is not guaranteed to be $(m-2)$-pseudocoherent, but it is **ind-$(m-2)$-pseudocoherent**.

**Lemma 1.1.7.** Let $R$ be a ring and $f \in R$. Let $M_0 \to M_1 \to \cdots$ be a sequence of surjective morphisms of pseudocoherent $R$-modules such that for each $i$, the quotient of $M_i$ by its $f$-power-torsion submodule $T_i$ is pseudocoherent. If the sequences $M_{0,f} \to M_{1,f} \to \cdots$ and $M_0/fM_0 \to M_1/fM_1 \to \cdots$ both stabilize, then so does the original sequence.

**Remark 1.1.8.** By Lemma 1.1.5, the following are equivalent:
- (a) $R$ is **coherent**, i.e. every finitely generated ideal is finitely presented. This holds for instance if $R$ is noetherian.
- (b) Every finitely presented $R$-module is pseudocoherent.
- (c) The pseudocoherent $R$-modules form an abelian category.

Even when $R$ is noetherian, every pseudocoherent $R$-module is guaranteed to be fpd only when $R$ is regular of finite dimension $m$, in which case every pseudocoherent module is $m$-fpd [44, Corollary 19.6].

**Remark 1.1.9.** There are many characterizations of coherent rings; see [57, Theorem 2.3.2]. If $\{R_i\}$ is a directed system of coherent rings with flat transition maps, then $\varinjlim_i R_i$ is coherent [57, Theorem 2.3.3]. For example, a polynomial ring in possibly infinitely many variables over a field is coherent, as is the perfect closure of such a ring when the base field has characteristic $p$.

**Remark 1.1.10.** If $R$ is separated and complete with respect to a non-zero-divisor $f$, and $R/fR$ is noetherian, then $R$ is noetherian. Indeed, $\mathrm{Gr}\,R = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty f^n R/f^{n+1}R \cong (R/fR)\llbracket T\rrbracket$, which is a completion of the noetherian ring $(R/fR)[T]$ and is therefore noetherian [44, Theorem 7.1]. For an ideal $I \subseteq R$, let $J \subseteq \mathrm{Gr}\,R$ be the ideal of leading terms, generated by the class in $f^nR/f^{n+1}R$ of each element of $I \cap f^nR$ for $n \geq 0$. Any set of elements of $I$ lifting a finite generating set of $J$ generates $I$. There is no corresponding statement for coherent rings: there exists a coherent ring $R$ such that $R\llbracket T\rrbracket$ is not coherent [57, §8.1].

**Remark 1.1.11.** Let $R$ be a Prüfer domain (an integral domain in which every finitely generated ideal is projective). Then $R$ is coherent by Remark 1.1.9, so every finitely presented $R$-module is pseudocoherent (Remark 1.1.8). Further:
- (a) Every finitely generated torsion-free $R$-module is projective [79].
- (b) If $M$ is finitely generated (resp. finitely presented), its torsion submodule $T$ is finitely generated (resp. finitely presented), and $M/T$ is finite projective by (a).
- (c) Every finitely presented $R$-module is $1$-fpd (by (b)).

**Remark 1.1.12.** It is not known whether the Fitting ideals of a pseudocoherent module (as in [91, Definition 1.1.2]) are themselves pseudocoherent. Fitting ideals are compatible with extension of scalars but not with restriction of scalars along a quotient. If $I$ is an ideal of $R$ and $M$ is a finitely generated $R$-module annihilated by $I$, the Fitting ideals of $M$ need not contain $I$. In particular, they are not determined by their images in $R/I$.

**Remark 1.1.13.** Let $R \to S$ be a ring homomorphism. In general, base extension of a pseudocoherent (resp. fpd) $R$-module need not be pseudocoherent (resp. fpd) over $S$. However:
- For $m \leq 1$, base extension of an $m$-pseudocoherent $R$-module is $m$-pseudocoherent over $S$. This fails in general for $m \geq 2$.
- If $S$ is flat over $R$, base extension takes $m$-pseudocoherent (resp. $m$-fpd) $R$-modules to $m$-pseudocoherent (resp. $m$-fpd) $S$-modules, and is exact on pseudocoherent modules.
- If $S$ is coherent, base extension takes pseudocoherent $R$-modules to pseudocoherent $S$-modules (Remark 1.1.8).
- If $S$ is noetherian and regular, base extension takes fpd $R$-modules to fpd $S$-modules (Remark 1.1.8).
- If $S$ is pseudocoherent as an $R$-module, base extension takes pseudocoherent $R$-modules to pseudocoherent $S$-modules [119, Tag 064Z]. An $S$-module is pseudocoherent if and only if it is pseudocoherent as an $R$-module. If $M$ is pseudocoherent over $R$, then $\mathrm{Tor}_i^R(M,S)$ is pseudocoherent over $S$ for all $i \geq 0$.
- If $S$ is fpd as an $R$-module, base extension need not take fpd $R$-modules to fpd $S$-modules. If $M$ is fpd over $R$, then $M \otimes_R S$ is fpd over $R$ by Remark 1.1.3, but need not be fpd over $S$, even if $S$ is noetherian (Example 1.1.14).

**Example 1.1.14.** Let $K$ be a field, $R = K[x,y]$, and $S = R/(xy)$. By Remark 1.1.8, every finitely generated $R$-module is fpd. In particular, $S$ and the ideal $I = (x,y)$ of $S$ are fpd over $R$. However, $I$ is not fpd over $S$. From the projective resolution
$$\cdots \, S^2 \xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto(by,ax)} S^2 \xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto(by,ax)} S^2 \xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto ax+by} I \to 0,$$
we have $\mathrm{Tor}_i^S(I, S/I) = (S/I)^2 \neq 0$ for all $i > 0$.

**Remark 1.1.15.** Let $R$ be a ring and $S$ an $R$-module. Then $S$ is flat over $R$ if and only if $\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(P,S) = 0$ for every $1$-pseudocoherent $P$. For $m > 1$, base extension from $R$ to $S$ defines an exact functor from $m$-pseudocoherent $R$-modules to $m$-pseudocoherent $S$-modules (as in Remark 1.1.13) if $\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(P,S) = 0$ for every $m$-pseudocoherent $P$. This condition does not imply $\mathrm{Tor}_i^R(P,S) = 0$ for $i > 1$.

## Topological definitions (§1.2)

**Definition 1.2.1.** A topological abelian group is **metrizable** if it is Hausdorff and admits a countable fundamental system of open neighbourhoods of $0$. This is equivalent to the topology being induced by some (not necessarily nonarchimedean) absolute value.

**Definition 1.2.2.** Let $R$ be a topological ring. The **natural topology** on a finitely generated $R$-module $M$ is the quotient topology induced by some surjection $R^n \to M$ of $R$-modules, where $R^n$ carries the product topology. The topology does not depend on the choice of surjection (by emulating the proof of [91, Lemma 2.2.6]). It is not guaranteed to be Hausdorff. If it is Hausdorff and the topology on $R$ is metrizable, then it is metrizable.

**Remark 1.2.3.** Let $R$ be a complete metrizable topological ring, and let $M$ be a finitely generated $R$-module with its natural topology. For any exact sequence $0 \to K \to F \to M \to 0$ with $F$ finite free, the map $F/K \to M$ is a homeomorphism. If $M$ is Hausdorff, then $K$ is closed and $M$ is complete. In general, $M$ surjects onto its completion.

**Remark 1.2.4.** For $R$ and $M$ as in Remark 1.2.3, there is no known direct relationship between $M$ being complete for the natural topology and the Fitting ideals of $M$ being closed in $R$. The only trivial fact is that finite projective $R$-modules are always complete for the natural topology.
