# Restatement

## Abstract

We construct many new topological types of compact $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds, that is, Riemannian 7-manifolds with holonomy group $\mathrm{G}_2$. We extend the twisted connected sum construction first developed by Kovalev and apply it to a large class of asymptotically cylindrical Calabi–Yau 3-folds built from semi-Fano 3-folds constructed previously by the authors. In many cases we determine the diffeomorphism type of the underlying smooth 7-manifolds completely. Many 2-connected 7-manifolds can be realised as twisted connected sums in several ways. Many of the $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds we construct contain compact rigid associative 3-folds. By varying the semi-Fanos used to build different $\mathrm{G}_2$-metrics on the same 7-manifold, the number of rigid associative 3-folds produced can be changed.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Twisted connected sums

- Every $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifold is Ricci-flat, so the construction yields Ricci-flat metrics on compact 7-manifolds.
- Kovalev's twisted connected sum [44] combines a pair of exponentially asymptotically cylindrical (ACyl) Calabi–Yau 3-folds $V_+$ and $V_-$. The ends of these 3-folds have the form $\mathbb{R}^+\times\mathbb{S}^1\times S_\pm$, where $S_\pm$ are smooth K3 surfaces.
- The cross-sections $\mathbb{T}^2\times S_\pm$ are identified using a diffeomorphism that exchanges the two circle factors of $\mathbb{T}^2$. The K3 surfaces are identified by a special diffeomorphism $\mathbf{r}\colon S_+\to S_-$, called a hyper-Kähler rotation, which is an isometry satisfying
$$\mathbf{r}^*I_- = J_+,\qquad \mathbf{r}^*J_- = I_+,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \mathbf{r}^*K_- = -K_+.$$
- The resulting compact 7-manifold $M_{\mathbf r}$ must have finite fundamental group for the holonomy to be the full $\mathrm{G}_2$. The construction gives a closed $\mathrm{G}_2$-structure with small torsion, and the perturbation theory of Joyce and Kovalev then yields a $\mathrm{G}_2$-holonomy metric.
- The two steps are: (i) construct (families of) exponentially ACyl Calabi–Yau structures on suitable smooth quasiprojective 3-folds; (ii) find ACyl Calabi–Yau 3-folds admitting a hyper-Kähler rotation within a given pair of families.
- The analytic input for (i) is a solution of the complex Monge–Ampère equation with strong estimates. The exponential asymptotics argument in [44] is not valid; a self-contained proof is given in [37].

### 1.2 Building blocks

- Kovalev [44] constructed ACyl Calabi–Yau 3-folds of Fano type by blowing up a smooth base locus of a generic anticanonical pencil in a smooth Fano 3-fold and removing a smooth anticanonical divisor.
- A smooth Fano 3-fold $F$ is a smooth projective variety with $-K_F$ ample. There are exactly 105 deformation families.
- A smooth weak Fano 3-fold is a smooth projective variety whose anticanonical bundle is big and nef but not ample. There are at least hundreds of thousands of deformation families.
- In [18] the authors constructed suitable quasiprojective 3-folds from any weak Fano 3-fold satisfying one further mild assumption. These are called ACyl Calabi–Yau 3-folds of weak Fano type.
- A **semi-Fano** 3-fold is a weak Fano 3-fold whose anticanonical morphism contracts no divisor to a point.¹
- Footnote ¹: There is no established terminology for this subclass; the term "semi-Fano" is the authors' own. Separately, "semi-Fano" has been used for a complex manifold with $-K_Y$ nef but not necessarily big. That usage is not well established.
- For semi-Fano 3-folds, certain cohomology vanishing theorems that hold for Fano 3-folds still hold.

### 1.3 Matching and hyper-Kähler rotations

- Kovalev's argument for hyper-Kähler rotations between Fano-type ACyl 3-folds relies on Lemma 6.47 in [44], which is false. The paper corrects and extends this construction.
- Hyper-Kähler rotations are constructed for many pairs of families of semi-Fano-type ACyl Calabi–Yau 3-folds.
- Kovalev–Lee [45] contain a similar construction for ACyl Calabi–Yau 3-folds of Fano or "nonsymplectic" type.
- Given a pair of families, there are typically several hyper-Kähler rotations $\mathbf r\colon S_+\to S_-$. The topology of $M$ depends on this choice.
- Using ACyl Calabi–Yau 3-folds from Fano or semi-Fano 3-folds of rank at most two, or from toric semi-Fano 3-folds, the paper obtains at least 50 million matching pairs producing 2-connected $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds.

### 1.4 Rigid curves and rigid associative 3-folds

- If $Y$ is Fano, then $-K_Y\cdot C>0$ for every algebraic curve $C$, so $C$ meets every anticanonical divisor.
- For semi-Fano $Y$ there can be curves $C$ with $K_Y\cdot C=0$. In many cases $C$ is a smooth rational curve with normal bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)$, and then $C$ is infinitesimally rigid.
- Such curves do not meet anticanonical divisors, so they give compact rigid holomorphic curves in the ACyl 3-folds.
- These produce rigid associative 3-folds diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{S}^1\times\mathbb{S}^2$ in the $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds.

### 1.5 $\mathrm{G}_2$-transitions

- Flops and conifold transitions relate weak Fano 3-folds. A conifold transition starting from a Fano 3-fold $F$ yields a weak Fano 3-fold $Y$, not a Fano one, unlike the Calabi–Yau case where $c_1=0$ is preserved.
- Blocks built from $F$ and $Y$ may be matched to the same other block, which motivates "$\mathrm{G}_2$-transitions" as an organising principle.

### 1.6 Topology

- The integral cohomology of many of the constructed $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds is computed, including torsion in $H^3$ and $H^4$ and the first Pontrjagin class $p_1$.
- Joyce and Kovalev had computed only the fundamental group and Betti numbers.
- The twisted connected sum often yields 2-connected 7-manifolds. Joyce's orbifold-resolution examples typically have large $b^2$; only one example in [42] has $b^2=0$ (see Remark 4.30).
- Using the classification of 2-connected 7-manifolds by Wall and Wilkens [74], the topological data determine the diffeomorphism (or almost-diffeomorphism) type of many of these 7-manifolds. These are the first compact $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds whose diffeomorphism type is known.
- These 7-manifolds are connected sums of a nontrivial $\mathbb{S}^3$-bundle over $\mathbb{S}^4$ with copies of $\mathbb{S}^3\times\mathbb{S}^4$.
- Using only rank one Fano 3-folds, as in Kovalev's original construction, the paper proves the following results (Section 7):
  - The simplest matching gives 2-connected $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds with torsion-free cohomology. In this case $b^3$ is the only Betti number, and 46 different values of $b^3$ occur.
  - At least 82 different smooth 7-manifolds arise this way, distinguished by $p_1$ when Betti numbers agree.
  - The homeomorphism type of all these 7-manifolds is determined. The diffeomorphism type is determined in most cases.
  - A pair of $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds has underlying 7-manifolds that are homeomorphic but not known to be diffeomorphic. Resolving this requires an extension of the Eells–Kuiper invariant, to be treated in [21]. The authors suspect the two 7-manifolds are diffeomorphic.
  - Other matchings lead to simply connected 7-manifolds with $H^2=0$ and nontrivial torsion in $H^3$. At least 41 other topological types arise this way.

### 1.7 Different $\mathrm{G}_2$-metrics on the same manifold

- There exist many 2-connected smooth 7-manifolds arising from several different twisted connected sums, sometimes many.
- Using semi-Fano blocks, one can obtain diffeomorphic $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds containing different numbers of obvious rigid associative 3-folds.
- Question: When do these $\mathrm{G}_2$-metrics on the same 7-manifold belong to different connected components of the moduli space of $\mathrm{G}_2$-metrics?
- In the examples, the diffeomorphism can be chosen so that the $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures are homotopic (connected by a continuous path of $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures without torsion constraint) [22].

### 1.8 Associative 3-folds

- Associative 3-folds are difficult to construct and their deformations can be obstructed.
- Rigid associative 3-folds diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{S}^1\times\mathbb{S}^2$ are exhibited in many of the constructed $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds. The use of semi-Fano blocks is essential. These are the first proven rigid compact associative 3-folds in compact $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifolds.
- Walpuski [72] constructed $\mathrm{G}_2$-instantons that bubble at rigid associative submanifolds, reversing the analysis of Tian [69].
- The approach is related to the higher-dimensional invariants of Donaldson–Thomas [27] and Donaldson–Segal [28].

### 1.9 M-theory remarks

- Every twisted connected sum $\mathrm{G}_2$-manifold is K3-fibred, because the building blocks are K3-fibred.
- Generically the only singular fibres are $A_1$ singularities.
- Whether the resulting almost coassociative K3-fibrations can be made coassociative K3-fibrations, as expected in [34, 1], is unknown.
- Physically relevant singular $\mathrm{G}_2$-spaces are discussed in [7, 1, 2, 9]. Further references are [4, 3, 5] and [10, 24, 64, 35, 61, 6].

### 1.10 Structure of the paper

- **Section 2** reviews $\mathrm{G}_2$, $\mathrm{G}_2$-structures, $\mathrm{G}_2$-holonomy metrics, calibrations, and relations to Calabi–Yau 3-folds, hyper-Kähler K3 surfaces, and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$.
- **Section 3** introduces ACyl Calabi–Yau 3-folds from building blocks and recalls Kovalev's twisted connected sum.
- **Section 4** develops tools to compute topological invariants. **Theorem 4.9** computes the integral cohomology groups and shows that the manifolds are simply connected under the paper's assumptions.
- **Section 5** treats associative submanifolds. **Theorem 5.3** gives persistence for a closed infinitesimally rigid associative 3-fold under small deformations. **Theorem 5.4** extends this to multi-parameter families. **Lemma 5.11** states that $\mathbb{S}^1\times C$ is rigid as an associative 3-fold if and only if $C$ is rigid as a holomorphic curve. **Proposition 5.15** states that each closed rigid holomorphic curve can be perturbed to a compact rigid associative $\mathbb{S}^1\times C$ for all sufficiently long necks.
- **Section 6** treats the matching problem. "Orthogonal gluing" yields solutions in many cases. "Primitive perpendicular gluing" is guaranteed to solve the matching problem when the Picard ranks of the deformation types $\mathcal{Y}_\pm$ add to at most 11. Orthogonal gluing gives $b^2(M)>0$ and requires compatibility of Picard lattices (see Example 6.8). Proposition 6.18 gives further conditions.
- **Section 7** gives examples and their topology. Handcrafted nonorthogonal gluing is treated in one example.
- **Section 8** discusses general possibilities, limitations, and the geography of achievable examples. The diffeomorphism classification of 2-connected cases requires computing an extension of the Eells–Kuiper invariant in the remaining cases. Simply connected cases with $\pi_2(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}$ are discussed.

## 2. Preliminaries on $\mathrm{G}_2$

**Proposition 2.4.**
(i) $\mathrm{G}_2$ is a compact 2-connected Lie group of dimension 14.
(ii) The stabiliser in $\mathrm{G}_2$ of a non-zero vector in $\mathbb{R}^7$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{SU}(3)$.
(iii) $\mathrm{G}_2$ acts transitively on the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^6\subset\mathbb{R}^7$.
(iv) The $\mathrm{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})$-orbit of $\varphi_0$ is open in $\Lambda^3(\mathbb{R}^7)^*$.

**Remark (unnumbered, following Proposition 2.4).** The set of non-degenerate 3-forms on $\mathbb{R}^7$ is the union of four connected components: two $\mathrm{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})$-orbits, each of which splits into two components inducing opposite orientations. The orbit not containing $\varphi_0$ consists of non-degenerate 3-forms whose induced bilinear form has signature $(3,4)$.

**Remark 2.7.** The stabiliser of $\psi_0$ in $\mathrm{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})$ is the subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathrm{G}_2$, where $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is generated by $-\operatorname{Id}$. Hence $\varphi_0$ can be recovered from $\psi_0$, modulo orientation.

**Lemma 2.8.** For all $u,v,w\in\mathbb{R}^7$:
$$\|u\times v\|^2 = \|u\|^2\|v\|^2 - g_0(u,v)^2, \tag{2.9a}$$
$$u\times(v\times w)+(u\times v)\times w = 2g_0(u,w)\,v - g_0(u,v)\,w - g_0(w,v)\,u, \tag{2.9b}$$
$$\varphi_0(u,v,w)^2 + \tfrac14\,|\chi_0(u,v,w)|^2 = |u\wedge v\wedge w|^2. \tag{2.9c}$$

**Definition 2.10.** Let $G$ be a subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})$. A **$G$-structure** on $V$ is a $G$-subbundle of $P$, i.e. an orbit of the induced action of $G$ on $P$. The space of all $G$-structures can be identified with the quotient space $P/G$.

**Definition 2.11.** Let $V$ be a real vector space of dimension 7. We call $\varphi\in\Lambda^3V^*$ a **$\mathrm{G}_2$-structure** (or **$\mathrm{G}_2$-form**) if there is a linear isomorphism $V\cong\mathbb{R}^7$ identifying $\varphi$ with $\varphi_0$.

**Definition 2.12.** Let $V$ be a real oriented 7-dimensional vector space. We call $\varphi\in\Lambda^3V^*$ a **positive 3-form** if there is an oriented linear isomorphism $V\cong\mathbb{R}^7$ identifying $\varphi$ with $\varphi_0$. Let $\Lambda^3_+V^*\subset\Lambda^3V^*$ denote the set of positive forms.

**Remark (unnumbered, following Definition 2.12).** The definition of "positive" agrees with that of Joyce [42], while Hitchin [40] uses "positive" where this paper uses "$\mathrm{G}_2$-form".
