# Restatement

## Abstract

This article extends Scholze's six functor formalism for diamonds to a very general class of stacky morphisms between v-stacks, using $\infty$-categorical techniques developed by Liu–Zheng.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Context and motivation

Grothendieck's six functors are $f^{*}$, $Rf_{*}$, $Rf_{!}$, $Rf^{!}$, $\otimes$, and internal Hom, acting on a suitable derived category of sheaves on a category of geometric objects. For Artin stacks, defining $Rf_{!}$ for stacky morphisms $f$ involves issues of homotopy coherence. These were resolved by Lu–Zheng [8], who applied $\infty$-categorical techniques to extend the full formalism to Artin stacks.

Fix a prime $p$ and a ring $\Lambda$ killed by some integer prime to $p$. Scholze [11] built the formalism for étale cohomology of diamonds. For any small v-stack $X$ he defined a triangulated category $D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$, which agrees with the left-completion of $D(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\Lambda)$ when $X$ is an analytic adic space, and which carries $\otimes$ and $R\mathscr{H}om$. For any morphism $f:X\to Y$ he constructed an adjoint pair $f^{*}$, $Rf_{*}$. For morphisms $f$ that are compactifiable, representable in locally spatial diamonds, and locally of finite dim.trg, he constructed $Rf_{!}$ and $Rf^{!}$, with composability, base change, and the projection formula. He also showed that for "cohomologically smooth" morphisms, $Rf^{!}$ is an invertible twist of $f^{*}$.

For applications one needs $Rf_{!}$ and $Rf^{!}$ for stacky (non-representable) $f$.

*Example (footnote 1).* For the structure map $f:[\ast/\underline{G(\mathbf{Q}_{p})}]\to\ast$, where $G/\mathbf{Q}_{p}$ is a linear algebraic group, $Rf_{!}$ is expected to be group homology and $Rf_{*}$ group cohomology. See [3, Example 4.2.7].

*Remark (footnote 2).* The results on the Kottwitz conjecture in [3] use the $!$-functor formalism, with its expected properties, for certain stacky maps of Artin v-stacks.

The present paper extends the $!$-functor formalism of [11] to a specific class of stacky maps, as required by [3].

### 1.2 Main results

**Definition 1.1.** A small v-stack $X$ is *decent* if it satisfies the following two conditions:

1. For any locally separated locally spatial diamond $T$ with a map $T\to X\times X$, the pullback $T\times_{X\times X,\Delta}X$ is a locally separated locally spatial diamond.
2. There exists a locally separated locally spatial diamond $U$ together with a surjective map $f:U\to X$ which is representable in locally spatial diamonds and which locally on $U$ is separated and cohomologically smooth.

Any choice of $f:U\to X$ as in (2) is called a *chart* of $X$.

**Remark 1.2.**

i. In (1) it is enough to quantify over all separated spatial $T$. Moreover, (1) implies that for any locally separated locally spatial diamonds $T,S$ with maps $T\to X\leftarrow S$, the fiber product $T\times_X S$ is a locally separated locally spatial diamond. Indeed, $T\times_X S\cong (T\times S)\times_{X\times X,\Delta}X$, and $T\times S$ is a locally separated locally spatial diamond.

ii. In (2) one may assume without loss of generality that $U$ is a separated locally spatial diamond and that $f$ is separated and cohomologically smooth. For example, one may replace $f$ by $\coprod U_{i}\to U\xrightarrow{f}X$, where $U=\bigcup U_{i}$ is an open cover by separated locally spatial diamonds such that all restrictions $f|_{U_{i}}$ are separated and cohomologically smooth. If these conditions hold, $f:U\to X$ is called a *clean chart*.

Further statements (without proof): decent v-stacks are Artin v-stacks in the sense of [1, Definition IV.1.1] (Proposition 4.4). All locally separated locally spatial diamonds are decent. For an analytic adic space $X$ over $\mathrm{Spa}\,\mathbf{Z}_{p}$, the diamond $X^{\lozenge}$ is decent. More generally, if $X$ is a locally separated v-sheaf admitting a surjective cohomologically smooth map $U\to X$ from a separated locally spatial diamond $U$, then $X$ is decent. This applies to $X=\Spd\mathbf{Z}_{p}$ and $X=\Div^{1}$, with $U=X\times\Spd\mathbf{F}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)$.

**Definition 1.3.**

i. A morphism $f:X\to Y$ between decent v-stacks is *fine* if there exists a commutative diagram
$$\begin{array}{ccc} W & \xrightarrow{\;g\;} & V \\ {\scriptstyle b}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle a} \\ X & \xrightarrow{\;f\;} & Y \end{array}$$
where the vertical maps are charts and $g$ is locally on $W$ compactifiable of finite dim.trg.

ii. A morphism $f:X\to Y$ between decent v-stacks is *$\ell$-cohomologically smooth* if there exists a commutative diagram of the same shape (1.1), with charts $a,b$, such that $g$ is locally on $W$ compactifiable of finite dim.trg and $\ell$-cohomologically smooth in the sense of [11].

**Theorem 1.4.** Let $\Lambda$ be a ring killed by some integer $n$ prime to $p$. If $f:X\to Y$ is any fine map of decent v-stacks, there is a natural functor
$$Rf_{!}:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$$
satisfying the following properties.

1. When $f$ is separated and representable in locally spatial diamonds, $Rf_{!}$ coincides with the functor constructed in [11].
2. There is a natural isomorphism $R(f\circ g)_{!}\cong Rf_{!}\circ Rg_{!}$ whenever fine morphisms $f$ and $g$ are composable. More precisely, $X\mapsto D_{\text{ét}}(X,\Lambda)$ and $f\mapsto Rf_{!}$ form a pseudo-functor from the 2-category of decent v-stacks with fine morphisms to the 2-category of triangulated categories.
3. (Projection formula) There is a natural isomorphism $Rf_{!}(A\otimes f^{*}B)\cong Rf_{!}A\otimes B$ for $A\in D_{\text{ét}}(X,\Lambda)$ and $B\in D_{\text{ét}}(Y,\Lambda)$.
4. (Proper base change) For a cartesian square of decent v-stacks
$$\begin{array}{ccc} X' & \xrightarrow{\;\tilde g\;} & X \\ {\scriptstyle f'}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle f} \\ Y' & \xrightarrow{\;g\;} & Y \end{array}$$
with $f$ fine, there is an isomorphism $g^{*}Rf_{!}\cong Rf'_{!}\tilde g^{*}$.
5. $Rf_{!}$ admits a right adjoint $Rf^{!}$.

**Heuristic for the construction of $Rf_{!}$.** Let $f:X\to Y$ be a fine map of decent v-stacks and $g:U\to X$ a chart. Set
$$g_{n}:U_{n}=\underbrace{U\times_{X}U\cdots\times_{X}U}_{n+1}\to X,$$
so that $U_{n}$ is a locally separated locally spatial diamond, and $g_{n}$ and $f\circ g_{n}$ are fine and 0-truncated. Given $Rh_{!}$ for fine 0-truncated $h$, one defines
$$Rf_{!}A=\mathrm{colim}_{n\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}}R(f\circ g_{n})_{!}Rg_{n}^{!}A, \tag{1.2}$$
where $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ is the simplex category. Proper base change and the projection formula follow formally from (1.2):
$$\begin{aligned}
Rf_{!}(A\otimes f^{*}B)&\cong \colim_{n}R(f\circ g_{n})_{!}Rg_{n}^{!}(A\otimes f^{*}B)\\
&\cong \colim_{n}R(f\circ g_{n})_{!}(Rg_{n}^{!}A\otimes Rg_{n}^{*}f^{*}B) &&\text{(smoothness of } g_{n})\\
&\cong \colim_{n}R(f\circ g_{n})_{!}Rg_{n}^{!}A\otimes B &&\text{(projection formula)}\\
&\cong Rf_{!}A\otimes B &&(\otimes \text{ is closed}).
\end{aligned}$$

Formula (1.2) follows from the expectation that the map
$$\mathrm{colim}_{n\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}}Rg_{n!}Rg_{n}^{!}A\to A \tag{1.3}$$
is an isomorphism ("cohomological codescent for surjective smooth maps"). Applying $Rf_{!}$ to (1.3), and using that $Rf_{!}$ is a left adjoint and so commutes with colimits, gives
$$Rf_{!}A\simeq\colim_{n}R(f\circ g_{n})_{!}Rg_{n}^{!}A.$$

Two difficulties arise. First, the colimit in (1.2) need not exist or behave naturally in triangulated categories. Second, even if $g$ is separated, $g_{n}$ and $f\circ g_{n}$ are only locally separated, so the formalism of [11] does not construct $R(f\circ g_{n})_{!}$ or $Rg_{n}^{!}$. The remedy for the first is to work $\infty$-categorically, using enhanced operation maps as in [8, 9] (reviewed in Section 2.1).

**Strategy for the enhanced operation map.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category of geometric objects with marked classes $\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{n}$ and a subset $I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}$. An enhanced operation map for $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{n},I)$ assigns to each $X\in\mathcal{C}$ a symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}(X)$, and to each morphism $X\to Y$ in $\mathcal{E}_{i}$ a functor $\mathcal{D}(X)\to\mathcal{D}(Y)$ (if $i\notin I$) or $\mathcal{D}(Y)\to\mathcal{D}(X)$ (if $i\in I$). This must be compatible with cartesian diagrams built from the $\mathcal{E}_{i}$ and with the symmetric monoidal structures. The main theorem of [8] gives such a map for (Artin stacks, locally finite type morphisms, all morphisms, $\{2\}$), encoding $f^{*}$ and $Rf_{!}$. The construction proceeds in stages, which are the model for the present paper:

1. An enhanced operation map (EOM) exists for (schemes, all morphisms, $\{1\}$) [8, §2.2]. It is restricted to (qc separated schemes, locally finite type morphisms, $\{1\}$).
2. Each qc separated locally finite type $f$ factors as $p\circ j$ with $j$ an open immersion and $p$ proper. Gluing [9, Theorem 5.4] extends the EOM to (qc separated schemes, proper morphisms, local isomorphisms, $\{1,2\}$) and to (qc separated schemes, proper morphisms, local isomorphisms, all morphisms, $\{1,2,3\}$).
3. [9, Proposition 1.4.4] passes to an EOM on (qc separated schemes, proper morphisms, local isomorphisms, all, $\{3\}$), reversing the arrows for the first two classes.
4. Gluing, applied in reverse, gives an EOM on (qc separated schemes, locally finite type morphisms, all, $\{2\}$).
5. The DESCENT program [8] extends the EOM to qs schemes, algebraic spaces, and Artin stacks.

The present main result follows the same strategy, with these differences:

1. The first stage is an EOM for (small coproducts of qcqs v-sheaves, all morphisms, $\{1\}$).
2. For $f$ a morphism of qcqs v-sheaves, representable in locally spatial diamonds and compactifiable of locally finite dim.trg, there is a factorization $f=p\circ j$ with $j$ an open immersion and $p$ proper, namely the canonical compactification $j:X\to\overline{X}^{/Y}$ [11, Proposition 18.6]. Here $p$ need not be representable in locally spatial diamonds, so the paper introduces *prespatial diamonds*, a larger auxiliary class preserved by canonical compactification. This gives an EOM on (small coproducts of qcqs v-sheaves, proper morphisms representable in prespatial diamonds, separated local isomorphisms, $\{1,2\}$), using the cohomological results of [11].
3. (Steps 3–4 are analogous to the scheme case.) This gives an EOM on (small coproducts of qcqs v-sheaves, morphisms representable in locally spatial diamonds and compactifiable of locally finite dim.trg, $\{2\}$).
4. DESCENT is applied repeatedly: from small coproducts of separated spatial diamonds to quasiseparated locally separated locally spatial diamonds; then to locally separated locally spatial diamonds; then to decent v-stacks with fine morphisms.

### 1.3 Comments and conventions

We use $\infty$-categories as simplicial sets satisfying the weak Kan condition (Lurie's model), as in [8, 9]. A category $\mathcal{C}$ is identified with its nerve $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})$, and a $(2,1)$-category with its Duskin nerve (see [7, Tag 009P]). The nerve functor is usually omitted from notation.

## Scenario 2.1

Let $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})$ be a marked $\infty$-category.

1. For each object $X\in\mathcal{C}$ there is a closed symmetric monoidal stable $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}(X)$. Also there is an internal hom bifunctor $R\mathscr{H}om(-,-)$ such that $R\mathscr{H}om(B,-)$ is right adjoint to $-\otimes B$ for all $B\in\mathcal{D}(X)$.
2. For any morphism $f:X\to Y$ there is a symmetric monoidal pullback functor $f^{*}:\mathcal{D}(Y)\to\mathcal{D}(X)$.
3. For any $p:X\to Y$ with $p\in\mathcal{F}$ there is an exceptional pushforward $p_{!}:\mathcal{D}(X)\to\mathcal{D}(Y)$.
4. The functors $f^{*}$ and $p_{!}$ commute with all direct sums, and so admit right adjoints $f_{*}$ and $p^{!}$.
5. For any cartesian square
$$\begin{array}{ccc} X' & \xrightarrow{\;g\;} & X \\ {\scriptstyle q}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle p} \\ Y' & \xrightarrow{\;f\;} & Y \end{array} \tag{2.1}$$
with $p\in\mathcal{F}$, there is a proper base change isomorphism $f^{*}p_{!}\simeq q_{!}g^{*}$.
6. There is a projection formula isomorphism $p_{!}A\otimes B\simeq p_{!}(A\otimes p^{*}B)$ for $A\in\mathcal{D}(X)$, $B\in\mathcal{D}(Y)$.

## Construction 2.3 (Symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories and commutative algebra objects)

We recall the definition of symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category [6, Definition 2.0.0.7]. Let $\mathcal{F}\mathrm{in}_{\ast}$ be the category of pointed finite sets, with objects $\langle n\rangle=\{\ast,1,\dots,n\}$, and morphisms $\langle m\rangle\to\langle n\rangle$ the functions preserving $\ast$. (Equivalently, the category of finite sets with partially defined functions as morphisms.)

A *symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category* is a coCartesian fibration of simplicial sets $p:\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{F}\mathrm{in}_{\ast})$ satisfying condition $(\ast)$. For $i=1,\dots,n$, let $\rho^{i}:\langle n\rangle\to\langle 1\rangle$ be the unique morphism with $(\rho^{i})^{-1}(1)=\{i\}$. Since $p$ is a coCartesian fibration, $\rho^{i}$ induces $\rho^{i}_{!}:\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\langle n\rangle}\to\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\langle 1\rangle}$. Condition $(\ast)$ states that the product of the $\rho^{i}_{!}$ is an equivalence $\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\langle n\rangle}\to(\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\langle 1\rangle})^{n}$ for all $n$.

One writes $\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\langle 1\rangle}$ for the underlying $\infty$-category. The unique active map $\alpha:\langle 2\rangle\to\langle 1\rangle$ gives the composition law $\otimes:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\langle 2\rangle}\to\mathcal{C}$. The remaining structure of $\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}$ supplies isomorphisms witnessing that $\otimes$ is unital, commutative, and associative, together with all higher coherences.

A *commutative algebra object* of $\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}$ is a section $s:\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{F}\mathrm{in}_{\ast})\to\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}$ of $p$. With $X=s(\langle 1\rangle)$, the map $\alpha$ induces $m:X\otimes X\to X$, and the rest of $s$ encodes that $m$ is unital, commutative, and associative. The commutative algebra objects form an $\infty$-category $\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})$ [6, Definition 2.1.3.1].

If $\mathcal{C}$ admits finite products (resp. coproducts), it has a canonical symmetric monoidal structure $\mathcal{C}^{\times}$ (resp. $\mathcal{C}^{\amalg}$) whose composition law is the product (resp. coproduct); see [6, Construction 2.4.1.4] (resp. [6, Construction 2.4.3.1]). In $\mathcal{C}^{\amalg}_{\langle n\rangle}$, objects are $n$-tuples $(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})$, and a 1-morphism $(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})\to(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})$ consists of $\alpha:\langle m\rangle\to\langle n\rangle$ together with morphisms $X_{i}\to Y_{\alpha(i)}$ for each $i$ with $\alpha(i)\neq\ast$. In particular $X_{1}\otimes X_{2}\cong X_{1}\amalg X_{2}$.

If $\mathcal{C}$ admits finite products, $\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}):=\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}^{\times})$. Thus $\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_{\infty})$ is the $\infty$-category of symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories.

There is a canonical functor $\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}^{\amalg})$ sending $A$ to the commutative algebra structure with composition law $A\amalg A\to A$. Moreover $\mathcal{C}^{\amalg}$ is the universal symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}$ admitting a functor $\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{D})$ [6, Theorem 2.4.3.18]. That is, for any such $\mathcal{D}$, monoidal functors $T:\mathcal{C}^{\amalg}\to\mathcal{D}$ are equivalent to functors $T^{\otimes}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{D})$.

For a category of geometric objects $\mathcal{C}$, one wishes to assign to each $X$ a symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}(X)$ coherently, so that morphisms $X\to Y$ induce functors $\mathcal{D}(Y)\to\mathcal{D}(X)$. This is accomplished by a functor $\mathcal{C}^{\op}\to\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_{\infty})$, equivalently a monoidal functor $\mathcal{C}^{\op,\amalg}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_{\infty}$.

## Theorem 2.6

Consider the marked $\infty$-category $(\mathcal{V}\mathrm{stk}^{\mathrm{dct}},\mathcal{F})$, where $\mathcal{V}\mathrm{stk}^{\mathrm{dct}}$ is the category of decent v-stacks and $\mathcal{F}$ is the class of fine morphisms. Fix a ring $\Lambda$ killed by some integer prime to $p$. Then there is an enhanced operation map
$${}_{\mathcal{V}\mathrm{stk}^{\mathrm{dct}}}\mathrm{EO}:\delta_{2,\{2\}}^{*}(\mathcal{V}\mathrm{stk}^{\mathrm{dct}}_{\amalg})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{F},\mathrm{all}}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_{\infty}$$
such that:

1. $\mathcal{D}(X)=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$ with its natural symmetric monoidal structure, for all $X\in\mathcal{V}\mathrm{stk}^{\mathrm{dct}}$.
2. $f^{*}$ coincides with the pullback functor on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}$ constructed in [11], for all morphisms $f$.
3. For morphisms $f\in\mathcal{F}$ which are separated and representable in locally spatial diamonds, $f_{!}$ coincides with the functor $Rf_{!}$ constructed in [11].

## Definition 3.1

A qcqs diamond $X$ is *prespatial* if there exists a spatial subdiamond $X_{0}\subset X$ such that $X_{0}(K,\mathcal{O}_{K})=X(K,\mathcal{O}_{K})$ for all perfectoid fields $K$.

A diamond $X$ is *locally prespatial* if there exists a locally spatial subdiamond $X_{0}\subset X$ such that the inclusion $X_{0}\to X$ is quasicompact and $X_{0}(K,\mathcal{O}_{K})=X(K,\mathcal{O}_{K})$ for all perfectoid fields $K$.

A pair $(X_{0},X)$ satisfying the above conditions is called a *(locally) prespatial pair*.

## Lemma 3.2

Let $(X_{0},X)$ be a (locally) prespatial pair, let $(Y_{0},Y)$ be a locally prespatial pair, and let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of diamonds. Then $(X_{0}\times_{Y}Y_{0},X)$ is also a (locally) prespatial pair.
