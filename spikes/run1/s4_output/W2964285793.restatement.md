# Restatement

## Abstract (restated)

The paper concerns a family of conformally invariant first-order elliptic operators on a Riemannian spin manifold. The Dirac operator is the first and simplest member. The paper gives the general definition of these operators and describes their basic properties. It gives particular attention to the Rarita–Schwinger operator, the second-simplest operator in the family, and describes its properties in more detail. The final part computes indices of the discussed operators.

## 1 Introduction

Let $M$ be a smooth oriented compact $n$-dimensional manifold, endowed with a Riemannian metric and a spin structure. Basic invariant differential operators on $M$ include the Laplacian (second order) and the Dirac operator (first order). Their properties are related to the geometry and topology of $M$. Their behaviour under conformal change of metric is studied in the framework of invariant operators on AHS manifolds. A list of conformally invariant operators of various orders is defined on any Riemannian spin manifold, and analogous notions and properties can be studied for them.

The Rarita–Schwinger operator is a prototype of more complicated first-order invariant operators on $M$ (see [Fra1, Fra2, FraS, MP, Pe1, Pe2, Pe3, Pe4, Pe5, RaS, US, Wa]). It acts on sections of the bundle associated with a more complicated representation of $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$. It is also included in a series of operators called higher spin Dirac operators, and in a series of operators called higher Rarita–Schwinger operators, defined on spin-valued symmetric tensor fields. The latter have been used in Clifford analysis in connection with monogenic differential forms (see [DSS, So1, So2, SoS, So3]) and symmetric functions (see [Som1]).

The aim of the paper is to collect and review facts that exist mostly only in preprint form, and to add new facts (index computations). The operators are defined, including their normalisation, and some of their properties are described. The main attention is on the Rarita–Schwinger operator. The paper discusses the following questions:

1. The description of conformally invariant first-order differential operators (including the Rarita–Schwinger operator).
2. The spectrum of the Rarita–Schwinger operator on the flat model, i.e. on spheres.
3. A complete description of polynomial solutions of the Rarita–Schwinger equation.
4. The index of the Rarita–Schwinger operator and of higher spin Dirac operators.

The first three parts are of review character, and most results there are taken from preprints. The index results are new.

## Definitions, remarks, theorems, lemmas and corollaries

**Definition 1** [BuSo]. Let $\mathbf{S}_{1/2}$ (for $n=2k+1$), resp. $\mathbf{S}_{1/2}=\mathbf{S}_{1/2}^{+}\oplus\mathbf{S}_{1/2}^{-}$ (for $n=2k$), denote the basic spinor representations with highest weights $\sigma=(\tfrac12,\ldots,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, resp. $\sigma^{\pm}=(\tfrac12,\ldots,\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac12)$. Let $\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}$ (for $n=2k+1$), resp. $\lambda^{\pm}\in\Lambda^{+}$ (for $n=2k$), be dominant weights with $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{k-1},\tfrac12)$, resp. $\lambda^{\pm}=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{k-1},\pm\tfrac12)$. Put $\lambda'=\lambda-\sigma\in\Lambda^{+}$, resp. $\lambda'=\lambda^{+}-\sigma^{+}\in\Lambda^{+}$. In even dimensions, write $\mathbf{V}_\lambda=\mathbf{V}_{\lambda^{+}}\oplus\mathbf{V}_{\lambda^{-}}$.

The representation $\mathbf{V}_\lambda$ appears with multiplicity one in the decomposition of $\mathbf{S}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{\lambda'}$ (it is the Cartan product of both representations). Hence
$$\mathbf{S}_{1/2}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{\lambda'}=\mathbf{V}_\lambda\oplus\mathbf{W},$$
where $\mathbf{W}$ is the sum of all other irreducible components. Let $D^{T}_{\lambda'}$ be the twisted Dirac operator on $S_{1/2}\otimes V_{\lambda'}$. Writing $D^{T}_{\lambda'}$ in block form gives four invariant operators, one of which is
$$D_\lambda:\Gamma(V_\lambda)\to\Gamma(V_\lambda).$$
Operators $D_\lambda$ defined in this way are called generalized (higher spin) Dirac operators.

**Remark 1.** The classical Dirac operator on $M$ is $D:\Gamma(S_{1/2})\to\Gamma(S_{1/2})$. With respect to a frame $\mathbf{e}$ it has the form
$$D(\sigma)=\sum_i e_i\,\nabla^S_{e_i}\sigma.$$

**Theorem 1** ([Wa]). The operator $D_T$, with respect to the decomposition $\Gamma(S_{1/2}\otimes T_C^*)=\Gamma(S_{1/2})\oplus\Gamma(S_{3/2})$, has the block form
$$\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2-n}{n}\,\iota\circ D\circ\iota^{-1} & 2\,\iota\circ\delta\\[2pt] \frac{2}{n}\,(\mathcal{T}\circ\iota^{-1}) & \mathcal{R}\end{pmatrix}.$$

**Lemma 1.** The eigenvalues of the Dirac operator on the sphere $S_n$ with standard metric are
$$\mu_l=\pm\left(\tfrac n2+l\right),\qquad l=0,1,2,\ldots,$$
with multiplicity $2^{[n/2]}\binom{l+n-1}{l}$.

**Theorem 2.** Let $D_{\lambda_j}=-\tilde D_j$, $0<j<n/2$, be the higher spin Dirac operators defined above, considered on the sphere $S_n$ with the standard metric. Their eigenvalues are
$$\mu^{1}_l=\pm\left(\tfrac n2+l\right),\qquad l=1,2,\ldots,$$
with multiplicity $2^{[n/2]}\binom{n+1}{j+1}\binom{l+n}{l-1}\dfrac{(n-2j)(j+1)}{(l+j)(l+n-j)}$, and
$$\mu^{2}_l=\pm\left[\frac{n-2j}{n-2j+2}\left(\tfrac n2+l\right)\right],\qquad l=1,2,\ldots,$$
with multiplicity $2^{[n/2]}\binom{n+1}{j}\binom{l+n}{l-1}\dfrac{(n-2j+2)j}{(l+j-1)(l+n-j+1)}$.

**Remark 2.** A spinor-valued 1-form $\psi$ on $M$ satisfying $\mu(\psi)=0$ is a solution of the Rarita–Schwinger equation if and only if there is a spinor field $\phi$ on $M$ such that $D_T\psi=\iota(\phi)$.

**Lemma 2.** Suppose $\psi\in\mathcal{P}_k(1)$. Then $D^{3}(\mathcal{L}(\psi))=0$.

**Lemma 3.** $\Xi(\psi_0)$ is a $k$-homogeneous solution of the equation (eq).

**Theorem 3.** Let $\psi$ be a $k$-homogeneous solution of the Rarita–Schwinger equation. Then there is a unique decomposition $\psi=\psi_1+\psi_2+\psi_3$ into ($k$-homogeneous) pieces with $\psi_j\in\mathcal{M}^j$, where
$$\mathcal{M}^1=\{\psi\in\Gamma(\mathbf{R}_0^{n+1},S_{3/2})\mid\mathcal{L}(\psi)=0\},$$
$$\mathcal{M}^2=\mathcal{T}'(\ker D)\subset\{\psi\in\Gamma(\mathbf{R}_0^{n+1},S_{3/2})\mid D\mathcal{L}(\psi)=0\},$$
$$\mathcal{M}^3=\Xi(\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(0))\subset\{\psi\in\Gamma(\mathbf{R}_0^{n+1},S_{3/2})\mid D\mathcal{L}(\psi)\neq0\text{ for }\psi\neq0\}.$$

**Remark 3.** All types of solutions are uniquely determined by their restriction to the unit sphere, and can be described by pure spherical data (see [BuLN]).

**Remark 4.** The classification of polynomial solutions of the Rarita–Schwinger equation can also be done for polynomial solutions of the general higher spin Dirac equation and of higher Rarita–Schwinger equations. Several papers by P. Van Lancker (Gent), F. Sommen (Gent), V. Souček (Prague) and the author are in preparation.

**Remark 5.** Chern classes $c_i(E)$ are represented by closed differential forms of degree $2i$, computable from the curvature form of any connection on $E$. By the index theorem, the index of the operator depends only on the bundles $E$ and $F$, not on the operator itself. In the following section, $\dim M=m=2n$ is assumed even, although this is not always mentioned; odd-dimensional cases are trivial.

**Theorem 4** (Atiyah–Singer, [BGV]). Let $M$ be an oriented spin manifold of dimension $m=2n$, let $\mathcal S$ be the spin bundle, and let $\mathcal W$ be an arbitrary vector bundle over $M$. If $D_W$ is the twisted Dirac operator on $\Gamma(M,\mathcal W\otimes\mathcal S)$, then
$$\mathrm{ind}\,D_W=(2\pi\mathbf{i})^{-n}\int_M\hat A(M)\,\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal W).$$

**Remark 6.** The index of the classical Dirac operator (called also Atiyah–Singer [LM]) $D^+_{1/2}:\Gamma(S^+)\to\Gamma(S^-)$ is
$$\mathrm{Ind}\,D^+_{1/2}:=\hat A_{1/2}[M]=(2\pi\mathbf{i})^{-n}\int_M\hat A(M).$$

**Corollary 1.** For the twisted Dirac operator on $M$, $\dim M=2n$,
$$D_T^+:\Gamma(T_C^*(M)\otimes S^+)\to\Gamma(T_C^*(M)\otimes S^-),$$
the index is given by
$$\mathrm{Ind}\,D^+_T=\int_M Ch(T_C^*(M))\hat A(M)=(2\pi\mathbf{i})^{-n}\int_M\Big(2n+p_1(M)+\tfrac1{12}(p_1(M)^2-2p_2(M))+\cdots\Big)\Big(1-\tfrac1{24}p_1(M)+\tfrac1{5760}(7p_1(M)^2-4p_2(M))+\cdots\Big).$$

**Theorem 5.** The index of the operator $D_j$ is
$$\mathrm{ind}\,D_j:=\hat A_{j/2}[M]=(2\pi\mathbf{i})^{-n}\int_M\big(Ch(\Lambda^{j-1})-Ch(\Lambda^{j})\big)\hat A(M).$$
