# Restatement

## Abstract

We construct an involutive duality on condensed spectra with locally compact homotopy groups, extending classical Pontryagin duality. We prove that dualization induces Pontryagin duality on homotopy groups, with degrees reversed, and that the canonical biduality map is an equivalence. In particular, every ordinary spectrum, viewed as a discrete condensed spectrum, satisfies biduality, without any finiteness assumptions on its homotopy groups.

## 1. Introduction

**Pontryagin duality.** For a locally compact abelian group $A$, its Pontryagin dual is
$$\widehat{A}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(A,\mathbb{T}),$$
endowed with the compact-open topology, where $\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. The Pontryagin duality theorem states that $\widehat{A}$ is locally compact and that the evaluation map $d_A\colon A\to\widehat{\widehat{A}}$ is an isomorphism. Pontryagin duality exchanges compact and discrete abelian groups. The formulation used is [9, Theorem 4.1(ii)–(iii)].

For a compactly generated Hausdorff topological abelian group $A$ and a Hausdorff topological abelian group $B$, there is a natural isomorphism
$$\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}(A,B)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(A,B)$$
of condensed abelian groups, where the right-hand side is the condensed abelian group associated with the compact-open topology [9, Proposition 4.2]. Hence
$$\widehat{A}=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}(A,\mathbb{T}).$$

For finite abelian groups, $A\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})$, since every character has finite image and $\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$.

**Brown–Comenetz duality.** Since $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is injective in $\mathrm{Ab}$, it admits an injective lift $I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}$ relative to the standard t-structure on spectra [7, Definition C.5.7.2, Proposition C.5.7.3, and Theorem C.5.7.4]. For a spectrum $X$, set
$$\widehat{X}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(X,I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}).$$
This satisfies $\pi_n\widehat{X}\cong\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Ab}}(\pi_{-n}X,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})$ naturally in $X$ and $n\in\mathbb{Z}$. If all homotopy groups of $X$ are finite, the evaluation map $X\to\widehat{\widehat{X}}$ is an equivalence [8, Theorem 7.4.1].

**Hoffmann–Spitzweck.** For every locally compact abelian group $A$,
$$\operatorname{Ext}^q_{\mathrm{LCA}}(A,\mathbb{T})=0\qquad(q\geq 1)\ \text{[4, Proposition 4.14(vii)]}.$$
The comparison functor $D^b(\mathrm{LCA})\to D(\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab}))$ is fully faithful [9, Corollary 4.9], and the proof identifies the corresponding derived internal Hom objects [9, Corollary 4.9, proof].

**Construction.** Let $\iota\colon(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{disc}}\to\mathbb{T}$ be the natural inclusion. Let $h^{\mathrm{disc}}\colon H((\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{disc}})\to I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{disc}}$ be the discrete condensation of the map corresponding to $\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}$ under the Brown–Comenetz universal property. Define $I_{\mathbb{T}}\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$ by the pushout square

$$\begin{array}{ccc}
H((\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{disc}}) & \xrightarrow{\ H\iota\ } & H\mathbb{T}\\
\Big\downarrow{\scriptstyle h^{\mathrm{disc}}} & \lrcorner & \Big\downarrow\\
I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{disc}} & \longrightarrow & I_{\mathbb{T}}
\end{array}$$

For $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}$, define
$$\widehat{X}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})}(X,I_{\mathbb{T}}).$$

**Main theorem.** With $I_{\mathbb{T}}$ and $\widehat{(-)}$ as above:

> **Theorem (Condensed Brown–Comenetz–Pontryagin duality).**
> (i) The spectrum $I_{\mathbb{T}}$ is coconnective, and there is a canonical isomorphism $\pi_0 I_{\mathbb{T}}\cong\mathbb{T}$.
> (ii) For every $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}$ and $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, there is a natural isomorphism $\pi_n\widehat{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{\pi_{-n}X}$.
> (iii) The canonical evaluation map $d_X\colon X\to\widehat{\widehat{X}}$ is an equivalence for every $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}$.
> Consequently, $\widehat{(-)}\colon\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}$ is a contravariant equivalence whose quasi-inverse is itself.

**Related work and acknowledgments.** Omitted from this restatement as non-mathematical.

## 2. Abelian and stable preliminaries

**Notation 2.1.** For an abelian category $\mathcal{A}$, $[X,Y]_{\mathcal{A}}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)$. For a stable $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, $[X,Y]_{\mathcal{C}}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{h\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)=\pi_0\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)$. In either case, $[X,Y]$ denotes the abelian group of morphisms from $X$ to $Y$.

**Definition 2.2 (Injective objects in an abelian category).** An object $J\in\mathcal{A}$ is *injective* if the contravariant functor $[-,J]_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}$ is exact. $\operatorname{Inj}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathcal{A}$ denotes the full subcategory of injective objects.

**Definition 2.3 (The heart of a t-structure).** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a stable $\infty$-category with a t-structure $(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0},\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0})$ in the sense of [6, Definition 1.2.1.4]. Its heart is $\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}:=\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}$. The functor
$$\pi_0:=\tau_{\leq 0}\tau_{\geq 0}\simeq\tau_{\geq 0}\tau_{\leq 0}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}$$
is defined. For $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, $\pi_n(X):=\pi_0(\Sigma^{-n}X)$ (homological indexing). The category $\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}$ is an ordinary abelian category [6, Definition 1.2.1.11 and Remark 1.2.1.12].

**Definition 2.4 (t-injective objects).** An object $Q\in\mathcal{C}$ is *t-injective* if $Q\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}$ and $[X,\Sigma Q]_{\mathcal{C}}=0$ for every $X\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}$. $\operatorname{Inj}_t(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{C}$ denotes the full subcategory of t-injective objects. Lurie calls these simply injective [7, Definition C.5.7.2].

**Proposition 2.5 (Lurie).** For $X,Q\in\mathcal{C}$, let
$$\rho_{X,Q}\colon[X,Q]_{\mathcal{C}}\to[\pi_0X,\pi_0Q]_{\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}}$$
be the canonical map induced by $\pi_0$. The following are equivalent:
(1) $Q$ is t-injective.
(1′) $\rho_{X,Q}$ is an isomorphism for every $X\in\mathcal{C}$.

**Theorem 2.6 (Lurie).** Let $\mathcal{P}$ be a Grothendieck prestable $\infty$-category, and let $\mathcal{C}:=\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal{P})$ with its canonical t-structure. Then $\pi_0$ induces an equivalence of ordinary categories
$$\pi_0\colon h\operatorname{Inj}_t(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Inj}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}).$$
In particular, every injective $J\in\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}$ admits a t-injective lift $I_J\in\mathcal{C}$ with an isomorphism $\pi_0I_J\cong J$. This lift is unique up to unique isomorphism in $h\mathcal{C}$ compatible with the identification. For every $X\in\mathcal{C}$,
$$[X,I_J]_{\mathcal{C}}\xrightarrow{\sim}[\pi_0X,J]_{\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}}$$
is an isomorphism, given by $\rho_{X,I_J}$ followed by $\pi_0I_J\cong J$. This universal property characterizes $I_J$ together with its identification.

**Definition 2.7 (Brown–Comenetz spectrum).** The Brown–Comenetz spectrum $I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathrm{Sp}$ is the essentially unique t-injective spectrum with $\pi_0I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$.

**Remark 2.8.** $I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}$ is not the Eilenberg–Mac Lane spectrum $H(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})$. The former represents $X\mapsto[\pi_0X,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}]_{\mathrm{Ab}}$ on all spectra; the latter represents ordinary cohomology with coefficients in $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$.

## 3. Condensed objects and spectra

**Definition 3.1 (The pro-étale site of a point).** Let $\mathrm{ProFin}$ be the category of profinite sets and continuous maps. The pro-étale site of a point $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$ is $\mathrm{ProFin}$ with the Grothendieck topology in which a finite family $\{S_i\to S\}_{i=1}^n$ covers if $\coprod_i S_i\to S$ is surjective [9, Definition 1.2].

**Definition 3.2 (Condensed objects).** For an ordinary category $\mathcal{E}$ with the required limits, a condensed $\mathcal{E}$-valued object is an $\mathcal{E}$-valued sheaf on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$:
$$\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{E}):=\operatorname{Shv}(\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{E}).$$
$\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})$ is the category of condensed abelian groups [9, Definition 1.2].

**Definition 3.3 (Condensed spectra).** Using hypercomplete sheaves,
$$\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{S}):=\operatorname{Shv}^{\mathrm{hyp}}(\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{S}).$$
This agrees with the pyknotic presentation [9, Proposition 2.3] and [1, Corollary 2.4.4]. Hypercompleteness ensures that homotopy objects detect equivalences. Since stabilization is computed pointwise,
$$\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp}):=\operatorname{Sp}(\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{S})).$$

**Definition 3.4 (Homotopy groups).** For $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$ and $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, $\pi_nX\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})$ is the sheaf associated with $S\mapsto\pi_n(X(S))$.

**Definition 3.5 (Condensed set of a space).** For a topological space $T$, $\underline{T}(S):=\operatorname{Cont}(S,T)$ for profinite $S$. If $T$ is a topological group, ring, or abelian group, $\underline{T}$ is correspondingly a condensed group, ring, or abelian group.

**Definition 3.6 (Discrete condensed objects).** For a set, group, ring, or abelian group $M$ with the discrete topology, $M^{\mathrm{disc}}(S)=\operatorname{Cont}(S,M)$, the locally constant maps $S\to M$.

**Definition 3.7 (Discrete condensed spectra).** The functor $(-)^{\mathrm{disc}}\colon\mathrm{Sp}\to\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$ is fully faithful and left adjoint to global sections. For a spectrum $E$ and profinite $S$,
$$E^{\mathrm{disc}}(S)=\operatorname*{colim}_{S\twoheadrightarrow S_0}E^{S_0},$$
where $S_0$ ranges over finite continuous quotients of $S$.

**Definition 3.8 (Eilenberg–Mac Lane condensed spectra).** For $A\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})$, $HA\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$ satisfies $\pi_0(HA)\cong A$ and $\pi_n(HA)=0$ for $n\neq 0$.

**Definition 3.9 (Symmetric monoidal structure).** $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$ is a presentable stable closed symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category with unit $\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{disc}}$ [7, Proposition 1.3.4.7]. The internal Hom $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})}(X,Y)$ satisfies
$$\operatorname{Map}(Z,\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(X,Y))\simeq\operatorname{Map}(Z\otimes X,Y)$$
for all $Z\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$.

**Notation 3.10.** $[X,Y]_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}(X,Y)$, and $[X,Y]_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})}:=\pi_0\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})}(X,Y)$.

**Definition 3.11.** $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}\subseteq\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$ is the full subcategory of objects $X$ for which $\pi_nX$ is represented by a locally compact abelian group for every $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.

**Corollary 3.12.** For a locally compact abelian group $A$,
$$\mathrm{R}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}(A,\mathbb{T})=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}(A,\mathbb{T})[0].$$

**Lemma 3.13.** For every profinite set $S$, $B_S:=(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{disc}}(S)$ is an injective abelian group.

**Lemma 3.14.** For every profinite set $S$, there is a canonical equivalence $I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{disc}}(S)\simeq I_{B_S}$.

**Definition 3.15 (The condensed Pontryagin dualizing spectrum).** $I_{\mathbb{T}}$ is defined by the pushout square of Section 1.

**Proposition 3.16.** For $I_{\mathbb{T}}$:
1. $I_{\mathbb{T}}\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\leq 0}$.
2. There is a canonical isomorphism $\pi_0I_{\mathbb{T}}\cong\mathbb{T}$ in $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})$.

## 4. Biduality

**Lemma 4.1 (Enriched restriction–coinduction).** For $M\in D(\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab}))$ and $Y\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$, there is a natural equivalence
$$\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})}(U(M),Y)\simeq U\bigl(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{D(\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab}))}(M,R(Y))\bigr).$$

**Lemma 4.2.** Let $h\colon H(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}$ correspond to $\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}$, and let $h^{\mathrm{disc}}$ be its discrete condensation. The adjoint
$$\widetilde{h}\colon H((\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{disc}})\to R(I_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{disc}})$$
is an equivalence in $D(\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab}))$.

**Lemma 4.3.** There is a canonical equivalence $R(I_{\mathbb{T}})\simeq H\mathbb{T}$ in $D(\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab}))$.

**Notation 4.4.** For $Y\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$, $\widehat{Y}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})}(Y,I_{\mathbb{T}})$. For $B\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})$, $\widehat{B}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}(B,\mathbb{T})$.

**Proposition 4.5 (Universal property of the dualizing spectrum).** Let $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}$. The natural morphism
$$\rho_X\colon\pi_0\widehat{X}\to\widehat{\pi_0X}$$
is an isomorphism. Here $\rho_X$ is adjoint to the pairing
$$\pi_0\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(X,I_{\mathbb{T}})\otimes\pi_0X\to\pi_0(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(X,I_{\mathbb{T}})\otimes X)\xrightarrow{\pi_0(\operatorname{ev}_X)}\pi_0I_{\mathbb{T}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{T}.$$

**Lemma 4.6.** For every locally compact abelian group $A$, there is a natural equivalence $\widehat{HA}\simeq H\widehat{A}$ (4.1), whose induced identification on $\pi_0$ is $\rho_{HA}$. Consequently, for every $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, $\widehat{\Sigma^nHA}\simeq\Sigma^{-n}H\widehat{A}$.

**Corollary 4.7 (Homotopy formula).** For every $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}$ and $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, Proposition 4.5 applied to $\Sigma^nX$ gives a natural isomorphism
$$\rho_{X,n}\colon\pi_n\widehat{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{\pi_{-n}X}.$$
In particular, $\widehat{X}\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}$.

**Definition 4.8 (Pontryagin and condensed Brown–Comenetz duality).** For $A\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})$, $\widehat{A}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})}(A,\mathbb{T})$ with evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_A\colon\widehat{A}\otimes A\to\mathbb{T}$. The Pontryagin biduality morphism $d_A\colon A\to\widehat{\widehat{A}}$ is the adjunct of
$$A\otimes\widehat{A}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\widehat{A}\otimes A\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}_A}\mathbb{T}.$$
For $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})$, $\widehat{X}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})}(X,I_{\mathbb{T}})$ with evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_X\colon\widehat{X}\otimes X\to I_{\mathbb{T}}$. The condensed Brown–Comenetz biduality morphism $d_X\colon X\to\widehat{\widehat{X}}$ is the adjunct of
$$X\otimes\widehat{X}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\widehat{X}\otimes X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}_X}I_{\mathbb{T}}.$$

**Theorem 4.9 (Compatibility with biduality).** Let $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}$. For every $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, the following diagram of condensed abelian groups commutes:

$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\pi_nX & \xrightarrow{\ \pi_n(d_X)\ } & \pi_n\widehat{\widehat{X}}\\
\Big\downarrow{\scriptstyle(-1)^n d_{\pi_nX}} & & \Big\downarrow{\scriptstyle\rho_{\widehat{X},n}}\\
\widehat{\widehat{\pi_nX}} & \xrightarrow{\ \widehat{\rho_{X,-n}}\ } & \widehat{\widehat{\pi_{-n}X}}
\end{array}$$

Note: the lower-right corner is printed as $\widehat{\pi_{-n}\widehat{X}}$ in the source, and the lower-left as $\widehat{\widehat{\pi_nX}}$; the diagram is reproduced as the arrow labels appear in the source.

**Theorem 4.10 (Condensed Brown–Comenetz–Pontryagin duality).** For every $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}$, the canonical biduality morphism $d_X\colon X\to\widehat{\widehat{X}}$ is an equivalence. Moreover, condensed Brown–Comenetz dualization restricts to a contravariant equivalence
$$\widehat{(-)}\colon\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}}^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Sp})_{\mathrm{lc}},$$
whose quasi-inverse is itself.

**Corollary 4.11 (Biduality for ordinary spectra).** For every spectrum $E$, the canonical map $E^{\mathrm{disc}}\to\widehat{\widehat{E^{\mathrm{disc}}}}$ is an equivalence of condensed spectra.

**Remark 4.12.** Condensed duality gives
$$\widehat{H(\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{disc}})}\simeq H\mathbb{T},\qquad\widehat{H\mathbb{T}}\simeq H(\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{disc}}).$$
By comparison, the classical Brown–Comenetz double dual of $H\mathbb{Z}$ is $H(\prod_p\mathbb{Z}_p)$, with biduality map induced by profinite completion.
