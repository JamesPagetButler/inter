## Section 1: Modular spectral triples for noncommutative 2-tori

**Lemma 1.1.** Let $\sigma(a)=kak^{-1}$ be the (non-unitary) inner automorphism of $\mathcal{A}$ associated to $k$. Then $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$ is a $\sigma$-twisted spectral triple.

**Remark 1.2.** The perturbation $D_{(k,\gamma)}$ of $D$ satisfies
$$D_{(k,\gamma)}=k^{E}Dk^{E},\qquad E=\frac{1+\gamma}{2}. \tag{10}$$

**Proposition 1.3.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$ be a $\sigma$-twisted spectral triple, let $\mathcal{A}^{\rm op}$ be the opposite algebra, and let $D^t$ be the transpose of $D$. Let $\sigma'$ be the automorphism of $\mathcal{A}^{\rm op}$ given by
$$\sigma'(a^{\rm op})=(\sigma^{-1}(a))^{\rm op}. \tag{12}$$
Then the action of $\mathcal{A}^{\rm op}$ in $\bar{\mathcal{H}}$, transposed from the action of $\mathcal{A}$ in $\mathcal{H}$, defines a $\sigma'$-twisted spectral triple
$$(\mathcal{A}^{\rm op},\bar{\mathcal H},D^t). \tag{13}$$

**Definition 1.4.** For a modular spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$, the transposed modular spectral triple is given by (13).

**Lemma 1.5.** The map
$$\psi:\mathcal{H}^{(1,0)}\to\mathcal{H}_0,\qquad \sum a\,d(b)\mapsto\sum a\,\delta(b)\in\mathcal{H}_0 \tag{30}$$
is a unitary $A_\theta^\infty$-bimodule isomorphism of $\mathcal{H}^{(1,0)}$ with $\mathcal{H}_0$.

**Definition 1.6.** A positive linear functional $\varphi$ on $A_\theta$ as in (31) is called a conformal weight with Weyl factor $e^{-h}$ and dilaton $h$. The normalized functional
$$\varphi_n(a)=\frac{\varphi_0(ae^{-h})}{\varphi_0(e^{-h})},\qquad a\in A_\theta, \tag{32}$$
is called the associated conformal state.

**Lemma 1.7.** The right multiplication $R_k a=ak$, for all $a\in A_\theta$, extends to an isometry $W:\mathcal{H}_0\to\mathcal{H}_\varphi$ and gives a unitary $A_\theta^\infty$-bimodule isomorphism of $\mathcal{H}_0$ with $\mathcal{H}_\varphi$.

**Lemma 1.8.** Let $k=e^{h/2}$, where $h=h^*\in A_\theta^\infty$ is the dilaton of $\varphi$, and let $R_k$ denote right multiplication by $k$ on $\mathcal{H}_0$.

(i) The operator $\tilde W^*D_\varphi\tilde W$ equals the self-adjoint unbounded operator
$$\tilde W^*D_\varphi\tilde W=\begin{pmatrix}0&R_k\delta^*\\ \delta R_k&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \delta=\delta_1+\bar\tau\delta_2,\quad \delta^*=\delta_1+\tau\delta_2. \tag{45}$$

(ii) The operator $\tilde J\tilde W^*D_\varphi\tilde W\tilde J$ equals the self-adjoint unbounded operator
$$\tilde J\tilde W^*D_\varphi\tilde W\tilde J=\begin{pmatrix}0&k\delta\\ \delta^*k&0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{46}$$

**Corollary 1.9.** Let $k=e^{h/2}$, with $h=h^*\in A_\theta^\infty$ the dilaton of $\varphi$.

(i) The left action of $A_\theta$ on $\tilde{\mathcal H}_\varphi$ together with $D_\varphi$ yields a graded spectral triple $(A_\theta,\tilde{\mathcal H}_\varphi,D_\varphi)$.

(ii) The right action $a\mapsto a^{\rm op}$ of $A_\theta$ on $\tilde{\mathcal H}_\varphi$ together with $D_\varphi$ yields a graded twisted spectral triple $(A^{\rm op}_\theta,\tilde{\mathcal H}_\varphi,D_\varphi)$, with bounded twisted commutators:
$$D_\varphi a^{\rm op}-(k^{-1}ak)^{\rm op}D_\varphi\in\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\mathcal H}_\varphi),\qquad \forall a\in A^\infty_\theta. \tag{47}$$

(iii) The transpose of the modular spectral triple $(A^{\rm op}_\theta,\tilde{\mathcal H}_\varphi,D_\varphi)$ is isomorphic to the perturbed spectral triple $(A_\theta,\mathcal H,\bar D_\varphi)$, where
$$\bar D_\varphi=\begin{pmatrix}0&k\delta\\ \delta^*k&0\end{pmatrix}\sim(\bar D)_{(k,\gamma)}. \tag{48}$$

**Definition 1.10.** The modular spectral triple of weight $\varphi$ is
$$(A_\theta^\infty,\mathcal H,\bar D_\varphi), \tag{50}$$
where $\mathcal H=\mathcal H_0\oplus\mathcal H_0$ as a left $A_\theta^\infty$-module and $\bar D_\varphi$ is given by (49).

**Lemma 1.11.** Let $k=e^{h/2}$, where $h=h^*\in A_\theta^\infty$ is the dilaton of $\varphi$.

(i) One has
$$\bar D_\varphi^2=\begin{pmatrix}k\triangle k&0\\0&\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi\end{pmatrix},\qquad \triangle=\delta\delta^*,\quad \triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi=\delta^*k^2\delta. \tag{52}$$

(ii) The Laplacian on functions is anti-unitarily equivalent to $\triangle_\varphi=k\triangle k$.

(ii) [label as printed] The operator $\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi=\delta^*k^2\delta$ is anti-unitarily equivalent to the Laplacian $\triangle^{(1,0)}_\varphi$ on forms of type $(1,0)$.

**Lemma 1.12.** Let $\varphi$ be a conformal weight with dilaton $h=h^*\in A_\theta^\infty$. The zeta functions satisfy
$$\zeta_{\triangle_\varphi}(z)=\zeta_{\triangle^{(1,0)}_\varphi}(z)=\zeta_{\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi}(z)=\zeta_{k\triangle k}(z). \tag{53}$$

## Section 2: Conformal invariants

**Theorem 2.1.** Under the above assumptions, the value at the origin $\zeta_{|D|}(0)$ of the zeta function is invariant under conformal deformations (54) of the spectral triple $(\mathcal A,\mathfrak H,D)$.

**Theorem 2.2.** The value at the origin of the zeta function of the Laplacian on functions is a conformal invariant:
$$\zeta_{\triangle_\varphi}(0)=\zeta_{\triangle}(0), \tag{64}$$
for any conformal weight $\varphi$ on $A_\theta$.

**Remark 2.3.** This gives a non-computational proof of the Gauss–Bonnet theorem for the noncommutative 2-torus (cf. [7], [14]).

## Section 3: Zeta functions and local invariants

**Lemma 3.1.** The functions $H_0(s,t)$, $H_1(s,t)=\cosh\!\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)H_0(s,t)$, and $S(s,t)$ satisfy:

1. They belong to $C^\infty_0(\mathbb R^2)$.
2. $H_j(t,s)=-H_j(s,t)$, $\;S(t,s)=S(s,t)$, and $S(s,t)\ge 0$.
3. $H_j(-s,-t)=-H_j(s,t)$, $\;S(-s,-t)=S(s,t)$.

**Theorem 3.2.** Let $\varphi$ be a conformal weight with dilaton $h=h^*\in A_\theta^\infty$, and let $k=e^{h/2}$. For any $a\in A_\theta^\infty$, the value at the origin of the zeta function associated with the modular spectral triple of weight $\varphi$ is
$$\mathrm{Tr}(a|\bar D_\varphi|^{-z})\big|_{z=0}=-\frac{\pi}{\tau_2}\,\varphi_0\!\Big(a\Big(K(\nabla)(\triangle(\log k))+H(\nabla_1,\nabla_2)(\square_{\Re}(\log k))+S(\nabla_1,\nabla_2)(\square_{\Im}(\log k))\Big)\Big)-\varphi_n(a)-\varphi_0(a), \tag{81}$$
where $H=H_0+H_1$. For the graded version,
$$\mathrm{Tr}(\gamma a|\bar D_\varphi|^{-z})\big|_{z=0}=-\frac{\pi}{\tau_2}\,\varphi_0\!\Big(a\Big(K_\gamma(\nabla)(\triangle(\log k))+H_\gamma(\nabla_1,\nabla_2)(\square_{\Re}(\log k))-S(\nabla_1,\nabla_2)(\square_{\Im}(\log k))\Big)\Big)-\varphi_n(a)+\varphi_0(a), \tag{82}$$
where $H_\gamma=H_0-H_1$.

## Section 4: Log-determinant functional and scalar curvature

**Lemma 4.1.** Let $\varphi$ be a conformal weight with dilaton $h=h^*\in A_\theta^\infty$. Then
$$\log\mathrm{Det}'(\triangle_\varphi)=\log\mathrm{Det}'\triangle+\log\varphi(1)-\frac{\pi}{\tau_2}\int_0^1\varphi_0\!\Big(h\Big(sK_0(s\nabla)(\triangle(\log k))+s^2H_0(s\nabla_1,s\nabla_2)(\square_{\Re}(\log k))\Big)\Big)\,ds. \tag{92}$$

**Lemma 4.2.** For $f(u)$ a function in the Schwartz space,
$$\varphi_0\big(h\,f(\nabla)(a)\big)=f(0)\,\varphi_0(ha),\qquad \forall a\in A_\theta^\infty. \tag{93}$$

**Lemma 4.3.** Let $k(u,v)$ be a Schwartz function such that $k(v,u)=-k(u,v)$. Then
$$\varphi_0\big(h\,k(\nabla_1,\nabla_2)(xx)\big)=-\tfrac12\,\varphi_0\big(\ell(\nabla)(x)\,x\big), \tag{97}$$
where
$$\ell(u)=u\,k(u,-u). \tag{98}$$

**Lemma 4.4.** One has
$$Q(x)=\varphi_0\big(K_2(\nabla)(x)\,x\big), \tag{103}$$
where
$$K_2(v)=\frac13+\frac{4}{v^2}-\frac{2\coth(v/2)}{v}. \tag{104}$$

**Remark 4.5.** $\triangle$ is isospectral to the Dolbeault Laplacian on $\mathrm T^2$, whose spectrum is the set of $|n+m\tau|^2$ for $n,m\in\mathbb Z$, $(n,m)\neq(0,0)$. Hence $\log\mathrm{Det}'\triangle$ is the same as for the ordinary 2-torus and is computed by the Kronecker limit formula, as in [20, Theorem 4.1]. Explicitly,
$$\log\mathrm{Det}'\triangle=-\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\sum_{(n,m)\neq(0,0)}|n+m\tau|^{-2s}=\log\big(4\pi^2|\eta(\tau)|^4\big),$$
where $\eta$ is the Dedekind eta function,
$$\eta(\tau)=e^{\frac{\pi i}{12}\tau}\prod_{n>0}\big(1-e^{2\pi i n\tau}\big).$$

**Theorem 4.6.** The functional $F(h)$ has the expression
$$F(h)=-\log\big(4\pi^2|\eta(\tau)|^4\big)+\frac{\pi}{4\tau_2}\varphi_0\Big(\big(K_2-\tfrac13\big)(\nabla_1)(\square_{\Re}(h))\Big). \tag{107}$$
One has $F(h)\ge F(0)$ for all $h$, and equality holds if and only if $h$ is a scalar.

**Remark 4.7.** The first term on the right-hand side of (108) [as printed] matches the expression of the functional in the commutative case (cf. [18, §3]). The second term is of a purely modular nature and is highly non-linear.

**Theorem 4.8.** The gradient of $F$ is given by
$$\mathrm{grad}_hF=\frac{\pi}{4\tau_2}\Big(\tilde K_0(\nabla)(\triangle(h))+\tfrac12\tilde H_0(\nabla_1,\nabla_2)(\square_{\Re}(h))\Big), \tag{114}$$
where
$$\tilde K_0(s)=4\frac{\sinh(s/2)}{s}K_0(s)=-2\Big(K_2(s)-\frac13\Big), \tag{115}$$
$$\tilde H_0(s,t)=4\frac{\sinh((s+t)/2)}{s+t}H_0(s,t). \tag{116}$$

**Remark 4.9.** Consider $\log\mathrm{Det}'(\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi)=-\zeta'_{\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi}(0)$. Start from $\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi=\delta^*k^2\delta$ and the one-parameter family
$$\triangle^{(0,1)}_\epsilon:=\delta^*e^{h+\varepsilon a}\delta,\qquad \epsilon\in\mathbb R.$$
Since
$$\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}e^{h+\varepsilon a}=\int_0^1e^{uh}\,a\,e^{(1-u)h}\,du,$$
one has
$$\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\triangle^{(0,1)}_\epsilon=\int_0^1\delta^\star e^{uh}\,a\,e^{(1-u)h}\,\delta\,du.$$
The Duhamel formula
$$\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}e^{-t\triangle^{(0,1)}_\epsilon}=-t\int_0^1e^{-vt\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi}\,\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\big(\triangle^{(0,1)}_\epsilon\big)\,e^{-(1-v)t\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi}\,dv$$
implies
$$\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathrm{Tr}\big(e^{-t\triangle^{(0,1)}_\epsilon}\big)=-\frac t2\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\big(e^{\frac{vh}{2}}ae^{-\frac{vh}{2}}\big)\big(e^{\frac h2}\delta e^{-t\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi}\delta^\star e^{\frac h2}\big)\Big)\,dv.$$
Moreover,
$$e^{\frac h2}\delta\,e^{-t\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi}\delta^\star\,e^{\frac h2}=e^{-t\triangle_\varphi}\,\triangle_\varphi.$$
This reverts to the expression (117), which involves the local formula for $\triangle_\varphi$ and not for $\triangle^{(0,1)}_\varphi$. Thus one recovers the same gradient expression (114).

**Theorem 4.10.** Let $G(u)$ be an even Schwartz function, and set
$$\Omega(h)=\varphi_0\big(G(\nabla_1)(\square_{\Re}(h))\big). \tag{127}$$
Then
$$\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Omega(h+\epsilon a)=-2\varphi_0\big(aG(\nabla)(\triangle(h))\big)+\varphi_0\big(a\,\omega_G(\nabla_1,\nabla_2)(\square_{\Re}(h))\big), \tag{128}$$
where $\omega_G$ is given by
$$\tfrac12\omega_G(s_1,s_2)=\frac{G(s_2)-G(s_1)}{s_1+s_2}+\frac{G(s_1+s_2)-G(s_2)}{s_1}-\frac{G(s_1+s_2)-G(s_1)}{s_2}. \tag{129}$$

**Lemma 4.11.** Let $G(u)$ be an even Schwartz function. For any $x,a\in A_\theta^\infty$,
$$\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\varphi_0\big(G(\log\Delta_{h+\varepsilon a})(x)\,x\big)=\varphi_0\big(a\,H(\nabla_1,\nabla_2)(xx)\big), \tag{130}$$
where
$$H(s_1,s_2)=2\frac{G(s_2)-G(s_1)}{s_1+s_2}. \tag{131}$$

**Lemma 4.12.** Let $G(u)$ be a Schwartz function. For any $a\in A_\theta^\infty$,
$$\varphi_0\big(G(\log\Delta_h)(\delta(h))\,\delta(a)\big)=\varphi_0\big(a\,L(\nabla_1,\nabla_2)(\delta(h)\delta(h))\big)-\varphi_0\big(a\,G(\log\Delta_h)\delta^2(h)\big), \tag{133}$$
where
$$L(s_1,s_2)=\frac{G(s_1+s_2)-G(s_2)}{s_1}-\frac{G(s_1+s_2)-G(s_1)}{s_2}. \tag{134}$$

## Section 5: Further remarks

**Proposition 5.1.** With the above notation, for $s\in\mathbb R$ the Ray–Singer determinant of $\triangle_{\varphi_s}$ is
$$\log\mathrm{Det}'(\triangle_{\varphi_s})=\log\big(4\pi^2|\eta(\tau)|^4\big)-\frac{\pi}{8\tau_2}\big(\alpha(p)+|\tau|^2\beta(p)\big)\,s^2\,\tilde K_0(s), \tag{141}$$
where
$$\alpha(p)=\frac1\pi\int_0^{2\pi}f_1(x)^2\,dx,\qquad \beta(p)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\big(f_0'(x)^2+2f_1'(x)^2\big)\,dx. \tag{142}$$

**Proposition 5.2.** Let $p=p^*=p^2$ be any projection, let $h=s\,p$ with $s\in\mathbb R$, and let $\varphi(x)=\varphi_0(xe^{-sp})$ be the associated conformal weight. The scalar curvature is
$$K_\varphi=\frac{\pi s}{4\tau_2}\Big(\tilde K_0(s)\triangle(p)+\frac s2\,\partial_s\tilde K_0(s)\big(p\,\triangle(p)\,p+(1-p)\,\triangle(p)\,(1-p)\big)\Big)e^{h}. \tag{149}$$

**Lemma 5.3.** For any $a\in A_\theta$,
$$\fint a\,\bar D_\varphi^{-2}=\frac{2\pi}{\tau_2}\varphi(a),\qquad \fint \gamma a\,\bar D_\varphi^{-2}=0. \tag{155}$$
(The integral sign with a minus is written $\fint$ here.)

**Remark 5.4.** In [10, Definition 1.147], the scalar curvature functional was introduced for spectral triples $(A,\mathcal H,D)$ of dimension 4 by
$$\mathcal R(a)=\fint a\,D^{-2},\qquad \forall a\in A. \tag{157}$$
The same formula with $D^{-(n-2)}$ in place of $D^{-2}$ works in dimension $n\neq2$, with a suitable normalization. For $n=2$, the normalization factor has a pole, and the analogue of (157) is
$$\mathcal R(a)=\mathrm a_2(a,D^2)=\zeta_{D^2}(a,0)+\mathrm{Tr}(Pa),\qquad \forall a\in A, \tag{158}$$
where $P$ is the orthogonal projection onto the kernel of $D$. In the even case, the general definition is refined to the chiral expression
$$\mathcal R_\gamma(a)=\fint E\,a\,D^{-2},\qquad E=\frac{1+\gamma}{2}.$$
