# Restatement

## Abstract

The paper proposes nonlocal operations as diagnostics of symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases protected by point group symmetries. These operations are "partial point group transformations": point group transformations restricted to a spatial subregion acting on a given ground-state wave function. The complex $U(1)$ phase of the ground-state expectation value of such a partial transformation is claimed to serve as an order parameter for these SPT phases, supported by analytical and numerical calculations. Examples include the $\mathbb{Z}_8$ and $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ invariants of topological superconductors protected by inversion symmetry in $(1+1)$ and $(3+1)$ dimensions, respectively, and the lens space topological invariants of $(2+1)$-dimensional fermionic topological phases.

## Section I: Introduction

### I.1 Topological phases with symmetries and TQFTs

Topological phases are gapped quantum phases that cannot be adiabatically connected to a trivial tensor-product state. A gapped phase that is trivial without symmetry but nontrivial once a set of symmetries is enforced is called a symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phase. Examples of fermionic SPT phases (topological insulators and superconductors) and bosonic SPT phases are cited as: Schnyder et al. 2008; Kitaev 2009; Ryu et al. 2010; Hasan and Kane 2010; Qi and Zhang 2011; Cho and Moore 2011; Fidkowski and Kitaev 2011; Gu and Wen 2014; Freed and Moore 2013; Wang et al. 2014; Kapustin et al. 2015; Hsieh et al. 2016; Witten 2016; Metlitski; Freed and Hopkins; Bhardwaj et al.; and Pollmann et al. 2010; Chen et al. 2011; Schuch et al. 2011; Chen et al. 2013; Lu and Vishwanath 2012; Levin and Gu 2012; Sule et al. 2013; Vishwanath and Senthil 2013; Kapustin a; Freed and Hopkins.

Topologically ordered phases, which are topologically distinct from a trivial state even without symmetry, are cited as: Wen and Niu 1990; Wen 2004; Moore and Read 1991; Kitaev 2003; Levin and Wen 2005; Kitaev 2006; Kong and Wen; Witten 1989; Turaev and Viro 1992; Dijkgraaf and Witten 1990; Propitius 1995. Symmetry-enriched topological phases are cited as: Wen 2002; Mesaros and Ran 2013; Essin and Hermele 2013; Hung and Wen 2013; Lu and Vishwanath 2016; Barkeshli et al.; Teo et al. 2015; Turaev; Turaev 2010.

Let $\widetilde{G}$ be the symmetry group, decomposed into unitary on-site symmetries $G$ and orientation-reversing symmetries (such as time-reversal and parity). If fermions are present, fermion parity is included in the latter part. The topological phases are coupled to a background $G$ gauge field, and the partition function is

$$
Z(X,\eta,A)=\int\prod_{i}D\phi_{i}\,e^{-S_{X}(\{\phi_{i}\},\eta,A)}. \tag{1.1}
$$

Here $X$ is a closed $(d+1)$-dimensional spacetime manifold, $\{\phi_i\}$ are the matter fields, $A$ is the background $G$ gauge field, and $\eta$ is a structure on $X$ (an orientation, a $\mathrm{Spin}$ structure, or a $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure). The effective action is $S_{\rm eff}(X,\eta,A)=-\ln Z(X,\eta,A)$. For topologically nontrivial gapped phases, $\mathrm{Im}\,S_{\rm eff}$ may contain a topological term that is insensitive to the metric and to small variations of $A$. The partition function is expected to define a $G$-equivariant TQFT (or $G$-equivariant Spin TQFT). Cited references: Turaev 2010; Moore and Segal; Kapustin and Turzillo; Gaiotto and Kapustin; Williamson and Wang 2017; Shiozaki and Ryu 2017; Kapustin et al. a; Bultinck et al. 2017; Kapustin et al. b.

For an SPT phase (unique ground state, "invertible" TQFT), the partition function depends only on the cobordism class of $(X,\eta,A)$ (Kapustin a). Two $(d+1)$-dimensional manifolds $X_1$ and $X_2$ with structures $\eta_{1,2}$ and fields $A_{1,2}$ are cobordant if there is a $(d+2)$-dimensional manifold $Y$ with an appropriate background gauge field such that $\partial Y = X_1 \sqcup X_2$. Disjoint union gives an Abelian group, the cobordism group $\Omega_{d+1}^{\rm str}(BG)$, where $BG$ is the classifying space of $G$ (Stong 2015; Gilkey 1989). For no symmetry, $BG = pt$. When the partition function is a pure phase, it defines a homomorphism

$$
Z:\Omega_{d+1}^{\rm str}(BG)\to U(1),\qquad (X,\eta,A)\mapsto Z(X,\eta,A). \tag{1.2}
$$

It was proposed that the torsion part $\mathrm{Tor}\,\Omega_{d+1}^{\rm str}(BG)$ classifies topological phases (Kapustin a; Kapustin et al. 2015; Freed and Hopkins).

### I.2 SPT invariants in terms of ground-state wave functions

The paper aims to construct topological invariants that take the same value throughout a gapped phase and can be computed within the operator formalism from a Hamiltonian or ground state. The invariants for fermionic SPT ground states, which are unique, are called SPT invariants. The approach is to simulate the path integral (1.1) for data $[X,\eta,A]$, with particular interest in the generator of $\Omega_{d+1}^{\rm str}(BG)$, called the generating manifold. Its $U(1)$ phase is the "least" topological phase, and phases for other manifolds are integer multiples of it.

The proposed setup consists of:

- (i) closed $d$-dimensional space manifolds $M$ on which the Hamiltonian is defined;
- (ii) orientation or spin structures $\eta_M$ on $M$;
- (iii) twisted boundary conditions (background flat gauge fields $A_M$) by on-site unitary symmetry $G$;
- (iv) symmetry operations $\hat g$ on the many-body Hilbert space;
- (v) the partial (point group) operation $\hat g_D$ on a subregion $D\subset M$.

Let $|GS(M,A_M,\eta_M)\rangle$ be the ground state of $H(\eta_M,A_M)$. Symmetry action gives phases $e^{i\Phi(M,\eta_M,A_M,g)}$ through

$$
\hat g\,|GS(M,\eta_M,A_M)\rangle = e^{i\Phi(M,\eta_M,A_M,g)}\,|GS(M,g(\eta_M),g(A_M))\rangle, \tag{1.3}
$$

where $g(\eta_M)$ and $g(A_M)$ are the mapped structure and boundary condition. When both states lie in the same Hilbert space,

$$
\langle GS(M,g(\eta_M),g(A_M))|\,\hat g\,|GS(M,\eta_M,A_M)\rangle = e^{i\Phi(M,\eta_M,A_M,g)}. \tag{1.4}
$$

The overlap (1.4) is interpreted as a spacetime path integral on the mapping torus

$$
X = M\times_f S^1 := M\times[0,1]/\big((x,0)\sim(f(x),1)\big), \tag{1.5}
$$

where $f:M\to M$ is the diffeomorphism induced by $\hat g$. Examples: $f=\mathrm{id}$, space inversion $f:x\mapsto -x$, or modular transformations on $M=T^2$. Modular actions on ground states have been studied in Zaletel et al. 2013; Hung and Wen 2014; He et al. 2014; Moradi and Wen 2015; Williamson et al. 2016; You and Cheng; Huang and Wei 2016.

### I.3 Partial point group operations and spacetime path integrals

For some symmetry classes, ingredients (i)–(iv) suffice (Resta 1998; Ryu and Hatsugai 2006; Pollmann and Turner 2012; Wen 2014; Zaletel 2014; Hung and Wen 2014; Williamson et al. 2016; Yonekura 2016). For example, $\Omega_2^{\rm Spin}(pt)=\mathbb{Z}_2$ is generated by $T^2$ with periodic boundary conditions in space and time (Moore and Segal; Kirby and Taylor 1990). For other classes, the generating manifold is not a mapping torus. For example, $\Omega_2^{\rm Pin^-}(pt)=\mathbb{Z}_8$ is generated by the real projective plane $\mathbb{R}P^2$ (Kirby and Taylor 1990; Kapustin et al. 2015). Since $\mathbb{R}P^2$ is not a mapping torus, ingredient (v), partial symmetry operations on a subregion $D$, is needed. Related partial-operation studies include Ryu and Hatsugai 2006; Pollmann and Turner 2012; Tu et al. 2013; Shapourian et al.; Shiozaki and Ryu 2017. Pollmann and Turner (2012) showed that the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant of the inversion-symmetric Haldane chain can be detected by partial inversion (Cen 2009), interpreted as a path integral on $\mathbb{R}P^2$ (Shiozaki and Ryu 2017). Tu et al. (2013) showed that partial lattice translation carries information on the central charge and topological spins of $(2+1)$-dimensional topologically ordered phases.

Consider a Hamiltonian invariant under $\widetilde G$, $\hat g H \hat g^{-1}=H$. The fermion operators transform as

$$
\hat g\,\psi_i^\dagger(\bm x)\,\hat g^{-1}=\psi_j^\dagger(g\cdot\bm x)\,U_{ji},\qquad \hat g|0\rangle_\psi=|0\rangle_\psi,\qquad g\in\widetilde G, \tag{1.6}
$$

where $\bm x\in M$, $i,j$ label internal degrees of freedom, and $|0\rangle_\psi$ is the Fock vacuum. A subregion $D\subset M$ closed under $\widetilde G$ satisfies $\bm x\in D \Leftrightarrow g\cdot\bm x\in D$ for all $g\in\widetilde G$. The partial transformation is

$$
\hat g_D\,\psi_i^\dagger(\bm x)\,\hat g_D^{-1}=\begin{cases}\psi_j^\dagger(g\cdot\bm x)\,U_{ji} & (\bm x\in D)\\ \psi_j^\dagger(\bm x) & (\bm x\notin D)\end{cases},\qquad \hat g_D|0\rangle_\psi=|0\rangle_\psi.
$$

The subregion $D$ is chosen with length scale much larger than the correlation length $\xi$. The invariant is $\langle GS|\hat g_D|GS\rangle$.

For a point group operation, $\langle GS|\hat g_D|GS\rangle$ corresponds to a path integral on a spacetime that generally is not a mapping torus. For partial reflection $\hat R_I$ on a segment $I$ of a $(1+1)$-dimensional system, the path-integral manifold is obtained by introducing a slit $[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\times[0,L]$ at $t=0$ and applying reflection on it. This is topologically equivalent to a cross-cap in the spacetime torus (Fig. 1). For $(1+1)$-dimensional topological superconductors protected by reflection symmetry (Shapourian et al.),

$$
\langle GS|\hat R_I|GS\rangle \longrightarrow \frac{e^{\pm\frac{\pi i}{4}}}{\sqrt2}\qquad (L,\,N-L\gg\xi),\qquad I=[0,L], \tag{1.9}
$$

where $I=[0,L]$ is an interval on a circle with $N$ sites. The phase $e^{\pm\pi i/4}$ reproduces the $\mathbb{Z}_8$ classification. The corresponding TQFT path integral on $\mathbb{R}P^2$ computes the $\mathbb{Z}_8$ Brown invariant of $\mathrm{Pin}^-$ manifolds (Kirby and Taylor 1990; Kapustin et al. 2015):

$$
Z(\mathbb{R}P^2,\eta_\pm)=e^{\pm\frac{\pi i}{4}}, \tag{1.10}
$$

where $\eta_\pm$ are the two $\mathrm{Pin}^-$ structures associated with the non-contractible loop. In the operator formalism, these correspond to the presence or absence of $(-1)^F$ in the partial reflection.

The spacetime manifold from the partial reflection (1.9) equals $\mathbb{R}P^2$ only up to a genus. For SPT phases (invertible TQFTs) this is not a problem. Precisely,

$$
\langle GS_a|\hat R_I|GS_a\rangle \sim Z\big((\mathbb{R}P^2,\eta_\pm)\#(S^1_a\times S^1_{ns})\big),\qquad a=ns,r, \tag{1.11}
$$

where $a$ specifies the boundary condition of $S^1$ ($r$/$ns$ for periodic/antiperiodic) and $X\#Y$ is the connected sum. Since $Z\big((\mathbb{R}P^2,\eta_\pm)\#(S^1_a\times S^1_{ns})\big)=Z(\mathbb{R}P^2,\eta_\pm)\,Z(S^1_a\times S^1_{ns})/Z(S^2)=Z(\mathbb{R}P^2,\eta_\pm)$, the result is $\langle GS_a|\hat R_I|GS_a\rangle\sim Z(\mathbb{R}P^2,\eta_\pm)$, i.e., the equivalence between (1.9) and (1.10). This equivalence holds only up to modulus, and $Z(S^1_a\times S^1_{ns})=1$ (see (II.1.1)). The argument also shows that the boundary condition is unimportant, and extends to partial point group transformations in any dimension for SPT phases.

For generic partial symmetry transformations, numerics and analysis give

$$
\langle GS|\hat g_D|GS\rangle=\exp\!\left[i\theta+\gamma-\alpha\,\frac{\mathrm{Area}(\partial D)}{\xi^{d-1}}+\cdots\right], \tag{1.12}
$$

as the leading contribution for $\mathrm{Area}(\partial D)/\xi^{d-1}\gg1$. A logarithmic term $\log[\mathrm{Area}(\partial D)/\xi^{d-1}]$ may also appear (see Eq. (5.31)). Here $\alpha$ is a dimensionless constant. The phase $\theta$ is quantized for sufficiently large $D$ and is a topological invariant. $\gamma$ is a scale-independent amplitude contribution, also topological. The phase $e^{i\theta}$ is expected to equal $Z(X,\eta,A)$.

The cobordism classification via TQFTs, in its original form, does not treat point group operations other than orientation-reversing ones. For orientation-reversing symmetries the partial transformations give generating manifolds. For orientation-preserving point group operations, the low-energy TQFTs are Lorentz invariant, so these operations can be implemented as on-site unitaries. For instance, $n$-fold rotations $C_n$ correspond to $\mathbb{Z}_n$ on-site symmetries, $G=\mathbb{Z}_n$ (see Song et al. 2017; Thorngren and Else). For non-chiral topological insulators in $(2+1)$d protected by $C_n$, the cobordism group is $\Omega_3^{\mathrm{Spin}^c}(B\mathbb{Z}_n)$ with generating manifold the lens space $L(n,1)$, which the partial $C_n$ rotations are claimed to simulate.

### I.4 Bulk–boundary correspondence and reduced density matrix

The area law for (1.12) is confirmed numerically in several examples and follows from bulk–boundary correspondence. The reduced density matrix is

$$
\rho_D=\mathrm{Tr}_{M\setminus D}\big[|GS\rangle\langle GS|\big]=\frac{e^{-H_E}}{\mathrm{Tr}_D\big[e^{-H_E}\big]}, \tag{1.13}
$$

where $H_E$ is the entanglement Hamiltonian. By bulk–boundary correspondence (Ryu and Hatsugai 2006; Li and Haldane 2008; Qi et al. 2012), in the low-energy subspace,

$$
H_E\sim\frac{\xi}{v}H_{\partial D}, \tag{1.14}
$$

where $H_{\partial D}$ describes the gapless boundary degrees of freedom, $\xi$ is the bulk correlation length, and $v$ is the velocity of the gapless excitation. The partial transformation $\hat g_D$ induces a symmetry $\hat g_{\partial D}$ on the boundary Hilbert space, and

$$
\langle GS|\hat g_D|GS\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}_D\big[\hat g_D\rho_D\big]\sim\frac{\mathrm{Tr}_{\partial D}\big[\hat g_{\partial D}e^{-\frac{\xi}{v}H_{\partial D}}\big]}{\mathrm{Tr}_{\partial D}\big[e^{-\frac{\xi}{v}H_{\partial D}}\big]}. \tag{1.15}
$$

The paper uses (1+1)-dimensional CFTs for $(2+1)$d SPT calculations and free-fermion boundary theories in higher dimensions to evaluate (1.12).

## I.5 Outline

The paper constructs and evaluates many-body SPT invariants for fermionic SPT phases with point group and unitary symmetries (Table 1). Partly overlapping results appeared in Shapourian et al. That earlier work treated $(1+1)$d and $(3+1)$d topological superconductors with orientation-reversing symmetries (inversion or time-reversal), and developed a path-integral representation via Suzuki–Trotter discretization of the thermal density matrix. The present work differs in focusing on point group symmetries and unitary symmetries in the operator formalism. Time-reversal requires partial transposition and is treated in the earlier work.

The sections are:

- **Sec. II**: topological invariants generated by mapping tori (no partial transformations needed).
- **Sec. III**: partial reflection in $(1+1)$d, showing that full symmetry transformations are insufficient for some classes, and that the partial reflection reproduces the $\mathbb{Z}_8$ invariant of class D superconductors with reflection symmetry.
- **Sec. IV**: partial $C_n$ rotations in $(2+1)$d, interpreted via modular properties of CFTs as lens-space TQFT partition functions, with the relation to $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ cobordism for on-site $\mathbb{Z}_n$.
- **Sec. V**: partial inversion in $(3+1)$d, constructing invariants for inversion-protected phases, plus the general formula for partial inversion in any even spacetime dimension using a boundary Dirac fermion on a sphere.
- **Sec. VI**: conclusions and outlook.

Four appendices contain technical details.
