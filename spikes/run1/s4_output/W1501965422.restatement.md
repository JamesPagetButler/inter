# Restatement

## Abstract (stated content)

- The article develops a theory of Grothendieck's six operations for derived categories in étale cohomology of Artin stacks, for both torsion and adic coefficients.
- It proves several properties of the operations, including the base change theorem in derived categories.
- The theory extends previous theories, including that of Laszlo and Olsson, where the operations are subject to more assumptions and the base change isomorphism is only constructed on the level of sheaves.
- The theory applies to higher Artin stacks.
- Perverse t-structures are defined on higher Artin stacks for general perversity, extending Gabber's work on schemes.
- The method uses stable $\infty$-categories (Lurie). Derived categories, functors and natural isomorphisms are enhanced to $\infty$-categories, and $\infty$-categorical (co)homological descent is introduced.
- A general technique for gluing subcategories of $\infty$-categories is developed, and categorical equivalences between simplicial sets associated to certain multisimplicial sets are obtained.
- One category-theoretical result generalises Deligne's gluing theory used in the construction of the extraordinary pushforward in étale cohomology of schemes.

## Introduction

The article combines three arXiv preprints: arXiv:1211.5294 (*Gluing restricted nerves of $\infty$-categories*), arXiv:1211.5948 (*Enhanced six operations and base change theorem for higher Artin stacks*) and arXiv:1404.1128 (*Enhanced adic formalism and perverse t-structures for higher Artin stacks*).

Previous theories of derived categories in étale cohomology on Artin stacks, and of six operations between them, were developed in [67] (Deligne–Mumford stacks), [50], [5], [57] and [47]. In [47] (Laszlo–Olsson), a technical condition on the base scheme excludes, for example, some spectra of fields.¹ In [47, §5], the base change isomorphism is constructed only on the level of usual cohomology sheaves. Previous works mostly treat only constructible sheaves.

¹ For example, the field $k(x_1,x_2,\dots)$ obtained by adjoining countably infinitely many variables to an algebraically closed field $k$ in which $\ell$ is invertible.

The enhancement used is the unbounded derived category viewed as a stable $\infty$-category in the sense of [53, Definition 1.1.1.9]. The six operations are first constructed for (coproducts of) quasi-compact separated schemes, and then extended to algebraic spaces, higher Deligne–Mumford stacks and higher Artin stacks by $\infty$-categorical descent.

### 0.1. Results for torsion coefficients

Results are stated for Artin stacks on the level of usual derived categories, which are the homotopy categories of the derived $\infty$-categories.

**Definitions.**
- An *algebraic space* is a sheaf in the big fppf site [3, 025Y] whose diagonal is representable (by schemes) and which admits an étale surjective map from a scheme (in $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}_{\mathcal{U}}$; see §0.7).
- An *Artin stack* is an algebraic stack in the sense of [3, 026O]: a stack in (1-)groupoids over $(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}_{\mathcal{U}})_{\mathrm{fppf}}$, whose diagonal is representable by algebraic spaces, and which admits a smooth surjective map from a scheme. Artin stacks are not assumed quasi-separated.

$\Lambda$ denotes a unital commutative ring, or more generally a ringed diagram (Definition 3.2.5). To an Artin stack $\mathcal{X}$ a triangulated category $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$ is associated.
- If $\mathcal{X}$ is Deligne–Mumford, $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$ is the unbounded derived category $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X}_{\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$ of $\Mod(\mathcal{X}_{\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$, the abelian category of $(\mathcal{X}_{\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$-modules, where $\mathcal{X}_{\acute{e}t}$ is the étale topos.
- In general, $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$ is equivalent to a full subcategory of $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$, the unbounded derived category of $(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$-modules, where $\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t}$ is the lisse-étale topos.

An $(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$-module $\mathscr{F}$ is equivalent to the following data:
- for each smooth $v\colon Y\to\mathcal{X}$ with $Y$ an algebraic space, an $(Y_{\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$-module $\mathscr{F}_v$;
- for each 2-commutative triangle with $v,v'$ smooth and $Y,Y'$ algebraic spaces, i.e. $v'\colon Y'\to\mathcal{X}$, $f\colon Y'\to Y$, $v\colon Y\to\mathcal{X}$, and $\sigma$ a 2-cell, a morphism $\tau_\sigma\colon f^*\mathscr{F}_v\to\mathscr{F}_{v'}$, which is an isomorphism if $f$ is étale;

such that $\{\tau_\sigma\}$ satisfies a natural cocycle condition [50, Lemme 12.2.1].

An $(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$-module $\mathscr{F}$ is *Cartesian* if all $\tau_\sigma$ are isomorphisms [50, Définition 12.3]. Let $\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{cart}}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$ be the full subcategory of $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$ spanned by complexes whose cohomology sheaves are all Cartesian. There is an equivalence
$$\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\simeq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{cart}}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda).$$

**Operations.** For a morphism $f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$ of Artin stacks, the following four operations are defined in §6.2:
$$f^*\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda),\qquad f_*\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda),$$
$$-\otimes_{\mathcal{X}}-\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\times\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda),$$
$$\mathcal{H}\mathrm{om}_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)^{op}\times\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda).$$
The pairs $(f^*,f_*)$ and $(-\otimes_{\mathcal{X}}\mathsf{K},\mathcal{H}\mathrm{om}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathsf{K},-))$, for every $\mathsf{K}\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{cart}}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$, are pairs of adjoint functors.

Fix a nonempty set $\Box$ of rational primes.
- A ring is *$\Box$-torsion* [2, Exposé IX, Définition 1.1] if each element is killed by an integer that is a product of primes in $\Box$.
- An Artin stack $\mathcal{X}$ is *$\Box$-coprime* if there exists a morphism $\mathcal{X}\to\Spec\mathbb{Z}[\Box^{-1}]$.

If $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$ are $\Box$-coprime (resp. Deligne–Mumford), $f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$ is locally of finite type, and $\Lambda$ is $\Box$-torsion (resp. torsion), there is a further pair of adjoint functors
$$f_!\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda),\qquad f^!\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda).$$

**Theorem 0.1.1 (Künneth Formula, Theorem 6.2.1).** Let $\Lambda$ be a $\Box$-torsion (resp. torsion) ring. Let
$$\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{Y}_1\times_{\mathcal{X}_1}\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{X}_2}\mathcal{Y}_2$$
be a diagram of $\Box$-coprime Artin stacks (resp. of arbitrary Deligne–Mumford stacks), with projections $q_1,q_2,p_1,p_2$, morphisms $f_1\colon\mathcal{Y}_1\to\mathcal{X}_1$, $f_2\colon\mathcal{Y}_2\to\mathcal{X}_2$, $f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$, where $f_1$ and $f_2$ are locally of finite type. Then there is a natural isomorphism of functors
$$f_!(q_1^*-\otimes_{\mathcal{Y}}q_2^*-)\simeq(p_1^*f_{1!}-)\otimes_{\mathcal{X}}(p_2^*f_{2!}-)\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y}_1,\Lambda)\times\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y}_2,\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda).$$

**Corollary 0.1.2 (Base Change).** Let $\Lambda$ be a $\Box$-torsion (resp. torsion) ring. Let $\mathcal{W}\xrightarrow{q}\mathcal{Y}$, $\mathcal{W}\xrightarrow{g}\mathcal{Z}$, $\mathcal{Z}\xrightarrow{p}\mathcal{X}$, $\mathcal{Y}\xrightarrow{f}\mathcal{X}$ be a Cartesian diagram of $\Box$-coprime Artin stacks (resp. of arbitrary Deligne–Mumford stacks), where $p$ is locally of finite type. Then there is a natural isomorphism of functors
$$f^*\circ p_!\simeq q_!\circ g^*\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Z},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda).$$

**Corollary 0.1.3 (Projection Formula).** Let $\Lambda$ be a $\Box$-torsion (resp. torsion) ring, and $f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$ a morphism locally of finite type of $\Box$-coprime Artin stacks (resp. of arbitrary Deligne–Mumford stacks). Then there is a natural isomorphism of functors
$$f_!(-\otimes_{\mathcal{Y}}f^*-)\simeq(f_!-)\otimes_{\mathcal{X}}-\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda)\times\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda).$$

**Theorem 0.1.4 (Trace map and Poincaré duality, Theorem 6.2.9).** Let $\Lambda$ be a $\Box$-torsion ring, and $f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$ a flat morphism locally of finite presentation of $\Box$-coprime Artin stacks. Then:

1. There is a functorial trace map
$$\Tr_f\colon\tau^{\geqslant 0}f_!\Lambda_{\mathcal{Y}}\langle d\rangle=\tau^{\geqslant 0}f_!(f^*\Lambda_{\mathcal{X}})\langle d\rangle\to\Lambda_{\mathcal{X}},$$
where $d$ is an integer greater than or equal to the dimension of every geometric fiber of $f$; $\Lambda_{\mathcal{X}}$ and $\Lambda_{\mathcal{Y}}$ denote constant sheaves placed in degree $0$; and $\langle d\rangle=[2d](d)$ is the composition of the shift by $2d$ and the $d$-th power of Tate's twist.

2. If $f$ is moreover smooth, the induced natural transformation $u_f\colon f_!\circ f^*\langle\dim f\rangle\to\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}}$ is a counit transformation, where $\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}}$ is the identity functor of $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$. In other words, there is a natural isomorphism of functors
$$f^*\langle\dim f\rangle\simeq f^!\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda).$$

**Corollary 0.1.5 (Smooth Base Change, Corollary 6.2.10).** Let $\Lambda$ be a $\Box$-torsion ring, and let $\mathcal{W}\xrightarrow{q}\mathcal{Y}$, $\mathcal{W}\xrightarrow{g}\mathcal{Z}$, $\mathcal{Z}\xrightarrow{p}\mathcal{X}$, $\mathcal{Y}\xrightarrow{f}\mathcal{X}$ be a Cartesian diagram of $\Box$-coprime Artin stacks where $p$ is smooth. Then the natural transformation of functors
$$p^*f_*\to g_*q^*\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Z},\Lambda)$$
is a natural isomorphism.

**Theorem 0.1.6 (Descent, Corollary 6.2.14).** Let $\Lambda$ be a ring, $f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$ a morphism of Artin stacks, and $y\colon\mathcal{Y}^+_0\to\mathcal{Y}$ a smooth surjective morphism. Let $\mathcal{Y}^+_\bullet$ be the Čech nerve of $y$, with $y_n\colon\mathcal{Y}^+_n\to\mathcal{Y}^+_{-1}=\mathcal{Y}$, and set $f_n=f\circ y_n\colon\mathcal{Y}^+_n\to\mathcal{X}$.

1. For every complex $\mathsf{K}\in\mathrm{D}^{\geqslant 0}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda)$ there is a convergent spectral sequence
$$\mathrm{E}_1^{p,q}=\mathrm{H}^q(f_{p*}y_p^*\mathsf{K})\Rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{p+q}f_*\mathsf{K}.$$

2. If $\mathcal{X}$ is $\Box$-coprime, $\Lambda$ is $\Box$-torsion, and $f$ is locally of finite type, then for every complex $\mathsf{K}\in\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda)$ there is a convergent spectral sequence
$$\tilde{\mathrm{E}}_1^{p,q}=\mathrm{H}^q(f_{-p!}y_{-p}^!\mathsf{K})\Rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{p+q}f_!\mathsf{K}.$$

**Remark 0.1.7.** Even in the case of schemes, Theorem 0.1.6 (2) seems to be a new result.

**Constructible sheaves.** Work over a $\Box$-coprime base scheme $\mathbb{S}$ that is either quasi-excellent finite-dimensional or regular of dimension $\leqslant 1$. Consider only Artin stacks $\mathcal{X}$ locally of finite type over $\mathbb{S}$. Let $\Lambda$ be a Noetherian $\Box$-torsion ring. Let $\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\subseteq\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$ be the full subcategory of objects whose pullback to every scheme $X$ of finite type over $\mathbb{S}$ has constructible cohomology sheaves in the usual sense. Let $\mathrm{D}^{(+)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$ (resp. $\mathrm{D}^{(-)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$) be the full subcategory spanned by complexes whose cohomology sheaves are locally bounded from below (resp. from above).

The six operations restrict to the following (Propositions 6.4.4 and 6.4.5):
$$f^*\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda),\qquad f^!\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda),$$
$$-\otimes_{\mathcal{X}}-\colon\mathrm{D}^{(-)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\times\mathrm{D}^{(-)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}^{(-)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda),$$
$$\mathcal{H}\mathrm{om}_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\mathrm{D}^{(-)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)^{op}\times\mathrm{D}^{(+)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}^{(+)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda).$$
If $f$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, there are also
$$f_*\colon\mathrm{D}^{(+)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}^{(+)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda),\qquad f_!\colon\mathrm{D}^{(-)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda)\to\mathrm{D}^{(-)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda).$$

When $\mathbb{S}$, $\Lambda$ and $f$ lie in the range of [47], the operations for constructible complexes are compatible with those of Laszlo and Olsson on usual derived categories. In particular, Corollary 0.1.2 implies that their operations satisfy base change in derived categories, which was left open in [47].

### 0.2. Why $\infty$-categories?

An $\infty$-category is a simplicial set with the right lifting property with respect to inner horn inclusions [54, Definition 1.1.2.4]. These are models for $(\infty,1)$-categories, i.e. higher categories whose $n$-morphisms are invertible for $n\geqslant 2$. The references are to Joyal [44], [43] (quasi-categories) and Lurie [54].

Let $u\colon X\to\mathcal{X}$ be an atlas (smooth surjective, $X$ an algebraic space), and $p_\alpha\colon X\times_{\mathcal{X}}X\to X$ ($\alpha=1,2$) the projections. For $\mathscr{F}\in\Mod_{\mathrm{cart}}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$ there is a natural isomorphism $\sigma\colon p_1^*u^*\mathscr{F}\xrightarrow{\sim}p_2^*u^*\mathscr{F}$ satisfying a cocycle condition. Conversely, any $\mathscr{G}\in\Mod(X_{\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$ with an isomorphism $\sigma\colon p_1^*\mathscr{G}\xrightarrow{\sim}p_2^*\mathscr{G}$ satisfying the same cocycle condition is isomorphic to $u^*\mathscr{F}$ for some $\mathscr{F}\in\Mod_{\mathrm{cart}}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$.

Formally, $\Mod_{\mathrm{cart}}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{lis}\text{-}\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$ is the 2-limit, in the 2-category of abelian categories, of the diagram
$$\Mod(X_{\acute{e}t},\Lambda)\rightrightarrows\Mod((X\times_{\mathcal{X}}X)_{\acute{e}t},\Lambda)\cdots\Mod((X\times_{\mathcal{X}}X\times_{\mathcal{X}}X)_{\acute{e}t},\Lambda),$$
built from $p_1^*,p_2^*$. Such 2-coherence is no more complicated than in Grothendieck's descent theory [34].

The naive descent property fails for derived categories. Otherwise the classifying stack $\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_m$ over an algebraically closed field would have finite cohomological dimension, which is false. When forming derived categories, too much information about the coherence of homotopy equivalences or quasi-isomorphisms is lost.

Cohomological descent in the sense of Deligne [2, Exposé Vbis] exists partially on the level of objects, and is used in [57] and [47]. It has two restrictions:
- Deligne's cohomological descent applies only to complexes bounded from below. A version for unbounded complexes in [47] requires further finiteness conditions and restriction to constructible complexes.
- Cohomological descent cannot be used directly to define the $!$-pushforward.

Writing $X_n=X\times_{\mathcal{X}}\dots\times_{\mathcal{X}}X$ ($(n+1)$-fold), $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$ is defined as the limit of the cosimplicial diagram
$$\mathcal{D}(X_{0,\acute{e}t},\Lambda)\overset{p_1^*,\,p_2^*}{\rightrightarrows}\mathcal{D}(X_{1,\acute{e}t},\Lambda)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{D}(X_{2,\acute{e}t},\Lambda)\cdots$$
in a suitable $\infty$-category of presentable stable $\infty$-categories (see Proposition 5.3.5). Here $\mathcal{D}(X_{n,\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$ is the derived $\infty$-category of the Grothendieck abelian category $\Mod(X_{n,\acute{e}t},\Lambda)$. Then $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$ is the homotopy category of $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$.

The diagram above does not record all higher cells (natural equivalences of functors, equivalences between them, and so on). The limit encodes an infinite hierarchy of homotopy equivalences, and the homotopy-coherence problem is the central difficulty. The approach uses the (marked) straightening/unstraightening construction, the Adjoint Functor Theorem, and the $\infty$-categorical Barr–Beck Theorem of Lurie [54], [53]. Further $\infty$-categorical techniques include:
- a coherent decomposition of morphisms (§1.4);
- gluing data from Cartesian diagrams to general ones (§1.5);
- partial adjoints along given directions (§2.2);
- a coherent choice of descent data (§4.2).

Lurie's theory is also used in [7] to study quasi-coherent sheaves on certain derived stacks.

### 0.3. Results for adic coefficients

This section extends §0.1 to the adic formalism. It extends SGA 5 [33], Deligne [17], Ekedahl [18] (for schemes), Behrend [5] and Laszlo–Olsson [48]. Base change in derived categories is proved, which was previously known only on the level of sheaves [48]. The constructibility assumption is dropped; the adic formalism applies to unrestricted derived categories. Results are stated on the level of usual derived categories; Chapter 7 gives complete results for higher Artin stacks and higher Deligne–Mumford stacks.

**Ringed diagrams.** Let $\mathcal{X}$ be an Artin stack and $\lambda=(\Xi,\Lambda)$ a ringed diagram: a functor $\Lambda$ from the opposite of a partially ordered set $\Xi$ to the category of unital commutative rings. A typical example is the projective system
$$\dots\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}\to\dots\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z},$$
with $\ell$ a fixed prime and transition maps the natural projections.

For each $\xi\in\Xi$, $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda(\xi))$ has an $\infty$-categorical enhancement $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda(\xi))$. There is a functor $\mathrm{N}(\Xi)^{op}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_\infty$ sending $\xi$ to $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda(\xi))$, with transition functors given by (derived) extension of scalars. Define
$$\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}:=\varprojlim_{\mathrm{N}(\Xi)^{op}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda(\xi)),$$
and let $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}$ be its homotopy category. The limit must be taken at the level of $\infty$-categories.

For $f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$ a morphism of Artin stacks, the operations are
$$f^{*\mathrm{a}}\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}},\qquad f_{*\mathrm{a}}\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}},$$
$$-\overset{\mathrm{a}}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{X}}-\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}\times\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}},$$
$$\mathcal{H}\mathrm{om}^{\mathrm{a}}_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}^{op}\times\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}.$$
The pairs $(f^{*\mathrm{a}},f_{*\mathrm{a}})$ and $(-\overset{\mathrm{a}}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathsf{K},\mathcal{H}\mathrm{om}^{\mathrm{a}}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathsf{K},-))$, for every $\mathsf{K}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}$, are pairs of adjoint functors.

If $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$ are $\Box$-coprime, $f$ is locally of finite type, and $\lambda$ is a $\Box$-torsion ringed diagram, there is a further pair of adjoint functors
$$f_{!\mathrm{a}}\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}},\qquad f^{!\mathrm{a}}\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{Y},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}.$$

These six operations satisfy the same properties as in §0.1. The category $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}$ is canonically equivalent to the full subcategory of $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)$ spanned by *adic complexes*. There is a colocalization functor $\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)\to\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\lambda)_{\mathrm{a}}$. Then:
- $f^{*\mathrm{a}}$, $f_{!\mathrm{a}}$ and $-\overset{\mathrm{a}}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{X}}-$ are restrictions of $f^*$, $f_!$ and $-\otimes_{\mathcal{X}}-$, since they preserve adic complexes;
- $f_{*\mathrm{a}}=\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ f_*$;
- $f^{!\mathrm{a}}=\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ f^!$;
- $\mathcal{H}\mathrm{om}^{\mathrm{a}}_{\mathcal{X}}=\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{H}\mathrm{om}_{\mathcal{X}}$.

See §7.2 and §7.3. The adic formalism does not assume constructibility; adic complexes are defined for any sheaves. This makes sense of divergent integrals on stacks over finite fields, as in [22].

**The $\mathfrak{m}$-adic formalism (§7.6).** Let $\Lambda$ be a ring and $\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\Lambda$ a principal ideal generated by a nonzerodivisor. The pair $(\Lambda,\mathfrak{m})$ gives a ringed diagram $\Lambda_\bullet$ over $\mathbb{N}=\{0\to1\to2\to\cdots\}$ with $\Lambda_n=\Lambda/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$. In this case the adic complexes are stable under the six operations. §7.7 shows that the constructible adic formalism coincides with Laszlo–Olsson [49] under their assumptions.

### 0.4. What do we need to enhance? (truncated in source)

Section 0.2 mentions the enhancement $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$ of the triangulated category $\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)$: a stable $\infty$-category whose homotopy category is naturally equivalent to $\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)$. For $f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$, the enhanced pullback is an exact functor
$$f^*\colon\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\Lambda)\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Y},\Lambda),\tag{0.1}$$
whose induced functor $\mathrm{h}f^*$ on homotopy categories is the usual $*$-pullback.

Descent requires putting all schemes and then algebraic spaces together. Let $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{qc.sep}}$ be the category of coproducts of quasi-compact separated schemes. The enhanced pullback for schemes is a functor
$$\prescript{\Lambda}{\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{qc.sep}}}{\mathrm{EO}}^*\colon\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{qc.sep}})^{op}\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathrm{st}},\tag{0.2}$$
where $\mathrm{N}$ is the nerve functor [54, Definition 1.1.2.1] and $\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathrm{st}}$ is a certain $\infty$-category of presentable stable $\infty$-categories. (0.1) is the image of the edge $f$ when $f$ lies in $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{qc.sep}}$. The construction of (0.2), and of its right adjoint (the enhanced $*$-pushforward), follows from the general construction in [53].

The enhancement of $!$-pushforward is harder. One needs a functor
$$\prescript{\Lambda}{\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{qc.sep}}}{\mathrm{EO}}_!\colon\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{qc.sep}})_F\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathrm{st}},$$
where $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{qc.sep}})_F$ is the subcategory allowing only morphisms locally of finite type. The basic idea uses the Nagata compactification theorem. For a morphism $f\colon Y\to X$ in $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{qc.sep}}$, locally of finite type, one chooses a relative compactification
$$Y\xrightarrow{i}\overline{Y}\xrightarrow{\overline{f}}\textstyle\coprod_I X\xrightarrow{p}X,$$
where $i$ is an open immersion (the source ends here).
