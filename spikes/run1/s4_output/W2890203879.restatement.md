# Restatement

## Abstract

We compute the information-theoretic von Neumann entropy of the state associated to the fermionic second quantization of a spectral triple. We show that this entropy is given by the spectral action of the spectral triple for a specific universal function. The function is related to the Riemann zeta function through the coefficients $c(d)$, by which it multiplies the $d$-dimensional terms in the heat expansion of the spectral triple. The coefficient $c(d)$ is the product of the Riemann xi function evaluated at $-d$, namely $\xi(-d)$, by an elementary expression. In particular, $c(4)$ is a rational multiple of $\zeta(5)$, and $c(2)$ is a rational multiple of $\zeta(3)$. The functional equation gives a duality between the coefficients in positive dimension, which govern the high-energy expansion, and the coefficients in negative dimension, exchanging even dimension with odd dimension.

## 1. Introduction

The spectral action principle [4] has so far been developed for arbitrary test functions of the form $\chi(D^2/\Lambda^2)$ of a spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$. For a fixed choice of $\chi$, the spectral action $\mathrm{Tr}(\chi(D^2/\Lambda^2))$ is an additive functional of the spectral triple: when evaluated on the direct sum of two spectral triples, it behaves additively.

Given $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$, one performs the fermionic second quantization using the Clifford algebra of the underlying real Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}$. One then evaluates the von Neumann information-theoretic entropy of the unique state satisfying the KMS condition at inverse temperature $\beta$, with respect to the time evolution of the Clifford algebra induced by $D$. Second quantization transforms direct sums into tensor products, and the von Neumann entropy of a tensor product of two states is the sum of the respective entropies. Hence this construction gives an additive functional of spectral triples, denoted $S(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)$ and called the entropy of the spectral triple.

In §2.1 the KMS condition is recalled, and in §2.2 the fermionic second quantization is recalled. In §3 it is shown that the entropy functional is the spectral action for the specific universal test function $\chi(x)=h(\sqrt{x})$. The main result, explored in §4, is the relation between the function $h$ and the Riemann zeta function.

## 2. Preliminaries

**Definition 2.1.** Let $(\mathcal{C},\sigma_t)$ be a $C^*$-dynamical system. For a given $0<\beta<\infty$, a state $\varphi$ on the unital $C^*$-algebra $\mathcal{C}$ satisfies the KMS condition at inverse temperature $\beta$ if for all $a,b\in\mathcal{C}$ there exists a function $F_{a,b}(z)$ which is holomorphic on the strip
$$I_\beta=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid 0<\Im(z)<\beta\},\tag{1}$$
continuous on the boundary $\partial I_\beta$ and bounded, with the property that for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$
$$F_{a,b}(t)=\varphi(a\sigma_t(b))\quad\text{and}\quad F_{a,b}(t+i\beta)=\varphi(\sigma_t(b)a).\tag{2}$$

**Proposition 2.2.** Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a complex Hilbert space and $D$ a self-adjoint operator in $\mathcal{H}$ with compact resolvent. Let $C:=\mathrm{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})$ be the complexified Clifford algebra of the underlying real Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}$, and let $\sigma_t\in\mathrm{Aut}(C)$ be the one-parameter group of automorphisms associated to $\exp(itD)\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})$. Then for any $\beta>0$ there exists a unique KMS$_\beta$ state $\psi_\beta$ on the $C^*$-dynamical system $(C,\sigma_t)$.

**Proposition 2.3.** Let $\mathcal{H}$, $D$, $C:=\mathrm{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})$, $\sigma_t\in\mathrm{Aut}(C)$ and $\psi_\beta$ be as in Proposition 2.2. If the operator $\exp(-\beta|D|)$ is of trace class, then the state $\psi_\beta$ is of type I, and the associated irreducible representation is given by the fermionic second quantization associated to the complex structure $I:=i\,\mathrm{sign}\,D$ on $\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}$.

**Lemma 2.4.** The representation $\gamma_I$ of the complexified Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)$ on the Fock space $\mathcal{L}(\exterior V_I)$ described above is irreducible.

**Remark 2.5.** The Shale–Stinespring Theorem [9] states that the automorphism of $\mathrm{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)$ defined by an orthogonal operator $T:V\to V$ is implementable by a unitary operator on the Fock space $\exterior V_I$ if and only if $T+ITI$ is Hilbert–Schmidt.

**Proposition 2.6.**
(i) The one-parameter group $\sigma_t\in\mathrm{Aut}(C)$ is implemented in the (physical) Fock representation by the one-parameter unitary group $W(t)=\exterior\exp(it|D|)$, i.e.
$$\gamma_I(\sigma_t(A))=\exterior(e^{it|D|})\,\gamma_I(A)\,\exterior(e^{-it|D|}),\quad \forall A\in\mathrm{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(V).\tag{4}$$
(ii) If $\exp(-\beta|D|)$ is of trace class, the state $\psi_\beta$ is of type I and is given by
$$\psi_\beta(A)=\frac{1}{Z}\,\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\exterior\exp(-\beta|D|)\,\gamma_I(A)\right),\quad \forall A\in\mathrm{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(V),\tag{5}$$
where the normalization factor $Z$ is finite.

## 3. Entropy as a spectral action

**Lemma 3.1.** Let $x>0$. The entropy of the partition of the unit interval into two intervals with ratio of sizes $x$ is given by
$$\mathcal{E}(x):=\log(x+1)-\frac{x\log(x)}{x+1}.$$

**Corollary 3.2.** One has $\mathcal{E}(x)=\mathcal{E}(1/x)$ for any $x>0$.

**Lemma 3.3.** Let $T\in\mathcal{L}^1(\mathcal{H})^+$ be a positive trace-class operator, and let $\phi$ be the state associated to $\exterior T$. Then
$$S(\phi)=\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{E}(T)).\tag{6}$$

**Theorem 3.4.** Let $\mathcal{H}$, $D$, $C:=\mathrm{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}})$, $\sigma_t\in\mathrm{Aut}(C)$ and $\psi_\beta$ be as in Proposition 2.2. If the operator $\exp(-\beta|D|)$ is of trace class, then the state $\psi_\beta$ is of type I, and its von Neumann entropy is equal to the spectral action $\mathrm{Tr}(h(\beta D))$ for the spectral function
$$h(x):=\mathcal{E}(e^{-x}).$$

## 4. The function $h$ and the Riemann zeta function

**Lemma 4.1.** The function $h(x)$ is an even positive function of the variable $x\in\mathbb{R}$. Its derivative is
$$h'(x)=-\frac{x}{4\cosh^{2}\!\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}.$$

**Lemma 4.2.** For $x\geq 0$,
$$\sum_{\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(2\pi n+\pi)^{2}-x}{\left((2\pi n+\pi)^{2}+x\right)^{2}}=\frac{1}{4\cosh^{2}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{2}\right)}.$$

**Lemma 4.3.** One has
$$g(t)=\frac{-1}{4\sqrt{\pi}\,t^{3/2}}\sum_{\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}n^{2}q^{n^{2}},\qquad q=e^{-\frac{1}{4t}}.\tag{9}$$

**Proposition 4.4.** One has
$$h(x)=\int_0^\infty e^{-tx^{2}}\,\tilde g(t)\,dt.\tag{10}$$

**Lemma 4.5.**
(i) One has
$$2\int_0^\infty h(x)\,x\,dx=\frac{9\,\zeta(3)}{2},\qquad 2\int_0^\infty h(x)\,x^{3}\,dx=\frac{225\,\zeta(5)}{4}.\tag{11}$$
(ii) More generally,
$$\int_0^\infty h(x)\,x^{\alpha}\,dx=\frac{1-2^{-\alpha-1}}{\alpha+1}\,\Gamma(\alpha+3)\,\zeta(\alpha+2).\tag{12}$$

**Lemma 4.6.** The coefficient of $t^{a}$ in the heat expansion is
$$\gamma(a)=\frac{1-2^{-2a}}{a}\,\pi^{-a}\,\xi(2a).$$
