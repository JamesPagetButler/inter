# Restatement

## Abstract

We show that the algebra $\mathcal{A}$ of a commutative unital spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ satisfying several additional conditions, slightly stronger than those proposed by Connes, is the algebra of smooth functions on a compact spin manifold.

## Introduction

Noncommutative Geometry, as developed over the past several years by Connes and coworkers, has produced many examples of "noncommutative spaces" [22]. To find a common framework for those examples, Connes proposed in [21] an axiomatic framework for "noncommutative spin manifolds". The geometry is carried by a spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$; the Riemannian spin geometry is recovered when $\mathcal{A}$ is a coordinate algebra of smooth functions on a manifold, $\mathcal{H}$ a Hilbert space of spinors, and $\mathcal{D}$ the Dirac operator determined by the spin structure and Riemannian metric. The question of reconstruction is whether the framework proposed by Connes, or some variation of it, suffices to determine this spin manifold structure whenever $\mathcal{A}$ is commutative. In [21] Connes expressed the hope that it could be so. In [50] a first attempt was presented, which was subsequently shown to fall short [28]. A detailed description of the reconstruction of many geometric features of a spin manifold was presented in [30], where the starting algebra $\mathcal{A}$ was assumed a priori to be the smooth functions on a compact manifold.

In this paper, using a slightly stronger set of conditions on a spectral triple, we show that from the further assumption of a commutative coordinate algebra $\mathcal{A}$ one can recover a compact boundaryless manifold whose smooth functions coincide with $\mathcal{A}$.

Poincaré duality in $KK$-theory was a key theme of Connes' axioms. Earlier results of Sullivan [58] indicate that in high dimensions, in the absence of 2-torsion and in the simply connected setting, Poincaré duality in $KO$-theory characterizes the homotopy type of a compact manifold. We do not use this approach for the reconstruction. Instead we use the formulation of Poincaré duality in noncommutative geometry phrased at the level of Hochschild chains, namely the "closedness condition" [18, VI.4.$\gamma$]. Poincaré duality in $KK$-theory plays no role in the reconstruction of a compact space $X$ with charts and smooth transition functions. It is needed afterwards to show that $X$ carries a $spin^c$ structure and to identify the class of $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ as the fundamental class of the $spin^c$ manifold, using Plymen's characterization of $spin^c$ structures [48] as Morita equivalence bimodules for the Clifford action induced by the metric.

Compactness of the manifold, equivalently the unitality of the coordinate algebra, is an essential technical feature of the proof. The reconstruction of noncompact manifolds is suggested under alternative conditions along the lines of [27, 51].

The proof that the Gelfand spectrum $X=\operatorname{sp}(\mathcal{A})$ is a differentiable manifold is divided into two steps. The first constructs a vector bundle over $X$ playing the role of the cotangent bundle; this uses all conditions except Poincaré duality in $KK$-theory and the metric condition. The generating sections are the 1-forms $[\mathcal{D},a^{j}_{\alpha}]$, for $j=1,\dots,p$ and $\alpha=1,\dots,n$, given by the orientability condition. The maps $a_{\alpha}=(a^{1}_{\alpha},\dots,a^{p}_{\alpha}):X\to\mathbb{R}^{p}$ are shown to provide coordinates on suitable open subsets of $X$, by proving that $a_{\alpha}$ is locally one-to-one and open. The tools are a Lipschitz functional calculus, measure-theoretic results of Voiculescu [61], basic point-set topology, and unique continuation properties for Dirac-type operators [5, 36]. The main tools are a multivariate $C^\infty$ functional calculus for regular spectral triples [51], and a Lipschitz functional calculus.

The plan of the paper is as follows. Section 2 gives background, including the $C^\infty$ functional calculus. Section 3 introduces the conditions on a spectral triple. Section 4 constructs the cotangent bundle. Section 5 develops a Lipschitz functional calculus. Sections 6 and 7 prove that $X$ is a manifold: the main issue is the absence of branch points in chart domains. The algebra generated by $\mathcal{A}$ and $[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{A}]$ is locally a direct sum of Clifford actions from one or several Riemannian metrics, for which $\mathcal{D}$ is locally a direct sum of Dirac-type operators; unique continuation of Dirac-type operators removes branch points. The Clifford actions are then assembled globally into the Clifford action of a single Riemannian metric. Section 8 shows how the spin$^c$ structure arises from Poincaré duality in $KK$-theory; the Dirac operator differs from $\mathcal{D}$ by at most an endomorphism of the spinor bundle. Section 9 collects remarks on the postulates and their variants. Appendix A establishes results on Hermitian pairings on finite projective modules. Appendix B examines further properties of the conditions, including the redundancy of the metric condition.

## Definitions, Remarks, Propositions, Lemmas and Conditions

**Definition 2.1.** A spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ consists of:
1. A faithful representation $\pi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ of a unital $*$-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ by bounded operators on a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$; and
2. A selfadjoint operator $\mathcal{D}$ on $\mathcal{H}$ with dense domain $\operatorname{Dom}\mathcal{D}$, such that for each $a\in\mathcal{A}$, $[\mathcal{D},\pi(a)]$ extends to a bounded operator on $\mathcal{H}$ and $\pi(a)(1+\mathcal{D}^{2})^{-1/2}$ is compact.

The spectral triple is *even* if there is $\Gamma=\Gamma^*\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ with $\Gamma^2=1$, such that $[\Gamma,\pi(a)]=0$ for all $a\in\mathcal{A}$ and $\Gamma\mathcal{D}+\mathcal{D}\Gamma=0$. If no such grading is available, the spectral triple is called *odd*.

**Remark 2.1.** Since $\mathcal{A}$ is faithfully represented on $\mathcal{H}$, we may omit $\pi$ and regard $\mathcal{A}$ as a subalgebra of $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$. Its norm closure $\overline{\mathcal{A}}=A$ is a $C^*$-algebra.

**Remark 2.2.** In this paper $\mathcal{A}$ is always assumed unital. Nonunital spectral triples were studied in [51, 52] under the assumption that $\mathcal{A}$ has a dense ideal with local units. Nonunital spectral triples arising from Moyal products were analysed in [27] (and anticipated in [29]). The Moyal example shows that a certain unitization of $\mathcal{A}$ must be treated as part of the data of a nonunital spectral triple, so it is proper to focus first on the unital case.

**Definition 2.2.** The operator $\mathcal{D}$ gives rise to two commuting derivations on $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$:
$$\underline{\mathrm{d}}x:=[\mathcal{D},x],\qquad \delta x:=[|\mathcal{D}|,x],\qquad x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).$$
By Definition 2.1, $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\operatorname{Dom}\underline{\mathrm{d}}:=\{x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):[\mathcal{D},x]\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\}$. A spectral triple is called $QC^\infty$ if
$$\mathcal{A}\cup\underline{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{A}\subseteq\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Dom}\delta^{m}.$$

**Remark 2.3.** The terminology $QC^\infty$ was introduced in [10] to distinguish "quantum" differentiability of operators from "classical" differentiability of smooth functions. One can also define $QC^k$, for $k\in\mathbb{N}$, by requiring $\mathcal{A}\cup\underline{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{A}\subseteq\operatorname{Dom}\delta^m$ for $m=1,\dots,k$. Such spectral triples are often called regular [21, 30], and were called smooth in [51].

**Definition 2.3.** For a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple, the seminorms
$$q_m(a):=\|\delta^m a\|,\qquad q'_m(a):=\|\delta^m([\mathcal{D},a])\|,\qquad m=0,1,2,\dots \tag{2.1}$$
determine a locally convex topology on $\mathcal{A}$ which is finer than the norm topology of $A$ (given by $q_0$ alone) and in which the involution is continuous. Let $\mathcal{A}_\delta$ denote the completion of $\mathcal{A}$ in the topology of (2.1).

**Lemma 2.4.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ be a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple. The Fréchet algebra $\mathcal{A}_\delta$ is a pre-$C^*$-algebra, and $(\mathcal{A}_\delta,\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ is also a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple.

**Lemma 2.5.** Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a Fréchet pre-$C^*$-algebra with $C^*$-completion $A$. If $\tilde q\in A$ is a projector (a selfadjoint idempotent) and $0<\varepsilon<1$, then there is a projector $q\in\mathcal{A}$ with $\|q-\tilde q\|<\varepsilon$.

**Proposition 2.6 ($C^\infty$ Functional Calculus).** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ be a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple, and suppose $\mathcal{A}$ is complete. Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}$ be $C^\infty$ in a neighbourhood of the spectrum of $a=a^*\in\mathcal{A}$. If $f(a)\in A$ is defined by the continuous functional calculus, then $f(a)$ lies in $\mathcal{A}$.

**Remark 2.7.** For each $a=a^*\in\mathcal{A}$, the $C^\infty$-functional calculus defines a continuous homomorphism $\Psi:C^\infty(U)\to\mathcal{A}$, where $U\subset\mathbb{R}$ is any open set containing the spectrum of $a$, and $C^\infty(U)$ carries the topology of uniform convergence of all derivatives on compact subsets.

**Proposition 2.8.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ be a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple. Let $a_1,\dots,a_n$ be mutually commuting selfadjoint elements of $\mathcal{A}$, with joint spectrum $\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^n$. Let $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{C}$ be $C^\infty$ and supported in a bounded open neighbourhood $U$ of $\Delta$. Then $f(a_1,\dots,a_n)$ lies in $\mathcal{A}_\delta$.

**Remark 2.9.** For most of this paper the spectral triples considered are commutative and satisfy the first order property [18, VI.4.$\gamma$], namely $[[\mathcal{D},a],b]=0$ for all $a,b\in\mathcal{A}$. In such cases, for any polynomial $p$,
$$[\mathcal{D},p(a_1,\dots,a_n)]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_j p(a_1,\dots,a_n)\,[\mathcal{D},a_j]. \tag{2.5}$$
By a $C^1$-approximation argument (see Proposition 5.1 below), $[\mathcal{D},f(a_1,\dots,a_n)]=\sum_j\partial_j f(a_1,\dots,a_n)\,[\mathcal{D},a_j]$ for any $f$ satisfying the hypotheses of Proposition 2.8. Since the spectral triple is $QC^\infty$, the right-hand side of (2.5) belongs to the smooth domain of $\delta$.

**Lemma 2.10.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ be a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple with $\mathcal{A}$ commutative and complete. Let $\{U_\alpha:\alpha=1,\dots,n\}$ be any finite open cover of the compact Hausdorff space $X=\operatorname{sp}(\mathcal{A})$. Then there exist $\phi_\alpha\in\mathcal{A}$, $\alpha=1,\dots,n$, such that
$$\operatorname{supp}\phi_\alpha\subseteq U_\alpha,\qquad 0\leq\phi_\alpha\leq 1,\qquad \sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\phi_\alpha=1. \tag{2.6}$$

**Corollary 2.11.** Given a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ with $\mathcal{A}$ commutative and complete, let $K\subset U\subset\operatorname{sp}(\mathcal{A})$, where $K$ is compact and $U$ is open. Then there is $\psi\in\mathcal{A}$ with $0\leq\psi\leq 1$, $\psi\equiv 1$ on $K$, and $\psi\equiv 0$ outside $U$.

**Lemma 2.12.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ be a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple with $\mathcal{A}$ commutative and complete. Let $a\in\mathcal{A}$ have compact support contained in an open subset $U\subset\operatorname{sp}(\mathcal{A})$. Then there exists $\phi\in\mathcal{A}$ with $\phi a=a$ and $\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset U$.

**Proposition 2.13.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ be a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple with $\mathcal{A}$ commutative and complete. Let $U\subset\operatorname{sp}(\mathcal{A})$ be open, and let $h\in\mathcal{A}$ satisfy $h(x)\neq 0$ for all $x\in U$. Then for every $a\in\mathcal{A}$ with $\operatorname{supp}a\subset U$, $\mathcal{A}$ contains the element $ah^{-1}\in C(\operatorname{sp}(\mathcal{A}))$ defined by
$$(ah^{-1})(x):=\begin{cases}a(x)/h(x)&\text{if }h(x)\neq 0,\\ 0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases} \tag{2.8}$$

**Corollary 2.14.** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ be a $QC^\infty$ spectral triple with $\mathcal{A}$ commutative and complete. Let $U\subset\operatorname{sp}(\mathcal{A})$ be open, and let $h\in M_k(\mathcal{A})$ be such that $h(x)\in M_k(\mathbb{C})$ is invertible for all $x\in U$. Let $a\in\mathcal{A}$ with $\operatorname{supp}a\subset U$. Then the element $ah^{-1}\in M_k(A)$, defined by
$$(ah^{-1})(x):=\begin{cases}(a(x)\otimes 1_k)\,h(x)^{-1}&\text{if }h(x)\neq 0,\\ 0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}$$
lies in the subalgebra $M_k(\mathcal{A})$.

**Definition 3.1.** A spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ is *$p^+$-summable*, with $1\leq p<\infty$, if $(1+\mathcal{D}^2)^{-1/2}\in\mathcal{L}^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{H})$.

**Remark 3.1.** If $\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{H})$, then $A:=\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}\in\mathcal{L}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{H})$, and hence $\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}$ is finite for any Dixmier trace $\operatorname{Tr}_\omega$. If $q>p$, then $\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-q}=A^{q/p}$ lies in the ideal $\mathcal{L}^1(\mathcal{H})$ of trace-class operators, so $\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-q}=0$. Hence there is at most one value of $p$ (independent of $\omega$) for which $\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}$ can be both finite and positive. Since
$$\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}=\omega\text{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu_k(\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p})$$
and, by [9], for any bounded positive sequence $\{t_n\}$,
$$\liminf_{n\to\infty}t_n\leq\omega\text{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}t_n\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}t_n,$$
it follows that if $\liminf t_n>0$, then every $\omega\text{-}\lim t_n$ is positive. If $0<\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}<\infty$ for all $\omega$, we call $p$ the *metric dimension* of the spectral triple.

**Definition 3.2.** If $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ is a commutative spectral triple for which the set (3.1) is bounded, the *metric topology* on the pure state space of $A$ is the topology defined by the distance function (3.2).

**Remark 3.2.** Equation (3.2) entails
$$|\phi(a)-\psi(a)|\leq\|[\mathcal{D},a]\|\,d(\phi,\psi), \tag{3.3}$$
so all $a\in\mathcal{A}$ are Lipschitz for the metric topology on $X$. When $[\mathcal{D},a]\neq 0$ for $a\notin\mathbb{C}1$, the two topologies coincide [47, 53] if and only if the set (3.1) is precompact in $A/\mathbb{C}1$.

**Remark 3.3.** A priori, the metric topology may be finer than the original weak$^*$ topology on $X$, and need not be compact unless the two topologies coincide. By convention, the topology of $X$ is its weak$^*$ topology unless the metric topology is explicitly invoked.

**Definition 3.3.** For a spectral triple $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$, $\mathcal{C_D(A)}$ denotes the subalgebra of $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ generated by $\mathcal{A}$ and $\underline{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{A}=[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{A}]$. The algebra representation $\pi_{\mathcal{D}}:\Omega^\bullet\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{C_D(A)}$ is defined by
$$\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(a_0\,da_1\dots da_k):=a_0\,[\mathcal{D},a_1]\dots[\mathcal{D},a_k].$$
$\Omega^\bullet\mathcal{A}$ is an involutive algebra with $(da)^*:=-d(a^*)$, and $\pi_{\mathcal{D}}$ is a $*$-representation of the Hochschild chains of $\mathcal{A}$ as operators on $\mathcal{H}$.

**Condition 1 (Dimension).** The spectral triple is $p^+$-summable for a fixed positive integer $p$, for which $\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}>0$ for all $\omega$. By the remark after Definition 3.1 this determines $p$ uniquely; $p$ is then called the *metric dimension* of the spectral triple.

**Remark 3.4.** A priori there is no reason why the growth of the eigenvalues of $\mathcal{D}$ should make $p$ an integer. However, the orientability condition below introduces another dimensionality parameter $p$, as the degree of a Hochschild cycle, which is necessarily an integer, and the two quantities are required to coincide. This formulation excludes certain "0-dimensional" cases, such as when $\mathcal{A}$ has finite linear dimension. For noncommutative 0-dimensional spectral triples built over matrix algebras, see [32, 37, 46].

**Condition 2 (Metric).** The set $\{\eta(a)\in\mathcal{A}/\mathbb{C}1:\|[\mathcal{D},a]\|\leq 1\}$ is norm-bounded in the Banach space $A/\mathbb{C}1$. This ensures that the character space $X=\operatorname{sp}(A)$ is a metric space [17] with the metric distance (3.2). (See Appendix B.)

**Remark 3.5.** Since $A$ is separable, the space $X$ with its weak$^*$ topology is metrizable. However, the metric (3.2) on $X$ does not necessarily give the weak$^*$ topology.

**Condition 3 (Regularity).** The spectral triple is $QC^\infty$, as in Definition 2.2. Without loss of generality, $\mathcal{A}$ is complete in the topology (2.1), and so is a Fréchet pre-$C^*$-algebra.

**Condition 4 (Finiteness).** The smooth domain of $\mathcal{D}$, $\mathcal{H}_\infty:=\bigcap_{m\geq 1}\operatorname{Dom}\mathcal{D}^m$, is a dense subspace of $\mathcal{H}$ which is a finitely generated projective $\mathcal{A}$-module. Moreover, there exists a Hermitian pairing $(\cdot\,|\,\cdot):\mathcal{H}_\infty\times\mathcal{H}_\infty\to\mathcal{A}$ making $(\mathcal{H}_\infty,(\cdot\,|\,\cdot))$ a full pre-$C^*$ right $\mathcal{A}$-module, such that for some Dixmier trace $\operatorname{Tr}_\Omega$,
$$\operatorname{Tr}_\Omega\bigl((\xi\,|\,\eta)\,\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}\bigr)=\langle\xi\,|\,\eta\rangle,\qquad \text{for all }\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_\infty. \tag{3.4}$$
Here $\langle\cdot\,|\,\cdot\rangle$ denotes the scalar product on $\mathcal{H}$.

**Remark 3.6.** Since $\mathcal{A}$ is commutative, $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ may be regarded as either a right or left $\mathcal{A}$-module. It is algebraically more convenient to treat it as a right module, so $\mathcal{H}_\infty\simeq q\,\mathcal{A}^m$, where $q\in M_m(\mathcal{A})$ is a selfadjoint idempotent.

**Remark 3.7.** It is proved in Appendix A that (up to positive scalar multiples) the only Hermitian form satisfying the conditions listed here is the standard one, $(\xi\,|\,\eta)=\sum_{j,k}\xi_j^*q_{jk}\eta_k$, on identifying $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ with $q\mathcal{A}^m$ with $q$ selfadjoint.

**Condition 5 (Absolute Continuity).** For all nonzero $a\in\mathcal{A}$ with $a\geq 0$, and for any $\omega$-limit, $\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\bigl(a\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}\bigr)>0.$

**Remark 3.8.** The absolute continuity condition subsumes the dimension condition, and the two should be regarded as one. This formulation eases the adaptation of the conditions to nonunital algebras [27, 52].

**Condition 6 (First Order).** The bounded operators in $[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{A}]$ commute with $\mathcal{A}$:
$$[[\mathcal{D},a],b]=0\qquad\text{for all }a,b\in\mathcal{A}. \tag{3.5}$$
Thus operators in $[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{A}]$ are endomorphisms of the $\mathcal{A}$-module $\mathcal{H}_\infty$, and more generally $\mathcal{C_D(A)}\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{H}_\infty)$.

**Remark 3.9.** For noncommutative algebras the first-order condition is more elaborate: one requires a commuting representation $\pi^\circ:\mathcal{A}^\circ\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ of the opposite algebra, with $[a,b^\circ]=0$. Then $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ is a right $\mathcal{A}$-module under $\xi\cdot b:=\pi^\circ(b)\xi$, and the first-order condition reads $[[\mathcal{D},a],b^\circ]=0$ for $a,b\in\mathcal{A}$, again giving $\mathcal{C_D(A)}\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{H}_\infty)$. In the commutative case we take $\pi^\circ=\pi$. (See [37] for examples of commutative algebras with different left and right actions on $\mathcal{H}$.)

**Condition 7 (Orientability).** Let $p$ be the metric dimension. The spectral triple is even, with $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading $\Gamma$, if and only if $p$ is even. For convenience, $\Gamma=1$ when $p$ is odd. The spectral triple is *orientable* if there exists a Hochschild $p$-cycle
$$\mathbf{c}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}a_\alpha^0\otimes a_\alpha^1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_\alpha^p\in Z_p(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}) \tag{3.6a}$$
whose Hochschild class $[\mathbf{c}]\in HH_p(\mathcal{A})$ is called the *orientation* of the spectral triple, such that
$$\pi_D(\mathbf{c})\equiv\sum_\alpha a_\alpha^0\,[\mathcal{D},a_\alpha^1]\dots[\mathcal{D},a_\alpha^p]=\Gamma. \tag{3.6b}$$

**Condition 8 (Poincaré duality).** The spectral triple determines a $KK$-homology class $\mu=[(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})]\in K^\bullet(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A})=K^\bullet(A\otimes A)$. We require that $\mu$ be a fundamental class, i.e., that the Kasparov product $\bullet\otimes_A\mu:K_\bullet(A)\to K^\bullet(A)$ [31] be an isomorphism. (See Section 8.)

**Condition 9 (Reality).** There is an antiunitary $J:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}$ such that $Ja^*J^{-1}=a$ for all $a\in\mathcal{A}$; moreover $J^2=\pm1$, $J\mathcal{D}J^{-1}=\pm\mathcal{D}$, and $J\Gamma J^{-1}=\pm\Gamma$ in the even case, with signs depending only on $p \bmod 8$ as follows.

For $p\bmod 8\in\{0,2,4,6\}$:

| $p \bmod 8$ | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $J^2=\pm1$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $J\mathcal{D}J^{-1}=\pm\mathcal{D}$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $J\Gamma J^{-1}=\pm\Gamma$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ |

For $p\bmod 8\in\{1,3,5,7\}$:

| $p \bmod 8$ | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $J^2=\pm1$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $J\mathcal{D}J^{-1}=\pm\mathcal{D}$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

For the origin of this sign table in $KR$-homology, see [30, Sec. 9.5].

**Remark 3.10.** For noncommutative $\mathcal{A}$ one would require $Ja^*J^{-1}=a^\circ$, or more precisely $J\pi(a)^*J^{-1}=\pi^\circ(a)$. Thus $J$ implements on $\mathcal{H}$ the involution $\tau:a\otimes b^\circ\mapsto b^*\otimes{a^*}^\circ$ of $\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}^\circ$.

**Condition 10 (Irreducibility).** The only operators in $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$, strongly commuting with $\mathcal{D}$ and with all $a\in\mathcal{A}$, are the scalars $\mathbb{C}1$.

**Condition 11 (Closedness).** The $p^+$-summable spectral triple satisfies: for any $a_1,\dots,a_p\in\mathcal{A}$, the operator $\Gamma\,[\mathcal{D},a_1]\dots[\mathcal{D},a_p]\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}$ has vanishing Dixmier trace; that is, for any $\operatorname{Tr}_\omega$,
$$\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\bigl(\Gamma\,[\mathcal{D},a_1]\dots[\mathcal{D},a_p]\,\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}\bigr)=0. \tag{3.7}$$

**Remark 3.11.** Setting $\Phi(a_0,\dots,a_p):=\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\bigl(\Gamma\,a_0\,[\mathcal{D},a_1]\dots[\mathcal{D},a_p]\,\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}\bigr)$, equation (3.7) may be rewritten [18, VI.2] as $B_0\Phi=0$, where $B_0$ acts on $(k+1)$-linear functionals by
$$(B_0\phi)(a_1,\dots,a_k):=\phi(1,a_1,\dots,a_k)+(-1)^k\phi(a_1,\dots,a_k,1).$$

**Lemma 3.12 (Connes).** Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})$ be $p^+$-summable and satisfy Condition 6. Then for each $k=0,1,\dots,p$ and $\eta\in\Omega^k\mathcal{A}$, a Hochschild cocycle $C_\eta\in Z^{p-k}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^*)$ is defined by
$$C_\eta(a^0,\dots,a^{p-k}):=\operatorname{Tr}_\omega\bigl(\Gamma\,\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)\,a^0\,[\mathcal{D},a^1]\dots[\mathcal{D},a^{p-k}]\,\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle^{-p}\bigr).$$
If Condition 11 also holds, then $C_\eta$ depends only on the class of $\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)$ in $\Lambda^k_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{A}$, and $B_0C_\eta=(-1)^kC_{d\eta}$.

**Lemma 3.13.** Under Conditions 6 and 10, the algebra $\mathcal{A}$ contains no nontrivial projector.

**Corollary 3.14.** Under Conditions 6 and 10, the space $X$ is connected.

**Lemma 3.15.** Under Condition 4, the dense subspace $\mathcal{H}_\infty\subset\mathcal{H}$ consists of continuous sections of a complex vector bundle $S\to X$. If the spectral triple is also irreducible, then $S$ has constant rank.

**Lemma 3.16.** Under Conditions 4 to 7, the algebra $\mathcal{C_D(A)}$ is a unital selfadjoint subalgebra of $\Gamma(X,\operatorname{End}S)$. The operator norm of each $T\in\mathcal{C_D(A)}$ coincides with its norm as an endomorphism of $S$.

**Corollary 3.17.** Under Conditions 4 to 7, the algebra of sections $\mathcal{C_D(A)}$ is pointwise a direct sum of matrix algebras:
$$(\mathcal{C_D(A)})_x\simeq M_{k_1}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{k_r}(\mathbb{C})\qquad\text{for }x\in X,$$
where $k_1+\cdots+k_r=N$.

**Proposition 3.18.** Let $T:\mathcal{H}_\infty\to\mathcal{H}_\infty$ be $\mathcal{A}$-linear. Then $T$ extends to a bounded operator on $\mathcal{H}$.

**Lemma 3.19.** Under Conditions 1, 4, 5 and 7, the $\mathcal{A}$-valued Hermitian pairing on $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ given by (3.4) is independent of the choice of Dixmier trace.

**Lemma 3.20.** Under Condition 7, the operator $\mathcal{D}$ satisfies the following formula on $\mathcal{H}_\infty$:
$$\mathcal{D}=\tfrac{1}{2}(-1)^{p-1}\Gamma\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{j-1}a^0_\alpha\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^1_\alpha\dots\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^{j-1}_\alpha\,[\mathcal{D}^2,a^j_\alpha]\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^{j+1}_\alpha\dots\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^p_\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}(-1)^{p-1}\Gamma\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}\Gamma, \tag{3.10}$$
where $\Gamma=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}a^0_\alpha\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^1_\alpha\dots\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^p_\alpha$ and $\underline{\mathrm{d}}\Gamma:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^0_\alpha\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^1_\alpha\dots\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^n_\alpha$.

**Corollary 3.21.** Under Conditions 6 and 7, the commutator $[\mathcal{D},a]$, for $a\in\mathcal{A}$, has the expansion
$$[\mathcal{D},a]=\tfrac{1}{2}(-1)^{p-1}\Gamma\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{j-1}a^0_\alpha\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^1_\alpha\dots\bigl(\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^j_\alpha\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}a+\underline{\mathrm{d}}a\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^j_\alpha\bigr)\dots\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^p_\alpha.$$

**Lemma 4.1.** For any $a,b\in\mathcal{A}$, the operator $[\mathcal{D},a]\,[\mathcal{D},b]+[\mathcal{D},b]\,[\mathcal{D},a]$ is a junk term.

**Lemma 4.2.** The image of $\Gamma=\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{c})$ in $\Lambda^p_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{A}$ is nonzero.

**Corollary 4.3.** Let $\Gamma'\in\mathcal{C_D(A)}$ be defined by
$$\Gamma':=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_p}(-1)^\sigma\sum_\alpha a^0_\alpha\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}a_\alpha^{\sigma(1)}\,\underline{\mathrm{d}}a_\alpha^{\sigma(2)}\dots\underline{\mathrm{d}}a_\alpha^{\sigma(p)}, \tag{4.2}$$
obtained by skewsymmetrizing the expression for $\Gamma$ from (3.6). If $a\in\mathcal{A}$ is positive and nonzero, then $a\Gamma'\neq 0$.

**Proposition 4.4.** There is an open cover $\{U_1,\dots,U_n\}$ of $X$ such that, for each $\alpha=1,\dots,n$, the operators $[\mathcal{D},a^1_\alpha],\dots,[\mathcal{D},a^p_\alpha]$ are pointwise linearly independent sections of $\Gamma(U_\alpha,\operatorname{End}S)$.

**Lemma 4.5.** Fix $\alpha\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ and let $a\in\mathcal{A}$, writing $a=:a^{p+1}_\alpha$ for notational convenience. Then
$$\frac{(-1)^{p-1}}{2(p+1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{p+1}}(-1)^\sigma a^0_\alpha[\mathcal{D},a^{\sigma(1)}_\alpha]\dots[\mathcal{D},a^{\sigma(p)}_\alpha]\,[\mathcal{D},a^{\sigma(p+1)}_\alpha]=0. \tag{4.4}$$

**Remark 4.6.** Denote by $\Gamma'_\alpha$ the $\alpha$-th summand of $\Gamma'$ in (4.2), and by $\Gamma'_\alpha\wedge\underline{\mathrm{d}}a$ the operator on the left-hand side of (4.4). Each $\underline{\mathrm{d}}a$ and each $\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^j_\alpha$, and therefore each $\Gamma'_\alpha$, is an endomorphism of $\mathcal{H}_\infty$. Lemma 4.5 shows that $\Gamma'_\alpha(x)\wedge\underline{\mathrm{d}}a(x)=0$ in $\operatorname{End}S_x$ for each $x\in U_\alpha$, where $\wedge$ denotes skewsymmetrization with respect to the several $\underline{\mathrm{d}}a^j_\alpha(x)$.
