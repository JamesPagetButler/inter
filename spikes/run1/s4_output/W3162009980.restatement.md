# Restatement

## Abstract

- There exist functors from the category of condensed sets to the category of pro-groups whose restriction to CW-spaces coincides with the ordinary notion of homotopy groups of topological spaces.
- These functors extend to the $\infty$-categories of condensed anima and pro-anima.
- The compact projective objects in the category of condensed sets are the extremally disconnected profinite sets.

## Introduction

The following statements appear in the introduction.

**Definition** (cf. Prop. 2.1.2). Let $\mathcal{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathcal{D}\mathrm{isc}$ be the category of extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff spaces, or equivalently, of extremally disconnected profinite sets (cf. Def. 1.1.5). A *condensed set* is a contravariant functor
$$T\colon \mathcal{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathcal{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{S}\mathrm{et},\qquad S \mapsto T(S),$$
satisfying $T(\emptyset)=\ast$ and sending finite disjoint unions to finite products.

**Proposition** (cf. Ex. 2.2.1, Prop. 2.2.3). Every (sufficiently nice) topological space $T$ can be regarded as a condensed set via
$$\underline{T}\colon \mathcal{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathcal{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{S}\mathrm{et},\qquad S \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}\mathrm{op}}(S,T).$$
This yields a fully faithful embedding $\mathcal{CW}\hookrightarrow \mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et)}$, where $\mathcal{CW}$ is the category of CW-spaces.

**Remark (overview, structure).** The following diagram of $\infty$-categories and functors is referred to in the introduction. Only the small right triangle is stated to commute. The nodes are $\mathcal{CW}$, $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et)}$, $\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et})$, $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}$ and $\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni})$, with chapter references Ch. 2, Ch. 3–4, Ch. 4, 6, Ch. 5 and Ch. 7 attached to the arrows.

**Remark (overview).** Besides the embedding $\mathcal{CW}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et)}$, a CW-space up to weak homotopy equivalence gives a Kan complex via the singular simplicial complex construction. This gives a (non-faithful) functor $\mathcal{CW}\to\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et})$. Here $\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et})$, or $\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}$ (Ch. 4), is the $\infty$-category obtained by inverting weak homotopy equivalences of topological spaces, equivalently of simplicial sets. Its objects are called anima. The $\infty$-categories $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et)}$ (Ch. 2) and $\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et})$ admit fully faithful embeddings

into a common $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}$ (Ch. 6). The objects of $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}$ are called condensed anima.

**Definition** (cf. Def. 6.1.5). Identify $\mathcal{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathcal{D}\mathrm{isc}$ with its $\infty$-category obtained by the nerve construction. A *condensed anima* is a contravariant $\infty$-functor
$$T\colon \mathcal{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathcal{D}\mathrm{isc}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni},\qquad S\mapsto T(S),$$
taking finite disjoint unions to finite products.

**Definition and Proposition** (cf. Def. 4.2.2, Prop. 4.2.7). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an ordinary cocomplete category generated under small colimits by its subcategory of compact projective objects $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{cp}}$. The *animation* of $\mathcal{C}$ is the $\infty$-category $\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}(\mathcal{C})$ freely generated under sifted colimits by $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{cp}}$. There is a fully faithful embedding $\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}(\mathcal{C})$.

The statement that $\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et})$, respectively $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}$, is equivalent to the animation of $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}$, respectively $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et)}$, is cited as (cf. **Definition and Proposition** (cf. Lemma 6.2.1, Prop. 6.2.2, Def. 6.2.3). Every extremally disconnected profinite set is a cofiltered limit $X=\lim_{i\in I}X_i$ of finite discrete spaces (cf. is a functor, where the colimit is filtered. It is a condensed anima which preserves cofiltered limits in $\mathcal{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathcal{D}\mathrm{isc}$, and it is called the *discrete condensed anima attached to $A$*.

**Theorem** (unnumbered in the source). The fully faithful embedding $\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}$ extends to a unique embedding $\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}$ preserving cofiltered limits, where $\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni})$ is the pro-$\infty$-category of pro-anima. This functor admits a left adjoint $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni})$. The homotopy group functors on (pointed) anima
$$\pi_0\colon\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\to\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et},\quad \pi_1\colon\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}_*\to\mathcal{G}\mathrm{rp},\quad \pi_n\colon\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}_*\to\mathcal{A}\mathrm{b}\mathcal{G}\mathrm{rp},$$
extend to unique cofiltered-limit-preserving functors
$$\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\pi_0)\colon\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni})\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}),\quad \mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\pi_1)\colon\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni})_*\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{G}\mathrm{rp}),\quad \mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\pi_n)\colon\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni})_*\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{b}\mathcal{G}\mathrm{rp}).$$
Composing these with the left adjoint $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni})$ gives functors
$$\widetilde{\pi}_0\colon\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}),\quad \widetilde{\pi}_1\colon\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}_*\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{G}\mathrm{rp}),\quad \widetilde{\pi}_n\colon\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ond(}\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni}\mathrm{)}_*\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{b}\mathcal{G}\mathrm{rp}),$$
whose restriction to CW-spaces coincides with the ordinary notion of homotopy groups of topological spaces.

**Connection to étale homotopy theory (stated).** To a sufficiently nice scheme $X$, the Artin–Mazur étale homotopy type assigns an object of $\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}(\mathcal{A}\mathrm{ni})$. This can be regarded as a condensed anima. The étale fundamental group of $X$ is a profinite group.

## Numbered statements from the main text

**Proposition 1.1.1.** Every surjection of compact Hausdorff spaces is a quotient map.

**Definition and Proposition 1.1.2** (Stone–Čech compactification). To every topological space $X$ one assigns a compact Hausdorff space $\beta X$ and a continuous map $\iota_X\colon X\to\beta X$ with the following universal property: for every compact Hausdorff space $K$ and every continuous map $f\colon X\to K$ there exists a unique continuous map $\beta f\colon\beta X\to K$ with $f=\beta f\circ\iota_X$. The space $\beta X$ is the Stone–Čech compactification of $X$. The assignment $X\mapsto\beta X$ is left adjoint to the embedding functor from compact Hausdorff spaces to topological spaces.

**Corollary 1.1.3.** Every limit of compact Hausdorff spaces is compact Hausdorff.

**Lemma 1.1.4.** Compact Hausdorff spaces are closed under finite coproducts and retracts.

**Definition 1.1.5** (Totally and extremally disconnected).
1. A topological space is *totally disconnected* if all connected subspaces are singletons.
2. A topological space is *extremally disconnected* if the closure of every open subset is open.

**Remark 1.1.6.** Hausdorff extremally disconnected spaces are totally disconnected. Proof sketch as given: for two points in a connected subspace, choose disjoint open neighbourhoods. The closure of one such neighbourhood is open and closed in the subspace and does not contain the other point. This contradicts connectedness, so a connected subspace contains at most one point.

**Proposition 1.1.7.** Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space. The following are equivalent.
- (i) $X$ is extremally disconnected.
- (ii) For every surjective continuous map $f\colon Y\to X$ with $Y$ compact Hausdorff, there exists a continuous section.
- (iii) For every map $X\to Z$ and every surjective map $Y\to Z$ of compact Hausdorff spaces, there exists a continuous map $X\to Y$ such that the triangle $X\to Y\to Z$ commutes with the given map $X\to Z$.

**Definition 1.1.8** (Disconnected compact Hausdorff spaces). Let $\mathcal{CH}\mathrm{aus}$ be the category of compact Hausdorff spaces with continuous maps. There are full subcategories
$$\mathcal{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathcal{D}\mathrm{isc}\subset\mathcal{S}\mathrm{tone}\subset\mathcal{CH}\mathrm{aus},$$
where:
1. $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{tone}$ is the subcategory of totally disconnected compact Hausdorff spaces, whose objects are called *Stone spaces*.
2. $\mathcal{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathcal{D}\mathrm{isc}$ is the subcategory of extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff spaces, i.e. spaces satisfying the equivalent properties of Proposition 1.1.7. Some authors call its objects *Stonean spaces* (cf. [BH19]).

**Corollary 1.1.9.** The Stone–Čech compactification of a discrete space is extremally disconnected.

**Corollary 1.1.10.** Every compact Hausdorff space admits a surjection from an extremally disconnected space.

**Theorem 1.1.11.** Every compact Hausdorff space is a coequalizer, in the category of all topological spaces, of extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff spaces.

**Definition 1.2.1** (Small functors). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a possibly large category. A functor $F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}$ is *small* if it is the left Kan extension of some presheaf with small domain. Equivalently, it is the left Kan extension of its restriction to some small full subcategory of $\mathcal{C}$.

**Lemma 1.2.2.** The small functors are precisely the small colimits of representables.

**Remark 1.2.3.** The small presheaves on a fixed category $\mathcal{C}$ form an ordinary cocomplete category, since small colimits of small colimits are small. It is the full subcategory of the presheaf category spanned by the small functors, and it is the free cocompletion of $\mathcal{C}$ under the Yoneda embedding by small colimits.

**Warning 1.2.4** (Dealing with large categories). The thesis considers large categories and their presheaf categories. Functors are implicitly assumed to be small. Set-theoretic issues are acknowledged at relevant points.

**Definition 1.2.5** (Filtered category). A non-empty index category $I$ is *filtered* if:
1. for every pair of objects $x,y$ of $I$ there exist an object $z$ and morphisms $x\to z$, $y\to z$; and
2. for every pair of objects $x,y$ and every pair of morphisms $f,g\colon x\to y$ there exists a morphism $h\colon y\to z$ with $h\circ f=h\circ g$.

A category $I$ is *cofiltered* if $I^{\mathrm{op}}$ is filtered.

**Definition 1.2.6** (Filtered and cofiltered diagrams). A diagram with filtered (resp. cofiltered) index category is called filtered (resp. cofiltered). Filtered (resp. cofiltered) (co)limits are (co)limits over filtered (resp. cofiltered) index categories.

**Definition 1.2.7** (Pro-categories). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a small category. $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})$ is the category whose objects are diagrams $X\colon I\to\mathcal{C}$ over a small cofiltered category $I$, with morphisms
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})}(X,Y)\coloneqq\lim_{j\in J}\mathrm{colim}_{i\in I}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_i,Y_j),$$
where $X_i=X(i)$ and $Y_j=Y(j)$. Composition is defined in the natural way. Objects of $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})$ are called pro-objects.

**Remark 1.2.8.** The category of pro-objects in $\mathcal{C}$ is the opposite of the category of ind-objects in $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$:
$$\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})\cong(\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}))^{\mathrm{op}}.$$

**Proposition 1.2.9** (Properties of pro-categories). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a small category.
1. There is a canonical full embedding $i\colon\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})$ sending $X\in\mathcal{C}$ to the diagram $\widetilde{X}\colon 1\to\mathcal{C}$ from the terminal index category.
2. $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})$ is equivalent to the full subcategory of $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et})^{\mathrm{op}}$ whose objects are cofiltered limits of $\{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_i,\cdot)\}_{i\in I}$ with $X_i\in\mathcal{C}$. It admits all cofiltered limits.

**Remark 1.2.10.** By part (2) of Proposition 1.2.9, pro-objects are small cofiltered limits of objects of $\mathcal{C}$. A pro-object in the essential image of $\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})$ is called *corepresentable*.

**Definition 1.2.11** (Profinite sets). A profinite set is a pro-object in the category $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}^{\mathrm{fin}}$ of finite sets. The category of profinite sets is denoted $\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}\mathcal{F}\mathrm{in}$.

**Proposition 1.2.12.** The category $\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ro}\mathcal{F}\mathrm{in}$ of profinite sets is equivalent to the category $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{tone}$ of Stone spaces. The equivalence sends a profinite set, viewed as a cofiltered diagram of finite discrete spaces, to its limit in topological spaces. Under this identification, a finite set is embedded as a profinite set by giving it the discrete topology.

**Remark 1.2.13.** Given a Stone space $X$, one attaches a cofiltered projective system of finite sets as follows. Let $\mathcal{I}$ be the set of finite decompositions $X=\coprod_{i\in I}U_i$ into non-empty open and closed subsets. Any two decompositions have a common refinement, so $\mathcal{I}$ is cofiltered, with morphisms $I'\to I$ corresponding to refinements. For $I\in\mathcal{I}$ let $X_I$ be the quotient of $X$ in which $x\sim y$ if $x,y$ lie in the same $U_i$. Then $X_I$ is finite and discrete. Refinement gives canonical maps $X_{I'}\to X_I$. Together with the maps $X\to X_I$, this yields a continuous map $X\to\lim_{I\in\mathcal{I}}X_I$, which is a homeomorphism (cf. [Stacks, Tag 08ZY]).

**Definition 1.2.14** (Grothendieck pretopology). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category with fibre products. A Grothendieck pretopology on $\mathcal{C}$ is a collection of families $\{U_i\to U\}_{i\in I}$ of morphisms, for each object $U\in\mathcal{C}$, called covering families of $U$, such that:
1. Every isomorphism $V\xrightarrow{\cong}U$ forms a covering family $\{V\xrightarrow{\cong}U\}$.
2. If $\{U_i\to U\}_{i\in I}$ is a covering family and $f\colon V\to U$ is any morphism, then $\{V\times_U U_i\to V\}_{i\in I}$ is a covering family.
3. If $\{U_i\to U\}_{i\in I}$ is a covering family and for each $i$ the family $\{U_{i,j}\to U_i\}_{j\in J_i}$ is a covering family, then $\{U_{i,j}\to U_i\to U\}_{i\in I,\,j\in J_i}$ is a covering family.

A category together with a family of coverings is called a *site*.

**Example 1.2.15** (Pretopology on $\mathcal{CH}\mathrm{aus}$ and $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{tone}$). Let $\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\mathcal{CH}\mathrm{aus}$ (respectively $\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\mathcal{S}\mathrm{tone}$), the category of (totally disconnected) compact Hausdorff spaces, which is closed under limits. Let $X$ be a (totally disconnected) compact Hausdorff space and $\{X_i\to X\}_{i\in I}$ a finite family of jointly surjective maps in $\mathcal{C}$, i.e. $\coprod_{i\in I}X_i\to X$ is surjective. These families define a Grothendieck pretopology on $\mathcal{C}$. The category $\mathcal{E}\mathrm{xtr}\mathcal{D}\mathrm{isc}$ does not have fibre products, so Definition 1.2.14 cannot be applied to it directly. This is addressed by Definition 1.2.19, which is not included in the provided text.

**Definition 1.2.16** (Sheaves on sites). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a site and $F\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}$ a presheaf. $F$ is a *sheaf* if for every $U\in\mathcal{C}$ and every covering $\{U_i\to U\}_{i\in I}$ the diagram
$$F(U)\to\prod_{i\in I}F(U_i)\rightrightarrows\prod_{i,j}F(U_i\times_U U_j)$$
is an equalizer, where the two parallel maps are induced by the canonical projections $p^1_{ij}\colon U_i\times_U U_j\to U_i$ and $p^2_{ij}\colon U_i\times_U U_j\to U_j$. The category of sheaves on $\mathcal{C}$ is denoted $\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{C})$.

**Definition 1.2.17** (Sieves on objects). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category and $X\in\mathcal{C}$. The over category $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$ has objects pairs $(Y,f\colon Y\to X)$, and morphisms $(Y',f')\to(Y,f)$ are morphisms $g\colon Y'\to Y$ in $\mathcal{C}$ with $f\circ g=f'$.
1. A *sieve* on $X$ is a full subcategory $S(X)$ of $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$ such that, for every morphism $g\colon(Y',f')\to(Y,f)$ in $\mathcal{C}_{/X}$, the pair $(Y',f')$ lies in $S(X)$ whenever $(Y,f)$ does.
