# Restatement of the Material

## Abstract

We use the basic expected properties of the Gray tensor product of $(\infty,2)$-categories to study (co)lax natural transformations. Using results of Riehl–Verity and Zaganidis we identify lax transformations between adjunctions and monads with commutative squares of (monadic) right adjoints. We also identify the colax transformations whose components are equivalences (generalizing the "icons" of Lack) with the 2-morphisms that arise from viewing $(\infty,2)$-categories as simplicial $\infty$-categories. Using this characterization we identify the $\infty$-category of monads on a fixed object and colax morphisms between them with the $\infty$-category of associative algebras in endomorphisms.

## Introduction

Three descriptions of monads on an $\infty$-category are considered:

1. A monad on $\mathcal{C}$ is an associative algebra in the monoidal $\infty$-category $\text{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})$ of endofunctors under composition.
2. A monad is a functor of $\infty$-categories that is a monadic right adjoint.
3. A monad is a functor of $(\infty,2)$-categories $\mathfrak{mnd}\to\text{CAT}_\infty$, where $\mathfrak{mnd}$ is the universal 2-category containing a monad and $\text{CAT}_\infty$ is the $(\infty,2)$-category of $\infty$-categories.

These three definitions are known to be equivalent by results of Lurie [22, §4.7.3] and Riehl–Verity [27]. (Footnote 1: Both prove versions of the monadicity theorem, which relate the first two and last two definitions, respectively. However, these comparisons only relate $\infty$-groupoids of monads.)

The main goal is to enhance these comparisons to take into account morphisms of monads. For description 1, the obvious notion of morphism is a homomorphism of algebras in $\text{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})$. For description 2, it is a commutative triangle $\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{f}\mathcal{B}$, $r\colon \mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}$, $r'\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{C}$, where $r$ and $r'$ are monadic right adjoints. (Footnote 2: On a fixed $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, monads in senses 1 and 2 have already been compared by Heine [15]. More generally, one can let $\mathcal{C}$ vary and consider commutative squares whose vertical morphisms are monadic right adjoints. For ordinary 2-categories, Street [30] showed that such squares of monadic right adjoints correspond to what he called monad functors, which are the same as lax natural transformations between functors from $\mathfrak{mnd}$.)

To compare 2 and 3, the paper studies lax transformations in the setting of $(\infty,2)$-categories, defined using the (lax) Gray tensor product. (Footnote 3: Several constructions have recently appeared; see Remark 3.6.) The paper assumes certain basic expected properties of this tensor product (see 3.5) and defines $\text{FUN}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X})_{\text{(co)lax}}$ of functors and (co)lax transformations between $(\infty,2)$-categories $\mathcal{Y}$ and $\mathcal{X}$ in §3. Specialising $\mathcal{Y}$ to the universal monad 2-category $\mathfrak{mnd}$ and the universal adjunction 2-category $\mathfrak{adj}$ gives $(\infty,2)$-categories $\text{MND}(\mathcal{X})_{\text{(co)lax}}$ and $\text{ADJ}(\mathcal{X})_{\text{(co)lax}}$ of monads and adjunctions, with (co)lax transformations as morphisms.

The comparison of 2 and 3 combines the following two results.

**Theorem 1.1.** For any $(\infty,2)$-category $\mathcal{X}$, restricting an adjunction to its right adjoint defines an equivalence of $(\infty,2)$-categories
$$\text{ADJ}(\mathcal{X})_{\text{lax}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\text{FUN}(C_1,\mathcal{X})_{\text{radj}},$$
where the latter is the $(\infty,2)$-category of morphisms in $\mathcal{X}$ that are right adjoints, with commutative squares as morphisms.

**Theorem 1.2.** The functor $\text{ADJ}(\text{CAT}_\infty)_{\text{lax}}\to\text{MND}(\text{CAT}_\infty)_{\text{lax}}$, taking an adjunction to its induced monad, has a fully faithful right adjoint with image the monadic adjunctions.

Theorem 1.1 is proved in §4 using a main result of [27], which gives this equivalence on the level of underlying $\infty$-groupoids. Theorem 1.2 is proved in §5 as a corollary of work of Zaganidis [32], whose thesis studied lax morphisms of adjunctions and monads in the framework of [27]. Combining the two theorems gives an equivalence
$$\text{MND}(\text{CAT}_\infty)_{\text{lax}}\simeq\text{FUN}(C_1,\text{CAT}_\infty)_{\text{mndradj}},$$
where the right-hand side is the $(\infty,2)$-category of morphisms in $\text{CAT}_\infty$ that are monadic right adjoints. (More generally, $\text{CAT}_\infty$ may be replaced by any $(\infty,2)$-category modelled by an $\infty$-cosmos in the sense of Riehl and Verity.)

The relation between descriptions 1 and 3 is then considered. Objects correspond because the one-object 2-category $\mathfrak{mnd}$ is the monoidal envelope of the non-symmetric associative operad. Relating morphisms requires understanding the connection between (co)lax transformations and 2-morphisms of monoidal $\infty$-categories. If $(\infty,2)$-categories are viewed (as complete 2-fold Segal spaces) as cocartesian fibrations over $\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}$, then for $(\infty,2)$-categories $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$ one defines an $\infty$-category $\text{Nat}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})$ consisting of functors over $\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}$ that preserve cocartesian morphisms, together with natural transformations between them.

**Theorem 1.3.** There is a functor $\text{Nat}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\to\text{Fun}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})_{\text{colax}}$ that identifies the domain with the wide subcategory of the $\infty$-category $\text{Fun}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})_{\text{colax}}$ (underlying $\text{FUN}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})_{\text{colax}}$) containing those colax transformations whose components are all equivalences.

The colax transformations in this subcategory are an $(\infty,2)$-categorical analogue of the "icons" of Lack [18].

Combining this with the non-symmetric analogues of the results on monoidal envelopes of $\infty$-operads from [22, §2.2.4] gives the following comparison of descriptions 1 and 3.

**Theorem 1.4.** For any object $X$ of an $(\infty,2)$-category $\mathcal{X}$, there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\text{Alg}(\text{End}_{\mathcal{X}}(X))\xrightarrow{\sim}\text{Mnd}(\mathcal{X})_{\text{colax},X},$$
between the $\infty$-category of associative algebras in the monoidal $\infty$-category of endomorphisms of $X$ under composition, and the fibre at $X$ of the underlying $\infty$-category $\text{Mnd}(\mathcal{X})_{\text{colax}}$ of $\text{MND}(\mathcal{X})_{\text{colax}}$.

This equivalence is compatible with the forgetful functors to endomorphisms of $X$. Replacing lax by colax morphisms gives an equivalence between $\text{Alg}(\text{End}_{\mathcal{X}}(X))^{\text{op}}$ and $\text{Mnd}(\mathcal{X})_{\text{lax},X}$. Combined with the first comparison, for $\mathcal{C}$ there are $\infty$-category equivalences
$$\text{Alg}(\text{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}))^{\text{op}}\simeq\text{Mnd}(\text{CAT}_\infty)_{\text{lax},\mathcal{C}}\simeq\text{Cat}_{\infty/\mathcal{C}}^{\text{mndradj}},$$
where the right-hand side is the full subcategory of $\text{Cat}_{\infty/\mathcal{C}}$ spanned by the monadic right adjoints.

**Notation 2.1.** $\mathcal{S}$ denotes the $\infty$-category of spaces, $\text{Cat}_\infty$ the $\infty$-category of $\infty$-categories, and $\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$ the $\infty$-category of $(\infty,2)$-categories.

**Notation 2.2.** For an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathcal{C}^\simeq$ denotes the underlying $\infty$-groupoid, the value at $\mathcal{C}$ of the right adjoint to the inclusion $\mathcal{S}\hookrightarrow\text{Cat}_\infty$. This inclusion also has a left adjoint, sending $\mathcal{C}$ to the $\infty$-groupoid obtained by inverting all morphisms, denoted $\|\mathcal{C}\|$.

**Definition 2.3.** $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ is the simplex category, with objects $[n]:=\{0,\ldots,n\}$ and order-preserving maps. A morphism $\phi\colon[m]\to[n]$ in $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ is *inert* if it is the inclusion of a subinterval, i.e. $\phi(i)=\phi(0)+i$ for $i=0,\ldots,m$, and *active* if it preserves the end points, i.e. $\phi(0)=0$ and $\phi(m)=n$.

**Definition 2.4.** For an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$ with finite limits, $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{C})$ denotes the full subcategory of $\text{Fun}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}},\mathcal{C})$ consisting of functors $X\colon\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{C}$ satisfying the *Segal condition*: the natural map
$$X_n\to X_1\times_{X_0}\cdots\times_{X_0}X_1,$$
induced by the inert maps $[0],[1]\to[n]$, is an equivalence for all $n$. Also $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{C})$ denotes the full subcategory $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{C}))$ of $\text{Fun}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}},\text{Fun}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}},\mathcal{C}))\simeq\text{Fun}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}},\mathcal{C})$, consisting of functors satisfying the Segal condition in each variable.

**Definition 2.5.** $\text{Seg}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\text{Cat}_\infty)$ denotes the full subcategory of $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\text{Cat}_\infty)$ consisting of Segal objects $X$ such that $X_0$ is an $\infty$-groupoid. $\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$ is the full subcategory of $\text{Seg}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\text{Cat}_\infty)$ consisting of functors $X$ satisfying the *completeness condition*, namely that the underlying Segal space $X^\simeq$ is complete in the sense of [25].

**Definition 2.6.** $\text{Seg}^{\text{2-fold}}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$ denotes the full subcategory of $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$ consisting of *2-fold Segal spaces*, i.e. objects $X$ such that $X_{0,\bullet}\colon\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{S}$ is constant.

**Remark 2.7.** The top right vertical morphism in Eq. 1 arises from the inclusion $\text{Cat}_\infty\hookrightarrow\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$ of $\infty$-categories as complete Segal objects, due to Rezk [25]. This also induces the other inclusions between the top two rows, and identifies $\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$ with the full subcategory of $\text{Seg}^{\text{2-fold}}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$ consisting of the complete 2-fold Segal spaces in the sense of Barwick [3].

**Definition 2.8.** The category $\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2$ has objects $[k](n_1,\ldots,n_k)$ for non-negative integers $k,n_1,\ldots,n_k$. A morphism $[k](n_1,\ldots,n_k)\to[l](m_1,\ldots,m_l)$ is given by a morphism $\phi\colon[k]\to[l]$ in $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ together with a morphism $\psi_{ij}\colon[n_i]\to[m_j]$ in $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ whenever $\phi(i-1)<j\leq\phi(i)$. Composition is defined in the obvious way, and a morphism is *inert* or *active* if $\phi$ and each $\psi_{ij}$ are inert or active, respectively.

**Remark 2.9.** The objects of $\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2$ can be thought of as globular pasting diagrams; for example, one such diagram corresponds to the object $[4](3,0,1,2)$. Equivalently, $\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2$ is a full subcategory of strict 2-categories: $[k](n_1,\ldots,n_k)$ is the strict 2-category with objects $0,\ldots,n$ whose category of morphisms $i\to j$ is $\prod_{i<k\leq j}[n_i]$ if $i\leq j$ and empty otherwise, with composition given by products.

**Notation 2.10.** 
$$C_0:=[0](),\qquad C_1:=[1](0),\qquad C_2:=[1](1).$$
$C_n$ is called the *$n$-cell*; it is the generic 2-category containing an $n$-morphism.

**Definition 2.11.** $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$ denotes the full subcategory of $\text{Fun}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}},\mathcal{S})$ consisting of functors $X$ satisfying:
- for every object $[k](n_1,\ldots,n_k)$, the morphism $X([k](n_1,\ldots,n_k))\to X([1](n_1))\times_{X(C_0)}\cdots\times_{X(C_0)}X([1](n_k))$ is an equivalence;

**Remark 2.12.** The bottom right vertical morphism in Eq. 1 is given by composition with $\tau\colon\boldsymbol{\Delta}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}\to\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2$, given on objects by $([k],[n])\mapsto[k]([n],\ldots,[n])$. This restricts to an equivalence between $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$ and $\text{Seg}^{\text{2-fold}}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$, and identifies $\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$ with the full subcategory of complete objects in $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$ in the sense of Rezk [26]. **Notation 3.1.** $\text{MaxCh}([k]\times[m])$ denotes the poset of maximal chains in the grid poset $[k]\times[m]$ (paths from $(0,0)$ to $(k,m)$), each of length $k+m$, with partial order generated by $\lrcorner\,\leq\,\urcorner$ (the rotated corner symbols in the source). It is isomorphic to the poset $\text{Sh}(k,m)$ of $(k,m)$-shuffles, ordered with $k+m$ as least element and $m+k$ as greatest element.

**Notation 3.2.** For non-negative integers $i\leq j$, $[i,j]$ denotes the ordered set $\{i<i+1<\cdots<j\}$, isomorphic to $[j-i]$. If $i>j$, $[i,j]=\emptyset$.

**Definition 3.3.** For $I=[n](x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ and $J=[m](y_1,\ldots,y_m)$ objects of $\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2$, the *Gray tensor product* $I\otimes^{\text{colax}}J$ is the 2-category with object set $\text{ob}([n])\times\text{ob}([m])$ and hom-posets
$$\text{Hom}((i,j),(i',j')):=\begin{cases}\text{MaxCh}([i,i']\times[j,j'])\times\prod_{i<s\leq i'}[x_s]\times\prod_{j<t\leq j'}[y_t],&i\leq i',\ j\leq j',\\\emptyset,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$
$$\cong\text{MaxCh}([i,i']\times[j,j'])\times I(i,i')\times J(j,j').$$
Composition combines composition in $I$ and $J$ with the natural inclusion
$$\text{MaxCh}([i,i']\times[j,j'])\times\text{MaxCh}([i',i'']\times[j',j''])\to\text{MaxCh}([i,i'']\times[j,j'']),$$
which combines a path from $(i,j)$ to $(i',j')$ with a path from $(i',j')$ to $(i'',j'')$ to give the subset of paths from $(i,j)$ to $(i'',j'')$ factoring through $(i',j')$. This gives a functor $\otimes^{\text{colax}}\colon\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2\times\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2\to\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$.

**Examples 3.4.** $[1](0)\otimes^{\text{colax}}[1](0)$ has 4 objects $00,01,10,11$, and $\text{Hom}(00,11)=(\lrcorner\leq\urcorner)$. The remaining hom categories are discrete: contractible if the indices are non-decreasing, empty if some index decreases. This is depicted as a colax square with corners $00,01,10,11$.

Similarly, $C_2\otimes^{\text{colax}}C_1=[1](1)\otimes^{\text{colax}}[1](0)$ has the shape of a cylinder (with side squares colax), with corners $00,01,10,11$. A diagram of shape $C_2\otimes^{\text{colax}}C_1$ in an $(\infty,2)$-category $\mathcal{X}$ consists of:
- objects $X,Y,X',Y'$;
- morphisms $f,g\colon X\to Y$, $f',g'\colon X'\to Y'$, $\xi\colon X\to X'$, $\eta\colon Y\to Y'$;
- 2-morphisms $\alpha\colon f\to g$, $\alpha'\colon f'\to g'$, $\phi\colon\eta f\to f'\xi$, $\psi\colon\eta g\to g'\xi$;
- an equivalence $\psi\circ(\eta\alpha)\simeq(\alpha'\xi)\circ\phi$ of 2-morphisms $\eta f\to g'\xi$.

**Assumption 3.5.** 
1. The functor $\otimes^{\text{colax}}$ satisfies the co-Segal condition (footnote 5: the Segal condition for $\phi^{\text{op}}\colon\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}$ in each variable) in each variable. Its unique extension to $\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2)\times\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2)\to\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$ preserving colimits in each variable therefore uniquely factors through a functor $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})\times\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})\to\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$ preserving colimits in each variable.
2. The functor $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})\times\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})\to\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$ takes fully faithful and essentially surjective morphisms in each variable to equivalences, and thus factors uniquely through a functor $\otimes^{\text{lax}}\colon\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}\times\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}\to\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$.
3. The restriction of $\otimes^{\text{lax}}$ to ordinary (strict) 2-categories agrees with the classical Gray tensor product. (Footnote 6: Only needed for gaunt 2-categories, meaning those with no non-trivial invertible 1- or 2-morphisms.)

**Remark 3.6.** Assumptions (1) and (2) have been proved by Y. Maehara [23], who shows that formally extending the ordinary Gray tensor product on $\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2$ gives a left Quillen bifunctor for $\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2$-sets. Other constructions of Gray tensor products in various models of $(\infty,2)$- (and $(\infty,n)$-) categories have appeared, including [7, 24, 6].

**Remark 3.7.** As observed by Ayala–Francis [1], a colimit diagram in $\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$ whose underlying diagrams of $\infty$-categories and $\infty$-groupoids are also colimit diagrams is a colimit in $\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$. This holds for the diagrams exhibiting the co-Segal condition for $\otimes^{\text{colax}}$, so one can take left Kan extensions to obtain a functor $\otimes^{\text{colax}}_{\text{Seg}}\colon\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})\times\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})\to\text{Seg}_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_2^{\text{op}}}(\mathcal{S})$, colimit-preserving in each variable, such that the square with vertical maps given by localization commutes with $\otimes^{\text{colax}}$ on $\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}$.

**Definition 3.8.** For $(\infty,2)$-categories $\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}$, $\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\text{colax}}\mathcal{Y}$ is the *colax Gray tensor product*. Also $\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\text{lax}}\mathcal{Y}:=\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\text{colax}}\mathcal{X}$ is the *lax Gray tensor product*.

**Definition 3.9.** The functor $\otimes^{\text{(co)lax}}$ preserves colimits in each variable, and so has adjoints $\text{FUN}(\text{–},\text{–})_{\text{(co)lax}}$ satisfying
$$\begin{split}\text{Map}_{\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}}(\mathcal{X},\text{FUN}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})_{\text{colax}})&\simeq\text{Map}_{\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\text{colax}}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z})\\&\simeq\text{Map}_{\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}}(\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\text{lax}}\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})\simeq\text{Map}_{\text{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}}(\mathcal{Y},\text{FUN}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z})_{\text{lax}}).\end{split}$$
A *(co)lax natural transformation* between functors $\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}$ is a functor of $(\infty,2)$-categories $\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\text{(co)lax}}\Delta^1\to\mathcal{Y}$. The $(\infty,2)$-category $\text{FUN}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})_{\text{(co)lax}}$ has functors as objects and (co)lax natural transformations as morphisms. The 2-morphisms are functors $\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\text{(co)lax}}C_2\to\mathcal{Y}$.
