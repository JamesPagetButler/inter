# Restatement

## Introduction

**Theorem A.** The natural embedding of $\mathsf{Pro}(\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{Ab}))$ into $\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab}))$ is not full. In particular, the left-hand side of the equation

$$\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathsf{Pro}(\mathsf{D}^{\geq 0}(\mathsf{Ab}))}\left(\text{“}\prod_{\omega}\text{”}\,\bigoplus_{\omega_{1}}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}\right)\not\cong\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathsf{D}^{\geq 0}(\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab}))}\left(\prod_{\omega}\bigoplus_{\omega_{1}}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}\right)$$

is concentrated in degree zero, but the right-hand side is not.

**Theorem B.** The following statements are all consistent; in fact each holds under the assumption that $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}[H]=0$ for all $n>0$ and all abelian groups $H$. Here $\mathbf{A}[H]$ is the natural generalization of the inverse system $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}[\mathbb{Z}]$ [54].

1. $\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathsf{D}^{\geq 0}(\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab}))}\left(\prod_{\omega}\bigoplus_{\omega}\mathbb{Z},\bigoplus_{\mu}\mathbb{Z}\right)$ is concentrated in degree zero for all cardinals $\mu$.
2. Whenever $H$ is an abelian group and $\mathbf{M}=(M_{i},\pi_{i,j},\omega)$ is an inverse sequence of countable abelian groups whose transition maps $\pi_{i,j}:M_{j}\to M_{i}$ are all surjective, then
$$\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab})}\left(\lim_{i<\omega}M_{i},H\right)=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}\displaylimits_{i<\omega}\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab})}\left(M_{i},H\right)$$
for all $n\geq 0$.
3. Whenever $p$ is a prime and $X$ is a separable solid $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$-Banach space, $\underline{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathsf{Solid}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{p})=0$ for all $i>0$.

**Theorem C.** Fix a natural number $n>0$, let $B^{n}$ denote the $n$-dimensional open ball, and let $Y^{n}$ denote the one-point compactification of $\coprod_{\omega_{1}}B^{n}$. Then
$$\overline{\mathrm{H}}_{n-1}\left(\coprod_{\omega}Y^{n}\right)\not\cong\bigoplus_{\omega}\overline{\mathrm{H}}_{n-1}\left(Y^{n}\right).$$

**Theorem D.**
1. For every field $K$ and $S\in\mathsf{ED}$, the constant sheaf $\mathcal{K}$ on $S$ is of injective dimension at most one.
2. If $S,T\in\mathsf{ED}$ are each Čech–Stone compactifications of discrete sets of cardinality at least $\aleph_{\omega}$, then the injective dimension of the constant sheaf $\mathcal{K}$ on $S\times T$ is infinite.
3. There exist compact projective condensed anima $S$ and $T$ whose product is not compact.

## Section 2

**Definition 2.1.** Fix an infinite cardinal $\kappa$ and let $\mathcal{C}=\mathsf{ProFin}_{\kappa}$. A $\kappa$-condensed set $F$ is a $\mathsf{Set}$-valued sheaf on the site $(\mathcal{C},\tau)$. Concretely, $F\in\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{Set})$ is $\kappa$-condensed iff:

1. $F(\varnothing)=*$ (i.e., $F(\varnothing)$ is a singleton, a terminal object of $\mathsf{Set}$);
2. the natural map $F(S\sqcup T)\to F(S)\times F(T)$ is a bijection for any $S,T\in\mathcal{C}$;
3. for any surjection $T\twoheadrightarrow S$ in $\mathcal{C}$ with induced maps $p_{1},p_{2}$ from the fiber product $T\times_{S}T$ to $T$, the natural map $F(S)\to\{x\in F(T)\mid p_{1}^{*}(x)=p_{2}^{*}(x)\}$ is a bijection.

Such $F$ form the objects of a category $\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Set})_{\kappa}$, with morphisms the natural transformations. For strong limit $\kappa\leq\lambda$ there are natural embeddings $\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Set})_{\kappa}\to\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Set})_{\lambda}$, and $\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Set})$ is the direct limit of this system of embeddings as $\kappa$ ranges over the strong limit cardinals.

**Theorem 2.2.** $\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab})$ is a complete and cocomplete abelian category.

**Question 2.3.** Is the natural map $\mathsf{Pro}(\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{Ab}))^{\mathtt{b}}\to\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab}))$ given by (3), “$\lim$” $C_{i}\mapsto\mathrm{Rlim}\,\underline{C}_{i}$, fully faithful?

**Definition 2.4.** Let $I$ and $J$ be nonempty sets. For any function $f:I\to[J]^{<\omega}$, let
$$A_{f}=\bigoplus_{X(f)}\mathbb{Z}.$$
For any $f\leq g$ in $({[J]^{<\omega}})^{I}$ there is a natural projection map $p_{fg}:A_{g}\to A_{f}$. These together comprise the inverse system
$$\mathbf{A}_{I,J}=\left(A_{f},p_{fg},({[J]^{<\omega}})^{I}\right).$$
For any abelian group $H$, let $\mathbf{A}_{I,J}[H]$ denote the inverse system defined by replacing each instance of $\mathbb{Z}$ above with $H$; similarly for the systems defined below.

**Proposition 2.5.** If the natural map $\mathsf{Pro}(\mathsf{D}^{\geq 0}(\mathsf{Ab}))^{\mathtt{b}}\to\mathsf{D}^{\geq 0}(\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab}))$ is fully faithful, then $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{I,J}[H]=0$ for all free abelian groups $H$, sets $I$ and $J$, and $n>0$.

**Remark 2.6.** Let $\kappa=|I|$ and $\lambda=|J|$. Bijections $I\to\kappa$ and $J\to\lambda$ determine an isomorphism of the systems $\mathbf{A}_{I,J}$ and $\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}$, so we may without loss of generality restrict to systems of the latter form. For nonzero cardinals $\kappa$ and $\lambda$, define inverse systems $\mathbf{B}_{\kappa,\lambda}=(B_{f},p_{fg},({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa})$ by $B_{f}=\prod_{X(f)}\mathbb{Z}$, and $(\mathbf{B}/\mathbf{A})_{\kappa,\lambda}=(B_{f}/A_{f},p_{fg},({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa})$, where the maps $p_{fg}$ are in each case the obvious projections. Note that
$$\lim\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}\cong\bigoplus_{\kappa}\prod_{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim\mathbf{B}_{\kappa,\lambda}\cong\prod_{\kappa\times\lambda}\mathbb{Z}.$$
Write $\mathbf{X}\restriction\Lambda$ for the restriction of an inverse system $\mathbf{X}$ to a suborder $\Lambda$ of its index set. Letting $[\omega]^{\mathrm{in}}$ denote the set of strictly initial segments of $\omega$, the restriction $\mathbf{A}_{\omega,\omega}\restriction({[\omega]^{\mathrm{in}}})^{\omega}$ is identified with the inverse system denoted $\mathbf{A}$ in [54] and [14] and much intervening literature. Since $[\omega]^{\mathrm{in}}$ is cofinal in $[\omega]^{<\omega}$, $\mathbf{A}_{\omega,\omega}$ and $\mathbf{A}$ are isomorphic in the category $\mathsf{pro}\text{-}\mathsf{Ab}$. The lemmas and definitions preceding Theorem 2.10 are routine generalizations to the systems $\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}$ of a sequence standard within the literature of $\mathbf{A}$.

**Lemma 2.7.** $\lim^{n}\mathbf{B}_{\kappa,\lambda}=0$ for $n>0$.

**Definition 2.8.** Let $\Phi=\langle\varphi_{f}:X(f)\to\mathbb{Z}\mid f\in({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa}\rangle$ be a family of functions.

- $\Phi$ is *coherent* if $\varphi_{f}=^{*}\varphi_{g}|_{X(f)}$ for all $f\leq g$ in $({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa}$.
- $\Phi$ is *trivial* if there is a function $\psi:\kappa\times\lambda\to\mathbb{Z}$ such that $\varphi_{f}=^{*}\psi|_{X(f)}$ for all $f\in({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa}$.

For any $(n+1)$-tuple $\vec{f}$, write $\vec{f}^{\,i}$ for the $n$-tuple formed by omitting the $i$th element of $\vec{f}$. Also write $X(\vec{f})$ for $\bigcap_{f\in\vec{f}}X(f)$, and let $\Phi=\langle\varphi_{\vec{f}}:X(\vec{f})\to\mathbb{Z}\mid\vec{f}\in(({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa})^{n}\rangle$ be a family of functions for some $n>1$. Here and throughout, such $\Phi$ are assumed *alternating*: $\varphi_{\vec{f}}=\mathrm{sign}(\sigma)\,\varphi_{\sigma\cdot\vec{f}}$ for all permutations $\sigma$ of $n$.

- $\Phi$ is *$n$-coherent* if $\sum_{i\leq n}(-1)^{i}\varphi_{\vec{f}^{\,i}}|_{X(\vec{f})}=^{*}0$ for all $\vec{f}\in(({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa})^{n+1}$.
- $\Phi$ is *$n$-trivial* if there exists $\Psi=\langle\psi_{\vec{f}}:X(\vec{f})\to\mathbb{Z}\mid\vec{f}\in(({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa})^{n-1}\rangle$ such that $\sum_{i<n}(-1)^{i}\psi_{\vec{f}^{\,i}}|_{X(\vec{f})}=^{*}\varphi_{\vec{f}}$ for all $\vec{f}\in(({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa})^{n}$.

**Lemma 2.9.** For all $n>0$, $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}=0$ if and only if every $n$-coherent family of functions indexed by $({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa}$ is trivial.

**Theorem 2.10.** The following hold for the inverse systems $\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}$:

1. If $\kappa$ or $\lambda$ is finite, then $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}=0$ for all $n>0$.
2. If $\kappa=\lambda=\aleph_{0}$, then it is consistent with the ZFC axioms that $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}=0$ for all $n>0$.
3. If $\kappa=\lambda=\aleph_{0}$ and $n>0$, then it is consistent with the ZFC axioms that $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}\neq 0$.
4. If $\mu\geq\kappa$, $\nu\geq\lambda$, and $n\geq 0$, then $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}$ forms a subgroup of $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{\mu,\nu}$.
5. If $\kappa=\aleph_{0}$ and $\lambda=\aleph_{1}$, then $\lim^{1}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}\neq 0$.

Moreover, for any nontrivial abelian group $H$, these results all equally hold if $\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}$ is replaced with $\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}[H]$.

**Corollary 2.11.** The functor of equation (3) is not full.

**Claim 2.12.** For any $\varphi:\omega\times\omega_{1}\to\mathbb{Z}$ there exist $\gamma<\omega_{1}$, an infinite $Y\subseteq\omega$, and $\alpha_{k}<\gamma$ with $\tau_{\gamma}(k,\alpha_{k})\neq\varphi(k,\alpha_{k})$ for all $k\in Y$.

**Theorem 2.13.** For any cardinals $\mu>0$ and $\lambda\geq\aleph_{0}$, the following assertions are equivalent:

1. $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{\aleph_{0},\lambda}[\bigoplus_{\mu}\mathbb{Z}]=0$ for all $n>0$.
2. $\mathrm{RHom}_{\mathsf{D}^{\geq 0}(\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab}))}\left(\prod_{\omega}\bigoplus_{\lambda}\underline{\mathbb{Z}},\bigoplus_{\mu}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\right)$ is concentrated in degree zero.
3. Whenever $\mathbf{M}=(M_{i},\pi_{i,j},\omega)$ is an inverse sequence of abelian groups of cardinality at most $\lambda$ whose transition maps $\pi_{i,j}:M_{j}\to M_{i}$ are all surjective, and $H$ is an abelian group of cardinality at most $\mu$, we have
$$\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab})}\left(\lim_{i<\omega}\underline{M_{i}},\underline{H}\right)=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}\displaylimits_{i<\omega}\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{\mathsf{Cond}(\mathsf{Ab})}\left(\underline{M_{i}},\underline{H}\right)$$
for all $n\geq 0$.

**Theorem 2.14.** Suppose that $\lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{\aleph_{0},\aleph_{0}}[H]=0$ for all $n>0$ and all abelian groups $H$. Then, for all primes $p$,
$$\mathrm{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}\left(\Big(\bigoplus_{\omega}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big)^{\wedge}_{p}\left[\tfrac{1}{p}\right],\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right)=\Big(\prod_{\omega}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big)\left[\tfrac{1}{p}\right].$$

**Theorem 2.15 (Goblot 1970).** Let $I$ index an inverse system $\mathbf{X}$ of abelian groups. If the cofinality of $I$ is less than or equal to $\aleph_{k}$, then $\lim^{n}\mathbf{X}=0$ for all $n>k+1$.

**Definition 2.16.** For any $n>0$, abelian group $H$, and ideals $\mathcal{I}$, $\mathcal{J}$ on a set $Y$, a family $\Phi=\langle\varphi_{\vec{I}}:\bigcap\vec{I}\to H\mid\vec{I}\in\mathcal{I}^{n}\rangle$ is *$n$-coherent (modulo $\mathcal{J}$)* if
$$\mathrm{supp}\left(\sum_{i\leq n}(-1)^{i}\varphi_{\vec{I}^{\,i}}\big|_{\bigcap\vec{I}}\right)\in\mathcal{J}$$
for any $\vec{I}\in\mathcal{I}^{n+1}$.

If $n=1$, $\Phi$ is *trivial* if there exists $\psi:Y\to H$ such that $\mathrm{supp}(\psi|_{I}-\varphi_{I})\in\mathcal{J}$ for all $I\in\mathcal{I}$. If $n>1$, $\Phi$ is *trivial* if there exists $\Psi=\langle\psi_{\vec{I}}:\bigcap\vec{I}\to H\mid\vec{I}\in\mathcal{I}^{n-1}\rangle$ such that
$$\mathrm{supp}\left(\sum_{i\leq n}(-1)^{i}\psi_{\vec{I}^{\,i}}\big|_{\bigcap\vec{I}}-\varphi_{\vec{I}}\right)\in\mathcal{J}$$
for all $\vec{I}\in\mathcal{I}^{n}$.

For any $\mathcal{I}$, $\mathcal{J}$, and $H$ as above, let $\mathbf{X}[\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},H]=(X_{I},\pi_{I,J},\mathcal{I})$ denote the $\mathcal{I}$-indexed inverse system with terms $X_{I}=\langle\varphi:I\to H\mid\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\in\mathcal{J}\rangle$ and transition maps $\pi_{I,J}:X_{J}\to X_{I}$, $\varphi\mapsto\varphi|_{I}$, for each $I\subseteq J$ in $\mathcal{I}$.

**Example 2.17.** If $Y=\kappa\times\lambda$, $\mathcal{I}=\{X(f)\mid f\in({[\lambda]^{<\omega}})^{\kappa}\}$, and $\mathcal{J}=[Y]^{<\omega}$, then $\mathbf{X}[\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},H]$ equals the system $\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}[H]$ of Definition 2.4.

**Lemma 2.18.** For all $n>0$ and $\mathcal{I}$, $\mathcal{J}$, and $H$ as in Definition 2.16, $\mathrm{R}^{n}\lim_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{X}[\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},H]\neq 0$ if and only if the associated $n$-coherent families of functions are all trivial.

**Example 2.19.** For any fixed regular cardinal $Y=\lambda$, letting $H=\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathcal{J}=[Y]^{<\omega}$, and $\mathcal{I}$ equal the bounded ideal on $\lambda$ (within which the initial segments of $\lambda$ are cofinal), recovers some of the most fundamental set-theoretic notions of coherence. The $n=1$ case of Definition 2.16 is that strongly associated to Todorcevic's walks apparatus [80]. Moreover,
$$\mathrm{R}^{n}\lim_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{X}[\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},H]\cong\mathrm{H}^{n}(\lambda;\mathcal{H})$$
for all $n>0$, where the right-hand terms are the sheaf cohomology groups of $\lambda$ (endowed with its order topology) with respect to the constant sheaf associated to $H$ (the argument is much as for [13, Thm. 3.2]).

**Theorem 2.20.** Suppose that $1\leq n<\omega$ and there is a stationary subset $S\subseteq S^{\aleph_{n+1}}_{\aleph_{n}}$ in the approachability ideal $I[\aleph_{n+1}]$. Then
$$\lim^{n+1}\mathbf{A}_{\aleph_{n},\aleph_{n+1}}\left[\bigoplus_{\omega_{n+1}}\mathbb{Z}\right]\neq 0.$$
If, additionally, $\diamondsuit(S)$ holds and there is a nontrivial $n$-coherent family $\langle\tau_{\vec{\eta}}:\bigwedge\vec{\eta}\to\mathbb{Z}\mid\vec{\eta}\in(\omega_{n})^{n}\rangle$, then $\lim^{n+1}\mathbf{A}_{\aleph_{n},\aleph_{n+1}}\neq 0$.

**Remark 2.21.** One may invoke, either by the description of the $\lim$ functor $\mathsf{Pro}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}$ in Section 2.1 or, in any particular case, by hand, the following: only cofinal phenomena matter in analyses of $\lim^{n}$ or $n$-coherence.

**Corollary 2.22.** If $\mathrm{V}=\mathrm{L}$, then $\lim^{n+1}\mathbf{A}_{\aleph_{n},\aleph_{n+1}}\neq 0$ for all $n<\omega$.

## Section 3

**Definition 3.1.** A homology theory $\mathrm{H}_{q}:\mathsf{Top}\to\mathsf{Ab}$ ($q\geq 0$) is *additive* if for every natural number $q$ and every family $\{X_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in A\}$ of topological spaces, the map
$$i^{*}_{q}:\bigoplus_{A}\mathrm{H}_{q}(X_{\alpha})\to\mathrm{H}_{q}\Big(\coprod_{A}X_{\alpha}\Big)$$
induced by the inclusion maps $i_{\alpha}:X_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\coprod_{A}X_{\alpha}$ is an isomorphism.

Similarly, a functor $F:\mathsf{Pro}(\mathsf{Ab})\to\mathsf{Ab}$ is *additive* if for every family $\{\mathbf{X}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in A\}$ of inverse systems of abelian groups, the map
$$i^{*}:\bigoplus_{A}F(\mathbf{X}_{\alpha})\to F\Big(\coprod_{A}\mathbf{X}_{\alpha}\Big)$$
induced by the inclusions $i_{\alpha}:\mathbf{X}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\coprod_{A}\mathbf{X}_{\alpha}$ is an isomorphism.

Adaptations of these definitions to subcategories $\mathcal{C}$ of $\mathsf{Top}$ and $\mathsf{Pro}(\mathsf{Ab})$ are straightforward; additivity there concerns only those sums of $\mathcal{C}$-objects which also fall within $\mathcal{C}$.

The strong homology group $\overline{\mathrm{H}}_{q}$ of a topological space $X$ is the $q$th homology group of the homotopy limit of the system of chain complexes given by applying the singular chain functor to any representative of the strong shape $\mathsf{sSh}(X)\in\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{Pro}(\mathsf{Pol}))$ of $X$ [29].

**Lemma 3.2.** For any $n,\lambda>0$, the space $Y^{n,\lambda}=\lim\mathbf{Y}^{n,\lambda}$ is homeomorphic to the one-point compactification of $\coprod_{\lambda}B^{n}$. Consequently, the system $\mathbf{Y}^{n,\lambda}$ defines a resolution of this space in the sense of [56] or [54].

**Theorem 3.3.** For any nonzero cardinals $\kappa$ and $\lambda$, let $\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}$ denote the inverse system of Definition 2.4. Then for any $n>0$, the strong homology groups of $Y^{n,\lambda}$ and its sums are as follows:
$$\overline{\mathrm{H}}_{q}(Y^{n,\lambda})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }q\neq 0,n\\ \prod_{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if }q=n\\ \mathbb{Z}&\text{if }q=0\end{cases}$$
and
$$\overline{\mathrm{H}}_{q}\left(\coprod_{\kappa}Y^{n,\lambda}\right)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }q>n\\ \lim^{n-q}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}&\text{if }0<q\leq n\\ \lim^{n}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}\oplus\bigoplus_{\kappa}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if }q=0.\end{cases}$$
In particular, if strong homology is additive, then $\lim^{s}\mathbf{A}_{\kappa,\lambda}=0$ for all $s,\kappa,\lambda>0$.

**Corollary 3.4.** There exists a non-metrizable ZFC counterexample to the additivity of strong homology.

**Corollary 3.5.** The functor $\lim^{n}:\mathsf{Pro}(\mathsf{Ab})\to\mathsf{Ab}$ is not additive for either $n=1$ or $n=2$.

**Notation 4.5.** If $K$ is a field or vector space, we write $\mathcal{K}$ for the associated constant sheaf on a topological space $X$. Where ambiguity may arise, we append a subscript indicating the base space, e.g. $\mathcal{K}_{X}$. The letter $\mathcal{I}$ is reserved for ideals, and $\mathcal{J}$ for injective resolutions or their constituent modules.

**Definition 4.6.** The stalk of a presheaf $\mathcal{F}$ at a point $x\in X$ is
$$\mathcal{F}_{x}:=\varinjlim_{U\ni x}\mathcal{F}(U).$$
A sheaf $\mathcal{G}$ on $X$ is fully determined by its stalks $\mathcal{G}_{x}$ ($x\in X$), so the sheafification $\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}$ of a presheaf $\mathcal{F}$ is the sheaf on $X$ with $\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{x}=\mathcal{F}_{x}$ for all $x\in X$.

Given sheaves $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{G}$ on $X$ and $Y$ respectively, and a map $f:X\to Y$, the direct image (pushforward) $f_{*}\mathcal{F}$ is the sheaf on $Y$ mapping each $U\in\tau(Y)$ to $\mathcal{F}(f^{-1}(U))$. The left adjoint to $f_{*}$ is the inverse image (pullback) $f^{*}$, sending $\mathcal{G}$ to the sheafification of the presheaf $U\mapsto\varinjlim_{V\supseteq f[U]}\mathcal{G}(U)$.

When $f$ is the inclusion of a locally closed subspace $X$ into $Y$, there is also $f_{!}$ sending $\mathcal{F}$ to the sheaf $f_{!}\mathcal{F}$ on $Y$ with stalks
$$(f_{!}\mathcal{F})_{y}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}_{y}&\text{if }y\in X\\ 0&\text{if }y\notin X.\end{cases}$$
In this case $f^{*}$ is left-inverse to $f_{!}$.

Suppose now that $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{G}$ are sheaves of $K$-modules for a fixed field $K$, and that $Y$ is a point. Then $\mathcal{G}$ identifies with a $K$-vector space $W$, $f^{*}\mathcal{G}$ is the constant sheaf $\mathcal{W}_{X}$, and $f_{*}\mathcal{F}$ is the vector space $\Gamma(X;\mathcal{F}):=\mathcal{F}(X)$ of global sections. The sheaf cohomology $K$-modules $\mathrm{H}^{q}(X;\mathcal{F})$ are, by definition, the evaluations at $\mathcal{F}$ of the right derived functors of $\Gamma(X;\cdot)$, viewed as a functor to $K$-$\mathsf{Mod}$ from the abelian category of sheaves of $K$-modules on $X$. By the aforementioned adjunction,
$$\mathrm{Ext}^{q}_{K}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{H}^{q}(X;\mathcal{F})$$
with the left-hand side computed in the category just described [45, 2.7.5]. There, injective objects have the standard definition; there are enough of them, and the injective dimension $\mathrm{injdim}_{X}(\mathcal{F})$ of a sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ is the minimal length of an injective resolution of $\mathcal{F}$.
