# Restatement

## Abstract

- The dimension of spacetime at the fundamental level is predicted by different approaches to quantum gravity to be generally not 4.
- The spectral dimension is the principal tool for measuring how the dimension changes between the IR and UV scales.
- Noncommutative geometry suggests that quantum spacetimes are characterized by a discrete complex set, the dimension spectrum.
- The paper claims that the spectral dimension and the dimension spectrum complement each other, and that the dimension spectrum is useful for understanding the UV behaviour of the spectral dimension.
- The paper performs an extended analysis of trouble spots and illustrates the results with two examples: the quantum sphere and the $\kappa$-Minkowski spacetime, for several Laplacians.
- For the quantum sphere, the spectral dimensions exhibit log-periodic oscillations whose amplitude decays rapidly as the deformation parameter tends to the classical value.
- For $\kappa$-Minkowski spacetime, no such oscillations occur for any of the three considered Laplacians.

## Introduction

**Spacetime and quantum gravity.**
- The spacetime concept as a differentiable manifold is fruitful for modelling gravitational phenomena, but the smooth geometry is generally expected to break down at small scales or high energies.
- Notions such as causality, distance and dimension must then be refined within the new mathematical structure.
- In some approaches the notion of spacetime survives only at an intermediate (semiclassical) level, while in the full theory spacetime breaks into discrete elements, determined by a fundamental length scale or a regularization cutoff (see Oriti 2018).
- The continuum limit (a transition from discrete proto-spacetime to continuous but quantum spacetime) is distinguished from the classical limit (recovery of the manifolds of general relativity).

**Effective dimension.**
- Calculations of the effective number of spacetime dimensions are a common method for characterising quantum spacetime (Mielczarek and Trześniewski 2018).
- Dimension can be defined in many ways, some mathematical and some physical, such as thermodynamic (Carlip 2017).
- The spectral dimension can be determined by spectral geometry or by a fictitious diffusion process. Its diffusion time parameter can be identified with the length scale at which the geometry is probed, so it is interpreted as a measure of how dimension changes with scale.
- The spectral dimension has been calculated for: causal dynamical triangulations (Ambjørn et al. 2005; Coumbe and Jurkiewicz 2015); Hořava–Lifshitz gravity (Hořava 2009); asymptotic safety (Lauscher and Reuter 2005); nonlocal quantum gravity (Modesto 2012); spin foam models (Steinhaus and Thürigen 2018); kinematical states of loop quantum gravity (Calcagni et al. 2015); causal sets (Carlip 2015; Belenchia et al. 2016); and multifractional spacetimes (Calcagni 2012).
- The almost universal prediction is dimensional reduction at the smallest scales (UV limit) to the value 2. General relativity would be power-counting renormalizable in this case.
- In some cases, values different from 2 are obtained in the UV limit, especially in non-classical phases of models with non-trivial phase diagrams.
- Similar behaviour occurs for quantum gravity in less than $3+1$ topological dimensions (Benedetti and Henson 2009; Hořava 2009; Lauscher and Reuter 2005; Calcagni 2012; see also Alesci and Arzano 2012).

**Noncommutative geometry.**
- The $\kappa$-Minkowski spacetime (Majid and Ruegg 1994), associated with the $\kappa$-Poincaré algebra (Lukierski et al. 1991; Lukierski et al. 1992), is widely considered in doubly/deformed special relativity and relative locality.
- The small-scale behaviour of the spectral dimension of $\kappa$-Minkowski spacetime, first calculated in Benedetti 2009, depends on the Laplacian (Arzano and Trześniewski 2014).
- For $3+1$-dimensional $\kappa$-Minkowski spacetime, there are at least three UV possibilities: the dimension decreasing to 3, growing to 6, or diverging.
- A noncommutative toy model with $\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$ momentum space exhibits dimensional reduction to 2 (Arzano and Nettel 2017).
- Complex values of the dimension of quantum spacetimes have been suggested (Calcagni 2017a; Calcagni 2017b; in the context of multifractional theories, Calcagni 2017c). Complex dimensions give rise to log-periodic oscillations in physical quantities (Dunne 2012), may affect the CMB spectrum (Calcagni 2017b), introduce stochastic noise to gravitational waves (Amelino-Camelia et al. 2017), and modify the thermodynamics of photons (Akkermans et al. 2010).
- Complex dimensions and log-periodic oscillations also arise in systems with discrete scale invariance, including some cases of holography (Flory 2018) and condensed matter physics, earthquakes and financial markets (Sornette 1998).

**Dimension spectrum.**
- Connes and Moscovici (1995) recognised that quantum spaces, understood as spaces determined by noncommutative algebras of observables, should be characterised by a discrete subset of the complex plane, the dimension spectrum, rather than a single number.
- In noncommutative geometry, the dimension spectrum is the set of poles of the spectral zeta functions of geometrical origin.
- These zeta functions are connected to the heat trace expansion via the Mellin transform (Gilkey 1995; Gilkey 2004; Eckstein and Iochum 2018).
- Spectral zeta functions and heat trace expansions are used in quantum field theory (Elizalde et al. 1994; Bytsenko et al. 2003; Avramidi 2002) and its noncommutative version (Gayral and Wulkenhaar 2013). The heat trace gives the one-loop effective action and describes short-distance behaviour of propagators, quantum anomalies and some non-perturbative effects (Vassilevich 2003).

**Podleś quantum sphere.**
- The Podleś quantum sphere (Podleś 1987) has a known dimension spectrum for a particular choice of geometry (Dąbrowski and Sitarz 2003). Its features (Eckstein et al. 2014) are:
  1. a dimension drop (dimensional reduction) from 2 to 0;
  2. poles of the spectral zeta function outside the real axis;
  3. second-order poles, characteristic of spaces with conical singularities (Lescure 2001);
  4. a convergent heat trace expansion, in contrast to smooth manifolds where it is only asymptotic.
- The paper states that these features are shared by two other Laplacians on the quantum sphere, but the fine details of their heat trace expansions differ (Arzano and Trześniewski 2014 for $\kappa$-Minkowski).
- The effective dimensionality of spaces with topological dimension lower than 4 is relevant for entanglement entropy (Arzano and Calcagni 2017), since entanglement entropy can be derived from the heat trace over the boundary of a region (Nesterov and Solodukhin 2011).

**Outline of the paper.**
- Section II: definitions of both concepts (spectral dimension and dimension spectrum) and identification of trouble spots.
- Section III: dimension spectra and spectral dimensions of three Laplacians on the Podleś quantum sphere (Podleś 1987). The paper states that the dimension drop observed by Benedetti (Benedetti 2009) has a finer structure with square-logarithmic decay and log-periodic oscillations. For $q$ close to the classical value 1, the oscillation amplitude is very small. Non-real numbers in the dimension spectrum attest to their presence for any $q$.
- Section IV: dimension spectra of three Laplacians on $\kappa$-Minkowski spacetime in 2, 3 and 4 topological dimensions, used to identify leading and subleading short-scale behaviour of the spectral dimensions from Arzano and Trześniewski 2014. This reveals an ambiguity in the definition of the spectral dimension related to the order of the Laplacian-like operator.
- Section V: summary and consequences for model-building in quantum gravity.
