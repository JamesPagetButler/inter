# Restatement

## Abstract

- A formulation of the noncommutative geometry of the standard model of particle physics with a Lorentzian signature metric is presented.
- Fermion doubling is eliminated in the Lorentzian case by modifying Connes' internal space geometry ["Gravity coupled with matter and the foundation of non-commutative geometry," Commun. Math. Phys. 182, 155–176 (1996)], so that the internal geometry has signature 6 (mod 8) rather than 0.
- The fermionic part of the Connes–Chamseddine spectral action is formulated. It is shown to admit an extension with right-handed neutrinos and the mass terms required by the seesaw mechanism of neutrino mass generation.

## Introduction and Section 1: The Euclidean model

**Background.** Connes discovered the hidden geometrical structure of the standard model using noncommutative geometry [C]. From the physical point of view, this model has two defects:

1. the space-time metric is Euclidean;
2. each particle appears four times, not once [LMMS, GIS].

**Purpose.** The paper gives the analogous geometric framework for the standard model with Lorentzian signature, which also solves the particle quadrupling problem. The model allows neutrino masses to be introduced by the seesaw mechanism.

**Real spectral triple.** Connes' formulation is a real spectral triple [CR]. It consists of:

- a Hilbert space $\mathcal H$;
- an algebra $\mathcal A$ of bounded operators on $\mathcal H$;
- a self-adjoint Dirac operator $D$ on $\mathcal H$;
- a chirality operator $\gamma$ on $\mathcal H$, with $\gamma=\gamma^*$ and $\gamma^2=1$, whose two eigenvalues label left-handed particles (eigenvalue $1$) and right-handed particles (eigenvalue $-1$);
- an antilinear map $J$ on $\mathcal H$, called the real structure.

The data satisfy axioms, some of which depend on the signature $\sigma \bmod 8$. For even $\sigma$, the axioms are

$$J^2=\epsilon,\qquad J\gamma=\epsilon''\gamma J,$$

where $\epsilon,\epsilon''=\pm1$ are given by the table in Eq. (3). The parameter $\sigma$ was previously identified by Connes as the dimension of the spectral triple. This identification holds for Euclidean geometries, where signature and dimension coincide. For a Lorentzian metric, signature and dimension differ.

**Product structure of the Euclidean model.** The Euclidean standard model is a product of:

- a real spectral triple $(\mathcal H_M,\mathcal A_M,D_M,\gamma_M,J_M)$ for the space-time manifold $M$;
- a finite-dimensional real spectral triple $(\mathcal H_F,\mathcal A_F,D_F,\gamma_F,J_F)$ for a noncommutative "internal space".

The Hilbert space $\mathcal H_F$ has a basis of the 45 elementary fermions, counting left- and right-handed particles separately, together with 45 further basis vectors for the antiparticles. The space $\mathcal H_M$ is the Hilbert space of Dirac spinors on $M$. The total fermion space is $\mathcal H=\mathcal H_M\otimes\mathcal H_F$ (Eq. (4)). The particle quadrupling is apparent from this construction.

**Quadrupling.** Take a left-handed electron $e_L\in\mathcal H_F$. It determines the subspace of wavefunctions

$$\phi=\psi\otimes e_L+\psi'\otimes\overline{e}_L\in\mathcal H,$$

where $\overline e_L=J_F e_L$ is the independent basis vector for the antiparticle, and $\psi,\psi'$ are arbitrary Dirac spinors. In the standard model, $e_L$ should carry only a Weyl spinor, so the degrees of freedom are overcounted fourfold.

**Attempted reduction.** One would like to reduce $\mathcal H$ by imposing the conditions [BK]

$$J\phi=\phi,\qquad \gamma\phi=\phi,$$

where $J=J_M\otimes J_F$ and $\gamma=\gamma_M\otimes\gamma_F$ are the real structure and chirality on $\mathcal H$. This is not consistent as stated. Consistency would require $J^2=1$ and $J\gamma=\gamma J$ (Eqs. (7) and (8)), which hold only when the total signature is zero. The total signature of the Euclidean model is four.
