# Restatement

## 1. Introduction

Let $M$ be a compact spin Riemannian manifold with a fixed spin structure. The canonically given (spin-) Dirac operator is an elliptic, essentially self-adjoint first-order differential operator. Its spectrum is therefore real, consists only of eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, and has $\pm\infty$ as its only accumulation points.

Explicit computations of Dirac spectra are usually possible only in the presence of enough symmetries; there are few such computations (cf. [Bä06, §1.1.2], [Gi, §2]).

On the sphere $S^n$, Hitchin [Hi74] determined the Dirac spectrum in dimension 3. S. Sulanke computed it in all dimensions in her Ph.D. thesis [Su79]. C. Bär gave an alternative proof in his Ph.D. thesis [Bä91] (see also [Bä96]). The Dirac spectrum of $S^n$ consists of the eigenvalues $\pm\left(k+\frac{n}{2}\right)$, $k\geq 0$, with multiplicities
$$2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}.$$

Let $\Gamma\subset\operatorname{SO}(n+1)$ act freely on $S^n$, and set $M_\Gamma:=\Gamma\backslash S^n$. Here $n=2m-1$, since the only manifold properly covered by an even-dimensional sphere is the real projective space. Spin structures on $M_\Gamma$ are in one-to-one correspondence with homomorphisms $\tau:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Spin}(2m)$ satisfying $\Theta\circ\tau=\operatorname{Id}_\Gamma$, where $\Theta:\operatorname{Spin}(2m)\to\operatorname{SO}(2m)$ is the universal covering homomorphism.

With $\lambda_k:=k+\tfrac{n}{2}=k+m-\tfrac12$, the multiplicity $\operatorname{mult}_{(M_\Gamma,\tau)}(\pm\lambda_k)$ satisfies
$$0\leq\operatorname{mult}_{(M_\Gamma,\tau)}(\pm\lambda_k)\leq 2^{m-1}\binom{k+2m-2}{2m-2}. \tag{1}$$

Bär [Bä96] gave closed formulas for the generating functions $F_{(M_\Gamma,\tau)}^{\pm}(z):=\sum_{k\geq 0}\operatorname{mult}_{(M_\Gamma,\tau)}(\pm\lambda_k)\,z^k$:
$$F_{(M_\Gamma,\tau)}^{\pm}(z)=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\chi^{\mp}(\tau(\gamma))-z\cdot\chi^{\pm}(\tau(\gamma))}{\det(1_{2m}-z\gamma)}, \tag{1.1}$$
where $\chi^\pm$ denote the half-spin characters. Formula (1.1) is used for particular cases (see [MPT11, §7] and [Teh13, §9–10]).

For a $(2m-1)$-dimensional lens space $L:=L(q;s_1,\dots,s_m)$ (notation in Subsection 2.2) with a spin structure $\tau$, one associates an affine congruence lattice $\mathcal{L}$ (Lemma 4.1) inside $\mathbb{E}^m:=(\tfrac12,\dots,\tfrac12)+\mathbb{Z}^m$. For $q$ odd, $L$ has one spin structure and
$$\mathcal{L}=\left\{\tfrac12(a_1,\dots,a_m)\in\mathbb{E}^m:\ a_1s_1+\dots+a_ms_m\equiv 0\pmod q\right\}. \tag{1.2}$$

The main result, Theorem 4.3, gives
$$\operatorname{mult}_{(L,\tau)}(-\lambda_k)=\sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{r+m-2}{m-2}N_{\mathcal{L}}(r,k-r),\qquad \operatorname{mult}_{(L,\tau)}(+\lambda_k)=\sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{r+m-2}{m-2}N_{\mathcal{L}}(r+1,k-r), \tag{1.3}$$
where $N_{\mathcal{L}}(r,k)$ is the number of $\mu=\tfrac12(a_1,\dots,a_m)\in\mathcal{L}$ with $\|\mu\|_1=k+\tfrac m2$, where $\|\mu\|_1:=\tfrac12\sum|a_j|$, and whose number of negative entries is congruent to $r$ modulo $2$.

Corollary 4.5 characterises Dirac isospectrality of lens spaces with fixed spin structures in terms of oriented $\|\cdot\|_1$-isospectrality of the associated lattices. Theorems 5.2, 5.4 and 5.6 give infinite families of Dirac isospectral lens spaces. Proposition 6.1 is a finiteness result, and Corollary 6.2 reduces Dirac isospectrality to finitely many multiplicity comparisons. Conjecture 6.3 and Remarks 6.4–6.7 concern dimension $7$, $p$-isospectrality, strong isospectrality, and homotopy equivalence.

Other known non-trivial examples of Dirac isospectral spin manifolds are [Bä96], [AB98], [MP06] (cf. [Gi, §6.1] and [P06]). The generalized Sunada method also applies to Dirac operators on spherical space forms [Bä96, Cor. 2], but cannot produce non-isometric lens spaces [Bä96, p. 79], and its examples are strongly isospectral, whereas strongly isospectral lens spaces are isometric (cf. [LMR13, Prop. 7.2]).

## 2. Preliminaries

**Proposition 2.1.** Let $M_\Gamma:=\Gamma\backslash S^{2m-1}$ be a spherical space form with spin structure induced by $\tau:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Spin}(2m)$. For each integer $k\geq 0$, let $\pi_k^\pm$ be the irreducible representations of $\operatorname{Spin}(2m)$ with highest weights
$$\Lambda_k^\pm:=\tfrac12\big((2k+1)\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\dots+\varepsilon_{m-1}\pm\varepsilon_m\big).$$
Then the eigenvalues of the Dirac operator $D$ on the spinor bundle of $M_\Gamma$ are $\pm\lambda_k$, with $\lambda_k:=k+\frac{2m-1}{2}$, and multiplicity
$$\operatorname{mult}_{(M_\Gamma,\tau)}(\pm\lambda_k):=\dim V_{\pi_k^\mp}^{\tau(\Gamma)}.$$

**Proposition 2.2.** Let $L=L(q;s_1,\dots,s_m)$ and $L'=L(q;s_1',\dots,s_m')$ be lens spaces. The following are equivalent.
1. $L$ is isometric to $L'$.
2. $L$ is diffeomorphic to $L'$.
3. $L$ is homeomorphic to $L'$.
4. There exist $\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$ coprime to $q$, $\epsilon_1,\dots,\epsilon_m\in\{\pm1\}$, and a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1,\dots,m\}$ such that $\ell\epsilon_j s_j\equiv s'_{\sigma(j)}\pmod q$.

**Remark 2.3.** The term $(-1)^{h_{q;s}}$ is included in (2.13) so that the spin structure is associated to a lens space independently of its parameters. Without this term, $L(q;s_1,\dots,s_m)$ and $L(q;s_1+q,s_2,\dots,s_m)$ coincide, but their spin structures are switched: $\tau_0=\tau'_1$ and $\tau_1=\tau'_0$.

**Theorem 2.4.** Let $L=L(q;s_1,\dots,s_m)$.
- If $q$ is odd, $L$ admits (up to equivalence) only one spin structure, induced by (2.12).
- If $q$ is even, $L$ admits no spin structure when $m$ is odd, and admits two inequivalent spin structures when $m$ is even, induced by $\tau_0$ and $\tau_1$ as in (2.13).

**Proposition 2.5.** Let $L=L(q;s_1,\dots,s_m)$ and $L'=L(q;s'_1,\dots,s'_m)$ be lens spaces, with projections $\pi:S^{2m-1}\to L$ and $\pi':S^{2m-1}\to L'$. Let $\sigma$ be a permutation of $\{1,\dots,m\}$, let $\epsilon_1,\dots,\epsilon_m\in\{\pm1\}$, and let $\Psi$ be the associated isometry of $S^{2m-1}$. Then there is an isometry $\psi:L\to L'$ with $\pi'\circ\Psi=\psi\circ\pi$ if and only if there exists $\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$ such that
$$\ell\epsilon_js_j\equiv s'_{\sigma(j)}\pmod q\qquad\text{for all } j. \tag{2.15}$$
In this case, $\psi$ preserves or reverses orientation according to $\epsilon=1$ or $\epsilon=-1$, where $\epsilon=\epsilon_1\cdots\epsilon_m$. Moreover, when this happens and $q$ is even, $\psi$ takes $\tau_h$ to $\tau'_{h'}$, where $\rho_j:=(\ell\epsilon_js_j-s'_{\sigma(j)})/q$ and
$$h'=h+h_{q;s}+h_{q;s'}+\sum_j\rho_j.$$

## 3. Affine congruence lattices

**Proposition 3.1.** Let $q$ be a positive odd integer, and let $s,s'\in\mathbb{Z}^m$ have all coordinates coprime to $q$. Let $\sigma$, $\epsilon_j$ and $\varphi$ be as in (3.4). Then $\varphi(\mathcal{L}(q;s))=\mathcal{L}(q;s')$ if and only if there exists $\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$ coprime to $q$ such that
$$\ell\epsilon_js_j\equiv s'_{\sigma(j)}\pmod q\qquad\text{for every } j.$$

**Proposition 3.2.** Let $q$ be a positive even integer, and let $s,s'\in\mathbb{Z}^m$ have all coordinates coprime to $q$. Let $\sigma$, $\epsilon_j$, $\rho\in\{0,1\}$ and $\varphi$ be as in (3.4). Then $\varphi(\mathcal{L}(q;s;h))=\mathcal{L}(q;s';h+\rho)$ for all $h\in\{0,1\}$ if and only if there exists $\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$ coprime to $q$ such that
$$\ell\epsilon_js_j\equiv s'_{\sigma(j)}\pmod q\ \text{ for all } j,\qquad\text{and}\qquad \rho\equiv\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{\ell\epsilon_js_j-s'_{\sigma(j)}}{q}\pmod 2.$$

**Corollary 3.3.** Let $q$ be a positive integer, and let $s,s'\in\mathbb{Z}^m$ have all coordinates coprime to $q$.
- If $q$ is odd, there is an $\epsilon$-oriented isometry $\psi$ between $L(q;s)$ and $L(q;s')$ if and only if there is an $\epsilon$-oriented $\|\cdot\|_1$-isometry $\varphi$ between $\mathcal{L}(q;s)$ and $\mathcal{L}(q;s')$.
- If $q$ is even and $\rho\in\{0,1\}$, there is an $\epsilon$-oriented isometry $\psi$ between $L(q;s)$ and $L(q;s')$ taking $\tau_h$ to $\tau'_{h+\rho}$ for $h=0,1$ if and only if there is an $\epsilon$-oriented $\|\cdot\|_1$-isometry $\varphi$ with $\varphi(\mathcal{L}(q;s;h))=\mathcal{L}(q;s;h+\rho)$ for $h=0,1$.

## 4. Dirac spectrum of lens spaces

**Lemma 4.1.** Let $L(q;s_1,\dots,s_m)=\Gamma\backslash S^{2m-1}$ be a lens space with spin structure $\tau$. Then
$$\dim V_{\pi_k^\pm}^{\tau(\Gamma)}=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma;\tau}}m_{\pi_k^\pm}(\mu),$$
where
$$\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma;\tau}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}(q;s) & \text{if } q \text{ is odd},\\ \mathcal{L}(q;s;h+h_{q;s}) & \text{if } q \text{ is even and } \tau=\tau_h.\end{cases}$$

**Lemma 4.2.** Let $k\geq0$ be an integer, $\mu\in P(G)$, and $r:=\|\Lambda_k^\pm\|_1-\|\mu\|_1$.
- If $\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^m$, then $m_{\pi_k^\pm}(\mu)=0$.
- If $\mu\in\mathbb{E}^m$, then
$$m_{\pi_k^+}(\mu)=\begin{cases}\binom{r+m-2}{m-2} & \text{if } r\geq0 \text{ and } R(\mu)\equiv r\pmod 2,\\ 0 & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}$$
$$m_{\pi_k^-}(\mu)=\begin{cases}\binom{r+m-2}{m-2} & \text{if } r\geq0 \text{ and } R(\mu)\equiv r+1\pmod 2,\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$

**Theorem 4.3.** Let $L=\Gamma\backslash S^{2m-1}$ be a lens space with spin structure $\tau$, and let $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma,\tau}$ be its associated affine congruence lattice. For $k\geq0$, let $\lambda_k=k+\tfrac{2m-1}{2}$. Then the eigenvalues of the Dirac operator on $L$ are $\pm\lambda_k$, with multiplicities
$$\operatorname{mult}_{(L,\tau)}(-\lambda_k)=\sum_{r=0}^k\binom{r+m-2}{m-2}N_{\mathcal{L}}(r,k-r),$$
$$\operatorname{mult}_{(L,\tau)}(+\lambda_k)=\sum_{r=0}^k\binom{r+m-2}{m-2}N_{\mathcal{L}}(r+1,k-r).$$

**Definition 4.4.** Two subsets $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{L}'$ of $\mathbb{E}^m$ are *oriented $\|\cdot\|_1$-isospectral* if $N_{\mathcal{L}}(\epsilon,k)=N_{\mathcal{L}'}(\epsilon,k)$ for every non-negative integer $k$ and every $\epsilon=0,1$. They are *$\|\cdot\|_1$-isospectral* if
$$N_{\mathcal{L}}(0,k)+N_{\mathcal{L}}(1,k)=N_{\mathcal{L}'}(0,k)+N_{\mathcal{L}'}(1,k)\qquad\text{for every } k\geq0.$$

**Corollary 4.5.** Let $L$ and $L'$ be lens spaces with spin structures $\tau,\tau'$ and associated affine congruence lattices $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{L}'$. Then $L$ and $L'$ are Dirac isospectral if and only if $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{L}'$ are oriented $\|\cdot\|_1$-isospectral.

**Corollary 4.6.** Under the assumptions of Corollary 4.5, when $m$ is odd (so $q$ is odd), $L$ and $L'$ are Dirac isospectral if and only if $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{L}'$ are $\|\cdot\|_1$-isospectral.

**Remark 4.7.** One may define *reversed $\|\cdot\|_1$-isospectrality* by $N_{\mathcal{L}}(\epsilon,k)=N_{\mathcal{L}'}(\epsilon+1,k)$ for every $\epsilon=0,1$. Under the hypotheses of Corollary 4.5, $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{L}'$ are reversed oriented $\|\cdot\|_1$-isospectral if and only if $L$ and $L'$ are *inverse Dirac isospectral*, that is, $F_{(L,\tau)}^\pm(z)=F_{(L',\tau')}^\mp(z)$ (see [Bo11, Prop. 2.15]). Changing the orientation of one lens space gives the usual Dirac isospectrality. Alternatively, multiplying exactly one parameter of $L'$ by $-1$ gives an isometric lens space $L''$ (the isometry reverses orientation) with $F_{(L,\tau)}^\pm(z)=F_{(L'',\tau'')}^\pm(z)$. Hence $L$ and $L''$ are Dirac isospectral.

## 5. Examples of Dirac isospectral lens spaces

**Remark 5.1.** Let $L:=L(16;1,3,5,7)$, which admits two spin structures $\tau_0,\tau_1$ by Proposition 2.4. The map $\Psi:S^7\to S^7$, $(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)\mapsto(z_3,\overline{z}_1,\overline{z}_4,z_2)$ induces an isometry of $L$ taking $\tau_0$ to $\tau_1$. This follows from Proposition 2.5 with $\sigma=(13)(34)(42)$, $\epsilon_2=\epsilon_3=-\epsilon_1=-\epsilon_4=1$, and $\ell=11$.

**Theorem 5.2.** Let $q=40$. For each $r\geq1$, set $m=4r+2$, and for each $0\leq p\leq\tfrac{m-2}{4}=r$ let
$$s^{(p)}=\big(\underbrace{1,11,\dots,1,11}_{m-2p},\ \underbrace{21,31,\dots,21,31}_{2p}\big).$$
Then the lens spaces in the family $\{L(q,s^{(p)}):0\leq p\leq r\}$ are pairwise non-isometric and, when each is endowed with the spin structure $\tau_0^{(p)}$ (see (2.13)), they are pairwise Dirac isospectral. The dimension of the lens spaces is $8r+3$, and the cardinality of the family is $r+1$.

**Remark 5.3.** For every $0\leq p\leq\tfrac{m-2}{4}$, one has $\mathcal{L}(40;s^{(0)})=\mathcal{L}(40;s^{(p)})$ (see (3.2)), although $L(40;s^{(0)})$ and $L(40;s^{(p)})$ are not isometric. Moreover, $\mathcal{L}(40;s^{(p)};0)$ and $\mathcal{L}(40;s^{(p)};1)$ are $\|\cdot\|_1$-isometric, so there is an isometry of $L(40;s^{(p)})$ switching $\tau_0^{(p)}$ and $\tau_1^{(p)}$.

**Theorem 5.4.** For any $r\geq1$, let
$$L=L(32r;\,1,\,1+4r,\,1+16r,\,1+28r).$$
Then $L$ admits no isometry taking $\tau_0$ to $\tau_1$, but $L$ is Dirac isospectral to itself when equipped with the two different spin structures.

**Lemma 5.5.** Let $\mu=\tfrac12(a,b,c,d)\in\mathcal{L}$. Then the following are equivalent:
- $b-d\equiv2\pmod 4$;
- $b+d\equiv0\pmod 4$;
- $a+c\equiv0\pmod 4$;
- $a-c\equiv2\pmod 4$.

Also, the following are equivalent:
- $b-d\equiv0\pmod 4$;
- $b+d\equiv2\pmod 4$;
- $a+c\equiv2\pmod 4$;
- $a-c\equiv0\pmod 4$.

**Theorem 5.6.** For any odd positive integer $r\geq7$, consider the lens spaces
$$L=L(r^2;\,1,\,1+r,\,1+2r,\,1+4r),\qquad L'=L(r^2;\,1,\,1-r,\,1-2r,\,1-4r),$$
each with its unique spin structure ($q:=r^2$ is odd). Then $L$ and $L'$ are non-isometric and Dirac isospectral.

**Lemma 5.7.** Let $r$ be an odd positive integer, and let $k=\omega r+k_0$ with $0\leq k_0<r$ and $\omega\geq0$. The number of elements $\mu\in\mathcal{L}$ lying in the orthant ${+}{+}{+}{-}$, ${+}{+}{-}{+}$, ${+}{-}{+}{+}$ or $\{-{+}{+}{+}\}$, with $\|\mu\|_1=k+2$, equals
$$\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\omega+\lfloor\frac{k_0-z_0}{r}\rfloor}\ \sum_{y_0=0}^{r-1}\ \sum_{\beta=\gamma+\lceil\frac{z_0-y_0}{r}\rceil}^{\omega+\lfloor\frac{k_0-y_0}{r}\rfloor}\left(\omega-\beta+1+\left\lfloor\frac{k_0-x_{y_0,\gamma}}{r}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{x_{y_0,\gamma}-y_0}{r}\right\rfloor\right), \tag{5.17}$$
where:
- $z_0$ is the unique integer with $0\leq z_0<r$ and $2z_0\equiv k+1\pmod r$;
- $x_{y_0,\gamma}$ is the unique integer with $0\leq x_{y_0,\gamma}<r$ satisfying
$$\begin{cases}
2x_{y_0,\gamma}\equiv 1-2y_0+12z_0-2\big(\omega-2\gamma+\tfrac{k_0+1-2z_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {+}{+}{+}{-},\\
2x_{y_0,\gamma}\equiv -3-6y_0+12z_0-2\big(\omega-2\gamma+\tfrac{k_0+1-2z_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {+}{+}{-}{+},\\
4x_{y_0,\gamma}\equiv -5-4y_0+10z_0-2\big(\omega-2\gamma+\tfrac{k_0+1-2z_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {+}{-}{+}{+},\\
2x_{y_0,\gamma}\equiv -7-4y_0+8z_0-2k_0-2\big(\omega-2\gamma+\tfrac{k_0+1-2z_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {-}{+}{+}{+}.
\end{cases} \tag{5.18}$$

Formula (5.17) also holds for $\mathcal{L}'$ with $x_{y_0,\gamma}$ replaced by $x'_{y_0,\gamma}$, where $x'_{y_0,\gamma}$ is the unique integer with $0\leq x'_{y_0,\gamma}<r$ satisfying
$$\begin{cases}
2x'_{y_0,\gamma}\equiv 1-2y_0+12z_0+2\big(\omega-2\gamma+\tfrac{k_0+1-2z_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {+}{+}{+}{-},\\
2x'_{y_0,\gamma}\equiv -3-6y_0+12z_0+2\big(\omega-2\gamma+\tfrac{k_0+1-2z_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {+}{+}{-}{+},\\
4x'_{y_0,\gamma}\equiv -5-4y_0+10z_0+2\big(\omega-2\gamma+\tfrac{k_0+1-2z_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {+}{-}{+}{+},\\
2x'_{y_0,\gamma}\equiv -7-4y_0+8z_0-2k_0+2\big(\omega-2\gamma+\tfrac{k_0+1-2z_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {-}{+}{+}{+}.
\end{cases} \tag{5.19}$$

**Lemma 5.8.** Let $r$ be an odd positive integer, and let $k=\omega r+k_0$ with $0\leq k_0<r$ and $\omega\geq0$. The number of elements $\mu\in\mathcal{L}$ lying in the orthant ${+}{+}{-}{-}$, ${+}{-}{+}{-}$ or ${+}{-}{-}{+}$, with $\|\mu\|_1=k+2$, equals

- $x_{z_0,\beta}$ is the unique integer with $0\leq x_{z_0,\beta}<r$ satisfying

2x_{z_0,\beta}\equiv 5+6y_0+4z_0-2\big(\omega-2\beta+\tfrac{k_0+1-2y_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {+}{+}{-}{-},\\
4x_{z_0,\beta}\equiv 3+6y_0+6z_0-2\big(\omega-2\beta+\tfrac{k_0+1-2y_0}{r}\big)\pmod r & \text{for } {+}{-}{+}{-},\\

\end{cases} \tag{5.27}$$

**Remark 5.9.** The proof of Theorem 5.6 is involved, since it gives formulas for $N_{\mathcal{L}}(r,k)$ for every $k$ and $r$. These formulas allow the Dirac spectrum to be computed explicitly via Theorem 4.3. The formulas can be checked by computer for low values of $r$ and $k$.

**Remark 5.10.** The computations in the next section suggest that Theorem 5.6 generalises to the pairs
$$L=L(r^2t;\,1,\,1+rt,\,1+2rt,\,1+4rt),\qquad L'=L(r^2t;\,1,\,1-rt,\,1-2rt,\,1-4rt),$$
where $r$ is an odd positive integer and $t$ is an arbitrary positive integer. When $t$ is even, $q=r^2t$ is even, so $L$ and $L'$ have two spin structures $\tau_0,\tau_1$ and $\tau'_0,\tau'_1$ respectively. Then $(L,\tau_0)$ and $(L',\tau'_0)$ are Dirac isospectral, and so are $(L,\tau_1)$ and $(L',\tau'_1)$.

## 6. Computational approach

**Proposition 6.1.** Let $\mathcal{L}$ be an affine congruence lattice of a spin lens space of dimension $2m-1$ with fundamental group of order $q$. Then
$$N_{\mathcal{L}}(\epsilon,k)=\sum_{\beta=0}^{\lfloor k/q\rfloor}\binom{\beta+m-1}{m-1}N^{\mathrm{red}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\epsilon,k-\beta q). \tag{6.1}$$

**Corollary 6.2.** Two $(2m-1)$-dimensional spin lens spaces $(L,\tau)$ and $(L',\tau')$ with fundamental groups of order $q$ are Dirac isospectral if and only if
$$\operatorname{mult}_{(L,\tau)}(\pm\lambda_k)=\operatorname{mult}_{(L',\tau')}(\pm\lambda_k)$$
for all $0\leq k<mq$, where $\pm\lambda_k=\pm\big(k+\tfrac{2m-1}{2}\big)$.

**Conjecture 6.3.** Two Dirac isospectral spin lens spaces of dimension $n\equiv1\pmod 4$ are necessarily isometric.

**Remark 6.4.** There seems to be a relation between Dirac isospectrality and $p$-isospectrality for all $p$ among $7$-dimensional lens spaces. Most examples of Dirac isospectral lens spaces in dimension $7$ are also $p$-isospectral for all $p$. For instance, the pairs in Theorem 5.6 are $p$-isospectral for all $p$. Indeed, $(0,1,2,4)$ is hereditarily good modulo any odd $r$ in the sense of [DD14, Def. 1], so by [DD14, Thm. 1] the lens spaces $L(r^2t;1,1+rt,1+2rt,1+4rt)$ and $L(r^2t;1,1-rt,1-2rt,1-4rt)$ are $p$-isospectral for all $p$, for every odd $r$ and every $t\geq1$.

Exceptions are the following pairs of Dirac isospectral lens spaces, which are not $p$-isospectral for any $p$:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| $L(75;1,4,14,16)$ | $L(150;1,11,29,31)$ | $L(300;1,19,41,79)$ |
| $L(75;1,4,11,19)$ | $L(150;1,11,19,41)$ | $L(300;1,19,59,61)$ |
| $L(75;1,4,11,34)$ | $L(150;1,11,31,59)$ | $L(300;1,19,41,139)$ |
| $L(75;1,4,14,31)$ | $L(150;1,11,19,71)$ | $L(300;1,19,59,121)$ |

Here, when $q$ is even, the lens spaces in each pair are Dirac isospectral if both are equipped with $\tau_0$ or both with $\tau_1$.

In the inverse direction, $L(100;1,9,11,29)$ and $L(100;1,9,11,31)$ are $p$-isospectral for all $p$, but are not Dirac isospectral for any choice of spin structures. The same holds for $L(100;1,9,21,39)$ and $L(100;1,9,29,31)$.

**Remark 6.5.** [LMR13] gives the first examples of non-isometric compact Riemannian manifolds that are isospectral for the Hodge–Laplacian on $p$-forms for every $p$, but are not strongly isospectral (isospectral for every natural differential operator on a natural bundle). The non-isometric Dirac isospectral lens spaces in Theorem 5.6 are also $p$-isospectral for every $p$. Hence these lens spaces are $\tau$-isospectral for every fundamental representation $\tau$ of $K\simeq\operatorname{Spin}(2m-1)$, but are not strongly isospectral, in the notation of [LMR13, §2, §8]. The fundamental representations of $\operatorname{Spin}(2m-1)$ are $\tau_1,\dots,\tau_{m-1}$. For $1\leq p\leq m-2$, $\tau_p$ has highest weight $\varepsilon_1+\dots+\varepsilon_p$ and induces the bundle of $p$-forms. The representation $\tau_{m-1}$ has highest weight $\tfrac12(\varepsilon_1+\dots+\varepsilon_{m-1})$ and induces the spinor bundle.

**Remark 6.6.** [DD14] extended and simplified the examples of [LMR13]. The authors gave a sufficient condition on the parameters of two lens spaces for them to be $p$-isospectral for all $p$. The authors of the present work asked whether an analogous result holds in the Dirac case.

**Remark 6.7.** All examples of Dirac isospectral lens spaces found here are homotopy-equivalent. This contrasts with the Laplace–Beltrami case, where Ikeda [Ik80] found isospectral non-homotopy-equivalent lens spaces in dimension $7$ with $q=13$. The reasons for this phenomenon remain unclear.
