# Restatement

## Abstract

We prove finiteness and Poincaré duality for pro-étale $\mathbf{Q}_p$-local systems on proper $p$-adic rigid-analytic spaces, in the framework of Banach–Colmez spaces, and establish their optimal cohomological vanishing bounds. We also obtain ordinary finiteness and duality for their arithmetic pro-étale cohomology. These results are deduced from analogous statements for perfect complexes on the Fargues–Fontaine curve, which reduce to Poincaré duality for perfect complexes over period rings. We give a simple proof of this duality via the diagrammatic argument of our previous work for finite coefficients. Along the way, we establish optimal $v$-descent for perfect complexes over certain period rings on affinoid perfectoid spaces.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1. Main results

Let $K$ be a nonarchimedean field of mixed characteristic $(0,p)$, and let $C\coloneqq\widehat{\overline{K}}$ be its completed algebraic closure. Étale cohomology of rigid-analytic spaces over $K$ was developed by Berkovich [Ber93] and Huber [Hub96]. This theory was later extended to diamonds and small $v$-sheaves in [Sch26a]. For coefficients $\underline{\mathbf{Z}/n}$ with $n$ prime to $p$, the theory parallels the algebraic theory of [SGA4, SGA4$\tfrac12$, SGA5]. For $p$-power torsion coefficients, many of these properties fail. In particular, $\mathbf{F}_p$-étale cohomology may be infinite-dimensional, may depend on the choice of algebraically closed ground field, and need not satisfy the Künneth formula or a general form of Poincaré duality.

Scholze [Sch13a] proved finiteness of étale $\mathbf{F}_p$-cohomology for proper rigid-analytic spaces. Bhatt–Hansen [BH22] developed Zariski-constructible sheaves and Verdier duality. Zavyalov [Zav25b] and Mann [Man22] independently proved Poincaré duality for smooth proper spaces. [LRZ24] proved a general form of Poincaré duality for arbitrary proper spaces. The goal is to extend this general form of Poincaré duality from torsion coefficients to $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-local systems on the pro-étale site of $X$, equivalently on its $v$-site.$^{1}$

> $^{1}$ For technical reasons, the $v$-site is used. The category of $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-local systems and their cohomology are insensitive to the choice of pro-étale or $v$-topology; see Lemma 3.1.14 and Proposition 3.4.7. Banach–Colmez spaces require either the big pro-étale topology or the $v$-topology. The introduction uses the $v$-topology, but all results remain valid for the big pro-étale topology.

For constant $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-coefficients, the torsion theory already gives the expected statements. If $X$ is proper over $C$, then $\operatorname{H}^i_v(X,\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)$ is finite-dimensional. If $X$ is moreover smooth, proper, and of equidimension $d$, there is a canonical Poincaré duality isomorphism
$$\operatorname{H}^{i}_{v}(X,\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})^{\vee}\simeq\operatorname{H}^{2d-i}_{v}(X,\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}(d)).$$
The same passage from torsion to rational coefficients applies to $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-local systems admitting a $\underline{\mathbf{Z}}_p$-lattice. Under an extra mild hypothesis, these finiteness and duality statements were established in [LLZ23], before the work of Mann and Zavyalov.

Cohomology of general $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-local systems was initiated by Kedlaya–Liu [KL16] and continued by Colmez–Gilles–Nizioł [Col22]. These local systems need not admit a $\underline{\mathbf{Z}}_p$-lattice, and their cohomology can be infinite-dimensional even when $X$ is a smooth projective curve (Example 1.1.4). Hence neither naive finiteness nor naive Poincaré duality holds for arbitrary pro-étale $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-local systems. The replacement is to consider the cohomology sheaves on the $v$-site of $\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)$, rather than the individual groups $\operatorname{H}^i(X,\mathbf{L})$. Finiteness and duality are formulated for these sheaves, whose natural finiteness class is given by Banach–Colmez spaces.

**Definition 1.1.1** ([Col02, LB18]). The category of Banach–Colmez spaces is the smallest weak Serre subcategory
$$\mathscr{BC}(C)\subset \operatorname{Shv}(\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})_{v};\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})$$
containing $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$ and $\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)}$. Denote by
$$\mathcal{D}^{b}_{\mathscr{BC}}(\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})_{v};\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\subset\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})_{v};\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})$$
the full subcategory of bounded objects whose cohomology sheaves lie in $\mathscr{BC}(C)$. For the $v$-topology see [Sch26a, §14]; for $\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)}$ see Definition 3.1.1.

Throughout the introduction, an analytic adic space over $\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbf{Q}_p,\mathbf{Z}_p)$ is identified with its associated diamond.

**Theorem 1.1.2** (Geometric Duality; Theorem 7.3.1 and Corollary 7.3.15). Let $f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)$ be a proper rigid-analytic space of dimension $d$, let $\omega^{\mathbf{Q}_p}_X$ be the dualizing complex on $X$, and let
$$\mathcal{E}\in \mathrm{Perf}_v(X;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)\cap\mathcal{D}^{[r,r']}(X_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)$$
for some $r,r'\in\mathbf{Z}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}$. Then:

1. $\mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\mathcal{E}$ lies in $\mathcal{D}^{[r,r'+2d]}_{\mathscr{BC}}\bigl(\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p\bigr)$.
2. There is a canonical trace map $\operatorname{Tr}_f\colon \mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\omega^{\mathbf{Q}_p}_X\to\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$ such that the induced duality morphism
$$\operatorname{PD}_f\colon \mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom_{\mathcal{D}(X_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}(\mathcal{E},\omega^{\mathbf{Q}_p}_X)\longrightarrow \mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom_{\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Spa}(C)_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}(\mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\mathcal{E},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)$$
(defined in Definition 7.2.8 (2)) is an equivalence.

**Remark 1.1.3.** The paper [ALBM24] establishes the finiteness and duality statements of Theorem 1.1.2, and their analogues for arbitrary smooth proper morphisms $f\colon X\to Y$. It does not address singular spaces or the cohomological bounds for $\mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\mathcal{E}$. For smooth proper morphisms $f\colon X\to Y$, the present methods also give an alternative and more elementary proof. This argument builds on the diagrammatic approach of [LRZ24], together with the trace and cycle class maps constructed there. Part (i) of Theorem 1.1.2 serves as the replacement for finite-dimensionality. For a $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-local system $\mathcal{E}$, it implies that $\mathrm{R}\Gamma_v(X,\mathcal{E})$ is concentrated in degrees $[0,2d]$, and that each $\operatorname{H}^i_v(X,\mathcal{E})$ can be constructed from $\mathbf{Q}_p$ and $C$ in finitely many steps.

**Example 1.1.4** ([Han18, Comment of PS]). Let
$$f\colon E\coloneqq \mathbf{G}_m^{\mathrm{an}}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbf{Q}_p,\mathbf{Z}_p).$$
This is a smooth proper rigid-analytic space over $\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbf{Q}_p,\mathbf{Z}_p)$ (the Tate elliptic curve), equipped with the natural $\mathbf{Z}$-torsor $\pi\colon\mathbf{G}_m^{\mathrm{an}}\to E$. Let $\mathscr{L}_+$ and $\mathscr{L}_-$ be the rank-one $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-local systems trivialized by $\pi$ with monodromy characters
$$\rho_+\colon\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{Q}_p^\times,\quad 1\mapsto p,\qquad\qquad \rho_-\colon\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{Q}_p^\times,\quad 1\mapsto p^{-1}.$$
Write $(-)_C$ for base change to $\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)$. Since neither $p$ nor $p^{-1}$ lies in $\mathbf{Z}_p^\times$, neither $\mathscr{L}_+$ nor $\mathscr{L}_-$ admits a $\underline{\mathbf{Z}}_p$-lattice. Their cohomology groups are infinite-dimensional.

The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence of $\pi$ gives
$$\mathrm{E}_2^{i,j}=\operatorname{H}^i\bigl(\mathbf{Z},\operatorname{H}^j_v(\mathbf{G}_{m,C}^{\mathrm{an}},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_p}\rho_+\bigr)\Longrightarrow \operatorname{H}^{i+j}_v(E_C,\mathscr{L}_{+,C}),$$
and there is an analogous spectral sequence for $\mathscr{L}_-$.

[CDN20, Th. 1.8] computes the $v$-cohomology (equivalently pro-étale cohomology) of $\mathbf{G}_{m,C}^{\mathrm{an}}$:
$$\operatorname{H}^0_v(\mathbf{G}_{m,C}^{\mathrm{an}},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)=\mathbf{Q}_p,\qquad \operatorname{H}^i_v(\mathbf{G}_{m,C}^{\mathrm{an}},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)=0\quad\text{for } i\geq 2,$$
and $\operatorname{H}^1_v(\mathbf{G}_{m,C}^{\mathrm{an}},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)$ fits into a short exact sequence
$$0\longrightarrow \bigl(\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{G}_{m,C}^{\mathrm{an}})/C\bigr)(-1)\longrightarrow \operatorname{H}^1_v(\mathbf{G}_{m,C}^{\mathrm{an}},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)\longrightarrow \mathbf{Q}_p(-1)\longrightarrow 0.$$

Combining this with the two Hochschild–Serre spectral sequences yields
$$\operatorname{H}^i_v(E_C,\mathscr{L}_{+,C})\simeq \operatorname{H}^i_v(E_C,\mathscr{L}_{-,C})\simeq\begin{cases} C(-1), & i=1\text{ or }2,\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\tag{1.1.5}$$
In particular, the nonzero groups in (1.1.5) are infinite-dimensional as $\mathbf{Q}_p$-vector spaces.

The computation refines to an equivalence of derived pushforwards:
$$\mathrm{R}f_{v,C,*}\mathscr{L}_{+,C}\simeq \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}(-1)[-1]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}(-1)[-2]\simeq \mathrm{R}f_{v,C,*}\mathscr{L}_{-,C}.\tag{1.1.6}$$

This gives a concrete illustration of Theorem 1.1.2. We have $\mathscr{L}_{+,C}^\vee\simeq\mathscr{L}_{-,C}$, and the dualizing complex of $E_C$ is $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p(1)[2]$. Together with
$$\mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom_{\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Spa}(C)_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p\bigr)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}(-1)[-1]$$
(proved in [ALB25, Th. 3.8]), this yields the sequence of isomorphisms
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{R}f_{v,C,*}\mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom_{\mathcal{D}(X_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}\bigl(\mathscr{L}_{+,C},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p(1)[2]\bigr)
&\simeq \mathrm{R}f_{v,C,*}\mathscr{L}_{-,C}(1)[2]\\
&\simeq \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}[1]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}\\
&\simeq \mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom_{\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Spa}(C)_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}(-1)[-1]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}(-1)[-2],\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p\bigr)\\
&\simeq \mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom_{\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Spa}(C)_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}\bigl(\mathrm{R}f_{v,C,*}\mathscr{L}_{+,C},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p\bigr).
\end{aligned}$$

**Theorem 1.1.7** (Arithmetic Duality; Corollary 7.4.9). Let $K$ be a finite extension of $\mathbf{Q}_p$, let $f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spa}(K,\mathcal{O}_K)$ be a proper rigid-analytic space of dimension $d$, and let
$$\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Perf}_v(X;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)\cap\mathcal{D}^{[r,r']}(X_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p).$$
Set $\omega_X^{\mathrm{ar}}\coloneqq\omega^{\mathbf{Q}_p}_X(1)[2]$. Then:

1. $\mathrm{R}\Gamma_v(X,\mathcal{E})$ lies in $\mathcal{D}^{[r,r'+2d+2]}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathbf{Q}_p)$.
2. There is a canonical trace map $\operatorname{Tr}_f\colon\mathrm{R}\Gamma_v(X,\omega^{\mathrm{ar}}_X)\to\mathbf{Q}_p$ such that the induced duality morphism
$$\operatorname{PD}_f\colon \mathrm{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(X_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}(\mathcal{E},\omega^{\mathrm{ar}}_X)\longrightarrow \mathrm{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{Q}_p)}(\mathrm{R}\Gamma_v(X,\mathcal{E}),\mathbf{Q}_p)$$
(defined in Definition 7.4.8 (2)) is an equivalence.

Theorem 1.1.7 is deduced from Theorem 1.1.2 using Fontaine's theory of almost $\mathbf{C}_p$-representations [Fon03, Fon20]. When $X$ is smooth, the finiteness and duality parts can also be deduced from [ALBM24]; see [ALBM24, Rmk. 6.2.3]. The proper case had not been addressed before.

The fact that arithmetic pro-étale cohomology (equivalently arithmetic $v$-cohomology) has simpler finiteness behaviour than its geometric counterpart goes back to [CGN23]. That paper proved a version of arithmetic Poincaré duality with constant coefficients for smooth partially proper rigid-analytic curves. Zhenghui Li [Li24] extended this to arbitrary smooth partially proper rigid-analytic spaces with constant coefficients.

**Example 1.1.8.** Let $G$ be the absolute Galois group of $\mathbf{Q}_p$. Since $E_C\to E$ is a pro-étale $G$-torsor, the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence gives
$$\mathrm{E}_2^{i,j}=\operatorname{H}^i_{\mathrm{cont}}\bigl(G,\operatorname{H}^j_v(E_C,\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(n))\bigr)\Longrightarrow \operatorname{H}^{i+j}_v(E,\mathscr{L}_+(n)).$$
Combining this with (1.1.5) and Tate's computation [Tat67, Ths. 1 and 2, pp. 176–177] of $\operatorname{H}^i_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,C(m))$, one obtains
$$\operatorname{H}^i_v\bigl(E,\mathscr{L}_+(n)\bigr)\simeq\begin{cases}\mathbf{Q}_p, & n=1\text{ and }i=1,3,\\ \mathbf{Q}_p^{\oplus 2}, & n=1\text{ and }i=2,\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$
The same argument computes the cohomology of $\mathscr{L}_-(n)$ for every $n$. Together with
$$\mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom_{\mathcal{D}(E_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}\bigl(\mathscr{L}_+(n),\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p(2)[4]\bigr)\simeq\mathscr{L}_-(2-n)[4],$$
these computations also verify the duality assertion of Theorem 1.1.7 directly.

### 1.2. Fargues–Fontaine curve perspective

[FF18] and [FS26, §2] define the (analytic) Fargues–Fontaine curve as
$$\mathrm{FF}_C\coloneqq Y_C/\varphi^{\mathbf{Z}}=\Bigl(\operatorname{Spa}\bigl(\mathbf{A}_{\inf}(C,\mathcal{O}_C),\mathbf{A}_{\inf}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)\bigr)\setminus \mathrm{V}(p\cdot[p^\flat])\Bigr)\big/\varphi^{\mathbf{Z}}.\tag{1.2.1}$$
This is an (infinite type) adic space over $\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbf{Q}_p,\mathbf{Z}_p)$; see also Section 6.1.

For a $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-local system $\mathcal{E}$ on a proper rigid-analytic space $X$ over $C$, one associates functorially a perfect complex $\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E})\in\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathrm{FF}_C;\mathcal{O})$ together with a canonical equivalence
$$\mathrm{R}\Gamma_v(X,\mathcal{E})\simeq \mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathrm{an}}\bigl(\mathrm{FF}_C,\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E})\bigr).\tag{1.2.2}$$
The complex $\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E})$ satisfies finiteness, duality, and the Künneth formula. Infinite-dimensionality and failure of the Künneth formula for $\mathrm{R}\Gamma_v(X,\mathcal{E})$ appear only after applying $\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathrm{FF}_C,-)$. This is because $\mathrm{FF}_C$ is not of finite type over $\mathbf{Q}_p$, and $\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathrm{FF}_C,-)$ is not symmetric monoidal.

[LB18, ALB25] constructed a fully faithful functor
$$\operatorname{Sol}\colon \mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathrm{FF}_C;\mathcal{O})\to \mathcal{D}\bigl(\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p\bigr),$$
which induces an identification
$$\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathrm{FF}_C;\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow[\sim]{\operatorname{Sol}}\mathcal{D}^b_{\mathscr{BC}}\bigl(\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p\bigr).$$

The Fargues–Fontaine curve has a point at infinity $\iota_\infty\colon\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{FF}_C$ [FS26, Def./Prop. II.1.22]. Pullback along it defines
$$\iota_\infty^*\colon\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathrm{FF}_C;\mathcal{O})\to\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{an}}(\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C);\mathcal{O})\simeq\mathcal{D}^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(C).$$

**Theorem 1.2.3** (Theorem 7.3.17). Let $f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)$ be a proper rigid-analytic space of dimension $d$, and let $\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Perf}_v^{[r,r']}(X;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)$ for some $r,r'\in\mathbf{Z}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}$. Then one can functorially assign a perfect complex $\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E})\in\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathrm{FF}_C;\mathcal{O})$ satisfying:

1. There is a functorial isomorphism $\operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E}))\simeq\mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\mathcal{E}$.
2. There is a functorial isomorphism $\iota_\infty^*\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E})\simeq \mathrm{R}\Gamma_v\bigl(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p}\mathcal{O}_X\bigr)$.
3. $\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E})$ lies in $\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{an}}^{[r,r'+2d]}(\mathrm{FF}_C;\mathcal{O})$.
4. If $(X',\mathcal{E}')$ is another such pair, there is a natural Künneth isomorphism
$$\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E})\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{H}_{X'}(\mathcal{E}')\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{H}_{X\times_C X'}(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}').$$
5. If $X$ is smooth of equidimension $d$, there is a functorial duality isomorphism $\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E})^\vee\simeq\mathcal{H}_X(\mathcal{E}^\vee(d)[2d])$.

While finiteness and duality admit formulations in terms of Banach–Colmez spaces, the Künneth formula fails at the level of $v$-sheaves, even in the basic example below.

**Example 1.2.4.** In the setting of Example 1.1.4,$^{2}$ the natural morphism
$$\mathrm{R}f_{v,C,*}\bigl(\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)\bigr)\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p}\mathrm{R}f_{v,C,*}\bigl(\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)\bigr)\to \mathrm{R}(f\times_C f)_{v,C,*}\bigl(\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)\boxtimes\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)\bigr)$$
is not an isomorphism. The left-hand side does not lie in $\mathcal{D}^b_{\mathscr{BC}}$, since it contains $\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p}\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}[-1]$ as a direct summand.

Under the solution functor, (1.1.6) corresponds to
$$\mathcal{H}_{E_C}\bigl(\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)\bigr)\simeq \iota_{\infty,*}C[-1]\oplus\iota_{\infty,*}C[-2].$$
Hence
$$\mathcal{H}_{E_C}\bigl(\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)\bigr)\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{FF}_C}}\mathcal{H}_{E_C}\bigl(\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)\bigr)\simeq \iota_{\infty,*}C[-1]\oplus(\iota_{\infty,*}C[-2])^{\oplus 3}\oplus(\iota_{\infty,*}C[-3])^{\oplus 3}\oplus\iota_{\infty,*}C[-4],$$
which is manifestly a perfect complex on $\mathrm{FF}_C$. By the Künneth isomorphism of Theorem 1.2.3 (4), this tensor product is identified with $\mathcal{H}_{E_C\times_C E_C}\bigl(\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)\boxtimes\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)\bigr)$. Applying the solution functor gives
$$\mathrm{R}(f\times_C f)_{v,C,*}(\mathscr{L}_{+,C}\boxtimes\mathscr{L}_{+,C})\simeq \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}[-1]\oplus(\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}[-2])^{\oplus 3}\oplus(\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}[-3])^{\oplus 3}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spa}C}[-4].$$

> $^{2}$ $\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)$ is used instead of $\mathscr{L}_{+,C}$ only to avoid Tate twists. A compatible system of $p$-power roots of unity in $C$ trivializes $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p(1)$, giving a noncanonical isomorphism $\mathscr{L}_{+,C}\simeq\mathscr{L}_{+,C}(1)$. The same discussion applies verbatim to $\mathscr{L}_{+,C}$.

Theorem 1.2.3 (2) gives information not directly visible through the intrinsic operations on the Banach–Colmez spaces $\mathrm{R}^if_{v,*}\mathcal{E}$. For example, using Theorem 1.2.3 (2), David Hansen [Han26, Rmk. on p. 2] showed that if $X$ is smooth and proper and $\mathbf{L}$ is a $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-local system on $X$, then
$$\sum_n(-1)^n\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_p}\mathrm{R}^nf_{v,*}\mathbf{L}=(\mathrm{rk}\,\mathbf{L})\cdot\Bigl(\sum_n(-1)^n\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_p}\operatorname{H}^n_v(X,\mathbf{Q}_p)\Bigr)=\mathrm{rk}\,\mathbf{L}\cdot\chi(X,\mathbf{Q}_p).$$
Here $\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_p}\mathrm{R}^nf_{v,*}\mathbf{L}$ is the $\mathbf{Q}_p$-dimension of the Banach–Colmez space $\mathrm{R}^nf_{v,*}\mathbf{L}$ in the sense of [Col02, p. 46]. This is the $p$-adic analytic analogue of the Euler characteristic formula $\chi(X,\mathbf{L})=\mathrm{rk}\,\mathbf{L}\cdot\chi(X,\mathbf{Q}_p)$ in algebraic geometry (see [Ill81, Cor. 2.11]).

### 1.3. Idea of the proof and further results

The proof of Theorem 1.1.2 uses three ingredients: a reduction to the smooth case, a reformulation over the Fargues–Fontaine curve, and a diagrammatic approach to the resulting duality statement on the curve. The passage to the curve is needed because the diagrammatic approach relies on a Künneth formula. This formula fails for $v$-sheaves (Example 1.2.4) but holds for perfect complexes on the Fargues–Fontaine curve.

#### 1.3.1. Reduction to the smooth case

The first step is to reduce to the case where $X$ is smooth proper over $C$. The key input is a proper trace map
$$\operatorname{Tr}_f\colon\mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\omega_X^{\mathbf{Q}_p}\longrightarrow\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p.$$
This rests on the analogous trace map with finite coefficients constructed in [LRZ24]. Its extension to $\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$-coefficients follows from a limit argument, together with the comparison between étale and $v$-cohomology for proper rigid-analytic spaces; see Lemma 7.1.3. The resulting trace map, together with resolution of singularities and induction on $\dim X$, reduces the finiteness and duality assertions of Theorem 1.1.2 to the smooth case.

In the smooth case, the following stronger relative statement is proved for arbitrary smooth proper morphisms of diamonds over $\operatorname{Spd}(\mathbf{Q}_p,\mathbf{Z}_p)$.

**Theorem 1.3.1** (Relative Geometric Duality; Theorem 6.5.7 and Corollary 6.6.15). Let $f\colon X'\to X$ be a smooth proper morphism of equidimension $d$ of diamonds over $\operatorname{Spd}(\mathbf{Q}_p,\mathbf{Z}_p)$, and let
$$\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Perf}_v(X';\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)\cap\mathcal{D}^{[r,r']}(X'_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)$$
for some $r,r'\in\mathbf{Z}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}$. Then:

1. $\mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\mathcal{E}$ lies in $\mathcal{D}^{[r,r'+2d]}_{\mathscr{BC}}(X_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)$.
2. There is a canonical trace map $\operatorname{tr}_f\colon\mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p(d)[2d]\to\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$ such that the induced duality morphism
$$\operatorname{PD}_f\colon \mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom_{\mathcal{D}(X'_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}\bigl(\mathcal{E},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p(d)[2d]\bigr)\longrightarrow \mathrm{R}\mathscr{H}\kern-0.5ptom_{\mathcal{D}(X_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)}\bigl(\mathrm{R}f_{v,*}\mathcal{E},\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p\bigr)$$
(defined in Definition 6.5.5 (3)) is an isomorphism.

Here Definition 4.3.3 gives the precise notion of a smooth proper morphism of equidimension $d$ of diamonds over $\operatorname{Spd}(\mathbf{Q}_p,\mathbf{Z}_p)$. This class includes the diamondifications of smooth proper morphisms of equidimension $d$ between locally noetherian or sousperfectoid adic spaces over $\mathbf{Q}_p$. The relative Banach–Colmez spaces $\mathcal{D}^{(b)}_{\mathscr{BC}}(X_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p)$ are defined in Definition 6.4.10. Specializing Theorem 1.3.1 to $X=\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)$ gives the smooth case used above.

#### 1.3.2. Reduction to duality on the Fargues–Fontaine curve

The solution functor of Section 1.2 gives an identification
$$\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathrm{FF}_C;\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{D}^b_{\mathscr{BC}}\bigl(\operatorname{Spa}(C,\mathcal{O}_C)_v;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_p\bigr).$$
It therefore suffices to prove duality on the curve, where the tensor formalism needed for the Künneth formula is available. To do this, "analytic perfect complexes on the relative Fargues–Fontaine curve" are introduced, with a pushforward functor that intertwines with the derived $v$-pushforward via the solution functor.

If $X$ is an adic space that is not perfectoid, the relative Fargues–Fontaine curve
$$\mathrm{FF}_X\coloneqq X^\lozenge\times\operatorname{Spd}(\mathbf{Q}_p,\mathbf{Z}_p)/\varphi^{\mathbf{Z}}\times\mathrm{id}$$
is only a diamond and need not carry an adic-space structure. Thus the usual definition of analytic perfect complexes does not apply directly. Perfect complexes in the $v$-topology on $\mathrm{FF}_X$ do not give the correct answer (Warning 6.2.3). Instead, using that $Y_{C,[1,p]}\subset Y_C$ is a fundamental domain for $\mathrm{FF}_C$, one defines
$$\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{an}}(\mathrm{FF}_X;\mathcal{O})\coloneqq\operatorname*{Eq}\Bigl(\mathrm{Perf}(X_v;\mathbf{B}_{[1,p]})\rightrightarrows\mathrm{Perf}(X_v;\mathbf{B}_{[1,1]})\Bigr).\tag{1.3.2}$$
Here $\mathbf{B}_{[1,p]}$ and $\mathbf{B}_{[1,1]}$ are the period sheaves geometrizing Berger's period rings [Ber02]; see Definition 3.1.1. The two arrows in (1.3.2) encode restriction and Frobenius.

To compare (1.3.2) with the usual category of analytic perfect complexes when $X=S$ is affinoid perfectoid, one needs the equivalence $\mathrm{Perf}_v(S;\mathbf{B}_I)\simeq\mathrm{Perf}(\mathbf{B}_I(S))$ for every closed interval $I\subset(0,\infty)$ with rational endpoints. This follows from the following descent theorem.

**Theorem 1.3.3** (see C.2.4, C.2.8, C.2.10, C.2.14, C.3.17, C.4.5, C.5.10, C.6.10, C.7.16, and C.7.22). Let $\mathrm{Perfd}^{\mathrm{aff}}_{/\mathbf{Q}_p}$ be the category of affinoid perfectoid spaces over $\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbf{Q}_p,\mathbf{Z}_p)$. Let $I\subset(0,\infty)$ be a closed interval with rational endpoints, let $n$ be an integer, let $\ell$ be a prime number (possibly equal to $p$), and let $r,r'\in\mathbf{Z}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}$. Then the assignments
$$\begin{gathered}
\mathrm{Perf}^{[r,r']}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(-;\underline{\mathbf{Z}/n}),\quad \mathrm{Perf}^{[r,r']}_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(-;\underline{\mathbf{Z}}_\ell),\quad \mathrm{Perf}^{[r,r']}_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(-;\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell),\quad \mathrm{Perf}^{[r,r']}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(-;\mathcal{O}^+/p),\\

\end{gathered}$$
regarded as functors $\mathrm{Perfd}^{\mathrm{aff},\mathrm{op}}_{/\mathbf{Q}_p}\to\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$, are hypercomplete $v$-sheaves of $\infty$-categories.

The fact that $\mathrm{Perf}(\mathbf{B}_I(-))$ is a $v$-sheaf was established previously in [ALB25] by different methods. Likewise, [ALB25] showed that $\mathrm{Perf}(\mathcal{O}(-))$ is a $v$-sheaf when restricted to affinoid perfectoid spaces admitting a morphism to a totally disconnected perfectoid space. Theorem 1.3.3 removes this restriction for $\mathrm{Perf}(\mathcal{O}(-))$, and its proof does not use the descent results of [Man22]. The method adapts to the derived setting the arguments of [Zav25b, Heu26] for $v$-descent of étale vector bundles over $\mathcal{O}$, $\mathcal{O}^+$, and $\mathcal{O}^+/p$. The corresponding statements for the remaining coefficient systems in Theorem 1.3.3 are stated as new in this context.
