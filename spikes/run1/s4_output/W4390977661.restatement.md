# Restatement

## Abstract

Faithfully flat descent of pseudo-coherent complexes in rigid geometry was proved by Mathew. In this paper, we generalize the result of Mathew to solid quasi-coherent complexes on rigid analytic varieties, which have been introduced by Clausen and Scholze by means of condensed mathematics.

## Introduction

The introduction recalls the following material.

- Faithfully flat descent in commutative algebra: for a faithfully flat map of rings $A \to B$, an $A$-module can be described as a $B$-module with a descent datum. Lurie and Mathew generalized this to complexes of modules [Lur18], [Mat16]. The generalization is used in [BS22, CS19].
- In rigid geometry: let $K$ be a complete non-archimedean field, and let $A \to B$ be a map of affinoid $K$-algebras which is faithfully flat as a map of ordinary rings. Then a coherent $A$-module can be described as a coherent $B$-module $N$ with an isomorphism $\tau \colon B \otimes_A N \cong N \otimes_A B$ of $B \otimes_A B$-modules satisfying the cocycle condition; such $\tau$ is called a descent datum. See [BG98, Con03].

**Theorem 0.1** ([Mat22, Theorem 1.4]). Let $K$ and $A \to B$ be as above. Let $B^{n/A}$ denote the $(n+1)$-fold completed tensor product of $B$ over $A$. Then we have an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathrm{PCoh}(A) \xrightarrow{\sim} \varprojlim_{[n]\in\Delta} \mathrm{PCoh}(B^{n/A}),$$
where $\mathrm{PCoh}(A)$ is the category of pseudo-coherent complexes of $A$-modules and $\Delta$ is the simplex category.

- Condensed mathematics (Clausen–Scholze [Sch19, Sch20]): topological spaces are generalized to condensed sets, which are sheaves of sets on the site of profinite sets with the topology given by finite jointly surjective families. Topological groups and rings are generalized similarly. The category of condensed abelian groups is abelian. Complete affinoid pairs are generalized to analytic rings. For an analytic ring $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{M})$, there is a symmetric monoidal stable $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{M})$. For a complete affinoid pair $(A, A^+)$, there is an analytic ring $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare$, and objects of $\mathcal{D}((A, A^+)_\blacksquare)$ are called solid quasi-coherent complexes over $(A, A^+)$.

**Theorem 0.2** ([And21, Theorem 4.1]). Let $X$ be an analytic affinoid adic space and let $U$ denote an arbitrary open affinoid subspace of $X$. Then the functor
$$U \mapsto \mathcal{D}((\mathcal{O}_X(U), \mathcal{O}_X^+(U))_\blacksquare)$$
defines a sheaf of $\infty$-categories on $X$.

The paper states the following main theorem.

**Theorem 0.3.** Let $K$ be a complete non-archimedean field, and let $A \to B$ be a faithfully flat map of affinoid $K$-algebras. Let $B^{n/A}$ denote the $(n+1)$-fold completed tensor product of $B$ over $A$. Then we have an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathcal{D}((A, A^\circ)_\blacksquare) \xrightarrow{\sim} \varprojlim_{[n]\in\Delta} \mathcal{D}((B^{n/A}, (B^{n/A})^\circ)_\blacksquare).$$

The paper's approach is as follows. The proof uses formal models. Mathew's descendability notion [Mat16] is recalled: a map $f\colon A \to B$ of discrete rings is descendable if the pro-object $\{\mathrm{Tot}_n(B^{\bullet/A})\}_n$ of $\mathcal{D}(A)$ is constant and converges to $A$, where $B^{\bullet/A}$ is the Čech nerve (Definition 2.13). Mathew shows [Mat16, Corollary 3.33] that finitely presented faithfully flat maps of discrete rings are descendable, and that for a descendable map there is an equivalence $\mathcal{D}(A) \xrightarrow{\sim} \varprojlim_{[n]\in\Delta} \mathcal{D}(B^{n/A})$, where $B^{n/A}$ is the $(n+1)$-fold derived tensor product.

**Theorem 0.4.** There exists an object $N_{B/A} \in \mathcal{D}(\underline{B})$ satisfying the following conditions:
- $N_{B/A}$ is $A_\blacksquare$-complete and compact as an object of $\mathcal{D}(A_\blacksquare)$.
- There is an equivalence of functors from $\mathcal{D}(A_\blacksquare)$ to $\mathcal{D}(B_\blacksquare)$:
$$-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_\blacksquare} B_\blacksquare \xrightarrow{\sim} R\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\underline{A}}(N_{B/A}, -). \tag{0.1}$$

In particular, $-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_\blacksquare} B_\blacksquare \colon \mathcal{D}(A_\blacksquare) \to \mathcal{D}(B_\blacksquare)$ commutes with small limits. The construction of $N_{B/A}$ and of (0.1) is functorial (see Remark 2.12).

**Theorem 0.5.** Let $A \to B$ be a finitely presented faithfully flat map of discrete rings. Let $B^{n/A}$ denote the $(n+1)$-fold tensor product of $B$ over $A$. Then we have an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathcal{D}(A_\blacksquare) \xrightarrow{\sim} \varprojlim_{[n]\in\Delta} \mathcal{D}((B^{n/A})_\blacksquare).$$

**Remark 0.6.** Mann proves faithfully flat descent for discrete rings by a different approach in his thesis [Man22]. He considers a stable monoidal $\infty$-category $\mathcal{E}(A_\blacksquare)$ instead of $\mathcal{D}(A_\blacksquare)$, and defines descendable maps of analytic rings.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 1 reviews analytic rings. Section 2 proves fppf descent for discrete rings in condensed mathematics. Section 3 introduces adic completeness and small adic rings. Section 4 proves Theorem 0.3. Section 5 recovers Theorem 0.1 from Theorem 0.3, following [And21, Section 5].

**Conventions.** All rings are unital and commutative, including condensed ones. $\Delta$ is the simplex category, $\Delta_s$ is its subcategory with injective morphisms, and $\Delta_{s,\leq m}$ is the full subcategory of $\Delta_s$ on $[n]$ for $0 \leq n \leq m$. For an f-adic ring $A$, $A^\circ$ denotes its ring of power-bounded elements. For a topological ring $A$, $A_{\mathrm{disc}}$ denotes the discrete ring with the same underlying ring.

## Section 1 (Statements)

**Definition 1.1** ([Sch19, Definition 1.2]). Let $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$ denote the pro-étale site of a point, i.e., the category of profinite sets with covers given by finite families of jointly surjective maps. A condensed set is a sheaf of sets on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$. A condensed ring, group, etc. is a sheaf of rings, groups, etc. on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$. The category of condensed sets is denoted $\mathrm{Cond}$.

**Example 1.3** ([Sch19, Proposition 2.15]). Let $X$ be a $T_1$ space. Then the presheaf $\underline{X}$ sending a profinite set $S$ to the set of continuous maps $S \to X$ is a condensed set. For a Hausdorff topological abelian group or ring $A$, one defines a condensed abelian group or ring $\underline{A}$.

**Theorem 1.5** ([Sch19, Theorem 1.10]). The category $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ is an abelian category satisfying Grothendieck's axioms (AB3), (AB4), (AB5), (AB6), (AB3*), and (AB4*). Furthermore, it is generated by compact projective objects.

**Definition 1.6** ([Sch19, Definitions 7.1, 7.4]). An uncompleted pre-analytic ring $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{M})$ is a condensed ring $\mathcal{A}$ with a functor $S \mapsto \mathcal{M}[S]$ from extremally disconnected sets to $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ sending finite disjoint unions to finite products, together with a natural transformation $\Phi_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})} \colon \underline{S} \to \mathcal{M}[S]$. The condensed ring $\mathcal{A}$ is the underlying condensed ring and $\mathcal{M}$ is the functor of measures.

An uncompleted pre-analytic ring is an uncompleted analytic ring if, for every complex $C\colon \cdots \to C_i \to \cdots \to C_1 \to C_0 \to 0$ of $\mathcal{A}$-modules such that each $C_i$ is a direct sum of objects $\mathcal{M}[T]$ for extremally disconnected sets $T$, the map $R\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M}[S], C) \to R\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}[S], C)$ is an equivalence for all extremally disconnected $S$.

An uncompleted analytic ring is an analytic ring if the map $\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{M}[\ast]$ is an isomorphism.

**Remark 1.7.** The notation for analytic rings agrees with [Sch20, Definition 12.1] and [Man22, Definition 2.3.1]. In [Sch19, Definition 7.1], an analytic ring is written $\mathcal{A}$ with underlying condensed ring $\underline{\mathcal{A}}$ and functor $S \mapsto \mathcal{A}[S]$. Both notations are used.

**Remark 1.8.** The terminology follows [Man22]. In [Sch19], uncompleted pre-analytic rings, uncompleted analytic rings, and analytic rings are called pre-analytic rings, analytic rings, and normalized analytic rings, respectively.

**Definition 1.9.** Let $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{M})$ be an uncompleted analytic ring.

1. $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})} \subset \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ is the full subcategory of $\mathcal{A}$-modules $M$ such that for all extremally disconnected $S$, the map $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M}[S], M) \to \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}[S], M) \cong M(S)$ is an isomorphism. An object of $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ is $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete if it lies in $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$.
2. $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}) \subset \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ is the full $\infty$-subcategory of complexes $M$ such that for all extremally disconnected $S$, the map $R\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M}[S], M) \to R\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}[S], M)$ is an equivalence. An object of $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ is $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete if it lies in $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$.

**Proposition 1.10** ([Sch19, Proposition 7.5], [And21, Lemma 5.27]). Let $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{M})$ be an uncompleted analytic ring.

1. - $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})} \subset \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ is an abelian category stable under limits, colimits, and extensions. The objects $\mathcal{M}[S]$, for $S$ extremally disconnected, form a family of compact projective generators.
   - The inclusion $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})} \to \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}}$ admits a left adjoint $M \mapsto M \otimes_{\mathcal{A}} (\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$, the unique colimit-preserving extension of $\mathcal{A}[S] \mapsto \mathcal{M}[S]$. There is a unique symmetric monoidal tensor product $-\otimes_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}-$ making this functor symmetric monoidal.
   - For $M, N \in \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$, $\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)$ is also $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete, and $\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,-)$ is an internal Hom of $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$.
2. - $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}) \subset \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ is a stable subcategory stable under limits and colimits. The image of $\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}) \to \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ is contained in $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$, and $\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}) \to \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ is an equivalence. For $M \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$, $R\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M}[S], M) \to R\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}[S], M)$ is an equivalence. An object $M \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ is $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete if and only if $H^i(M)$ is $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete for all $i \in \mathbb{Z}$.
   - The inclusion $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}) \to \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ admits a left adjoint $M \mapsto M \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{A}} (\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$, which is a left derived functor of $M \mapsto M \otimes_{\mathcal{A}} (\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$. There is a unique symmetric monoidal tensor product $-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}-$ making this functor symmetric monoidal.
   - For $M, N \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$, $R\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)$ is also $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete, and $R\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,-)$ is an internal Hom of $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$.

**Definition 1.11** ([Sch19, Lecture VII, page 47]). Let $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$ and $(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ be uncompleted analytic rings. A map of uncompleted analytic rings $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}) \to (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ is a map $\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ of underlying condensed rings such that for all extremally disconnected $S$, the $\mathcal{A}$-module $\mathcal{N}[S]$ is $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete.

**Remark 1.12.** For a map $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}) \to (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ of uncompleted analytic rings, every object $M \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ is $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete, since $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ is generated by $\mathcal{N}[S]$ for extremally disconnected $S$ under shifts and sifted colimits, and each $\mathcal{N}[S]$ is $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$-complete.

**Proposition 1.13** ([Sch19, Proposition 7.7]). Let $f\colon (\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}) \to (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ be a map of uncompleted analytic rings.

1. The composite $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{A}} \xrightarrow{-\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}} \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathcal{B}} \xrightarrow{-\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})} \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})}$ factors over $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$, via a functor $M \mapsto M \otimes_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})} (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$. This is the left adjoint of the forgetful functor $\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})} \to \mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})}$.
2. The composite $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}) \xrightarrow{-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}} \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B}) \xrightarrow{-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})} \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$ factors over $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$, via a functor $M \mapsto M \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})} (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N})$. This is the left adjoint of the forgetful functor $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N}) \to \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M})$.

**Theorem 1.14** ([Sch20, Proposition 12.12]). The $\infty$-category $\mathrm{AnRing}$ admits all small colimits. In particular, $\mathrm{AnRing}$ admits pushouts. Moreover, sifted colimits in $\mathrm{AnRing}$ commute with the functor $(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}) \mapsto \mathcal{M}[S]$ to $\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab}))$ for every extremally disconnected $S$, where $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ab})$ is the category of condensed abelian groups.

**Proposition 1.15** ([Sch20, Proposition 12.12]). Let
$$\begin{array}{ccc} (\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}) & \to & (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}) \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ (\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}) & \to & (\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}) \end{array}$$
be a pushout diagram in $\mathrm{AnRing}$. Then for any $M \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})$, $M$ is $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}})$-complete if and only if $M$ is $(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}})$-complete and $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})$-complete.

**Lemma 1.16** ([Sch20, Proposition 12.12]). Let the diagram of Proposition 1.15 be a commutative diagram in $\mathrm{AnRing}$. Assume:

1. $\mathcal{E}$ is $(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}})$-complete, and the natural map $\mathcal{C} \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})} (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}) \to \mathcal{E}$ is an equivalence.
2. For any $M \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})$, $M$ is $(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}})$-complete if and only if $M$ is $(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}})$-complete and $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})$-complete.

Then the diagram is a pushout.

**Definition 1.17.** A map $f\colon (\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}) \to (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}})$ in $\mathrm{AnRing}$ is *steady* if for all maps $g\colon (\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}) \to (\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})$ in $\mathrm{AnRing}$ and all $M \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})$, the object $M \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})} (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}})$, which a priori lies in $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{C} \otimes_{\mathcal{A}} \mathcal{B})$, is $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})$-complete.

**Remark 1.18.** The original definition [Sch20, Definition 12.13] of steady maps differs from Definition 1.17. It is claimed in [Sch20, page 83] that the two are equivalent, but this is not true. This causes no issues in the result of [Sch20], because only Definition 1.17 is used there.

**Proposition 1.19** ([Sch20, Proposition 12.14]). A map $f\colon (\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}}) \to (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}})$ in $\mathrm{AnRing}$ is steady if and only if for all pushout diagrams of the form in Proposition 1.15 (with $f$ the top horizontal map) and all $M \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})$, the base change map
$$(M|_{\mathcal{A}}) \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{A}})} (\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}) \to (M \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})} (\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{E}}))|_{\mathcal{B}}$$
is an equivalence.

**Construction 1.20** ([Sch19, Example 7.3, Theorem 8.1]). Let $A$ be a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra. The uncompleted pre-analytic ring $A_\blacksquare$ is defined as follows.

- The underlying ring is $\underline{A}$.
- For an extremally disconnected set $S = \varprojlim_i S_i$ (each $S_i$ finite), $A_\blacksquare[S] = \varprojlim_i \underline{A}[S_i]$.
- The natural transformation $\underline{S} \to A_\blacksquare[S]$ is defined in the obvious way.

Then $A_\blacksquare$ is an analytic ring.

**Construction 1.21** ([And21, Lemma 3.19, Definition 3.20]). Let $(A, A^+)$ be an affinoid pair with $A$ a discrete ring. The uncompleted pre-analytic ring $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare$ is defined as follows.

- The underlying ring is $\underline{A}$.
- For an extremally disconnected set $S$, $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare[S] = \varinjlim_{B \subset A^+} \underline{A} \otimes_{\underline{B}} B_\blacksquare[S]$, where the colimit is over all finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-subalgebras $B$ of $A^+$.
- The natural transformation $\underline{S} \to (A,A^+)_\blacksquare[S]$ is defined in the obvious way.

Then $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare$ is an analytic ring. If $A^+ = A$, we write $A_\blacksquare$ for $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare$. This notation is compatible with Construction 1.20.

**Construction 1.22** ([And21, Lemma 3.25, Theorem 3.28]). Let $(A, A^+)$ be a complete affinoid pair. The uncompleted pre-analytic ring $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare$ is defined as follows.

- The underlying ring is $\underline{A}$.
- For an extremally disconnected set $S$, $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare[S] = \underline{A} \otimes_{(A_{\mathrm{disc}}, A^+_{\mathrm{disc}})_\blacksquare} (A_{\mathrm{disc}}, A^+_{\mathrm{disc}})_\blacksquare[S]$.
- The natural transformation $\underline{S} \to (A,A^+)_\blacksquare[S]$ is defined in the obvious way.

Then $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare$ is an analytic ring. If $A^+ = A$, we write $A_\blacksquare$ for $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare$.

**Proposition 1.23** ([And21, Proposition 3.22]). Let $f\colon A \to B$ be an integral map of discrete rings. Then $M \in \mathcal{D}(\underline{B})$ is $B_\blacksquare$-complete if and only if $M$ is $A_\blacksquare$-complete.

**Proposition 1.24** ([And21, Proposition 3.32]). Let $A$ be an f-adic ring, and let $f_I = \{f_i \mid i \in I\}$ be a collection of elements of $A^\circ$. Let $A^+$ be the minimal ring of integral elements containing $f_I$. Then $M \in \mathcal{D}(\underline{A})$ is $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare$-complete if and only if $M$ is $\mathbb{Z}[f_i]_\blacksquare$-complete for all $i \in I$, where $\mathbb{Z}[f_i]$ is regarded as a discrete ring.

**Remark 1.25.** It follows from Proposition 1.23 and Proposition 1.24 that for a map $(A, A^+) \to (B, B^+)$ of complete affinoid pairs, $(B, B^+)_\blacksquare[S]$ is $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare$-complete for any extremally disconnected $S$. In particular, there is a map $(A, A^+)_\blacksquare \to (B, B^+)_\blacksquare$ of analytic rings.

**Proposition 1.26** ([And21, Proposition 3.34]). Let $\mathrm{cAff}$ denote the category of complete affinoid pairs. The functor
$$\mathrm{cAff} \to \mathrm{AnRing};\quad (A, A^+) \mapsto (A, A^+)_\blacksquare$$
is fully faithful. Moreover, the image of this functor is contained in the full $\infty$-subcategory of ($0$-truncated) analytic rings.

**Proposition 1.27** ([And21, Proposition 3.33]). Let $A$ be a discrete ring, and let $B, C$ be discrete $A$-algebras such that $B \otimes_A C \simeq B \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_A C$. Then
$$B_\blacksquare \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_\blacksquare} C_\blacksquare \simeq (B \otimes_A C)_\blacksquare.$$

**Lemma 1.28** ([And21, Lemma 3.8]). The natural map $\mathbb{Z}_\blacksquare \to \mathbb{Z}[T]_\blacksquare$ in $\mathrm{AnRing}$ is steady.

**Lemma 1.29** ([And21, Lemma 3.14]). Let $M$ be a prodiscrete topological abelian group, and let $M\langle T_1,\ldots,T_n\rangle$ denote the topological abelian group of convergent formal power series in $n$ variables. Then
$$M \otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{Z}_\blacksquare} \mathbb{Z}[T_1,\ldots,T_n]_\blacksquare \simeq \underline{M\langle T_1,\ldots,T_n\rangle}.$$
