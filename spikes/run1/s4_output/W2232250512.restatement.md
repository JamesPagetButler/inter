# Restatement

## Introduction

**Setting.** Let $(M^n,g)$ be a connected Riemannian spin manifold carrying a family of metric connections $\nabla^s$ with totally skew-symmetric torsion, "say $4sT$" for a non-trivial 3-form $T\in\bigwedge^3M$ and $s\in\mathbb{R}$. The same symbol denotes the lift of $\nabla$ to the spinor bundle $\Sigma\to M$. Clifford multiplication is $\mu:TM\otimes\Sigma\to\Sigma$, with $X\cdot\varphi:=\mu(X\otimes\varphi)$.

**KsT.** If there exist $s\neq 0$ and $\zeta\neq 0$ such that

$$\nabla^{s}_{X}\varphi=\zeta\,X\cdot\varphi \qquad (\ast)$$

for every vector field $X$ on $M$, then $\varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma)$ is a Killing spinor with torsion (KsT). For $s=0$ this is the Riemannian Killing spinor equation [15, 28].

**TsT.** A twistor spinor with torsion (TsT) is a section $\varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma)$ satisfying, for some $s\neq 0$,

$$\nabla^{s}_{X}\varphi+\frac{1}{n}X\cdot D^{s}\varphi=0,\qquad (\ast\ast)$$

where $D^{s}=\mu\circ\nabla^{s}$ is the induced Dirac operator.

**Eigenvalue estimates.** TsT and some KsT realize the equality case of eigenvalue estimates for the cubic Dirac operator $\not{D}=D^{g}+\frac14 T$, the Dirac operator of the connection with torsion $T/3$. The universal estimate $\beta_{\rm univ}$ rests on a generalized Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula [16, 41, 24, 31, 1, 6]. The twistorial estimate $\beta_{\rm tw}$ uses Penrose's twistor operator for $\nabla^s$ [3, 17]. Its equality case occurs when the Riemannian scalar curvature is constant and the spinor is a TsT for a specific parameter $s$ depending on $n$.

**Characteristic connection.** Throughout, $(M^n,g)$ carries a non-integrable $G$-structure $(G\subsetneq SO_n)$ with characteristic connection $\nabla^c$. This is a $G$-invariant metric connection with non-trivial skew torsion $T$, preserving the tensor fields defining the $G$-structure [2]. Since $s=1/4$ gives torsion $T$, one sets $\nabla^{1/4}=\nabla^c$. The condition $\nabla^cT=0$ implies $\delta T=0$. It also makes $\Ric^c$ and $\Ric^s$ symmetric and splits $\Sigma=\bigoplus_\gamma\Sigma_\gamma$ into eigenbundles of the $T$-action.

**Equations (I), (II), (III).** For a triple $(M^n,g,T)$ carrying a $\nabla^c$-parallel KsT with respect to $\nabla^s=\nabla^g+2sT$ and Killing number $\zeta=3\gamma(1-4s)/4n\neq0$, the following hold:

$$\Ric^{g}=\frac{9(n-1)\gamma^{2}}{4n^{2}}\,\Id_{TM},\qquad\text{(I)}$$

together with $T\cdot\varphi=\gamma\varphi$. Further,

$$\Ric^{c}=\frac{3(n-3)\gamma^{2}}{n^{2}}\,\Id_{TM},\qquad\text{(II)}$$

and

$$\Ric^{s}=\frac{\Scal^{s}}{n}\,g\quad\forall\ s\in\mathbb{R}.\qquad\text{(III)}$$

**Constants.** $\kappa:=3\gamma/4n$ and $\zeta:=3(1-4s)\gamma/4n$.

**Results referenced in the introduction.**
- Theorem 2.5, Theorem 3.6, Theorem 3.7, Corollary 3.10.
- Proposition 3.2 and Proposition 5.1.
- Theorem 4.1 (nearly Kähler, dimension 6) and Theorem 4.2 (nearly parallel $G_2$, dimension 7).
- Corollary 6.3: the integrability condition for general KsT, with $s=(n-1)/4(n-3)$ [3, Thm. A.2], reduces for a $\nabla^c$-parallel KsT with $\zeta=3\gamma(1-4s)/4n$ to the twistor equation with torsion.
- Proposition 6.4: for $3<n\le 8$ and $\varphi\in\Sigma_\gamma$ with $\nabla^c\varphi=0$, $\beta_{\rm tw}(\gamma)=\beta_{\rm univ}(\gamma)$ is equivalent to $\varphi$ being a real Killing spinor with $\kappa=3\gamma/4n$.

## Section 1

**Definition 1.1** ([3]). A twistor spinor with torsion (TsT) is a spinor field solving $\nabla^{s}_{X}\varphi+(1/n)\,X\cdot D^{s}(\varphi)=0$ for all $X\in\Gamma(TM)$. Equivalently, it is an element of $\ker P^{s}$, where $P^{s}$ is the twistor operator of $\nabla^s$:

$$\Gamma(\Sigma)\xrightarrow{\ \nabla^{s}\ }\Gamma(TM\otimes\Sigma)\xrightarrow{\ p\ }\Gamma(\ker\mu),\qquad P^{s}=p\circ\nabla^{s},$$

where $p:TM\otimes\Sigma\to\ker\mu\subset TM\otimes\Sigma$ is the orthogonal projection onto the kernel of Clifford multiplication. Locally,

$$p(X\otimes\varphi):=X\otimes\varphi+\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_i\otimes e_i\cdot X\cdot\varphi,\qquad P^{s}\varphi:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_i\otimes\Big\{\nabla^{s}_{e_i}\varphi+\frac1n e_i\cdot D^{s}\varphi\Big\}.$$

## Section 2

**Lemma 2.1** ([17, Lem. 1.13]). For all $\varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma)$ and $X\in\Gamma(TM)$,

$$\sum_i e_i\cdot R^{s}(X,e_i)\varphi=-\frac12\Ric^{s}(X)\cdot\varphi+s(3-4s)(X\lrcorner\sigma_T)\cdot\varphi.$$

**Lemma 2.2.** For any $\varphi\in\ker(P^s)$ and any vector field $X$,

$$-\frac12\Ric^{s}(X)\cdot\varphi=-\frac{8s}{n}(X\lrcorner T)\cdot D^{s}(\varphi)+\frac{n-2}{n}\nabla^{s}_{X}\big(D^{s}(\varphi)\big)-\frac1n X\cdot(D^{s})^{2}(\varphi)-s(3-4s)(X\lrcorner\sigma_T)\cdot\varphi,$$

$$\frac12\Scal^{s}\varphi=-\frac{24s}{n}T\cdot D^{s}(\varphi)+\frac{2(n-1)}{n}(D^{s})^{2}(\varphi)-4s(3-4s)\sigma_T\cdot\varphi.$$

**Corollary 2.3** ([17, Lem. 1.14]). Let $\varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma)$ be a KsT with respect to $\nabla^s=\nabla^g+2sT$ and Killing number $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$. Then

$$\Ric^{s}(X)\cdot\varphi=4(n-1)\zeta^{2}X\cdot\varphi-16s\zeta(X\lrcorner T)\cdot\varphi+2s(3-4s)(X\lrcorner\sigma_T)\cdot\varphi,$$

$$\Scal^{s}\varphi=4n(n-1)\zeta^{2}\varphi+48s\zeta\,T\cdot\varphi-8s(3-4s)\sigma_T\cdot\varphi.$$

**Lemma 2.4.** Define $\nabla^{s,E}:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(T^*M\otimes E)$ on $E=\Sigma\oplus\Sigma$ by

$$\nabla^{s,E}_X(\varphi_1\oplus\varphi_2)=\Big(\nabla^{s}_X\varphi_1+\frac1n X\cdot\varphi_2\Big)\oplus\Big(-\frac n2\,\mathrm{Sch}^{s}(X)\cdot\varphi_1-\frac{sn(3-4s)}{(n-1)(n-2)}\big[X\cdot dT+(n-1)(X\lrcorner\sigma_T)\big]\cdot\varphi_1$$

$$\qquad-\frac{sn}{(n-1)(n-2)}\Big[\frac{8(n-1)}{n}(X\lrcorner T)+\frac{12}{n}X\cdot T\Big]\cdot\varphi_2+\nabla^{s}_X\varphi_2\Big),$$

for $X\in\Gamma(TM)$ and $\varphi_1\oplus\varphi_2\in\Gamma(\Sigma\oplus\Sigma)$. Then $\nabla^{s,E}$ is a covariant derivative on $E$.

**Theorem 2.5.** Let $(M^n,g,T)$ $(n\ge3)$ be a connected Riemannian spin manifold with $\nabla^cT=0$. Then every twistor spinor with torsion $\varphi\in\ker(P^s)$ satisfies $\nabla^{s,E}_X\big(\varphi\oplus D^s(\varphi)\big)=0$. Conversely, if $\varphi\oplus\psi\in\Gamma(E)$ is $\nabla^{s,E}$-parallel, then $\varphi$ is a TsT with $D^s(\varphi)=\psi$.

**Corollary 2.6.** Let $(M^n,g,T)$ $(n\ge3)$ be as in Theorem 2.5.
- (a) $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ker(P^s)\le 2^{[\frac n2]+1}$.
- (b) If $\varphi$ and $D^s(\varphi)$ vanish at some $p\in M$ and $\varphi\in\ker(P^s)$, then $\varphi\equiv0$.

**Proposition 2.7.** Let $(M^n,g,T)$ $(n\ge3)$ be as in Theorem 2.5. Then every zero of a TsT $0\neq\varphi\in\ker(P^s)$ is isolated, i.e. the zero set of $\varphi$ is discrete.

## Section 3

**Theorem 3.1** ([31, Cor. 3.2], [11, Thm. 1.1], [3, p. 306]). Let $\varphi_0$ be a $\nabla^c$-parallel spinor. Then

$$\Scal^{c}\varphi_0=-2\,dT\cdot\varphi_0=-4\,\sigma_T\cdot\varphi_0,\qquad(\ast)$$

$$\Ric^{c}(X)\cdot\varphi_0=\frac12\big(X\lrcorner dT+\nabla^{c}_{X}T\big)\cdot\varphi_0.$$

In particular the scalar curvatures $\Scal^c$ and $\Scal^g$ are constant. If in addition $\varphi\in\Sigma_\gamma$ for some $\gamma\neq0$, then

$$\Scal^{g}=2\gamma^{2}-\frac12\|T\|^{2},\qquad \Scal^{c}=2(\gamma^{2}-\|T\|^{2}),\qquad \Ric^{c}(X)=\frac12(X\lrcorner dT)\cdot\varphi_0=(X\lrcorner\sigma_T)\cdot\varphi_0.$$

**Proposition 3.2.** Let $(M^n,g,T)$ be a compact connected Riemannian spin manifold with $\nabla^cT=0$ and positive scalar curvature. Let $\varphi_0\in\Gamma(\Sigma)$ be non-trivial with

$$\nabla^{c}_X\varphi_0=\nabla^{g}_X\varphi_0+\frac14(X\lrcorner T)\cdot\varphi_0=0\ \ \forall X\in\Gamma(TM),\qquad T\cdot\varphi_0=\gamma\varphi_0,\qquad\text{for some }\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.\qquad(3.1)$$

Then $\varphi_0$ is a real Killing spinor (with respect to $g$) if and only if the constant eigenvalue $0\neq\gamma\in\Spec(T)$ satisfies

$$\gamma^{2}=\frac{4n}{9(n-1)}\Scal^{g}.\qquad(\dagger)$$

If this holds, the Killing number is $\kappa:=3\gamma/4n$, and

$$(X\lrcorner T)\cdot\varphi_0+\frac{3\gamma}{n}X\cdot\varphi_0=0,\qquad\forall X\in\Gamma(TM).\qquad(3.2)$$

For $n\le8$, $(\dagger)$ is equivalent to $\gamma^{2}=\frac{2n}{9-n}\|T\|^{2}$, or to $\Scal^{g}=\frac{9(n-1)}{2(9-n)}\|T\|^{2}$. In that case the symmetric endomorphism $dT$ acts on $\varphi_0$ by

$$dT\cdot\varphi_0=-\frac{3\gamma^{2}(n-3)}{2n}\varphi_0.$$

**Example 3.3.** Let $(M^6,g,J)$ be a strict 6-dimensional nearly Kähler manifold (Section 4.1). It admits two $\nabla^c$-parallel spinors $\varphi^\pm\in\Sigma_\gamma$ with $\gamma=\pm2\|T\|$ [31]. The scalar curvature is $\Scal^g=\frac{15}{2}\|T\|^2$, which coincides with $\frac{9(n-1)}{4n}\gamma^2=\frac{9(n-1)}{2(9-n)}\|T\|^2$. By Proposition 3.2, $\varphi^\pm$ are real Killing spinors with $\kappa=3\gamma/4n=\pm\|T\|/4$, a known result of [30, 38]. In particular

$$dT\cdot\varphi^\pm=-\frac12\Scal^{c}\cdot\varphi^\pm=-\frac{3\gamma^{2}(n-3)}{2n}\varphi^\pm,\qquad\text{i.e. } dT\cdot\varphi^\pm=-3\|T\|^{2}\varphi^\pm$$

(see also [31, Lem. 10.7]).

Consider a 7-dimensional nearly parallel $G_2$-manifold (Section 4.2). There is a unique $\nabla^c$-parallel spinor $\varphi_0$ with $\gamma=-\sqrt7\|T\|$. The scalar curvature $\Scal^g=\frac{27}{2}\|T\|^2$ coincides with $\frac{9(n-1)}{4n}\gamma^2=\frac{9(n-1)}{2(9-n)}\|T\|^2$. Hence $\varphi_0$ is a Killing spinor with $\kappa=3\gamma/4n=-\frac{3}{4\sqrt7}\|T\|$, a known result of [34, 31]. In particular $dT\cdot\varphi_0=-\frac{3\gamma^2(n-3)}{2n}\varphi_0$, i.e. $dT\cdot\varphi_0=-6\|T\|^2\varphi_0$ (see also [31, Ex. 5.2]).

**Lemma 3.4.** Assume $(M^n,g,T)$ is as in Proposition 3.2 and $n=\dim M>3$. Let $0\neq\varphi_0\in\Gamma(\Sigma)$ satisfy (3.1). Then $(X\wedge T)\cdot\varphi_0\neq0$ for at least one vector field $X$.

**Lemma 3.5.** Let $0\neq\gamma\in\Spec(T)$. A real Killing spinor $\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(M^n,g)_{\frac{3\gamma}{4n}}$ is characteristic, i.e. $D^c(\varphi)=0$, if and only if $\varphi\in\Sigma_\gamma$.

**Theorem 3.6.** Let $(M^n,g,T)$ be a compact connected Riemannian spin manifold with $\nabla^cT=0$ and positive scalar curvature $\Scal^g=\frac{9(n-1)\gamma^2}{4n}$ for some constant $0\neq\gamma\in\Spec(T)$. Suppose the symmetric endomorphism

$$dT+\frac12\Big[\frac{9(n-1)}{4n}\gamma^{2}-\frac32\|T\|^{2}\Big]$$

acts on $\Sigma$ with non-negative eigenvalues. Then the following classes of spinors, if they exist, coincide:

$$\ker(\nabla^c)\cong\ker(D^c)\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Spec(T)}\Big[\Gamma(\Sigma_\gamma)\cap\mathcal{K}(M^n,g)_{\frac{3\gamma}{4n}}\Big].$$

**Theorem 3.7.** Let $(M^n,g,T)$ be a compact connected Riemannian spin manifold with $\nabla^cT=0$. Let $\varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma)$ satisfy $\nabla^c\varphi=0$, where $\nabla^c=\nabla^g+\frac12T$. Let $\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$. The following are equivalent:
- (a) $\varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma_\gamma)\cap\ker(P^s):=\ker(P^s|_{\Sigma_\gamma})$ with respect to the family $\{\nabla^s:s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1/4\}\}$;
- (b) $\varphi\in\mathcal{K}^{s}(M,g)_\zeta$ with respect to the family $\{\nabla^s:s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,1/4\}\}$, with $\zeta=3(1-4s)\gamma/4n$;
- (c) $\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(M,g)_\kappa$ with $\kappa=3\gamma/4n$.

**Corollary 3.8.** Let $(M^n,g,T)$ satisfy $\nabla^cT=0$, and let $0\neq\varphi$ be a $\nabla^c$-parallel spinor satisfying one of conditions (a), (b) or (c) in Theorem 3.7. Then for every vector field $X$,

$$(X\wedge T)\cdot\varphi=\frac{(n-3)\gamma}{n}X\cdot\varphi.$$

Conversely, if $\varphi\in\Sigma_\gamma$ is $\nabla^c$-parallel and satisfies this relation for all $X\in\Gamma(TM)$ and some real $0\neq\gamma\in\Spec(T)$, then the conditions of Theorem 3.7 hold. In particular $\varphi$ is a real Killing spinor with respect to $g$ with Killing number $\kappa=\frac{3\gamma}{4n}$.

For $n=3$ one has $(X\wedge T)\cdot\varphi=0$ identically, i.e. $(X\lrcorner T)\cdot\varphi=-\gamma X\cdot\varphi=-X\cdot T\cdot\varphi$.

**Lemma 3.9.** Let $0\neq\gamma\in\Spec(T)$. Then:
- (a) A KsT $\varphi\in\mathcal{K}^{s}(M,g)_{\frac{3\gamma(1-4s)}{4n}}$ for some $s\neq0,1/4$ is characteristic if and only if $\varphi\in\Sigma_\gamma$.
- (b) A TsT $\varphi\in\ker(P^s|_{\Sigma_\gamma})$ for some $s\neq0,1/4$ is characteristic if and only if $\varphi$ is a $D^s$-eigenspinor with eigenvalue $-\frac{3\gamma(1-4s)}{4}$, i.e. $\varphi\in\mathcal{K}^{s}(M,g)_{\frac{3\gamma(1-4s)}{4n}}\cap\Gamma(\Sigma_\gamma)$.

In particular, for $s=0$, a twistor spinor $\varphi\in\ker(P^g|_{\Sigma_\gamma})$ is characteristic if and only if $D^g(\varphi)=-\frac{3\gamma}{4}\varphi$, i.e. $\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(M,g)_{\frac{3\gamma}{4n}}\cap\Gamma(\Sigma_\gamma)$.

**Corollary 3.10.** Let $(M^n,g,T)$ be a compact connected Riemannian spin manifold with $\nabla^cT=0$ and positive scalar curvature $\Scal^g=\frac{9(n-1)\gamma^2}{4n}$ for some $0\neq\gamma\in\Spec(T)$. Suppose the symmetric endomorphism $dT+\frac12\big[\frac{9(n-1)}{4n}\gamma^2-\frac32\|T\|^2\big]$ acts on $\Sigma$ with non-negative eigenvalues. Then the following classes of spinors, if they exist, coincide:

$$\ker(\nabla^c)\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Spec(T)}\Big[\Gamma(\Sigma_\gamma)\cap\mathcal{K}(M,g)_{\frac{3\gamma}{4n}}\Big]\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Spec(T)}\Big[\Gamma(\Sigma_\gamma)\cap\mathcal{K}^{s}(M,g)_{\frac{3(1-4s)\gamma}{4n}}\Big]\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Spec(T)}\Big[\ker(P^s|_{\Sigma_\gamma})\cap\ker(D^c)\Big].$$

Here $s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,1/4\}$ for the second set, and $s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1/4\}$ for the third.

**Theorem 4.1.** On a 6-dimensional nearly Kähler manifold $(M^6,g,J)$ with characteristic connection $\nabla^c$, the following classes of spinors coincide:
1. TsT with respect to $\{\nabla^s:s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1/4\}\}$, lying in $\Sigma_{\pm2\|T\|}$;
2. KsT with respect to $\{\nabla^s:s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,1/4\}\}$, with $\zeta:=\mp\frac{(4s-1)}{4}\|T\|$;
3. Riemannian Killing spinors;
4. $\nabla^c$-parallel spinors.

**Theorem 4.2.** On a nearly parallel $G_2$-manifold $(M^7,g,\omega)$ with characteristic connection $\nabla^c$, the following classes of spinors coincide:
1. TsT with respect to $\{\nabla^s:s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1/4\}\}$, lying in $\Sigma_{-\frac{7\tau_0}{6}}\equiv\Sigma_{-\sqrt7\|T\|}$;
2. KsT with respect to $\{\nabla^s:s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,1/4\}\}$, with $\zeta:=\frac{(4s-1)\tau_0}{8}=\frac{3(4s-1)\|T\|}{4\sqrt7}$;
3. Riemannian Killing spinors;
4. $\nabla^c$-parallel spinors.

## Section 5

**Proposition 5.1.** Assume $\nabla^cT=0$ and that $(M^n,g,T)$ is complete and admits a $\nabla^c$-parallel spinor $0\neq\varphi\in\Sigma_\gamma$ ($\mathbb{R}\ni\gamma\neq0$) lying in $\ker(P^s)$ for some $s\neq1/4$. Then for every $s\in\mathbb{R}$,

$$\Ric^{s}(X)\cdot\varphi=\frac{6\gamma^{2}}{n^{2}}\Big[\frac{6(n-1)(1-4s)^{2}+96s(1-4s)+16s(3-4s)(n-3)}{16}\Big]X\cdot\varphi,$$

$$\Scal^{s}\varphi=\frac{6\gamma^{2}}{n}\Big[\frac{6(n-1)(1-4s)^{2}+96s(1-4s)+16s(3-4s)(n-3)}{16}\Big]\varphi.$$

In particular:
- (a) $(M^n,g)$ is a compact Einstein manifold with constant positive scalar curvature $\Scal^g=\frac{9(n-1)\gamma^2}{4n}$.
- (b) For any $n>3$, $(M^n,g,T)$ is a strict $\nabla^c$-Einstein manifold with parallel torsion and constant scalar curvature $\Scal^c=\frac{3(n-3)\gamma^2}{n}$. For $n=3$, $(M^3,g,T)$ is $\Ric^c$-flat.
- (c) $(M^n,g,T)$ is $\nabla^s$-Einstein (with non-parallel torsion) for any $s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,1/4\}$, i.e. $\Ric^s=\frac{\Scal^s}{n}g$.

**Example 5.2** (see also [31, Prop. 10.4]). Consider a nearly Kähler manifold $(M^6,g,J)$. There exist two $\nabla^c$-parallel spinors $\varphi^\pm$ with $\gamma=\pm2\|T\|$, which are both TsT for some $s\neq1/4$. By Proposition 5.1,

$$\Ric^{s}(X)\cdot\varphi^\pm=\frac{(5-16s^2)}{4}\|T\|^2X\cdot\varphi^\pm=\frac{(5-16s^2)}{2}\tau_0X\cdot\varphi^\pm,\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R},$$

and in particular

$$\Ric^{c}(X)\cdot\varphi^\pm=\frac{3(n-3)\gamma^2}{n^2}X\cdot\varphi^\pm\ \Rightarrow\ \Ric^{c}(X)\cdot\varphi^\pm=\|T\|^2X\cdot\varphi^\pm=2\tau_0X\cdot\varphi^\pm,$$

$$\Ric^{g}(X)\cdot\varphi^\pm=\frac{9(n-1)\gamma^2}{4n^2}X\cdot\varphi^\pm\ \Rightarrow\ \Ric^{g}(X)\cdot\varphi^\pm=\frac54\|T\|^2\varphi^\pm=\frac52\tau_0X\cdot\varphi^\pm.$$

Relation (4.1) gives a direct computation of $\Ric^c$. It yields

$$\Big[(X\lrcorner T)\cdot T-T\cdot(X\lrcorner T)\Big]\cdot\varphi^\pm=\pm2\|T\|(X\lrcorner T)\cdot\varphi^\pm\pm\|T\|\,T\cdot X\cdot\varphi^\pm$$

$$=-2\|T\|^2X\cdot\varphi^\pm\pm\|T\|\Big(-2(X\lrcorner T)\cdot\varphi^\pm-X\cdot T\cdot\varphi^\pm\Big)$$

$$=-2\|T\|^2X\cdot\varphi^\pm\pm\|T\|\Big(\pm2\|T\|X\cdot\varphi^\pm\mp2\|T\|X\cdot\varphi^\pm\Big)=-2\|T\|^2X\cdot\varphi^\pm,$$

and the result follows from $(\clubsuit)$. For $s=0,1/4$ this recovers [31, Prop. 10.4]. The method is different, since it does not use the Einstein property of $g$ or the relation $\Ric^c=\Ric^g-\frac14S$. The formula $\Ric^s=\Ric^g-4s^2S$ gives the same results for all $s\in\mathbb{R}$, since $S:=2\tau_0\Id$ [31, Prop. 10.4].

**Example 5.3** (see also [31, Thm. 5.1, Ex. 5.2]). Consider a nearly parallel $G_2$-manifold $(M^7,g,\omega)$. There is a unique $\nabla^c$-parallel spinor $\varphi_0$ with $\gamma=-\sqrt7\|T\|$. By Proposition 5.1,

$$\Ric^{s}(X)\cdot\varphi_0=\frac{6(9-16s^2)}{28}\|T\|^2X\cdot\varphi_0=\frac{(9-16s^2)}{24}\tau_0^2X\cdot\varphi_0,\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R},$$

and in particular

$$\Ric^{c}(X)\cdot\varphi_0=\frac{12}{7}\|T\|^2X\cdot\varphi_0=\frac{\tau_0^2}{3}X\cdot\varphi_0,\qquad \Ric^{g}(X)\cdot\varphi_0=\frac{27}{14}\|T\|^2X\cdot\varphi_0=\frac{3\tau_0^2}{8}X\cdot\varphi_0.$$

Using (4.2) and (4.3),

$$\Big[(X\lrcorner T)\cdot T-T\cdot(X\lrcorner T)\Big]\cdot\varphi_0=-\frac{24}{7}\|T\|^2X\cdot\varphi_0,$$

and the result follows from $(\clubsuit)$. The formula $\Ric^s=\Ric^g-4s^2S$ with $S:=\frac{\tau_0^2}{6}\Id$ reproduces these results [31, Ex. 5.2].
