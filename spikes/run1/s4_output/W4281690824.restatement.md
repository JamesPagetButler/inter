# Restatement

## Abstract

We give a calculus of mates for functors to the $(\infty,2)$-category of $\infty$-categories. We extend Lurie's unstraightening equivalences to show that (op)lax natural transformations correspond to maps of (co)cartesian fibrations that need not preserve (co)cartesian edges. As an application, we obtain an equivalence between lax symmetric monoidal structures on right adjoint functors and oplax symmetric monoidal structures on the left adjoint functors between symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories. This equivalence is compatible with both horizontal and vertical composition of such structures. As the technical core, we study new types of fibrations over a product of two $\infty$-categories. We show how they can be dualised over one of the two factors, and how they encode functors out of the Gray tensor product of $(\infty,2)$-categories.

## Introduction: statements

**Mates of squares.** Let $L\colon \bullet\to\bullet$ and $L'\colon \bullet\to\bullet$ be left adjoints with right adjoints $R$ and $R'$, and let $f$ and $g$ be the remaining sides of a square. Given an oplax square, i.e. a 2-morphism $\alpha\colon L'f\to gL$, the mate is the lax square with horizontal right adjoints $R$ and $R'$ and 2-morphism
$$\beta\colon fR\longrightarrow R'L'fR\xrightarrow{\ \alpha\ }R'gLR\longrightarrow R'g,$$
where the first and last maps use the unit of $L'\dashv R'$ and the counit of $L\dashv R$. The text states that this procedure and its dual give an isomorphism between oplax squares with horizontal left adjoints and lax squares with horizontal right adjoints, and that this correspondence is compatible with vertical and horizontal composition of squares.

**Lax and oplax transformations.** For 2-categories $C,D$, a lax transformation $\phi$ between $F,G\colon C\to D$ has, for each morphism $f\colon x\to y$ in $C$, a lax naturality square with components $\phi_x$, $\phi_y$, $F(f)$ and $G(f)$. Oplax transformations are defined similarly.

**Monoidal structures.** A commutative monoid in an $\infty$-category $X$ with finite products is a functor $M\colon\mathrm{Fin}_*\to X$ such that the Segal maps $M_{\langle n\rangle}\to\prod_{i=1}^n M_{\langle 1\rangle}$ are equivalences, where $\langle n\rangle=\{0,\dots,n\}$ is pointed by $0$. A morphism $\phi\colon\langle n\rangle\to\langle m\rangle$ in $\mathrm{Fin}_*$ is *inert* if it restricts to an isomorphism $\langle n\rangle\setminus\{\phi^{-1}(0)\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\langle m\rangle\setminus\{0\}$.

- A symmetric monoidal structure on $C$ is a cocartesian fibration $C^\otimes\to\mathrm{Fin}_*$ or a cartesian fibration $C_\otimes\to\mathrm{Fin}_*^{\mathrm{op}}$.
- A strong monoidal functor corresponds to a commutative triangle over $\mathrm{Fin}_*$ (resp. $\mathrm{Fin}_*^{\mathrm{op}}$) in which $f^\otimes$ preserves cocartesian morphisms (resp. $f_\otimes$ preserves cartesian morphisms).
- A lax symmetric monoidal functor (in the sense of [Lu14]) is such a triangle in which $f^\otimes$ preserves only the cocartesian morphisms covering inert morphisms.
- An oplax symmetric monoidal functor is such a triangle in which $f_\otimes$ preserves only the cartesian morphisms covering inert morphisms.
- The opposite $(C_\otimes)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Fin}_*$ is the cocartesian fibration encoding the symmetric monoidal structure on $C^{\mathrm{op}}$. Hence an oplax monoidal functor can also be described as a lax monoidal functor $C^{\mathrm{op}}\to D^{\mathrm{op}}$.

[Lu14] shows that the right adjoint of a strong monoidal functor admits a lax monoidal structure. [To20] constructs a lax monoidal structure, without coherences, on the right adjoint of an oplax monoidal functor, using a span category construction. [To22, To23] gives another proof of Proposition A, based on the two universal properties of Day convolution. [HHLN21] compares Torii's construction with the one in this text.

**Proposition A.** Given two symmetric monoidal $\infty$-categories $C$ and $D$, the extraction of adjoints gives inverse equivalences between the $\infty$-category of lax symmetric monoidal right adjoints $C^\otimes\to D^\otimes$ and the opposite of the $\infty$-category of oplax monoidal left adjoints $D_\otimes\to C_\otimes$.

**Theorem B.** Let $B$ be an $\infty$-category. Then there is an equivalence of $(\infty,2)$-categories
$$\mathbf{Cocart}^{\mathrm{lax},\mathrm{R}}(B)\simeq\Big(\mathbf{Cart}^{\mathrm{opl},\mathrm{L}}(B^{\mathrm{op}})\Big)^{(1,2)\text{-op}},$$
extracting adjoints fibrewise. Here the left-hand side has cocartesian fibrations over $B$ as objects, fibrewise right adjoint functors (which need not preserve cocartesian lifts) as 1-morphisms, and natural transformations between these as 2-morphisms. The right-hand side is defined dually, using cartesian fibrations and fibrewise right adjoints, with the directions of 1- and 2-morphisms reversed by the superscript. These equivalences are natural in pulling back along the base.

**Corollary C.** For any $\infty$-operad $O$, the extraction of adjoints gives a canonical equivalence of $(\infty,2)$-categories
$$\mathbf{MonCat}_O^{\mathrm{lax},\mathrm{R}}\simeq\Big(\mathbf{MonCat}_O^{\mathrm{opl},\mathrm{L}}\Big)^{(1,2)\text{-op}},$$
where the left-hand side is the $(\infty,2)$-category of $O$-monoidal $\infty$-categories, lax $O$-monoidal functors that admit (objectwise) left adjoints, and $O$-monoidal transformations. The right-hand side is defined dually, using oplax $O$-monoidal functors that admit right adjoints. These equivalences are natural in pulling back along operad maps in the base.

The text states that Proposition A follows from Corollary C with $O=\mathbb{E}_\infty$, and that Corollary C extends a result of Hinich [Hi15]: the internal mapping functor $[-,-]\colon C^{\mathrm{op}}\times C\to C$ of a closed $\mathbb{E}_{n+1}$-monoidal $\infty$-category $C$ admits a canonical lax $\mathbb{E}_n$-monoidal structure, for $1\le n\le\infty$. Hinich established the case $n=\infty$ in [Hi15] by different means.

**Fibrational description (stated in the introduction).** Functors $A\to\mathrm{Cocart}^{\mathrm{lax}}(B)$ correspond under covariant unstraightening to functors $p=(p_1,p_2)\colon E\to A\times B$ such that:
1. $p_1$ is a cocartesian fibration;
2. $p_1$-cocartesian morphisms are sent to equivalences by $p_2$;
3. for every $a\in A$, the functor $(p_2)_a\colon E_a\to B$ on the fibre over $a$ is a cocartesian fibration.

Such a functor is called a **Gray fibration**. Dually, functors $A^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Cocart}^{\mathrm{lax}}(B)$ correspond under contravariant unstraightening to functors $p=(p_1,p_2)\colon E\to A\times B$ such that (1) $p_1$ is a cartesian fibration, (2) $p_1$-cartesian morphisms are sent to equivalences by $p_2$, and (3) for every $a\in A$, $(p_2)_a\colon E_a\to B$ is a cocartesian fibration. Such a functor is called a **curved orthofibration**. Op-Gray fibrations, the cartesian dual of Gray fibrations, encode functors $A^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Cart}^{\mathrm{opl}}(B)$. Curved orthofibrations are self-dual: they can also be characterised by $p_2$ being a cocartesian fibration, $p_2$-cocartesian morphisms being sent to equivalences by $p_1$, and the functors $(p_1)_b\colon E_b\to A$ being cartesian fibrations.

**Theorem D.** There are natural equivalences of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathrm{Gray}(A,B)\simeq\mathrm{CrvOrtho}(A^{\mathrm{op}},B)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{OpGray}(A,B)\simeq\mathrm{CrvOrtho}(A,B^{\mathrm{op}}),$$
where $\mathrm{Gray}(A,B)$ and $\mathrm{OpGray}(A,B)$ are the $\infty$-categories of Gray fibrations and op-Gray fibrations over $A\times B$, and $\mathrm{CrvOrtho}(A^{\mathrm{op}},B)$ is the $\infty$-category of curved orthofibrations over $A^{\mathrm{op}}\times B$. In both cases the morphisms are required to preserve the defining (co)cartesian morphisms.

The text notes that bifibrations are exactly the curved orthofibrations whose fibres are $\infty$-groupoids, and that these correspond to left and right fibrations under the equivalences above. An equivalence of this kind was first established by Stevenson in [St18]. The text also cites [AMR22, Lemma B.5.7] and [AMR22, Remark 4.1.7] as related work at the level of spaces of objects.

**Theorem E.** There are natural equivalences of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathrm{Gray}(A,B)\simeq\mathrm{Fun}(A\boxtimes B,\mathbf{Cat}),$$
and consequently natural equivalences of $(\infty,2)$-categories
$$\mathbf{Cocart}^{\mathrm{lax}}(B)\simeq\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{lax}}(B,\mathbf{Cat}),\qquad \mathbf{Cart}^{\mathrm{opl}}(B)\simeq\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{opl}}(B^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Cat}),$$
given on objects by straightening of (co)cartesian fibrations. Here the targets have functors as objects, (op)lax natural transformations as morphisms, and modifications as 2-morphisms.

The $(\infty,2)$-categories $\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{lax}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})$ and $\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{opl}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})$ are defined universally by
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Cat}_2}(-,\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{lax}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Cat}_2}(-\boxtimes\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}),$$
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Cat}_2}(-,\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{opl}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Cat}_2}(\mathbf{X}\boxtimes-,\mathbf{Y}),$$
as functors $\mathrm{Cat}_2\to\mathrm{Gpd}$. Here $\boxtimes$ is the Gray tensor product. The 1-morphisms of $\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{lax}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})$ correspond to lax natural transformations, and similarly for oplax transformations. The text states that the cocartesian unstraightening of a functor $B\to\mathrm{Cat}$ has the universal property of the lax colimit: it is the left adjoint of the constant diagram functor $\mathrm{Cat}\to\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{lax}}(B,\mathbf{Cat})$.

**Corollary F.** Extracting adjoints gives an equivalence of $(\infty,2)$-categories
$$\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{lax},\mathrm{R}}(B,\mathbf{Cat})\simeq\Big(\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{opl},\mathrm{L}}(B,\mathbf{Cat})\Big)^{(1,2)\text{-op}}$$
for every $\infty$-category $B$. The superscript $\mathrm{R}$ denotes the locally full (1-full) sub-$(\infty,2)$-category of $\mathbf{Fun}^{\mathrm{lax}}(B,\mathbf{Cat})$ spanned by lax natural transformations that admit pointwise left adjoints; the right-hand side is defined dually. These equivalences are natural for restriction in $B$.

## Section 2: Definitions, notation and statements

**2.1.1 Definition.** Let $p\colon X\to S$ be a functor of $\infty$-categories. A morphism $\alpha\colon y\to z$ of $X$ is *$p$-cartesian* if, for every $x\in X$, the square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathrm{Map}_X(x,y) & \xrightarrow{\ \alpha_*\ } & \mathrm{Map}_X(x,z)\\
\downarrow{\scriptstyle p} & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle p}\\
\mathrm{Map}_S(p(x),p(y)) & \xrightarrow{\ p(\alpha)_*\ } & \mathrm{Map}_S(p(x),p(z))
\end{array}$$
is a pullback square in $\mathrm{Gpd}$. Dually, $\alpha$ is *$p$-cocartesian* if it is $p^{\mathrm{op}}$-cartesian as a morphism of $X^{\mathrm{op}}$. Equivalently, for every $x\in X$ the square with $\mathrm{Map}_X(z,x)$, $\mathrm{Map}_X(y,x)$, $\mathrm{Map}_S(p(z),p(x))$ and $\mathrm{Map}_S(p(y),p(x))$ (with maps $\alpha^*$ and $p(\alpha)^*$) is a pullback.

**2.1.2 Notation.** For $p\colon X\to B$, a $p$-cocartesian morphism $x\to y$ is sometimes written $x\rightarrowtail y$, and a $p$-cartesian morphism $x\to y$ is sometimes written $x\twoheadrightarrow y$.

**2.1.3 Definition.** Let $p\colon X\to S$ be a functor of $\infty$-categories and $T\subseteq S$ a subcategory. $X$ *has all $p$-cartesian lifts over $T$* if for every morphism $f\colon a\to b$ in $T$ and every object $x$ with $p(x)\simeq b$, there is a filler of the commutative square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
[0] & \xrightarrow{\ x\ } & X\\
{\scriptstyle d_0}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle p}\\
[1] & \xrightarrow{\ f\ } & S
\end{array}$$
which is a $p$-cartesian morphism. Dually, $X$ *has all $p$-cocartesian lifts over $T$* if $X^{\mathrm{op}}$ has all $p^{\mathrm{op}}$-cartesian lifts over $T^{\mathrm{op}}$. The functor $p$ is a *cartesian fibration* if $X$ has all $p$-cartesian lifts over $S$, and a *cocartesian fibration* if $X$ has all $p$-cocartesian lifts over $S$.

**2.1.4 Notation.** $\mathrm{Cocart}^{\mathrm{lax}}(S)$ and $\mathrm{Cart}^{\mathrm{opl}}(S)$ denote the full subcategories of $\mathrm{Cat}/S$ spanned by cocartesian and cartesian fibrations, respectively. $\mathrm{Cocart}(S)$ and $\mathrm{Cart}(S)$ denote the wide subcategories in which morphisms are required to preserve (co)cartesian edges.

**2.1.5 Remark.** The definition in 2.1.3 is an invariant version of the definition for quasicategories in [Lu09a, Definition 2.4.2.1]. A map $p$ between quasicategories is a (co)cartesian fibration in the present sense if and only if, for some (equivalently any) factorisation of $p$ into a categorical equivalence followed by a categorical fibration, the latter is a (co)cartesian fibration in Lurie's sense.

**2.1.6 Definition.** Let $p\colon X\to S$ be a functor of $\infty$-categories. A morphism $\alpha\colon y\to z$ of $X$ is *locally $p$-(co)cartesian* if it is a (co)cartesian morphism for the pullback $X\times_S[1]\to[1]$ of $p$ along $p(\alpha)\colon[1]\to S$. The functor $p$ is a *locally (co)cartesian fibration* if $X\times_S[1]\to[1]$ is a (co)cartesian fibration for every map $[1]\to S$.

**2.1.7 Notation.** $\mathrm{LocCocart}^{\mathrm{lax}}(S)$ and $\mathrm{LocCart}^{\mathrm{opl}}(S)$ denote the full subcategories of $\mathrm{Cat}/S$ spanned by locally cocartesian and locally cartesian fibrations. $\mathrm{LocCocart}(S)$ and $\mathrm{LocCart}(S)$ denote the wide subcategories in which morphisms are required to preserve locally (co)cartesian morphisms.

**2.1.8 Definition.** A functor $p\colon X\to S$ that is both a cartesian and a cocartesian fibration is a *bicartesian fibration*. $\mathrm{Bicart}^{(\mathrm{op})\mathrm{lax}}(S)$ denotes the full subcategory of $\mathrm{Cat}/S$ spanned by bicartesian fibrations.

**2.1.9 Remark.** In the category theory literature, bicartesian fibrations are often called "bifibrations". This text instead uses "bifibration" in the sense of [Lu09a], see 2.3.14.
