# Restatement

## Abstract

We study Poincaré Duality in the context of abstract 6-functor formalisms. We give a small and simple list of assumptions that implies Poincaré Duality. As an application, we give new uniform (and essentially formal) proofs of some previously established Poincaré Duality results.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1. General overview

A formalism of 6-functors is an approach to formalizing cohomology theories. It consists of a coefficient theory $D(X)$ for each space $X$, together with six operations
$$\left(f^{*},\, f_{*},\, \otimes,\, \underline{\mathrm{Hom}},\, f_{!},\, f^{!}\right),$$
satisfying the following axioms:

1. every second functor is a right adjoint to the previous one;
2. $f^{*}$ is symmetric monoidal;
3. $f_{!}$ commutes with arbitrary base change and satisfies the projection formula.

The first 6-functor formalism was introduced by A. Grothendieck in [SGA IV] for étale cohomology of schemes. In loc. cit., for each integer $n$, the assignment $X \mapsto D(X_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})$ is promoted to a 6-functor formalism. Other cohomology theories also carry 6-functor formalisms, for example $D$-modules and mixed Hodge modules. Recent examples include [LZ17], [Sch17], [CS19], [CS22], [Man22b], [Man22a], and [Sch22].

In each of these formalisms, any smooth morphism $f\colon X \to Y$ admits an invertible object $\omega_f \in D(X)$, called the dualizing object, and an equivalence
$$f^{!}(-) \simeq f^{*}(-) \otimes \omega_f$$
of functors $D(Y) \to D(X)$. It is often possible to give a precise formula for $\omega_f$.

The proofs of Poincaré Duality differ in each case. There is no uniform approach to this question in the general setting.$^{1}$ The main goal of the paper is to provide an approach to Poincaré Duality that applies in any 6-functor formalism. A consequence of this approach is a method for proving Poincaré Duality without understanding the whole coefficient category $D(X)$. This applies to formalisms such as [Sch17], [CS22], and [Man22b], where $D(X)$ is defined via descent.

The paper gives new proofs of Poincaré Duality in étale cohomology of schemes and adic spaces. These proofs use proper base change and the computation of the cohomology of $\mathbf{P}^1$. The methods are also applied to the 6-functor formalism $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{a}}_{\Box}(-;\mathcal{O}^{+}/p)^{\varphi}$ constructed by L. Mann in [Man22b], giving a proof of Poincaré Duality there that does not use explicit computations with the formal model of the torus.

$^{1}$ *There is a different approach to 6-functor formalisms based on motivic triangulated categories. In that setup, Poincaré Duality is a non-trivial consequence of the axioms. See Section 1.4.*

### 1.2. Our results

#### 1.2.1. Formulation of the questions

Fix a base scheme (resp. locally noetherian analytic adic space) $S$. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the category of locally finitely presented $S$-schemes (resp. locally finite type adic $S$-spaces). Let $\mathcal{D}\colon \mathrm{Corr}(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_{\infty}$ be a 6-functor formalism in the sense of Definition 2.3.10.

There is a notion of cohomologically smooth morphism with respect to any 6-functor formalism $\mathcal{D}$ (Definition 2.3.7). Roughly, a morphism $f\colon X \to Y$ is cohomologically smooth if it universally satisfies the conclusion of Poincaré Duality, after identifying the dualizing object $\omega_f$ with the Tate twist.

The question of proving Poincaré Duality reduces to the following three essentially independent questions.

**Question 1.2.1.** What is a minimalistic set of conditions on $\mathcal{D}$ that would ensure that any smooth morphism $f\colon X \to Y$ is cohomologically smooth?

**Question 1.2.2.** If every smooth morphism is cohomologically smooth, is there a reasonable formula for the dualizing object $\omega_f$?

**Question 1.2.3.** Is there a minimalistic set of conditions on $\mathcal{D}$ that would ensure that $\omega_f$ is equal to the Tate twist (appropriately defined)?

The paper answers all three questions. The answer to Question 1.2.1 is optimal: it characterizes all such $\mathcal{D}$. For Questions 1.2.2 and 1.2.3, the answers cover the examples of 6-functors established so far.

**Remark 1.2.4.** The answers are uniform for schemes and adic spaces. The same results hold in any "geometry" in which, for any $f\colon X \to Y$, the diagonal $X \to X \times_Y X$ is locally closed, and which admits a reasonable notion of vector bundles and blow-ups. The arguments apply to complex-analytic spaces, formal schemes, or derived schemes with almost no change. The paper works only with schemes and adic spaces.

**The main obstacle.** Suppose the correct formula for $\omega_f$ has been guessed. Proving Poincaré Duality then reduces to constructing an isomorphism
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{D(Y)}\left(\mathcal{F},\, f^{*}\mathcal{G} \otimes \omega_f\right) \simeq \mathrm{Hom}_{D(X)}\left(f_{!}\mathcal{F},\, \mathcal{G}\right),$$
functorial in $\mathcal{F} \in D(X)$ and $\mathcal{G} \in D(Y)$. For a general 6-functor formalism, there is little control over $D(X)$ and $D(Y)$. The problem is therefore to prove adjunction without a detailed understanding of these categories.

#### 1.2.2. Our answers

To address Question 1.2.1, the exact conditions needed to prove cohomological smoothness of a single morphism $f$ are separated via the concept of a trace-cycle theory. Fix $f\colon X \to Y$, the diagonal $\Delta\colon X \to X \times_Y X$, and the projections $p_1, p_2\colon X \times_Y X \to X$.

**Definition 1.2.5** (Definition 3.2.4). A *trace-cycle theory* on $f$ is a triple $(\omega_f, \mathrm{tr}_f, \mathrm{cl}_\Delta)$ consisting of:

1. an invertible object $\omega_f \in D(X)$;
2. a trace morphism $\mathrm{tr}_f\colon f_{!}\,\omega_f \to \mathbf{1}_Y$ in the homotopy category $D(Y)$;
3. a cycle map $\mathrm{cl}_\Delta\colon \Delta_{!}\mathbf{1}_X \to p_2^{*}\omega_f$ in the homotopy category $D(X \times_S X)$,

such that the following two diagrams commute.$^{2}$

- *Diagram (1):* the composite $\mathbf{1}_X \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } p_{1,!}(\Delta_!\mathbf{1}_X) \xrightarrow{\ p_{1,!}(\mathrm{cl}_\Delta)\ } p_{1,!}(p_2^{*}\omega_f) \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{tr}_{p_1}\ } \mathbf{1}_X$ is the identity of $\mathbf{1}_X$.
- *Diagram (2):* the composite $\omega_f \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } p_{2,!}(p_1^{*}\omega_f \otimes \Delta_!\mathbf{1}_X) \xrightarrow{\ p_{2,!}(\mathrm{id}\otimes \mathrm{cl}_\Delta)\ } p_{2,!}(p_1^{*}\omega_f \otimes p_2^{*}\omega_f) \xrightarrow{\ \wr\ } p_{2,!}p_1^{*}\omega_f \otimes \omega_f \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{tr}_{p_2}\otimes \mathrm{id}\ } \mathbf{1}_X \otimes \omega_f$ is the identity of $\omega_f$, where the isomorphism $\wr$ is the projection formula isomorphism.

$^{2}$ *See Construction 3.2.2 for the precise definition of $\mathrm{tr}_{p_i}$. It is the corresponding base change of $\mathrm{tr}_f$.*

**Theorem 1.2.6** (Theorem 3.3.1, Remark 3.3.2). Let $f\colon X \to Y$ be a morphism in $\mathcal{C}$. Then $f$ is cohomologically smooth if and only if $f$ admits a trace-cycle theory $(\omega_f, \mathrm{tr}_f, \mathrm{cl}_\Delta)$.

**Theorem 1.2.7** (Theorem 3.3.3). The relative projective line $g\colon \mathbf{P}^1_S \to S$ admits a trace-cycle theory $(\omega_g, \mathrm{tr}_g, \mathrm{cl}_\Delta)$ if and only if every smooth morphism $f\colon X \to Y$ is cohomologically smooth with respect to $\mathcal{D}$.

**Construction 1.2.8** (Variant 4.1.3). Assume that all smooth morphisms are cohomologically smooth with respect to $\mathcal{D}$. Let $f\colon \mathrm{V}_X(\mathcal{E}) \to X$ be the total space of a vector bundle $\mathcal{E}$ on $X$, with zero section $s\colon X \to \mathrm{V}_X(\mathcal{E})$. Define
$$C_X(\mathcal{E}) = s^{*}f^{!}\mathbf{1}_X \in D(X).$$

**Theorem 1.2.9** (Theorem 4.2.8 and Theorem 4.2.12). Suppose $\mathcal{D}$ is weakly motivic or geometric (Definition 4.2.1 and Definition 4.2.9). Let $f\colon X \to Y$ be a smooth morphism. Then there is a canonical isomorphism
$$f^{!}\mathbf{1}_Y \simeq C_X(\mathrm{T}_f) \in D(X),$$
where $\mathrm{T}_f$ is the relative tangent bundle of $f$.

To apply Theorem 1.2.9, one first establishes Theorem 1.2.7 to guarantee that all smooth morphisms are cohomologically smooth. The applicability of Theorem 1.2.9 then reduces to computing the cohomology of the affine line or studying invertible objects on the projective line.

For Question 1.2.3, the main tool is the notion of first Chern classes.

**Notation 1.2.10.** Fix an invertible object $\mathbf{1}_S\langle 1\rangle \in D(S)$. For each $f\colon X \to S$, define
$$\mathbf{1}_X\langle 1\rangle := f^{*}\mathbf{1}_S\langle 1\rangle \in D(X).$$
For each integer $d \geq 0$, define $\mathbf{1}_X\langle d\rangle := \mathbf{1}_X\langle 1\rangle^{\otimes d}$. For $d \leq 0$, define $\mathbf{1}_X\langle d\rangle := \mathbf{1}_X\langle -d\rangle^{\vee}$.

Theories of first Chern classes (weak, and strong) are defined with respect to $\mathbf{1}_X\langle 1\rangle$ (Definition 5.2.4 and Definition 5.2.8). A weak theory assigns to each line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ on a space $X$ a first Chern class $c_1(\mathcal{L}) \in \mathrm{H}^0(X, \mathbf{1}_X\langle 1\rangle)$, in an additive and sufficiently functorial way. A theory of first Chern classes is a weak theory satisfying the projective bundle formula for $\mathbf{P}^1_S \to S$. A strong theory is a weak theory satisfying the projective bundle formula for $\mathbf{P}^d_S \to S$ for all $d \geq 1$.

By Definition 5.2.8, if $c_1$ is a theory of first Chern classes, then
$$\mathbf{1}_S\langle -1\rangle \simeq \mathrm{Cone}\left(\mathbf{1}_S \to f_{*}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{P}^1_S}\right).$$
Hence the invertible object $\mathbf{1}_S\langle 1\rangle$ is unique up to isomorphism.

**Theorem 1.2.11** (Theorem 5.7.7). Let $\mathcal{D}$ be a 6-functor formalism satisfying the excision axiom (Definition 2.1.8) and admitting a theory of first Chern classes $c_1$. Let $f\colon X \to Y$ be a smooth morphism of pure relative dimension $d$. Then the right adjoint to $f_{!}\colon D(X) \to D(Y)$ is given by
$$f^{!}(-) = f^{*}(-) \otimes \mathbf{1}_X\langle d\rangle\colon D(Y) \to D(X).$$

*Note: the source text refers to "Question 1.2.11" at this point; this is as printed, and the question concerned is Question 1.2.3.*

**Theorem 1.2.12** (Theorem 5.7.6). Suppose $\mathcal{D}$ is either weakly $\mathbf{A}^1$-invariant or pre-geometric (Definition 2.1.10 and Definition 4.2.9). Let $c_1$ be a strong theory of first Chern classes on $\mathcal{D}$ underlying a theory of cycle maps $\mathrm{cl}_\bullet$ (Definition 5.3.3). Let $f\colon X \to Y$ be a smooth morphism of pure relative dimension $d$. Then the right adjoint to $f_{!}\colon D(X) \to D(Y)$ is given by
$$f^{!}(-) = f^{*}(-) \otimes \mathbf{1}_X\langle d\rangle\colon D(Y) \to D(X).$$

### 1.3. Applications

#### 1.3.1. Simplification of previous proofs

**Theorem 1.3.1** ([SGA IV, Exp. XVIII, Thm. 3.2.5], Remark 6.1.9). Let $Y$ be a scheme, $f\colon X \to Y$ a smooth morphism of pure dimension $d$, and $n$ an integer invertible in $\mathcal{O}_Y$. Then the functor
$$\mathrm{R}f_{!}\colon D(X_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}) \to D(Y_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})$$
admits a right adjoint given by
$$f^{*}(d)[2d]\colon D(Y_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}) \to D(X_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}).$$

**Theorem 1.3.2** ([Hub96, Thm. 7.5.3], Theorem 6.1.8). Let $Y$ be a locally noetherian analytic adic space, and $f\colon X \to Y$ a smooth morphism of pure dimension $d$. Let $n$ be an integer invertible in $\mathcal{O}_Y^{+}$. Then the functor
$$\mathrm{R}f_{!}\colon D(X_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}) \to D(Y_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})$$
admits a right adjoint given by
$$f^{*}(d)[2d]\colon D(Y_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}) \to D(X_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}).$$

*Remark (as stated in the text).* Theorem 1.3.1 is slightly stronger than [SGA IV, Exp. XVII, Thm. 3.2.5], since $f$ is not assumed separated and the derived categories are not assumed bounded. Theorem 1.3.2 is slightly stronger than [Hub96, Thm. 7.5.3] in the same way.

**Remark 1.3.3.** In [Sch17], Scholze constructs the theory of étale cohomology of diamonds, generalizing the theory of étale cohomology of $p$-adic spaces developed in [Hub96]. [Sch17] is essentially independent of [Hub96], except for quasi-compact base change and Poincaré Duality. The proof of Theorem 1.3.2 can be repeated verbatim for diamonds, making it independent of [Hub96].

**Application 1.3.4.**

1. *(Cohomological purity.)* If $i\colon X \to Y$ is a (Zariski) closed immersion of smooth $S$-schemes (resp. adic spaces) of pure dimensions $d_X$ and $d_Y$, then
$$\mathrm{R}i^{!}\underline{\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}} \simeq \underline{\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}}(-c)[-2c],$$
where $c = d_Y - d_Y$ (as printed). This follows from Poincaré Duality and the isomorphism $\mathrm{R}i^{!} \circ \mathrm{R}f_Y^{!} \simeq \mathrm{R}f_X^{!}$, where $f_X$ and $f_Y$ are the structure morphisms.

2. *(Smooth base change.)* Theorem 1.3.1 (resp. Theorem 1.3.2) and Proposition 2.3.9 imply smooth base change in étale cohomology.$^{3}$

3. *(Preservation of constructible sheaves.)* If $f\colon X \to Y$ is a smooth qcqs morphism, then $\mathrm{R}f_{!}$ restricts to
$$\mathrm{R}f_{!}\colon D^{(b)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(X_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}) \to D^{(b)}_{\mathrm{cons}}(Y_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}).$$
Assuming $Y$ is qcqs and $f$ has pure dimension $d$, [Zav23, Lemma 10.2] reduces this to showing that $\mathrm{R}f_{!}\colon D^{\geq -N}(X_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}) \to D^{\geq -N}(Y_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})$ preserves compact objects for every integer $N$. This holds because the right adjoint $\mathrm{R}f^{!} = f^{*}(d)[2d]$ commutes with infinite direct sums and has finite cohomological dimension.

4. *(Preservation of lisse sheaves.)* If $f\colon X \to Y$ is proper and smooth, then $\mathrm{R}f_{*}$ restricts to
$$\mathrm{R}f_{*}\colon D^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(X_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}) \to D^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(Y_{\text{ét}}; \mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}).$$
One reduces to the case where $n = \ell$ is prime. By [Zav23, Lemma 10.1], it suffices to show that $\mathrm{R}f_{*}$ preserves dualizable objects. By Poincaré Duality, for a dualizable object $L$, the object $\mathrm{R}f_{*}L$ is dualizable with dual $\mathrm{R}f_{*}(L^{\vee}(d)[2d])$.

$^{3}$ *The text notes that the only other known proof of smooth base change, [SGA IV, Exp. XVI, Cor. 1.2], is the original one. The classical proof of Poincaré Duality uses smooth base change as an input, so smooth base change cannot be deduced from that classical proof together with Proposition 2.3.9.*

**Theorem 1.3.5** ([Man22b, Thm. 3.10.20], Theorem 6.2.9). Let $Y$ be a locally noetherian analytic adic space over $\operatorname{Spa}(\mathbf{Q}_p, \mathbf{Z}_p)$, and $f\colon X \to Y$ a smooth morphism of pure dimension $d$. Then the functor
$$f_{!}\colon \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{a}}_{\Box}(X; \mathcal{O}_X^{+}/p)^{\varphi} \to \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{a}}_{\Box}(Y; \mathcal{O}_Y^{+}/p)^{\varphi}$$
admits a right adjoint given by
$$f^{*} \otimes \mathcal{O}_X^{+,\mathrm{a}}/p\,(d)[2d]\colon \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{a}}_{\Box}(Y; \mathcal{O}_Y^{+}/p)^{\varphi} \to \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{a}}_{\Box}(X; \mathcal{O}_X^{+}/p)^{\varphi}.$$

### 1.6. Terminology

We say that an analytic adic space $X$ is *locally noetherian* if there is an open covering
$$X = \bigcup_{i \in I} \operatorname{Spa}(A_i, A_i^{+})$$
by affinoids with strongly noetherian Tate rings $A_i$. Such spaces are sometimes called *locally strongly noetherian*. The definitions of locally finite type, locally weakly finite type, and locally $+$-weakly finite type morphisms of locally noetherian adic spaces follow [Hub96, Def. 1.3.3].
