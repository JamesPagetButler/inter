# Introduction

The Dirac operator on a Riemannian spin manifold is a natural elliptic first-order differential operator. If the underlying manifold is closed, its spectrum is discrete. Examples for which the spectrum can be computed explicitly can be used to test conjectures. For instance, they show that the spectrum does not determine the manifold, not even topologically. There are Dirac isospectral pairs of spherical space forms with different fundamental groups [3].

The following table lists the closed Riemannian spin manifolds for which the Dirac spectrum has been computed.

The paper computes the Dirac spectrum for the 3-dimensional Heisenberg manifolds. These are of the form $M = \Gamma\backslash G$, where $G$ is the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group and $\Gamma$ is a cocompact lattice. The metric comes from a left-invariant metric on $G$. The methods also work in higher dimensions.

The Heisenberg manifolds are circle bundles over the flat 2-torus. Their explicit Dirac eigenvalue formulas (Theorem 3.1) describe the behaviour under collapse to the 2-torus, in which the fibre length tends to zero. The behaviour depends on the spin structure. For some spin structures, all eigenvalues tend to $\pm\infty$. For the others, most eigenvalues tend to $\pm\infty$, but some converge to exactly the eigenvalues of the torus (Corollary 3.3). Section 4 shows that this holds in general for the Dirac operator on the total space of a collapsing circle bundle with totally geodesic fibres over any Riemannian manifold.

There are two types of spin structures, called projectable and nonprojectable. In the nonprojectable case, all eigenvalues tend to $\pm\infty$ (Theorem 4.5). In the projectable case, some eigenvalues converge to exactly the eigenvalues of the base (Theorem 4.1).

As an example, this is applied to the Hopf fibration over $\mathbb{C}P^m$, using explicit Dirac eigenvalue formulas on Berger spheres [4]:
- For $m$ even (when $\mathbb{C}P^m$ is nonspin), all eigenvalues tend to $\pm\infty$.
- For $m$ odd (when $\mathbb{C}P^m$ is spin), the limits of the eigenvalues give a computation of the Dirac spectrum of $\mathbb{C}P^m$ that confirms and simplifies the results in [9, 10, 30]. Multiplicities are given in Theorem 4.6.

Section 5 discusses how the Dirac spectrum changes on certain pairs or continuous families of Riemannian nilmanifolds. A Riemannian nilmanifold is a nilmanifold $\Gamma\backslash G$ with a Riemannian metric whose lift to $G$ is left-invariant. Here $G$ is a simply connected nilpotent Lie group and $\Gamma$ is a cocompact discrete subgroup. This class generalises the Heisenberg manifolds.

Besides the Dirac spectrum, the paper considers the following spectral invariants: the spectrum of the Laplacian on functions, the spectrum of the Laplacian on forms, the length spectrum (the set of lengths of all closed geodesics), and the marked length spectrum (the set of lengths of closed geodesics in each free homotopy class).

Several known results concern these invariants:
- Milnor [24] gave a pair of nonisometric Riemannian 16-dimensional tori that are isospectral for the Laplacian on functions.
- Gordon and Wilson [18] constructed a continuous family of nonisometric Riemannian 2-step nilmanifolds with the same spectrum of the Laplacian on functions and forms.
- For the 2-step case with a left-invariant metric, Eberlein [13, Theorem 5.20] showed that two 2-step nilmanifolds with the same marked length spectrum arise via almost inner automorphisms, and hence have the same spectrum of the Laplacian on functions and on forms. This statement is false if the condition "2-step" is dropped.
- Gornet [17] constructed two families of deformations of 3-step Riemannian nilmanifolds that are isospectral for the Laplacian on functions and for the marked length spectrum, but not for the spectrum of the Laplacian on 1-forms.

The paper shows that these Gornet families are Dirac isospectral for the nonprojectable spin structures, while they have nonconstant Dirac spectrum for the projectable spin structures.

# Statements

**THEOREM 3.1.** Spin structures on the 3-dimensional Heisenberg manifold $M(r,d,T)$, with $d>0$, $T>0$, $r\in\mathbb{N}$, correspond to triples $\delta=(\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)\in(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^3$. The case $\delta_3=-1$ occurs if and only if $r$ is even. The eigenvalues of the Dirac operator are as follows.

**A.** If $\delta_3=+1$:

- **a)** $\lambda^{\pm}_{\alpha,\beta} := -\dfrac{d^2T}{4} \pm 2\pi d\sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}$, with multiplicity 1, for each $\alpha\in\frac{1}{2r}\cdot\mathbb{Z}$ with $e^{2\pi i r\alpha}=\delta_1$ and each $\beta\in\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathbb{Z}$ with $e^{2\pi i\beta}=\delta_2$.
- **b)** $\lambda_\tau := -\dfrac{d^2T}{4} - 2\pi\tau T^{-1}$, with multiplicity $2\tau r$, for each $\tau\in\mathbb{N}$.
- **c)** $\lambda^{\pm}_{\tau,k} := -\dfrac{d^2T}{4} \pm 2\sqrt{\pi^2\tau^2T^{-2} + k\pi d^2\tau}$, with multiplicity $2\tau r$, for all $\tau, k\in\mathbb{N}$.

**B.** If $\delta_3=-1$:

- **a)** $\lambda_\tau := -\dfrac{d^2T}{4} - 2\pi\tau T^{-1}$, with multiplicity $2\tau r$, for each $\tau\in\mathbb{N}_0+\frac12$.
- **b)** $\lambda^{\pm}_{\tau,k} := -\dfrac{d^2T}{4} \pm 2\sqrt{\pi^2\tau^2T^{-2} + k\pi d^2\tau}$, with multiplicity $2\tau r$, for each $\tau\in\mathbb{N}_0+\frac12$ and each $k\in\mathbb{N}$.

**COROLLARY 3.2.** For each $N\in\mathbb{N}$, the 3-dimensional Heisenberg manifold $M=\Gamma_r\backslash G$ with spin structure corresponding to $\delta$ possesses a left-invariant metric such that the dimension of the space of harmonic spinors is at least $N$.

**COROLLARY 3.3.** If the Heisenberg manifold $M(r,d,T)$ collapses to the 2-torus, i.e. $T\to 0$, then the Dirac eigenvalues behave as follows.

- **A.** If $\delta_3=+1$: there are eigenvalues converging to those of the 2-torus. All other eigenvalues tend to $\pm\infty$.
- **B.** If $\delta_3=-1$: all eigenvalues tend to $\pm\infty$.

**THEOREM 4.1.** Let $M$ be a closed Riemannian spin manifold. Let $S^1$ act isometrically on $M$, and assume that the orbits have constant length. Let $N=M/S^1$ carry the induced Riemannian metric, and let $\tilde g_\ell$ be the metric on $M$ described above. Let $E\to N$ be a Hermitian vector bundle with a metric connection $\nabla^E$. Suppose that the spin structure on $M$ is projectable and that $N$ carries the induced spin structure. Let $\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots$ be the eigenvalues of the twisted Dirac operator $D^E$ on $\Sigma N\otimes E\to N$. Then the eigenvalues $(\lambda_{j,k}(\ell))_{j\in\mathbb{N},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}}$ of the twisted Dirac operator $\tilde D^\ell$ for $\tilde g_\ell$ on $\Sigma M\otimes\pi^*E\to M$ can be numbered so that they depend continuously on $\ell$ and such that, as $\ell\to 0$:

1. For any $j\in\mathbb{N}$ and $k\in\mathbb{Z}$, $\ell\cdot\lambda_{j,k}(\ell)\to k$. In particular, $\lambda_{j,k}(\ell)\to\pm\infty$ if $k\neq 0$.
2. If $n=\dim N$ is even, then $\lambda_{j,0}(\ell)\to\mu_j$. If $n=\dim N$ is odd, then
$$\lambda_{2j-1,0}(\ell)\to\mu_j,\qquad \lambda_{2j,0}(\ell)\to-\mu_j.$$

In both cases, the convergence of $\lambda_{j,0}(\ell)$ is uniform in $j$.

**REMARK 4.2.** The theorem also holds for a family of nonconstant length functions $\ell:N\to\mathbb{R}^+$, provided that $\ell$ and $(1/\ell)\,\mathrm{grad}\,\ell$ converge uniformly to $0$. The proof is analogous to the one below, except that some formulas contain an additional term depending on $\mathrm{grad}\,\ell$.

**LEMMA 4.3.** If $\tilde\Psi$ is a smooth section of $\Sigma M$, then
$$\nabla_K\tilde\Psi-\mathcal{L}_K\tilde\Psi=\frac{\ell^2}{4}\,\gamma(d\omega)\tilde\Psi.$$

**LEMMA 4.4.** Let $n$ be even. There is a homothety of Hilbert spaces $Q_k: L^2(\Sigma N\otimes L^{-k})\to V_k$ such that the horizontal covariant derivative is given by
$$\nabla_{\tilde X}Q_k(\Psi)=Q_k(\nabla_X\Psi)+\frac{\ell}{4}\,\gamma(K/\ell)\,\gamma(\tilde V_X)\,Q_k(\Psi),$$
where $V_X$ is the vector field on $N$ satisfying $d\omega(\tilde X,\cdot)=\langle\tilde V_X,\cdot\rangle$. Clifford multiplication is preserved, i.e.
$$Q_k(\gamma(X)\Psi)=\gamma(\tilde X)Q_k(\Psi).$$

**THEOREM 4.5.** Let $M$ be a closed Riemannian spin manifold. Let $S^1$ act isometrically on $M$, and assume that the orbits have constant length. Let $N=M/S^1$ carry the induced Riemannian metric, and let $\tilde g_\ell$ be the metric on $M$ described above. Let $E\to N$ be a Hermitian vector bundle with a metric connection $\nabla^E$. Suppose that the spin structure on $M$ is nonprojectable. Then the eigenvalues $(\lambda_{j,k}(\ell))_{j\in\mathbb{N},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}+(1/2)}$ of the twisted Dirac operator $\tilde D^\ell$ for $\tilde g_\ell$ on $\Sigma M\otimes\pi^*E\to M$ can be numbered so that they depend continuously on $\ell$ and such that, as $\ell\to 0$,
$$\ell\cdot\lambda_{j,k}(\ell)\to k\qquad\forall\, j=1,2,\dots.$$
In particular, $\lambda_{j,k}(\ell)\to\pm\infty$.

**THEOREM 4.6.** The Dirac operator on $\mathbb{C}P^m$, $m$ odd, has the eigenvalues
$$\pm 2\sqrt{\left(a_1+\frac{m+1}{2}\right)\left(a_2+\frac{m+1}{2}\right)},$$
where $a_1,a_2\in\mathbb{N}_0$ and $|a_1-a_2|\leq\frac{m-1}{2}$, with multiplicity
$$\frac{(a_1+m)!\,(a_2+m)!\,(a_1+a_2+m+1)}{a_1!\,a_2!\left(a_1+\frac{m+1}{2}\right)\left(a_2+\frac{m+1}{2}\right)m!\left(a_1-a_2+\frac{m-1}{2}\right)!\left(a_2-a_1+\frac{m-1}{2}\right)!}.$$

**THEOREM 5.1.** If $(\Gamma,\varepsilon)$ and $(\Gamma',\varepsilon')$ are representation equivalent, then the corresponding Dirac operators have the same spectrum for any left-invariant Riemannian metric on $G$.

**COROLLARY 5.2.** If $(\Gamma,\varepsilon)$ and $(\Gamma',\varepsilon')$ are representation equivalent, then $\Gamma\backslash G$ and $\Gamma'\backslash G$ have the same volume for any left-invariant volume form on $G$.

**THEOREM 5.3.** Let $G$ be a simply connected, strictly nonsingular nilpotent Lie group with left-invariant metric. Let $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_2$ be cocompact, discrete subgroups of $G$ such that
$$\Gamma_1\cap Z(G)=\Gamma_2\cap Z(G)\;(=:\Gamma_Z),$$
and let $\varepsilon_i:\Gamma_i\to\{-1,1\}$ be homomorphisms. Define the volume quotient
$$v:=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\overline{\Gamma}_1\backslash\overline{G})}{\mathrm{vol}(\overline{\Gamma}_2\backslash\overline{G})}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma_1\backslash G)}{\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma_2\backslash G)}\in\mathbb{Q}^+.$$
Then $(\Gamma_1,\varepsilon_1)$ and $(\Gamma_2,\varepsilon_2)$ are representation equivalent if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

1. $\varepsilon_1|_{\Gamma_Z}\equiv\varepsilon_2|_{\Gamma_Z}\equiv 1$, and $(\overline\Gamma_1,\overline\varepsilon_1)$ and $(\overline\Gamma_2,\overline\varepsilon_2)$ are representation equivalent for $\overline G$.
2. $\varepsilon_1|_{\Gamma_Z}\equiv\varepsilon_2|_{\Gamma_Z}\not\equiv 1$ and $v=1$.

**REMARK 5.4.** If, under the assumptions of Theorem 5.3, $\varepsilon_1|_{\Gamma_Z}\equiv\varepsilon_2|_{\Gamma_Z}\not\equiv 1$ but $v\neq 1$, then $(\Gamma_1,\varepsilon_1)$ and $(\Gamma_2,\varepsilon_2)$ are "representation equivalent up to multiplicity $v$". That is, $L^2_{\varepsilon_1}(\Gamma_1\backslash G)$ and $L^2_{\varepsilon_2}(\Gamma_2\backslash G)$ have the same irreducible components, and for every irreducible component $H$ with multiplicities $m_1,m_2$ we have $v=m_1/m_2$.

**LEMMA 5.5.** If $\rho:=\tau|_{\mathfrak z}\in L_{\Gamma,\varepsilon}-\{0\}$, then
$$m([\tau])=\left|\int_{\overline\Gamma\backslash\overline G}\underbrace{b_\rho\wedge\ldots\wedge b_\rho}_{d\text{-times}}\right|=\mathrm{vol}(\overline\Gamma\backslash\overline G)\cdot c_{G,\tau},$$
where $c_{G,\tau}$ depends only on $G$, on $\tau$, and on the volume form on $\overline G$. If $\rho:=\tau|_{\mathfrak z}\in\mathfrak z^*-(L_{\Gamma,\varepsilon}\cup\{0\})$, then $m([\tau])=0$.

**THEOREM 5.6.** There is a family of nilmanifolds having:

1. constant spectrum of the Laplacian on functions;
2. nonconstant spectrum of the Laplacian on 1-forms;
3. constant marked length spectrum;
4. nonconstant spectrum of the Dirac operator for projectable spin structures;
5. constant spectrum of the Dirac operator for nonprojectable spin structures.
