# Restatement

## Abstract

For a quasicompact quasiseparated scheme $X$, the hypercomplete pro-étale $\infty$-topos (as introduced by Bhatt and Scholze) is equivalent to the $\infty$-category of continuous representations of the Galois category $\mathrm{Gal}(X)$ with values in the $\infty$-category of pyknotic spaces. In particular, internally to pyknotic spaces, the hypercomplete pro-étale $\infty$-topos of $X$ is an $\infty$-category of presheaves.

## Introduction

### Main results and background

For a quasicompact quasiseparated scheme $X$, the Galois category $\mathrm{Gal}(X)$ of $X$, as introduced in [1], is the $\infty$-category of points of the étale $\infty$-topos $X_{\et}$. This $\infty$-category is a $1$-category with the following description: the objects are the geometric points of $X$, and the morphisms are specializations between them.

In [1], the authors refine $\mathrm{Gal}(X)$ to a pro-$\infty$-category whose underlying $\infty$-category recovers the $\infty$-category of points of $X_{\et}$. A pyknotic $\infty$-category is a hypersheaf of $\infty$-categories on the site of profinite sets. $\mathrm{Gal}(X)$ is regarded as a pyknotic $\infty$-category via $K \mapsto \mathrm{Fun}(K, \mathrm{Gal}(X))$, for a profinite set $K$, where $\mathrm{Fun}(K,\mathrm{Gal}(X))$ denotes the $\infty$-category of functors of pro-$\infty$-categories from $K$ to $\mathrm{Gal}(X)$. For pyknotic $\infty$-categories $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$, $\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})$ denotes the $\infty$-category of continuous functors (Definition 3.3).

Let $\mathcal{S}_\pi$ be the $\infty$-category of $\pi$-finite spaces, regarded as the discrete pyknotic $\infty$-category $\mathcal{S}_\pi^{\disc}$ (the constant sheaf on profinite sets with value $\mathcal{S}_\pi$). The exodromy equivalence (see [1, Corollary 0.5.2, Corollary 13.6.3]) gives a natural equivalence
$$\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathrm{Gal}(X), \mathcal{S}_\pi^{\disc}) \simeq X_{\et}^{\constr},$$
between continuous functors from $\mathrm{Gal}(X)$ to $\mathcal{S}_\pi^{\disc}$ and constructible étale sheaves on $X$.

As explained in [1], this equivalence holds more generally for spectral $\infty$-topoi (see [1, Theorem 0.4.7, Definition 9.2.1]). These are $\infty$-topoi $\mathcal{X}$ with a geometric morphism $f_*\colon \mathcal{X}\to\Sh(S)$, where $S$ is a spectral topological space, such that $f_*$ exhibits $\operatorname{Pt}(\mathcal{X})$ as an $S$-stratified space (see [1, Proposition 9.2.5] for the precise statement). Such $\mathcal{X}$ is called a spectral $S$-stratified $\infty$-topos. The main example is the canonical geometric morphism $X_{\et}\to X_{\operatorname{Zar}}$ for a qcqs scheme $X$.

To a spectral $S$-stratified $\infty$-topos $\mathcal{X}$ one associates a pro-$\infty$-category $\hat{\Pi}^S_{(\infty,1)}(\mathcal{X})$, the profinite $S$-stratified shape of $\mathcal{X}$, whose underlying $\infty$-category recovers the $\infty$-category of points of $\mathcal{X}$. Barwick–Glasman–Haine show an equivalence
$$\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(\hat{\Pi}^S_{(\infty,1)}(\mathcal{X}), \mathcal{S}_\pi^{\disc}\big) \simeq \mathcal{X}^{S\operatorname{-constr}},$$
where $\mathcal{X}^{S\operatorname{-constr}}$ is the $\infty$-category of $S$-constructible objects of $\mathcal{X}$ [1, Theorem 0.4.7 and Definition 9.4.7]. For $\mathcal{X} = X_{\et}$ this recovers the previous equivalence.

For a pyknotic $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, Construction [2, Construction 4.1.4] equips the $\infty$-category of pyknotic spaces $\Pyk(\mathcal{S})$ with the pyknotic structure $K\mapsto \Pyk(\mathcal{S})_{/K}$. The resulting pyknotic $\infty$-category is denoted $\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})$.

**Theorem 1.1.** Let $S$ be a spectral topological space and $\mathcal{X}$ an $S$-stratified spectral $\infty$-topos. Then the exodromy equivalence induces an equivalence of $\infty$-topoi
$$\mathcal{X}^{\pyk} \xrightarrow{\simeq} \mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(\hat{\Pi}^S_{(\infty,1)}(\mathcal{X}), \mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})\big),$$
between the pyknotification $\mathcal{X}^{\pyk}$ of $\mathcal{X}$ and the $\infty$-category of continuous functors from the profinite stratified shape $\hat{\Pi}^S_{(\infty,1)}(\mathcal{X})$ to the $\infty$-category of pyknotic spaces. Here the pyknotification$^1$ is defined as the $\infty$-category of hypersheaves on the category of pro-$S$-constructible sheaves $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}^{S\operatorname{-constr}})$, where the topology is generated by finite families that are jointly effective epimorphisms (see Definition 2.10).

$^1$ The pyknotification was introduced in [2, Construction 3.3.2] under the name *solidification*.

The $1$-categorical analogue of this construction was considered in [13, §7.1]. By Example 7.1.7 of loc. cit., for a qcqs scheme $X$, the pyknotification $X_{\et}^{\pyk}$ is equivalent to the $\infty$-topos of pro-étale hypersheaves $X_{\proet}^{\hyp}$ on $X$, as defined by Bhatt–Scholze in [3].

**Corollary 1.2.** Let $X$ be a qcqs scheme. Then the exodromy equivalence induces an equivalence of $\infty$-topoi
$$X_{\proet}^{\hyp} \xrightarrow{\simeq} \mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(\mathrm{Gal}(X), \mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})\big).$$

The introduction further notes that the pro-étale topos is locally weakly contractible [3, Proposition 4.2.8], which is deduced from the main result in Section 5. It also states that for any morphism $f\colon X\to Y$ of schemes, the functor $f^*\colon Y_{\proet}^{\hyp}\to X_{\proet}^{\hyp}$ commutes with all small limits (Corollary 4.18). This generalizes [3, Corollary 6.1.5], which treats closed immersions only.

### Technical outline

- Section 2 studies pro-objects and effective epimorphisms in them, and constructs the effective epimorphism topology on $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$ for a bounded $\infty$-pretopos $\mathcal{X}_0$, thereby defining the pyknotification of a bounded coherent $\infty$-topos.
- Section 3 gives an alternative description of $\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S}))$ for a pyknotic $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, as an $\infty$-category of continuous left fibrations over $\mathcal{C}$ (Theorem 3.11).
- Section 4 proves Theorem 1.1. The embedding $\mathcal{S}_\pi \hookrightarrow \Pyk(\mathcal{S})$ and the exodromy equivalence give an embedding
$$\mathcal{X}^{S\operatorname{-constr}} \simeq \mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(\hat{\Pi}^S_{(\infty,1)}(\mathcal{X}), \mathcal{S}_\pi\big) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(\hat{\Pi}^S_{(\infty,1)}(\mathcal{X}), \mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})\big),$$
which extends to a fully faithful embedding (Proposition 3.32)
$$\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}^{S\operatorname{-constr}}) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(\hat{\Pi}^S_{(\infty,1)}(\mathcal{X}), \mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})\big).$$

### Conventions

Following [2], fix tiny and small universes determined by strongly inaccessible cardinals $\delta_0<\delta_1$. For a strongly inaccessible $\delta$, a $\delta$-$\infty$-topos is a left exact accessible localization of $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{S}_\delta)$ for a $\delta$-small $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$. An "$\infty$-topos" means a $\delta_1$-$\infty$-topos. A spectral $S$-stratified $\infty$-topos $\mathcal{X}$ is always a $\delta_0$-$\infty$-topos, so $\mathcal{X}^{S\operatorname{-constr}}$ is tiny.

$\Pyk(\mathcal{S})$ is the $\infty$-topos of hypersheaves with values in $\mathcal{S}_{\delta_1}$ on the site of tiny profinite sets, with finite jointly epimorphic families as coverings. Every profinite set is covered by an extremally disconnected set, and every epimorphism $T\twoheadrightarrow K$ of profinite sets onto an extremally disconnected $K$ has a section. Writing $\Proj$ for the category of tiny extremally disconnected sets,
$$\Pyk(\mathcal{S}) = \mathrm{Fun}^{\times}\big(\Proj^{\operatorname{op}}, \mathcal{S}_{\delta_1}\big).$$
Hence $\Pyk(\mathcal{S})$ is hypercomplete and even Postnikov-complete. See [2, §2.2].

## Section 2: Pro-objects and the pyknotification

**Recollection 2.1.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a tiny $\infty$-category. The $\infty$-category $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})$ of pro-objects is the essentially unique $\infty$-category with a functor $i\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})$ such that, for every $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}$ with tiny cofiltered limits, precomposition with $i$ induces an equivalence
$$\mathrm{Fun}'(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}) \xrightarrow{i^*} \mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}).$$
Here $\mathrm{Fun}'(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})$ denotes the $\infty$-category of tiny cofiltered-limit-preserving functors. $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})$ exists by the dual of [10, Proposition 5.3.6.2] and is equivalent to $\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}})^{\operatorname{op}}$. It is small and locally tiny, but in general not tiny.

**Recollection 2.2.** Following [12, Definition A.6.1.1], an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{X}_0$ is an $\infty$-pretopos if:
i) $\mathcal{X}_0$ has finite limits;
ii) finite coproducts exist in $\mathcal{X}_0$ and are universal and disjoint;
iii) groupoid objects in $\mathcal{X}_0$ are effective and their geometric realizations are universal.

An $\infty$-pretopos $\mathcal{X}_0$ is bounded if it is small and every object is $n$-truncated for some integer $n$. If $\mathcal{X}$ is a coherent $\infty$-topos in the sense of [12, Definition A.2.0.12], the full subcategory $\mathcal{X}^{\coh}_{<\infty}$ spanned by truncated coherent objects is a bounded $\infty$-pretopos [12, Example 7.4.4]. Conversely, every bounded $\infty$-pretopos arises this way by [12, Theorem 7.5.3]. See [12, Appendix A] or [1, §3] for background.

**Example 2.3.** If $X$ is a qcqs scheme, the $\infty$-topos $X_{\et}$ is coherent. The $\infty$-category $X_{\et,<\infty}^{\coh}$ of truncated coherent objects is equivalent to the $\infty$-category $X_{\et}^{\constr}$ of constructible étale sheaves, which is therefore an $\infty$-pretopos. More generally, for any spectral $S$-stratified $\infty$-topos $\mathcal{X}\to\Sh(S)$ there is an equivalence $\mathcal{X}^{\coh}_{<\infty}\simeq\mathcal{X}^{S\operatorname{-constr}}$ [1, Corollary 9.5.5].

**2.4.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category with finite limits and geometric realizations of groupoid objects. A morphism $f\colon X\to Y$ in $\mathcal{C}$ is an effective epimorphism if the canonical morphism $|\check{C}(f)_\bullet| \to Y$ is an equivalence, where $\check{C}(f)_\bullet$ is the Čech nerve of $f$.

**Lemma 2.5.** Let $\mathcal{X}_0$ be a tiny bounded $\infty$-pretopos, and let $f_\bullet\colon I\to\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^1,\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0))$ be a tiny cofiltered diagram of effective epimorphisms. Then $\lim_i f_i$, viewed as a morphism in $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$, is an effective epimorphism.

**Proposition 2.6.** Let $\mathcal{X}_0$ be an $\infty$-pretopos. Then $\mathcal{X}_0$ admits a factorization system $(S_L, S_R)$ (in the sense of [10, §5.2.8]), where $S_L$ is the class of effective epimorphisms and $S_R$ is the class of $(-1)$-truncated morphisms.

**Proposition 2.7.** Let $\mathcal{X}_0$ be a tiny bounded $\infty$-pretopos. Then:
i) The effective epimorphisms and $(-1)$-truncated morphisms form a factorization system on $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$.
ii) A morphism in $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$ is an effective epimorphism if and only if it can be written as a tiny inverse limit of effective epimorphisms in $\mathcal{X}_0$.
iii) Effective epimorphisms are stable under pullback in $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$.

**Lemma 2.8.** Let $\mathcal{X}_0$ be a tiny $\infty$-pretopos. Then finite coproducts are universal in $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$.

**Corollary 2.9.** Let $\mathcal{X}_0$ be a bounded $\infty$-pretopos. Define a collection of morphisms $\{C_i\to D\}_{i\in I}$ in $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$ to be a covering if and only if there is a finite subset $J\subseteq I$ such that the induced map $\coprod_{j\in J} C_j \to D$ is an effective epimorphism in $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$. This defines a topology on $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$.

**Definition 2.10.** Let $\mathcal{X}_0$ be a tiny bounded $\infty$-pretopos. The topology on $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0)$ from Corollary 2.9 is the effective epimorphism topology. For a bounded coherent $\delta_0$-$\infty$-topos $\mathcal{X}$, the pyknotification of $\mathcal{X}$ is
$$\mathcal{X}^{\pyk} = \Sh^{\hyp}_{\eff}\big(\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}^{\coh}_{<\infty})\big).$$

**Remark 2.11.** The pyknotification of a bounded coherent $\infty$-topos first appeared in [2, Construction 3.3.2] under the name *solidification*. Since that word is also used in [4] in an unrelated way, a different name is used here.

**Example 2.12.** For $\mathcal{X} = \mathcal{S}$, the $\infty$-category of bounded coherent objects is $\mathcal{S}_\pi$. Write $\mathcal{S}_\pi^\wedge$ for $\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{S}_\pi)$, the $\infty$-category of profinite spaces. By [1, Proposition 13.4.9], every pro-finite set admits an effective epimorphism from a profinite set. Hence the full subcategory $\mathrm{Pro}(\operatorname{Set}^{\fin})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_\pi^\wedge$ is a basis for the effective epimorphism topology, and
$$\mathcal{S}^{\pyk}\simeq\Pyk(\mathcal{S}).$$

**Example 2.13.** For a qcqs scheme $X$, every object of $\mathrm{Pro}(X_{\et}^{\constr})$ admits an effective epimorphism from an object of $\mathrm{Pro}(X^{\constr}_{\leq 0})$ by [2, Proposition 3.3.8]. Hence $X_{\et}^{\pyk}$ is the hypercomplete $\infty$-topos associated to the $1$-topos $\Sh_{\eff}(\mathrm{Pro}(X^{\constr}_{\leq 0}),\operatorname{Set})$ introduced in [13, §7.1]. By [13, Example 7.1.7], $X_{\et}^{\pyk}$ is equivalent to the hypercomplete $\infty$-topos $X_{\proet}^{\hyp}$ of pro-étale sheaves defined by Bhatt–Scholze in [3].

**Remark 2.14.** One can also consider $\Sh_{\eff}(\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{X}_0))$, the $\infty$-topos of all sheaves for the effective epimorphism topology, instead of only hypersheaves. The hypercomplete version in Definition 2.10 is preferred: in many cases of interest $\mathcal{X}^{\pyk}$ is Postnikov complete and has a set of compact projective generators (see Theorem 5.7). The non-hypercomplete version does not have this property in general [2, Warning 2.2.2].

## Section 3: Pyknotic categories and left fibrations

**Definition 3.1.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category with finite products. A pyknotic object in $\mathcal{C}$ is a functor $\Proj^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathcal{C}$ that preserves finite products. Write $\Pyk(\mathcal{C}) = \mathrm{Fun}^{\times}(\Proj^{\operatorname{op}},\mathcal{C})$. Pyknotic objects in $\Cat_\infty$ are called pyknotic $\infty$-categories.

**Definition 3.2.** A pyknotic $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$ is small if $\mathcal{C}(K)$ is a small $\infty$-category for every $K\in\Proj$.

**Recollection 3.3.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$ be pyknotic $\infty$-categories. By [1, Definition 13.3.16], the $\infty$-category of continuous functors is
$$\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}) = \int_K \mathrm{Fun}\big(\mathcal{C}(K),\mathcal{D}(K)\big).$$
By [6, Proposition 2.3], the maximal subgroupoid of $\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})$ is equivalent to the mapping space $\map_{\Pyk(\Cat_\infty)}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})$. In particular, objects of $\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})$ are morphisms of pyknotic $\infty$-categories, i.e. natural transformations $\mathcal{C}(-)\to\mathcal{D}(-)$.

**Example 3.4.** $\Pyk(\mathcal{S})$ carries a pyknotic structure given by $\Pyk(\mathcal{S})_{/-}$. It sends an extremally disconnected $K$ to $\Pyk(\mathcal{S})_{/K}$, and a morphism $f\colon K\to K'$ to the pullback functor $f^*\colon\Pyk(\mathcal{S})_{/K}\to\Pyk(\mathcal{S})_{/K'}$. This pyknotic $\infty$-category is denoted $\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})$.

**Recollection 3.5** ([1, Construction 13.3.10]). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category with finite products. Evaluation at the point defines $\Gamma_*\colon\Pyk(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}$. For $X\in\Pyk(\mathcal{C})$, $\Gamma_*(X)$ is the underlying object of $X$. If $\mathcal{C}$ is presentable, $\Gamma_*$ has a left adjoint $(-)^{\disc}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\Pyk(\mathcal{C})$. For $X\in\mathcal{C}$, $X^{\disc}$ is the discrete pyknotic object attached to $X$. Explicitly, for $K\in\Proj$ written as $K=\lim_{i\in I}K_i$ with each $K_i$ a finite set,
$$X^{\disc}(K)\simeq\operatorname*{colim}_{i\in I} X^{K_i},$$
where $X^{K_i}$ denotes $\prod_{K_i}X$.

**Definition 3.6.** A continuous functor $f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}$ in $\Pyk(\Cat_\infty)$ is a left fibration if, for every $K\in\Proj$, the functor $\mathcal{C}(K)\to\mathcal{D}(K)$ is a left fibration. $\Lfib^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C})$ denotes the full subcategory of $\Pyk(\Cat_\infty)_{/\mathcal{C}}$ spanned by the left fibrations.

**Example 3.7.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an ordinary $\infty$-category and $p\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{C}$ an ordinary left fibration. Then $p$ induces a functor $\mathcal{F}^{\disc}\to\mathcal{C}^{\disc}$ of discrete pyknotic categories. For $K=\lim_{i\in I}K_i$ with each $K_i$ finite, the induced functor $\mathcal{F}^{\disc}(K)\to\mathcal{C}^{\disc}(K)$ is
$$\operatorname*{colim}_i \mathcal{F}^{K_i}\longrightarrow\operatorname*{colim}_i\mathcal{C}^{K_i}.$$
As a filtered colimit of left fibrations, this is equivalent to a left fibration. Hence $\mathcal{F}^{\disc}\to\mathcal{C}^{\disc}$ is a left fibration in the continuous sense.

**Example 3.8.** For an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, $\Tw(\mathcal{C})$ denotes its twisted arrow $\infty$-category [11, Tag 03JG]. For a pyknotic $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, the continuous twisted arrow $\infty$-category $\Tw^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C})$ is the pyknotic $\infty$-category $K\mapsto\Tw(\mathcal{C}(K))$. It comes with a canonical functor $\Tw^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\times\mathcal{C}$, which is a continuous left fibration by [11, Tag 03JQ].

**Recollection 3.9.** For a small $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, there is a natural equivalence
$$\Un\colon\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\Cat_\infty)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Cocart(\mathcal{C})$$
between $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\Cat_\infty)$ and the $\infty$-category $\Cocart(\mathcal{C})$ of cocartesian fibrations over $\mathcal{C}$. Its inverse is denoted $\St$. For $f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\Cat_\infty$, $\Un(f)$ is the unstraightening of $f$. $\Un$ restricts to an equivalence $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{S})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Lfib(\mathcal{C})$. By [11, Tag 028T], this is described as follows: a functor $f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{S}$ is sent to the left fibration $p\colon\Un(f)\to\mathcal{C}$ determined by the pullback square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}\Un(f) & \xrightarrow{\pi} & \mathcal{S}_\ast\\ {\scriptstyle p}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle}\\ \mathcal{C} & \xrightarrow{f} & \mathcal{S},\end{array}$$
where $\mathcal{S}_\ast$ is the $\infty$-category of pointed spaces and $\pi$ is the forgetful functor. $\pi$ is called the universal left fibration.

**Definition 3.10.** The pyknotic $\infty$-category of pointed pyknotic spaces is
$$\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S}_\ast)\colon\Proj^{\operatorname{op}}\to\Cat_\infty,\qquad K\mapsto\mathrm{Fun}^{\times}\big((\Proj_{/K})^{\operatorname{op}},\mathcal{S}_\ast\big)\simeq\Pyk(\mathcal{S})(K)_{\ast/}.$$
The forgetful functor $\pi\colon\mathcal{S}_\ast\to\mathcal{S}$ induces a continuous left fibration $\pi^{\mathrm{cts}}\colon\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})_\ast\to\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})$, the universal continuous left fibration.

**Theorem 3.11.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a small pyknotic $\infty$-category. Then there is a canonical equivalence
$$\Un^{\mathrm{cts}}\colon\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})\big)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Lfib^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C}),$$
described as follows. A continuous functor $f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})$ is sent to the left fibration $\Un^{\mathrm{cts}}(f)\to\mathcal{C}$ determined by the pullback square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}\Un^{\mathrm{cts}}(f) & \to & \mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S}_\ast)\\ \downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle\pi^{\mathrm{cts}}}\\ \mathcal{C} & \xrightarrow{f} & \mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S}).\end{array}$$
The inverse sends a continuous left fibration $\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{C}$ to the continuous functor whose component at $f\colon K'\to K\in\Tw(\Proj)$ is the assignment
$$\begin{aligned}\mathcal{C}(K)\times(\Proj_{/K'})^{\operatorname{op}} &\longrightarrow \mathcal{S}\\ (x_K,\ \alpha\colon T\to K') &\longmapsto \St(\mathcal{F}(T))\big((f\circ\alpha)^*(x_K)\big).\end{aligned}$$

**Lemma 3.12.** Let $p\colon E\to C$ and $q\colon B\to C$ be cocartesian fibrations, and let $f\colon E\to B$ be a functor over $C$ that preserves cocartesian edges. Assume that for every $c\in C$, the induced functor $f_c\colon E_c\to B_c$ is equivalent to a left fibration. Then $f$ is equivalent to a left fibration.

**Theorem 3.13.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category and $F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\Cat_\infty$ a functor. There is a natural equivalence
$$\operatorname*{colim}_{\Tw(\mathcal{C})} F(-)\times\mathcal{C}_{-/}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Un(F)$$
of functors $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\Cat_\infty)\to\Cat_\infty$. Here $\Un(F)$ is the total space of the cocartesian fibration classifying $F$.

**Corollary 3.14.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category and $F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\Cat_\infty$ a functor. There is a natural equivalence
$$\operatorname*{colim}_{\Tw(\mathcal{C})} F(-)\times\mathcal{C}_{-/}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Un(F)$$
of cocartesian fibrations over $\mathcal{C}$. In particular, for any $f\colon x\to y$ in $\mathcal{C}$, the canonical functor
$$c_f\colon F(x)\times\mathcal{C}_{y/}\to\operatorname*{colim}_{\Tw(\mathcal{C})} F(-)\times\mathcal{C}_{-/}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Un(F)$$
preserves cocartesian edges.

**Lemma 3.15.** Let $q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{D}$ and $p\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}$ be cocartesian fibrations, and let $\alpha\colon\Delta^1\to\mathcal{D}$ be a $p$-cocartesian edge. Then the functor classified by the cocartesian fibration $\Delta^1\times_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{E}\to\Delta^1$ is given by taking fibers over $\alpha(0)$ in the commutative square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}\mathcal{E}_{p\alpha(0)} & \xrightarrow{p(\alpha)_!} & \mathcal{E}_{p\alpha(1)}\\ \downarrow & & \downarrow\\ \mathcal{D}_{p\alpha(0)} & \xrightarrow{p(\alpha)_!} & \mathcal{D}_{p\alpha(1)}.\end{array}$$

**Lemma 3.16.** The right adjoint of $\Un\colon\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\Cat_\infty)\to\Cat_\infty$ sends an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{X}$ to the functor $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}_{-/},\mathcal{X})$.

**Lemma 3.17.** Let $f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}$ be a left fibration of $\infty$-categories. Then the commutative square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}R(\mathcal{X}) & \to & \mathcal{X}\\ \downarrow & & \downarrow\\ R(\mathcal{Y}) & \to & \mathcal{Y},\end{array}$$
induced by the adjunction $F\dashv R$, is a pullback.

**Definition 3.18.** Let $\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\colon\Proj^{\operatorname{op}}\to\Cat_\infty$ be functors.
i) $\Lfib^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}cts}}(\mathcal{C})$ denotes the full subcategory of $\mathrm{Fun}(\Proj^{\operatorname{op}},\Cat_\infty)_{/\mathcal{C}}$ spanned by the objectwise left fibrations.
ii) $\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}cts}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}) = \int_K\mathrm{Fun}\big(\mathcal{C}(K),\mathcal{D}(K)\big)$.

**Proposition 3.19.** Let $\mathcal{C}\colon\Proj^{\operatorname{op}}\to\Cat_\infty$ be a functor. There is a canonical equivalence
$$\Un^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}cts}}\colon\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}cts}}\big(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{Fun}((\Proj_{/-})^{\operatorname{op}},\mathcal{S})\big)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Lfib^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}cts}}(\mathcal{C}).$$
It is described as follows. A natural transformation $\mathcal{C}(-)\to\mathrm{Fun}((\Proj_{/-})^{\operatorname{op}},\mathcal{S})$ is sent to the pullback of $\pi_*$ along $f$, i.e. to the left fibration $\Un^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}cts}}(f)\to\mathcal{C}(-)$ determined by the pullback square with $\mathrm{Fun}((\Proj_{/-})^{\operatorname{op}},\mathcal{S}_\ast)\xrightarrow{\pi_*}\mathrm{Fun}((\Proj_{/-})^{\operatorname{op}},\mathcal{S})$ and $f$. The inverse sends a left fibration $\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{C}$ to the natural transformation whose component at $f\colon K'\to K\in\Tw(\Proj)$ is
$$\begin{aligned}\mathcal{C}(K)\times(\Proj_{/K'})^{\operatorname{op}} &\longrightarrow \mathcal{S}\\ (x_K,\ \alpha\colon T\to K') &\longmapsto \St(\mathcal{F}(T))\big((f\circ\alpha)^*(x_K)\big).\end{aligned}$$

**Corollary 3.20.** Let $F$ be a pyknotic space. Then there is a canonical equivalence
$$\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})_{/F}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(F,\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})\big).$$

**Remark 3.21.** Let $f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}$ be a functor of pyknotic $\infty$-categories. By the naturality in Theorem 3.13, the equivalences of Theorem 3.11 fit into a commutative square
$$\begin{array}{ccc}\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(\mathcal{D},\Pyk(\mathcal{S})\big) & \xrightarrow{\simeq} & \Lfib^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{D})\\ \downarrow{\scriptstyle f^*} & & \downarrow\\ \mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{cts}}\big(\mathcal{C},\mathbf{Pyk}(\mathcal{S})\big) & \xrightarrow{\simeq} & \Lfib^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{C}),\end{array}$$
where the lower horizontal functor is given by pulling back a continuous left fibration along $f$.
