# Restatement

## Abstract

The paper defines "animated affinoid algebras" and proves some of their basic properties. It then generalizes the fppf-descent results of Mann [Man22b] and Mikami [Mik22] (for discrete rings or affinoid algebras) to discrete animated rings and animated affinoid algebras.

## Introduction

### 0.1 Animated affinoid $K$-algebras

Let $K$ be a complete non-archimedean field. The paper uses condensed mathematics [Sch19] to define animated affinoid $K$-algebras and studies quasi-coherent complexes over them. The definition is motivated by the following observations.

A naive definition would require a solid condensed animated $K$-algebra $A$ with $\pi_0 A$ an affinoid $K$-algebra in the usual sense. More precisely, there should be an affinoid $K$-algebra $A'$ in the usual sense such that the associated condensed ring $\underline{A'}$ is isomorphic to $\pi_0 A$. For such $A$, the map $(K,\mathbb{Z})_{\blacksquare}\to(A,\mathbb{Z})_{\blacksquare}$ is not necessarily steady [Man22b, Definition 2.3.16]. If $A$ is nuclear as an object of $\mathcal{D}((K,\mathbb{Z})_{\blacksquare})$, then $(K,\mathbb{Z})_{\blacksquare}\to(A,\mathbb{Z})_{\blacksquare}$ is steady by [Sch20, Proposition 13.14].

**Definition 0.1.** An animated affinoid $K$-algebra is a condensed animated $K$-algebra $A$ satisfying:

1. The static solid ring $\pi_0 A$ is an affinoid $K$-algebra in the usual sense.
2. The object $A$ of $\mathcal{D}(K)^{\mathrm{cond}}$ is $(K,\mathbb{Z})_{\blacksquare}$-complete and nuclear as an object of $\mathcal{D}((K,\mathbb{Z})_{\blacksquare})$.

### 0.2 Fppf-descent

Let $A\to B$ be a faithfully flat map of discrete animated rings, and let $B^{n/A}$ denote the $n$-fold derived tensor product of $B$ over $A$. The following is proved in [Lur18, Mat16].

**Theorem 0.2.** There is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathcal{D}(A)\xrightarrow{\sim}\varprojlim_{[n]\in\Delta}\mathcal{D}(B^{n+1/A}),$$
where $\mathcal{D}(A)$ is the derived $\infty$-category of $A$-modules.

**Theorem 0.3.** Let $A\to B$ be a finitely presented faithfully flat map of discrete rings. Then there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})\xrightarrow{\sim}\varprojlim_{[n]\in\Delta}\mathcal{D}\big((B^{n+1/A})_{\blacksquare}\big),$$
where $\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})$ is the derived $\infty$-category of $A_{\blacksquare}$-modules.

[Man22b] and [Mik22] proved Theorem 0.3 independently. The proof in [Mik22] uses a compact object $N_{B/A}\in\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})$ such that
$$-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_{\blacksquare}}B_{\blacksquare}\simeq R\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_A(N_{B/A},-),$$
where $-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_{\blacksquare}}B_{\blacksquare}\colon\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})\to\mathcal{D}(B_{\blacksquare})$ is composed with the forgetful functor $\mathcal{D}(B_{\blacksquare})\to\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})$.

**Theorem 0.4.** Let $A\to B$ be a faithfully flat map of discrete animated rings such that $\pi_0 f\colon\pi_0A\to\pi_0B$ is finitely presented. Then there exists a compact object $N_{B/A}\in\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})$ such that there is an equivalence of functors
$$-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_{\blacksquare}}B_{\blacksquare}\simeq R\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_A(N_{B/A},-)\colon\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})\to\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare}).$$

The object $N_{B/A}$ represents the functor $\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})\to\mathrm{Ani}$, $M\mapsto\mathrm{Map}_A(A,M\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_{\blacksquare}}B_{\blacksquare})$ (Lemma 4.12).

**Theorem 0.5.** Let $A\to B$ be a faithfully flat map of discrete animated rings such that $\pi_0 f$ is finitely presented. Then there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})\xrightarrow{\sim}\varprojlim_{[n]\in\Delta}\mathcal{D}\big((B^{n+1/A})_{\blacksquare}\big).$$

The proof of Theorem 0.5 uses the following steps. The functor $-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_{\blacksquare}}B_{\blacksquare}$ preserves small limits, by comparison with $-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_0A_{\blacksquare}}\pi_0B_{\blacksquare}$. The adjoint functor theorem [Lur09, Corollary 5.5.2.9] is then applied. The sequence $\{N_{B/A}^{\otimes n}\}_{n\ge1}$ is uniformly bounded above (Theorem 4.14), which follows from uniform boundedness of $\{N_{\pi_0B/\pi_0A}^{\otimes n}\}_{n\ge1}$ [Mik22, Proposition 2.20]. This implies that $A_{\blacksquare}\to B_{\blacksquare}$ is descendable in the sense of [Man22b].

**Theorem 0.6.** Let $f\colon A\to B$ be a faithfully flat morphism of animated affinoid $K$-algebras. Let $B^{n/A}$ denote the $n$-fold derived tensor product of $B$ over $(A,\mathbb{Z})_{\blacksquare}$. Then there is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories
$$\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})\xrightarrow{\sim}\varprojlim_{[n]\in\Delta}\mathcal{D}\big((B^{(n+1)/A})_{\blacksquare}\big).$$

## Notation and conventions

- All rings, including condensed ones, are unital and commutative.
- For an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, $0$-truncated objects are called *static* objects. Discrete rings are ordinary rings. Discrete animated rings are animated rings.
- A compactly generated topological ring $A$ is identified with the condensed ring $\underline{A}$ [Sch19, Proposition 1.7].
- "Analytic animated ring" and "uncompleted analytic animated ring" are as in [Man22b].
- For an uncompleted analytic animated ring $\mathcal{A}$, $\underline{\mathcal{A}}$ is its underlying condensed animated ring. An object $M\in\mathcal{D}(\underline{\mathcal{A}})$ is $\mathcal{A}$-complete if it lies in $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$.
- An extremally disconnected set is an extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff space.
- "f-adic ring" and "affinoid pair" are used in place of "Huber ring" and "Huber pair". For an f-adic ring $A$, $A^\circ$ denotes its ring of power-bounded elements.
- $\mathrm{Ani}$ is the $\infty$-category of anima, equivalent to the $\infty$-category of spaces [Lur09, Definition 1.2.16.1]. $\mathcal{C}at_\infty$ is the $\infty$-category of small $\infty$-categories.
- $\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)$ (or $\mathrm{Map}(X,Y)$) is the mapping anima.
- $\Delta$ is the simplex category, with objects $[n]=\{0,\ldots,n\}$ for $n\ge0$. $\Delta_s\subset\Delta$ is the subcategory with injective morphisms. $\Delta_{s,\le m}$ is the full subcategory of $\Delta_s$ on $[n]$ for $0\le n\le m$.

## Section 1: Preliminaries on cardinals and condensed objects

**Definition 1.1** [Man22b, Definition 2.1.1]. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category with all small colimits.

1. For a strong limit cardinal $\kappa$, let $\mathrm{Exd}_\kappa$ denote the category of $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected sets. Let $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})_\kappa$ be the full $\infty$-subcategory of $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Exd}_\kappa^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{C})$ consisting of functors that preserve all finite products. This is the $\infty$-category of $\kappa$-condensed objects in $\mathcal{C}$.
2. For strong limit cardinals $\kappa\le\kappa'$, there is a fully faithful left adjoint $\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Cond}_{\kappa'}(\mathcal{C})$ to the restriction functor, given by left Kan extension. Define
$$\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\varinjlim_\kappa\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})_\kappa,$$
with the colimit over all strong limit cardinals. This is the $\infty$-category of condensed objects in $\mathcal{C}$.
3. An object $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})$ is *discrete* if $X\in\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})_\omega$. Taking the colimit of the restrictions $\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Cond}_\omega(\mathcal{C})$ gives the *discretization functor* $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})_\omega$. It is right adjoint to the fully faithful inclusion $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})_\omega\to\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})$.

**Definition 1.2.** Let $\kappa$ be a strong limit cardinal.

1. $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$ (resp. $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}$) denotes the $\infty$-category of $\kappa$-condensed (resp. condensed) objects in $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})$.
2. $\mathrm{AniRing}^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$ (resp. $\mathrm{AniRing}^{\mathrm{cond}}$) denotes the $\infty$-category of $\kappa$-condensed (resp. condensed) objects in $\mathrm{AniRing}$.

**Remark 1.3.**

1. $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}_\omega\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}$ is equivalent to $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})$. The discretization $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}\to\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})$, $M\mapsto M_{\mathrm{disc}}$, is t-exact.
2. $\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Cond}(\mathbb{Z})_\kappa)\simeq\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$ [Man22b, Proposition 2.1.13].
3. $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}$ has a natural closed symmetric monoidal structure and a left complete t-structure [Man22b, Proposition 2.1.11]. Its heart is $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathbb{Z})$.
4. $\mathrm{AniRing}^{\mathrm{cond}}_\omega\simeq\mathrm{AniRing}$. Animated rings are identified with discrete condensed animated rings.
5. $\mathrm{Ani}(\mathrm{Cond}(\mathrm{Ring})_\kappa)\simeq\mathrm{AniRing}^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$ [Sch20, Lemma 11.8], where $\mathrm{Ani}$ is the animation [Sch20, Definition 11.4].

**Definition 1.4.** Let $A$ be a condensed animated ring. Regard $A$ as an $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-algebra in $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}$. Define $\mathcal{D}(A)^{\mathrm{cond}}$ as the $\infty$-category of $A$-module objects in $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}$. If $A\in\mathrm{AniRing}^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$, let $\mathcal{D}(A)^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa\subset\mathcal{D}(A)^{\mathrm{cond}}$ be the full subcategory of objects whose underlying objects lie in $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$.

**Lemma 1.5.** Let $A$ be a condensed animated ring.

1. $\mathcal{D}(A)$ is a stable closed symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category with all small limits and colimits. It has a natural left complete t-structure compatible with products and filtered colimits.
2. For every strong limit cardinal $\kappa$ with $A\in\mathrm{AniRing}^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$, $\mathcal{D}(A)_\kappa$ is stably presentable. It is compactly generated by the compact objects $A[S]=A\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[S]$ for $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected sets $S$. Moreover, $\mathcal{D}(A)_\kappa$ is closed under small colimits, tensor products, and truncations on $\mathcal{D}(A)$.

**Lemma 1.6.** Let $A$ be a condensed animated ring and $\lambda$ a strong limit cardinal. For a strong limit cardinal $\kappa$ whose cofinality is at least $\lambda$ and such that $A\in\mathrm{AniRing}^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$, $\mathcal{D}(A)_\kappa$ is closed under $\lambda$-small limits in $\mathcal{D}(A)$.

**Definition 1.7.** Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an analytic animated ring [Man22b, Definition 2.3.10]. For a strong limit cardinal $\kappa$ such that $\underline{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$, define $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})_\kappa\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ as the smallest full $\infty$-subcategory that contains $\mathcal{A}[S]$ for all $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected sets $S$, and is closed under small colimits and shifts.

**Lemma 1.8** [Man22b, Proposition 2.3.4]. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an analytic animated ring.

1. $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ is a stable $\infty$-category. It is closed under small limits and colimits, and under truncations on $\mathcal{D}(\underline{\mathcal{A}})$. It is generated under small colimits and shifts by the objects $\mathcal{A}[S]$ for extremally disconnected sets $S$.
2. Let $\kappa$ be a strong limit cardinal with $\underline{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$. Then $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})_\kappa$ is stably presentable. It is compactly generated by the compact objects $\mathcal{A}[S]$ for $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected $S$. It is closed under small colimits and tensor products in $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$.
3. Let $\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}$ be a morphism of analytic animated rings, and let $\kappa$ be a strong limit cardinal with $\underline{\mathcal{A}},\underline{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})_\kappa$. Then $-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}\colon\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B})$ restricts to a symmetric monoidal functor $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})_\kappa\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B})_\kappa$.

**Definition 1.9.** An *uncountable solid cutoff cardinal* $\kappa$ is a cardinal such that:

1. $\kappa$ is a strong limit cardinal.
2. $\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)>\omega$.
3. For every cardinal $\lambda<\kappa$, there is a strong limit cardinal $\kappa_\lambda<\kappa$ with $\mathrm{cf}(\kappa_\lambda)>\lambda$.

**Lemma 1.10.** For every cardinal $\lambda$, there is an uncountable solid cutoff cardinal $\kappa>\lambda$.

**Remark 1.11.** The analytic animated ring $\pi_0\mathcal{A}$ is not necessarily an analytic ring in the sense of [Sch19, Definition 7.4]. This is because $(\pi_0\mathcal{A})[S]=\mathcal{A}[S]\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}\pi_0\underline{\mathcal{A}}$ is not necessarily static.

**Proposition 1.12.** Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an analytic animated ring and $\kappa$ a strong limit cardinal.

1. Suppose $\mathcal{A}[S]\in\mathcal{D}(\underline{\mathcal{A}})_\kappa$ for every $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected set $S$. Then $\mathcal{D}(\underline{\mathcal{A}})_\kappa\supset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})_\kappa$. In particular, $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})_\kappa$ is closed under truncations on $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$.
2. Assume:
   - (a) $\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)>\omega$;
   - (b) for every $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected set $S$, $(\pi_0\mathcal{A})[S]\in\mathcal{D}(\underline{\pi_0\mathcal{A}})_\kappa$;
   - (c) for every integer $n\ge0$, $\pi_n(\underline{\mathcal{A}})\in\mathcal{D}(\underline{\pi_0\mathcal{A}})_\kappa$.

   Then $\mathcal{D}(\underline{\mathcal{A}})_\kappa\supset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})_\kappa$.

**Corollary 1.13** [Man22b, Lemma 2.9.12]. Let $A$ be an animated ring and $\kappa$ an uncountable solid cutoff cardinal. Then $\mathcal{D}(A)^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa\supset\mathcal{D}(A_{\blacksquare})_\kappa$.

**Remark 1.14.** In the setting of Corollary 1.13, $A_{\blacksquare}[S]\in\mathcal{D}(A)^{\mathrm{cond}}_\kappa$ can be proved directly for an animated ring $A$ (see the proof of [Man22b, Lemma 2.9.12]). The corollary is proved above in a form that is comparable to the case of animated affinoid algebras.

## Section 2: Nuclear modules and animated affinoid algebras

**Definition 2.1.** Let $A$ be a condensed animated ring and $A_0\to\pi_0(A)$ a morphism of condensed rings. Let $(A_0)_{\mathrm{disc}}$ denote the discretization of $A_0$. This is an ordinary ring, and $((A_0)_{\mathrm{disc}})_{\blacksquare}=((A_0)_{\mathrm{disc}},(A_0)_{\mathrm{disc}})_{\blacksquare}$ is an analytic ring [Man22b, Definition 2.9.3]. Define the uncompleted analytic animated ring $(A,A_0)_{\blacksquare}$ as follows.

- If $A$ is static, there is a natural morphism $(A_0)_{\mathrm{disc}}\to A$. Define $(A,A_0)_{\blacksquare}$ as the condensed animated ring $A$ with the induced uncompleted analytic animated ring structure from $((A_0)_{\mathrm{disc}})_{\blacksquare}$ [Sch20, Proposition 12.8], or an uncompleted version of [Man22b, Definition 2.3.13].
- For general $A$, define $(A,A_0)_{\blacksquare}$ as the condensed animated ring $A$ with the uncompleted analytic animated ring structure induced from $(\pi_0(A),A_0)_{\blacksquare}$, using [Sch20, Proposition 12.21].

**Remark 2.2.** An object $M\in\mathcal{D}(A)$ is $(A,A_0)_{\blacksquare}$-complete if and only if each $H^i(M)$ is $((A_0)_{\mathrm{disc}})_{\blacksquare}$-complete. Equivalently, each $H^i(M)$ is $(\pi_0A,A_0)_{\blacksquare}$-complete, for every $i\in\mathbb{Z}$. In particular, if $\pi_n(A)$ is $((A_0)_{\mathrm{disc}})_{\blacksquare}$-complete for every $n\ge0$, then $(A,A_0)_{\blacksquare}$ is an analytic animated ring. This holds, for example, when $A$ is a limit of objects in $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{cond}}_\omega$.

**Definition 2.3.** An object $M\in\mathcal{D}(A)$ is *Banach over $A$* if $M$ is $t$-adically complete and $M/^{\mathbb{L}}t$ is discrete.

**Lemma 2.4.** The full $\infty$-subcategory of $\mathcal{D}(A)$ spanned by the Banach objects is closed under truncations on $\mathcal{D}(A)$.

**Proposition 2.5.** Let $M\in\mathcal{D}(A)$ be a filtered colimit of Banach objects of $\mathcal{D}(A)$. Then for every subring $A_0$ of $\pi_0(A)_{\mathrm{disc}}$, $M$ is $(A,A_0)_{\blacksquare}$-complete and nuclear as an object of $\mathcal{D}((A,A_0)_{\blacksquare})$.

**Proposition 2.6.** Let $M\in\mathcal{D}((A,A_0)_{\blacksquare})$ be nuclear. Then $M$ is a filtered colimit of Banach objects of $\mathcal{D}(A)$.

**Corollary 2.7.** Let $A_0,A_0'$ be subrings of $\pi_0(A)_{\mathrm{disc}}$, and let $M\in\mathcal{D}(A)$. Then $M$ is nuclear as an object of $\mathcal{D}((A,A_0)_{\blacksquare})$ if and only if $M$ is nuclear as an object of $\mathcal{D}((A,A_0')_{\blacksquare})$.

**Corollary 2.8.** Let $\kappa$ be a strong limit cardinal with $\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)>\omega$. Let $A_0$ be a subring of $\pi_0(A)_{\mathrm{disc}}$. Then every nuclear object $M$ of $(A,A_0)_{\blacksquare}$ lies in $\mathcal{D}(A)_\kappa$.

**Lemma 2.9.** Let $\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}$ be a morphism of analytic animated rings. Assume the analytic animated ring structure of $\mathcal{B}$ is induced from $\mathcal{A}$, and that $\underline{\mathcal{B}}$ is nuclear as an object of $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$. Then $M\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B})$ is nuclear as an object of $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})$ if and only if $M$ is nuclear as an object of $\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{B})$.
