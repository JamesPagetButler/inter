# Restatement

## Abstract

We develop some basic concepts in the theory of higher categories internal to an arbitrary $\infty$-topos. We define internal left and right fibrations and prove a version of the Grothendieck construction and of Yoneda's lemma for internal categories.

## 1. Introduction

### Main results

Let $\mathcal{B}$ be an $\infty$-topos. A *$\mathcal{B}$-category* is a category internal to $\mathcal{B}$. The $\infty$-category $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$ of $\mathcal{B}$-categories is defined as the full subcategory of the $\infty$-category of simplicial objects in $\mathcal{B}$ that satisfies the Segal condition and univalence, mimicking the definition of complete Segal spaces given by Rezk [Rez01], as previously done by Lurie [Lur09b] and Rasekh [Ras18].

Since complete Segal spaces model $\infty$-categories, the latter can be identified with $\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$, and one obtains an equivalence
$$\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})\simeq \mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{Cat}_\infty}(\mathcal{B})$$
between the $\infty$-category of $\mathcal{B}$-categories and the $\infty$-category of $\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$-valued sheaves on $\mathcal{B}$. Consequently, the sheaf $\mathcal{B}_{/-}$ that sends $A\in\mathcal{B}$ to the slice $\infty$-category $\mathcal{B}_{/A}$ defines a (large) $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{\Omega}$, called the *internal universe* of $\mathcal{B}$.

The category $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$ is cartesian closed. Hence, for any two $\mathcal{B}$-categories $\mathsf{C}$ and $\mathsf{D}$ there is a $\mathcal{B}$-category $[\mathsf{C},\mathsf{D}]\in\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$ of functors between $\mathsf{C}$ and $\mathsf{D}$.

**Main result 1 (internal Grothendieck construction).** For any $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$, one defines a large $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{LFib}_{\mathsf{C}}$ whose objects are left fibrations over $\mathsf{C}$. The following holds:

**Theorem 4.5.1.** There is a canonical equivalence $[\mathsf{C},\mathsf{\Omega}]\simeq \mathsf{LFib}_{\mathsf{C}}$ that is natural in $\mathsf{C}$.

**Main result 2 (internal Yoneda lemma).** For any $\mathcal{B}$-category $\mathsf{C}$, one constructs a mapping bifunctor
$$\Map_{\mathsf{C}}(-,-)\colon \mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{\Omega},$$
whose transpose gives the Yoneda embedding $h\colon\mathsf{C}\to[\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega}]$. Let $\ev\colon \mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times[\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega}]\to\mathsf{\Omega}$ be the evaluation functor, i.e. the counit of the adjunction $\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times-\dashv[\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},-]$.

**Theorem 4.7.8.** There is a commutative diagram of (large) $\mathcal{B}$-categories
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times[\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega}] & \xrightarrow{\ \ev\ } & \mathsf{\Omega} \\
\big\downarrow{\scriptstyle h\times \id} & & \big\uparrow{\scriptstyle \Map_{[\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega}]}(-,-)} \\
[\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega}]^{\mathrm{op}}\times[\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{\Omega}] & & 
\end{array}
$$

In particular, Theorem 4.7.8 implies that the Yoneda embedding $h$ is a fully faithful functor.

### Related work

Lawvere formulated axioms for a theory of categories in 1963 [Law63]. In subsequent years, Bénabou developed the theory of internal categories in a presheaf topos through the notion of fibred categories. For a detailed exposition of the theory of categories in a $1$-topos, see [Joh02].

Riehl and Shulman [RS17] proposed a synthetic approach to the theory of $\infty$-categories. The theory of categories in an $\infty$-topos is expected to provide categorical semantics for their type theory (cf. [RS17, Remark A.14]). The authors state that their version of Yoneda's lemma can therefore be formally deduced from [RS17], but that their approach differs in that it is derived from the internal Grothendieck construction, which is not featured in [RS17].

Barwick, Dotto, Glasman, Nardin and Shah [BDG+16] developed the theory of $\infty$-categories parametrised by a base $\infty$-category. Such parametrised $\infty$-categories are a special case of categories in an arbitrary $\infty$-topos; they correspond precisely to categories in presheaf $\infty$-topoi.

Rasekh has previously worked out some aspects of the theory of internal higher categories in [Ras18].

## 2. Preliminaries on $\infty$-toposes and factorisation systems

**Remark 2.4.1.** If $\mathcal{B}$ is an $\infty$-topos, there are a priori two ways to define the universe enlargement $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}$ relative to the universe $\mathbf{W}$:

1. Apply the construction to the pair $\mathbf{U}\in\mathbf{W}$, i.e. define $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}=\mathrm{Shv}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathcal{B})$.
2. Apply the construction first to $\mathbf{U}\in\mathbf{V}$ and then to $\mathbf{V}\in\mathbf{W}$, i.e. set $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}=\mathrm{Shv}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}})$, where the right-hand side denotes the $\infty$-category of functors $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{S}}$ that commute with $\mathbf{V}$-small limits.

Both approaches give the same object. Upon identifying $\mathbf{U}$ with a regular cardinal in $\mathbf{V}$, one may identify $\mathcal{B}$ with $\widehat{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathbf{U}}(\mathcal{B})$. Then [Lur09a, Proposition 5.3.5.10] implies that the inclusion $\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{B}}$ induces an equivalence
$$\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathbf{U}\text{-filt.}}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}},\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{op}})\simeq \mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{B},\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{op}}),$$
where the left-hand side is the full subcategory of $\mathrm{Fun}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}},\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{op}})$ spanned by the functors that preserve $\mathbf{U}$-filtered colimits. Furthermore, [Lur09a, Proposition 5.5.1.9] implies that this equivalence restricts to an equivalence
$$\mathrm{Shv}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}})\simeq\mathrm{Shv}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathcal{B}).$$
Its proof does not require $\widehat{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{op}}$ to be presentable relative to $\mathbf{V}$, but only that it admits $\mathbf{V}$-small colimits.

**Lemma 2.4.2.** For every $A\in\mathcal{B}$, the equivalence $\PSh_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathcal{B}_{/A})\simeq \PSh_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathcal{B})_{/A}$ restricts to an equivalence
$$\widehat{\mathcal{B}_{/A}}\simeq \widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{/A}.$$

**Proposition 2.4.3.** There is a canonical equivalence $\widehat{\mathcal{B}_{/-}}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{/-}$ of functors $\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\widehat{\mathrm{Cat}}_\infty$.

**Proposition 2.4.4.** For any $\infty$-topos $\mathcal{B}$, the inclusion $\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{B}}$ commutes with small limits and colimits, and the internal mapping bifunctor on $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}$ restricts to the internal mapping bifunctor on $\mathcal{B}$.

**Definition 2.5.1.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category. A *factorisation system* is a pair $(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R})$ of classes of maps in $\mathcal{C}$ such that:
1. Every map $f$ in $\mathcal{C}$ admits a factorisation $f\simeq rl$ with $r\in\mathcal{R}$ and $l\in\mathcal{L}$.
2. $\mathcal{L}^{\bot}=\mathcal{R}$ and ${}^{\bot}\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{L}$.

**Proposition 2.5.2.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category and let $(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R})$ be a factorisation system in $\mathcal{C}$. Then:
1. The intersection $\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{R}$ is precisely the collection of equivalences in $\mathcal{C}$.
2. If $g\in\mathcal{L}$, then $fg\in\mathcal{L}$ if and only if $f\in\mathcal{L}$. Dually, if $f\in\mathcal{R}$, then $fg\in\mathcal{R}$ if and only if $g\in\mathcal{R}$.
3. $\mathcal{R}$ is stable under pullbacks, and $\mathcal{L}$ is stable under pushouts.
4. Both $\mathcal{R}$ and $\mathcal{L}$ are stable under retracts.
5. $\mathcal{R}$ is stable under all limits that exist in $\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^1,\mathcal{C})$, and dually $\mathcal{L}$ is stable under all colimits that exist in $\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^1,\mathcal{C})$.

**Remark 2.5.3.** Suppose $(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R})$ is a factorisation system in an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$ with a final object $1\in\mathcal{C}$. Let $f\colon c\to d$ be a map in $\mathcal{C}$, and let $L\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{R}_{/1}$ be a left adjoint to the inclusion. Consider the commutative diagram with rows $c\to d$ and $L(c)\to L(d)$, and terminal row $1\xrightarrow{\id}1$, where the vertical composites are determined by the factorisations of the terminal maps $\pi_c\colon c\to 1$ and $\pi_d\colon d\to 1$ into maps in $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{R}$.

- If $f\in\mathcal{L}$, then item (2) of Proposition 2.5.2 implies that $L(f)\in\mathcal{L}$. Item (2) also implies that $L(f)\in\mathcal{R}$. Hence $L(f)$ is an equivalence. So $L$ sends maps in $\mathcal{L}$ to equivalences in $\mathcal{R}_{/1}$.
- The converse does not hold in general: a map sent to an equivalence by $L$ need not lie in $\mathcal{L}$.
- A notable exception is the case where $\pi_d\colon d\to 1$ already lies in $\mathcal{L}$. Then $d\to L(d)$ is an equivalence, so if $L(f)$ is an equivalence, then $f\in\mathcal{L}$.

**Proposition 2.5.4** ([Lur09a, Proposition 5.5.5.7]). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a presentable $\infty$-category and let $S$ be a small set of maps in $\mathcal{C}$. Then there is a factorisation system $(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R})$ in $\mathcal{C}$ with $\mathcal{R}=S^{\bot}$ and $\mathcal{L}={}^{\bot}\mathcal{R}$.

**Definition 2.5.5.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a presentable $\infty$-category and let $S$ be a class of maps in $\mathcal{C}$. Then $S$ is *saturated* if:
1. $S$ contains all equivalences in $\mathcal{C}$ and is closed under composition;
2. $S$ is closed under small colimits in $\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^1,\mathcal{C})$;
3. $S$ is closed under pushouts.

**Proposition 2.5.6.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a presentable $\infty$-category and let $S$ be a small set of maps in $\mathcal{C}$. Let $(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R})$ be the associated factorisation system. Then $\mathcal{L}$ is the smallest saturated class of maps that contains $S$.

**Proposition 2.5.7** ([ABFJ20, Proposition 3.2.9]). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a presentable and cartesian closed $\infty$-category and let $S$ be a small set of maps in $\mathcal{C}$. Then there is a factorisation system $(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R})$ in $\mathcal{C}$ such that $\mathcal{R}=S^{\Vdash}$ and $\mathcal{L}={}^{\Vdash}\mathcal{R}={}^{\bot}\mathcal{R}$.

**Proposition 2.5.8.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a presentable and cartesian closed $\infty$-category and let $S$ be a small set of maps in $\mathcal{C}$. Let $(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R})$ be the factorisation system provided by Proposition 2.5.7. Then $\mathcal{L}$ is the smallest saturated class of maps that contains $\{c\times f \mid c\in\mathcal{C},\ f\in S\}$.

**Example 2.5.9.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a presentable and cartesian closed $\infty$-category.
- A map $f\colon c\to d$ is a *monomorphism* if it is internally right orthogonal to the codiagonal $1\sqcup 1\to 1$, where $1$ is the final object of $\mathcal{C}$. By construction, a map is a monomorphism if and only if its diagonal is an equivalence.
- A map is a *strong epimorphism* if it is internally left orthogonal to every monomorphism.
- By Proposition 2.5.7, one obtains a factorisation system whose left class consists of strong epimorphisms and whose right class consists of monomorphisms.
- If $\mathcal{C}$ is an $\infty$-topos, then strong epimorphisms are precisely covers, i.e. effective epimorphisms in the terminology of [Lur09a]. This follows from the fact that covers and monomorphisms form a factorisation system in any $\infty$-topos [Lur09a, Example 5.2.8.16], together with the fact that the left class of a factorisation system is determined by its right class.

## 3. Categories internal to an $\infty$-topos

**Remark 3.1.1.** For $A\in\mathcal{B}$, regard $A$ as a constant simplicial object via the diagonal functor $\iota\colon\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_\Delta$. Since products in $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ are computed objectwise, the endofunctor $A\times-$ on $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ is equivalent to postcomposition with $A\times-\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}$. The latter has a right adjoint $[A,-]\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}$. Since postcomposition with an adjunction induces an adjunction on functor $\infty$-categories, uniqueness of adjoints implies that the internal mapping object functor $[A,-]\colon\mathcal{B}_\Delta\to\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ is obtained by applying the internal mapping object functor of $\mathcal{B}$ levelwise. More precisely, the restriction of the internal mapping object bifunctor $[-,-]$ on $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ along $\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{B}_\Delta\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ is equivalent to the transpose of the composite
$$\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{B}_\Delta\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\id\times\ev_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}}\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{B}\xrightarrow{[-,-]}\mathcal{B},$$
where $\ev_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}$ is the counit of $-\times\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\dashv\Fun(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}},-)$, and the second functor is the internal mapping object bifunctor on $\mathcal{B}$. In particular, the internal mapping object bifunctor on $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ restricts to that on $\mathcal{B}$.

**Proposition 3.1.2.** The composite
$$\mathcal{B}_\Delta\xrightarrow{(-)^{\Delta^\bullet}}\PSh_{\mathcal{B}_\Delta}(\Delta)\xrightarrow{(-)_0}\mathcal{B}_\Delta,$$
where the second arrow is postcomposition with the evaluation $(-)_0\colon\mathcal{B}_\Delta\to\mathcal{B}$, is equivalent to the identity functor on $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$.

**Lemma 3.1.3.** Let $S$ be a saturated class of maps in $\mathcal{B}$ that contains the maps $s^0\colon\Delta^1\otimes C\to C$ for every $C\in\mathcal{B}_\Delta$. Then $S$ contains the projection $\Delta^n\otimes A\to A$ for every $n\geq 0$ and every $A\in\mathcal{B}$.

**Proposition 3.1.4.** For any simplicial object $C\in\mathcal{B}_\Delta$, the following are equivalent:
1. $C$ lies in the essential image of the diagonal functor $\iota\colon\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_\Delta$.
2. $C$ is internally local with respect to $s^0\colon\Delta^1\to\Delta^0$.

**Definition 3.1.5.** A simplicial object in $\mathcal{B}$ is a *$\mathcal{B}$-groupoid* if it satisfies the equivalent conditions of Proposition 3.1.4. We denote by $\Grpd(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ the full subcategory spanned by the $\mathcal{B}$-groupoids.

**Definition 3.2.1.** For $n\geq 1$, let $I^n=\Delta^1\sqcup_{\Delta^0}\cdots\sqcup_{\Delta^0}\Delta^1\subset\Delta^n$ be the *spine* of $\Delta^n$, i.e. the simplicial sub-$\infty$-groupoid of $\Delta^n$ spanned by the inclusions $d^{\{i-1,i\}}\colon\Delta^1\subset\Delta^n$ for $i=1,\dots,n$. Let $E^1$ be the simplicial $\infty$-groupoid defined by the pushout square
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\Delta^1\sqcup\Delta^1 & \xrightarrow{\ s^0\sqcup s^0\ } & \Delta^0\sqcup\Delta^0 \\
\big\downarrow{\scriptstyle d^{\{0,2\}}\sqcup d^{\{1,3\}}} & \lrcorner & \big\downarrow \\
\Delta^3 & \longrightarrow & E^1.
\end{array}
$$
We refer to $E^1$ as the *walking equivalence*.

**Remark 3.2.2.** Many authors define the walking equivalence as the nerve of the category with two objects and a unique isomorphism between them. The simplicial set $E^1$ of Definition 3.2.1 differs in that it consists of a map together with separate left and right inverses.

**Remark 3.2.3.** In the situation of Definition 3.2.1, the colimits defining $I^n$ and $E^1$ can be computed as ordinary ($1$-categorical) pushouts of simplicial sets.

*Justification as given in the text.* Colimits in $\mathcal{S}_\Delta$ are computed levelwise, so it suffices to show that for each $k\geq 0$, the colimits in $\mathcal{S}$ of the diagrams in $\Set\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}$ defining $I^n_k$ and $E^1_k$ can be computed as ordinary colimits in $\Set$. The Quillen model structure on simplicial sets is left proper, so ordinary pushouts along monomorphisms present homotopy pushouts in $\mathcal{S}$. Since $I^n$ is an iterated pushout along monomorphisms in $\mathcal{S}_\Delta$, $I^n_k$ is an iterated pushout along monomorphisms in $\Set$, which gives the claim for $I^n$. For $E^1$, it fits into a commutative diagram of pushouts in which the lower-left and upper-right squares are computed by ordinary pushouts of simplicial sets (with $\Delta^1\hookrightarrow\Delta^3\to\Delta^3\sqcup_{\Delta^1}\Delta^0$ a monomorphism). The claim follows.

**Definition 3.2.4.** An object $C\in\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ is a *$\mathcal{B}$-category* if:
- (Segal conditions) $C$ is internally local with respect to $I^2\hookrightarrow\Delta^2$, and
- (univalence) $C$ is internally local with respect to $E^1\to\Delta^0$.

We denote the full subcategory of $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ spanned by the $\mathcal{B}$-categories by $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$.

**Lemma 3.2.5.** The following sets generate the same saturated class of maps in $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$:
1. $S=\{I^2\otimes K\hookrightarrow\Delta^2\otimes K \mid K\in\mathcal{B}_\Delta\}$;
2. $T=\{I^n\otimes A\hookrightarrow\Delta^n\otimes A \mid n\geq 2,\ A\in\mathcal{B}\}$;
3. $U=\{I^n\otimes K\hookrightarrow\Delta^n\otimes K \mid n\geq 2,\ K\in\mathcal{B}_\Delta\}$.

**Lemma 3.2.6.** Let $S$ be a strongly saturated class of maps in $\mathcal{B}$ that contains the maps $E^1\otimes A\to A$ and $I^n\otimes A\hookrightarrow\Delta^n\otimes A$ for all $A\in\mathcal{B}$ and all $n\geq 0$. Then $S$ contains the maps $E^1\otimes C\to C$ for all $C\in\mathcal{B}_\Delta$.

**Proposition 3.2.7.** Let $C$ be a simplicial object in $\mathcal{B}$. The following are equivalent:
1. $C$ is a $\mathcal{B}$-category.
2. For all $n\geq 2$, the map $C_n\to C_1\times_{C_0}\cdots\times_{C_0}C_1$ is an equivalence, and the map $C_0\to (C_0\times C_0)\times_{C_1\times C_1}C_3$ is an equivalence.

**Remark 3.2.8.** Proposition 3.2.7 allows one to define a $\mathcal{C}$-category, for any $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$ with finite limits, as a simplicial object $C\in\mathcal{C}_\Delta$ satisfying the second condition of Proposition 3.2.7.

**Proposition 3.2.9.** The inclusion $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ exhibits $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$ as an accessible localisation of $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$. In particular, $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$ is presentable.

**Remark 3.2.10.**
- Since $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$ is an accessible localisation of $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$, the inclusion $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ creates small limits.
- Colimits of small diagrams are usually neither created nor preserved by the inclusion. They are computed by applying the localisation $\mathcal{B}_\Delta\to\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$ to the colimits in $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$.
- Filtered colimits are created by the inclusion. Since such colimits commute with finite limits, Proposition 3.2.7 shows that the colimit in $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ of a small filtered diagram of $\mathcal{B}$-categories lies in $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$, and is therefore its colimit in $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$. Since colimits are universal in $\mathcal{B}_\Delta$, filtered colimits are universal in $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$.

**Proposition 3.2.11.** The full subcategory $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_\Delta$ is an exponential ideal, and hence is cartesian closed. In other words, if $\mathsf{C}$ is a $\mathcal{B}$-category and $D$ is a simplicial object in $\mathcal{B}$, then the internal mapping object $[D,\mathsf{C}]$ is a $\mathcal{B}$-category.

**Corollary 3.2.12.** The localisation functor $\mathcal{B}_\Delta\to\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})$ preserves finite products.

**Proposition 3.2.13.** The groupoidification functor $(-)^{\mathrm{gpd}}\colon\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})\to\Grpd(\mathcal{B})$ commutes with small colimits and finite products.

**Proposition 3.2.14.** The core $\mathcal{B}$-groupoid functor $(-)^{\simeq}\colon\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{B})\to\Grpd(\mathcal{B})$ commutes with small filtered colimits and small limits.

**Example 3.2.15.** Proposition 3.2.7 shows that an $\mathcal{S}$-category is precisely a complete Segal space as developed by Rezk [Rez01]. By a theorem of Joyal and Tierney [JT07], the $\infty$-category of complete Segal spaces is a model for $\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$. That is, the left Kan extension of the inclusion $\Delta\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$ determines an equivalence $\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{S})\simeq\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$.

**Lemma 3.3.1.** Let $F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}$ be a functor between $\infty$-categories that admit finite limits, such that $F$ preserves pullbacks, and such that for every map $f\colon c\to c'$ in $\mathcal{C}$ the square
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
F(c\times c) & \longrightarrow & F(c'\times c') \\
\downarrow & & \downarrow \\
F(c)\times F(c) & \longrightarrow & F(c')\times F(c')
\end{array}
$$
with vertical maps and horizontal maps $F(f\times f)$ and $F(f)\times F(f)$ respectively, is cartesian. Then the induced functor $\mathcal{C}_\Delta\to\mathcal{D}_\Delta$, given by postcomposition with $F$, sends $\mathcal{C}$-categories to $\mathcal{D}$-categories, and therefore restricts to a functor
$$F\colon\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Cat}(\mathcal{D}).$$
In particular, any left exact functor $F$ between finitely complete $\infty$-categories induces a functor on categories.
