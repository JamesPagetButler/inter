# Restatement

## Abstract

Let $\Perf$ denote the category of perfectoid spaces of characteristic $p$. The paper develops a six-functor formalism for the étale cohomology of diamonds, and more generally of small v-stacks on $\Perf$. Using a natural functor from analytic adic spaces over $\mathbf{Z}_p$ to diamonds that identifies étale sites, it induces a similar formalism for analytic adic spaces over $\mathbf{Z}_p$. In the noetherian setting, this recovers the formalism from Huber's book.

## Introduction

### Setting

Let $X$ be an analytic adic space over $\mathbf{Z}_p$ on which $p$ is topologically nilpotent. Under noetherian hypotheses, $X$ has an étale site $X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}$ [Hub96]. For a ring $\Lambda$ such that $n\Lambda=0$ for some $n$ prime to $p$, there is the (left-completed) derived category $D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$ of étale sheaves of $\Lambda$-modules on $X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}$. It carries $-\otimes_\Lambda -$ and $R\sHom_\Lambda(-,-)$.

For a map $f:Y\to X$, there are adjoint functors $f^{\ast}:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$ and $Rf_{\ast}:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$. Under certain conditions, there is $Rf_{!}:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$ with right adjoint $Rf^{!}:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$. If $f$ is smooth, Poincaré duality identifies $Rf^{!}$ as a twist of $f^{\ast}$. Under suitable hypotheses (for example, for adic spaces corresponding to rigid spaces over a fixed complete nonarchimedean base field), these results appear in [Hub96].

### Diamonds

**Definition 1.1.** A map $f:Y=\Spa(S,S^{+})\to X=\Spa(R,R^{+})$ of affinoid perfectoid spaces is *affinoid pro-étale* if it can be written as a cofiltered limit of étale maps $Y_{i}=\Spa(S_{i},S_{i}^{+})\to X$ of affinoid perfectoid spaces. More generally, a map $f:Y\to X$ of perfectoid spaces is *pro-étale* if it is locally on the source and target affinoid pro-étale.

Let $\Perf$ denote the category of perfectoid spaces of characteristic $p$.

**Topologies on $\Perf$.** Four topologies are considered, each refining the previous.

1. *Analytic topology:* a cover $\{f_i:X_i\to X\}$ consists of open immersions $X_i\hookrightarrow X$ that jointly cover $X$.
2. *Étale topology:* a cover consists of étale maps $X_i\to X$ that jointly cover $X$.
3. *Pro-étale topology:* a cover consists of pro-étale maps $X_i\to X$ such that for every quasicompact open $U\subset X$, there are finitely many indices $i$ and quasicompact opens $U_i\subset X_i$ whose images jointly cover $U$.
4. *v-topology:* a cover consists of any maps $X_i\to X$ such that for every quasicompact open $U\subset X$, there are finitely many indices $i$ and quasicompact opens $U_i\subset X_i$ whose images jointly cover $U$.

The last condition in cases (3) and (4) is the same as the condition in the fpqc topology for schemes. It is automatic in cases (1) and (2), since étale maps are open.

**Theorem 1.2.** The v-topology on $\Perf$ is subcanonical. For any affinoid perfectoid space $X=\Spa(R,R^{+})$,
$$H^{0}_{v}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=R,\qquad H^{0}_{v}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+})=R^{+},$$
for $i>0$, $H^{i}_{v}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0$, and $H^{i}_{v}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+})$ is almost zero.

**Definition 1.3.** Let $Y$ be a pro-étale sheaf on $\Perf$. Then $Y$ is a *diamond* if $Y$ can be written as a quotient $Y=X/R$ of a perfectoid space $X$ by a pro-étale equivalence relation $R\subset X\times X$.

It is stated that all diamonds are v-sheaves.

For a diamond $Y$, the underlying topological space is $|Y|=|X|/|R|$ when $Y=X/R$ as above. There is a bijection between open subspaces of $|Y|$ and open subfunctors of $Y$. If $Y$ is quasicompact as a v-sheaf, then $|Y|$ is quasicompact. The converse is not true in general.

**Definition 1.4.** A diamond $Y$ is *spatial* if it is qcqs, and $|Y|$ admits a basis for its topology given by sets $|U|$, where $U\subset Y$ ranges over quasicompact open subdiamonds. More generally, $Y$ is *locally spatial* if it admits an open cover by spatial diamonds.

Any perfectoid space $X$ defines a locally spatial diamond, which is spatial precisely when $X$ is qcqs. If $X$ is a (locally) spatial diamond, then $|X|$ is a (locally) spectral topological space. Moreover, $X$ is quasicompact (resp. quasiseparated) as a v-sheaf precisely when $|X|$ is quasicompact (resp. quasiseparated) as a topological space.

For a locally spatial diamond $X$, $X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}$ is defined as consisting of (locally separated) étale maps $Y\to X$ from diamonds $Y$, which are automatically locally spatial.

**Theorem 1.5.** There is a natural functor from analytic adic spaces over $\mathbf{Z}_p$ to locally spatial diamonds, denoted $X\mapsto X^{\diamondsuit}$, satisfying
$$X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\cong X^{\diamondsuit}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}.$$
The functor is given as follows. Choose a pro-étale surjection $\widetilde{X}\to X$ from a perfectoid space $\widetilde{X}$, and let $R\subset\widetilde{X}\times\widetilde{X}$ be the induced equivalence relation. Then $R$ is pro-étale over $\widetilde{X}$, and thus perfectoid itself. One has
$$X^{\diamondsuit}=\widetilde{X}^{\flat}/R^{\flat}.$$

### Derived categories on locally spatial diamonds

**Theorem 1.6.** Let $X$ be a locally spatial diamond. Then pullback induces a fully faithful functor
$$\widehat{D}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\Lambda)\to D(X_{v},\Lambda),$$
where $\widehat{D}$ denotes the left-completion of the derived category, and $D(X_v,\Lambda)$ is already left-complete. Moreover, let $A\in D(X_v,\Lambda)$ and let $f:Y\to X$ be a map of locally spatial diamonds that is surjective as a map of v-sheaves. If $f^{\ast}A\in\widehat{D}(Y_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\Lambda)$, then $A\in\widehat{D}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\Lambda)$.

### Small v-stacks

**Definition 1.7.** Let $X$ be a small v-stack, and consider the site $X_v$ of all perfectoid spaces over $X$, with the v-topology. Define the full subcategory $D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\subset D(X_v,\Lambda)$ to consist of all $A\in D(X_v,\Lambda)$ such that for every (equivalently, for one surjective) map $f:Y\to X$ from a locally spatial diamond $Y$, one has $f^{\ast}A\in\widehat{D}(Y_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\Lambda)$.

The following operations are defined on these categories.

1. *Tensor product.* There is a derived tensor product
$$-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}-:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\times D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda),$$
compatible with the inclusions into $D(X_v,\Lambda)$ and with the usual derived tensor product on $X_v$.

2. *Internal Hom.* There is
$$R\sHom_{\Lambda}(-,-):D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)^{\mathrm{op}}\times D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda),$$
characterized by
$$R\Hom_{D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)}(A,R\sHom_\Lambda(B,C))=R\Hom_{D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)}(A\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_\Lambda B,C)$$
for all $A,B,C\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$. In particular, for $A=\Lambda$,
$$R\Gamma(X,R\sHom_\Lambda(B,C))=R\Hom_{D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)}(B,C).$$
In general, the formation of $R\sHom_\Lambda$ does not commute with the inclusion $D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\subset D(X_v,\Lambda)$.

3. *Pullback.* For any map $f:Y\to X$ of small v-stacks, there is $f^{\ast}:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$, compatible with the inclusions into $D(X_v,\Lambda)$ and $D(Y_v,\Lambda)$ and with the pullback $D(X_v,\Lambda)\to D(Y_v,\Lambda)$.

4. *Pushforward.* For any map $f:Y\to X$ of small v-stacks, there is $Rf_{\ast}:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$, right adjoint to $f^{\ast}$. In general, $Rf_{\ast}$ does not commute with the inclusions into $D(X_v,\Lambda)$ and $D(Y_v,\Lambda)$. This does hold if $f$ is qcqs and one starts with an object of $D^{+}$.

5. *Pushforward with proper supports.* For any map $f:Y\to X$ of small v-stacks that is compactifiable (cf. Definition 22.2), representable in locally spatial diamonds (cf. Definition 13.3), and with (locally) $\dimtrg f<\infty$ (cf. Definition 21.7), there is $Rf_{!}:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$.

6. *Exceptional pullback.* For any map $f:Y\to X$ of small v-stacks that is compactifiable, representable in locally spatial diamonds, and with (locally) $\dimtrg f<\infty$, there is $Rf^{!}:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$, right adjoint to $Rf_{!}$.

Operations 1–4 are defined in Section 17, operation 5 in Section 22 (in particular Definition 22.18), and operation 6 in Section 23.

**Theorem 1.8.** Let $f:Y\to X$ be a map of small v-stacks.

1. For all $A,B\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$,
$$f^{\ast}A\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_\Lambda f^{\ast}B\cong f^{\ast}(A\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_\Lambda B).$$
2. For all $A\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$ and $B\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$,
$$Rf_{\ast}R\sHom_\Lambda(f^{\ast}A,B)\cong R\sHom_\Lambda(A,Rf_{\ast}B).$$
3. Assume $f$ is compactifiable and representable in locally spatial diamonds with locally $\dimtrg f<\infty$. For all $A\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$ and $B\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$,
$$Rf_{!}(A\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_\Lambda f^{\ast}B)\cong Rf_{!}A\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_\Lambda B.$$
4. Assume $f$ is compactifiable and representable in locally spatial diamonds with locally $\dimtrg f<\infty$. For all $A\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$ and $B\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$,
$$R\sHom_\Lambda(Rf_{!}A,B)\cong Rf_{\ast}R\sHom_\Lambda(A,Rf^{!}B).$$
5. Assume $f$ is compactifiable and representable in locally spatial diamonds with locally $\dimtrg f<\infty$. For all $A,B\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$,
$$Rf^{!}R\sHom_\Lambda(A,B)\cong R\sHom_\Lambda(f^{\ast}A,Rf^{!}B).$$

Parts (1) and (2) are proved in Section 17. Part (3) is Proposition 22.23. Parts (4) and (5) are formal consequences of (3) and adjunctions, cf. Proposition 23.3.

**Theorem 1.9.** Let
$$\begin{array}{ccc} Y' & \xrightarrow{\ \widetilde{g}\ } & Y\\ {\scriptstyle f'}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle f}\\ X' & \xrightarrow{\ g\ } & X \end{array}$$
be a cartesian diagram of small v-stacks.

1. Assume $f$ is qcqs and $A\in D^{+}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$. Then $g^{\ast}Rf_{\ast}A\cong Rf'_{\ast}\widetilde{g}^{\ast}A$.
2. Assume $f$ is compactifiable and representable in locally spatial diamonds with locally $\dimtrg f<\infty$, and $A\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$. Then $g^{\ast}Rf_{!}A\cong Rf'_{!}\widetilde{g}^{\ast}A$.
3. Assume $g$ is compactifiable and representable in locally spatial diamonds with locally $\dimtrg f<\infty$, and $A\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda)$. Then $Rg^{!}Rf_{\ast}A\cong Rf'_{\ast}R\widetilde{g}^{!}A$.

Part (1) is Proposition 17.6, part (2) is Proposition 22.19, and part (3) is Proposition 23.16.

### Smooth morphisms

Fix a prime $\ell\neq p$, and assume $\Lambda$ is killed by a power of $\ell$. Definition 23.8 defines $\ell$-cohomologically smooth morphisms for any prime $\ell\neq p$ (the notion depends on $\ell$); this class includes étale maps.

**Theorem 1.10.** Let $f:Y\to X$ be a map of small v-stacks that is separated and representable in locally spatial diamonds with locally $\dimtrg f<\infty$. The property of $f$ being $\ell$-cohomologically smooth is v-local on the target and $\ell$-cohomologically smooth local on the source. Composites and base changes of $\ell$-cohomologically smooth maps are $\ell$-cohomologically smooth. Smooth maps of analytic adic spaces over $\mathbf{Z}_p$ give rise to $\ell$-cohomologically smooth morphisms under $X\mapsto X^{\diamondsuit}$.

Assume $f$ is $\ell$-cohomologically smooth and $\Lambda$ is $\ell$-power torsion.

1. There is a natural equivalence of functors
$$Rf^{!}A\cong f^{\ast}A\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_\Lambda Rf^{!}\Lambda:D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\Lambda),$$
where $Rf^{!}\Lambda$ is v-locally isomorphic to $\Lambda[n]$ for some $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.
2. For $A,B\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$, there is a natural equivalence
$$f^{\ast}R\sHom_\Lambda(A,B)\cong R\sHom_\Lambda(f^{\ast}A,f^{\ast}B).$$
3. Let
$$\begin{array}{ccc} Y' & \xrightarrow{\ \widetilde{g}\ } & Y\\ {\scriptstyle f'}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle f}\\ X' & \xrightarrow{\ g\ } & X \end{array}$$
be a cartesian diagram of small v-stacks, with $f$ $\ell$-cohomologically smooth as before. Then for all $A\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X',\Lambda)$,
$$f^{\ast}Rg_{\ast}A\cong R\widetilde{g}_{\ast}f'^{\ast}A.$$
Moreover, for all $A\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)$,
$$\widetilde{g}^{\ast}Rf^{!}A\cong Rf'^{!}g^{\ast}A \quad\text{and}\quad f'^{\ast}Rg^{!}A\cong R\widetilde{g}^{!}f^{\ast}A.$$

Sections 23 and 24 contain the proofs of these results.

**Theorem 1.11.** Let $f:Y\to X$ be a quasicompact $\ell$-cohomologically smooth map (in particular, compactifiable, representable in locally spatial diamonds, and with $\dimtrg f<\infty$) of small v-stacks. For $\Lambda=\mathbb{F}_\ell$, let $D_{\mathrm{cons}}(X,\mathbb{F}_\ell)\subset D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ be the full subcategory of constructible objects, defined as follows. Containment in $D_{\mathrm{cons}}$ can be checked v-locally. On affinoid perfectoid spaces, $D_{\mathrm{cons}}$ consists of bounded complexes that become locally constant and finite-dimensional in each degree over a constructible stratification. Here "constructible" refers to the standard notion for spectral spaces. For all $A\in D_{\mathrm{cons}}(Y,\mathbb{F}_\ell)$,
$$Rf_{!}A\in D_{\mathrm{cons}}(X,\mathbb{F}_\ell).$$
Section 23 proves this theorem. Constructibility can be checked on cohomology sheaves.

**Theorem 1.12.** Let $C$ be a complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean field of characteristic $p$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_C$. Let $X$ be a locally spatial diamond that is separated and $\ell$-cohomologically smooth over $\Spa(C,\mathcal{O}_C)$ for some $\ell\neq p$. Let $A\in D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ be a bounded complex with constructible cohomology. Then the double (naive) duality map
$$A\to R\sHom_{\mathbb{F}_\ell}\big(R\sHom_{\mathbb{F}_\ell}(A,\mathbb{F}_\ell),\mathbb{F}_\ell\big)$$
is an equivalence. Equivalently, since $Rf^{!}\mathbb{F}_\ell$ is invertible, the double Verdier duality map
$$A\to R\sHom_{\mathbb{F}_\ell}\big(R\sHom_{\mathbb{F}_\ell}(A,Rf^{!}\mathbb{F}_\ell),Rf^{!}\mathbb{F}_\ell\big)$$
is an equivalence. Moreover, if $X$ is quasicompact, then $H^{i}(X,A)$ is finite for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$. This is Theorem 25.1.

### Change of algebraically closed base field

**Theorem 1.13.** Let $X$ be a small v-stack.

1. Assume $X$ lives over $k$, where $k$ is a discrete algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and $k'/k$ is an extension of discrete algebraically closed fields. Let $X'=X\times_k k'$. Then the pullback functor $D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X',\Lambda)$ is fully faithful.
2. Assume $X$ lives over $k$, where $k$ is an algebraically closed discrete field of characteristic $p$. Let $C/k$ be an algebraically closed complete nonarchimedean field, and $X'=X\times_k\Spa(C,C^{+})$ for some open and bounded valuation subring $C^{+}\subset C$ containing $k$. Then the pullback functor $D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X',\Lambda)$ is fully faithful.
3. Assume $X$ lives over $\Spa(C,C^{+})$, where $C$ is an algebraically closed complete nonarchimedean field with an open and bounded valuation subring $C^{+}\subset C$. Let $C'/C$ be an extension of algebraically closed complete nonarchimedean fields, and $C'^{+}\subset C'$ an open and bounded valuation subring containing $C^{+}$, such that $\Spa(C',C'^{+})\to\Spa(C,C^{+})$ is surjective. Then for $X'=X\times_{\Spa(C,C^{+})}\Spa(C',C'^{+})$, the pullback functor $D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\Lambda)\to D_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X',\Lambda)$ is fully faithful.

This is Theorem 19.5.

### Totally disconnected spaces

**Definition 1.14.** Let $X$ be a perfectoid space. Then $X$ is *totally disconnected* (resp. *strictly totally disconnected*) if $X$ is quasicompact and every open cover of $X$ splits (resp. and every étale cover of $X$ splits).

**Proposition 1.15.** Let $X$ be a perfectoid space. Then $X$ is totally disconnected (resp. strictly totally disconnected) if and only if $X$ is affinoid, and every connected component of $X$ is of the form $\Spa(K,K^{+})$, where $K$ is a perfectoid field with an open and bounded valuation subring $K^{+}\subset K$ (resp. and $K$ is algebraically closed).

**Lemma 1.16.** Let $X$ be any quasicompact perfectoid space. Then there is a pro-étale cover $\widetilde{X}\to X$ such that $\widetilde{X}$ is strictly totally disconnected.

**Lemma 1.17.** Let $X=\Spa(R,R^{+})$ be a totally disconnected perfectoid space, and let $f:Y=\Spa(S,S^{+})\to X$ be any map of perfectoid spaces. Then $R^{+}/\varpi\to S^{+}/\varpi$ is flat, for any pseudouniformizer $\varpi\in R$.

**Lemma 1.18.** Let $X$ be a strictly totally disconnected perfectoid space. Then a quasicompact separated map $f:Y\to X$ of perfectoid spaces is pro-étale if and only if, for every rank-$1$ point $x=\Spa(C(x),\mathcal{O}_{C(x)})\in X$, the fibre of $f$ over $x$ is isomorphic to $\underline{S_x}\times\Spa(C(x),\mathcal{O}_{C(x)})$ for some profinite set $S_x$. In this case, $f$ is affinoid pro-étale. (Separatedness of morphisms of perfectoid spaces is Definition 5.10.)

### v-descent

**Theorem 1.19.** The following prestacks are v-stacks.

1. The prestack of affinoid perfectoid spaces over the category of totally disconnected perfectoid spaces.
2. The prestack of separated pro-étale perfectoid spaces over the category of strictly totally disconnected perfectoid spaces.
3. The prestack of separated étale maps over the category of all perfectoid spaces.

**Definition 1.20.** A locally separated map $f:Y\to X$ of v-stacks is *étale* (resp. *quasi-pro-étale*) if, for every perfectoid space (resp. every strictly totally disconnected perfectoid space) $Z$ mapping to $X$, the fibre product $Y\times_X Z$ is representable by a perfectoid space, and étale (resp. pro-étale) over $Z$.

**Proposition 1.21.** A v-sheaf $Y$ is a diamond if and only if there is a surjective quasi-pro-étale map $X\to Y$ from a perfectoid space $X$.
