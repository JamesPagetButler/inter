# Restatement

## Abstract (mathematical content only)

- The paper analyses the $SO(4)$ Landau models on $S^3$ in the $SU(2)$ monopole background and their matrix geometry.
- Schwinger and Dirac gauges for the $SU(2)$ monopole give a coordinate representation of $SO(4)$ operators and wavefunctions. The gauge fixing yields algebraic relations of the operators and $SO(4)$ covariance of the eigenfunctions.
- With the spin connection of $S^3$, an $SO(4)$-invariant Weyl–Landau operator is constructed. Its eigenvalue problem is solved with explicit eigenstates, which include the known free Weyl operator eigenstates in the free-field limit.
- Eigenvalue problems are also treated for the massive Dirac–Landau and supersymmetric Landau models.
- By the level projection method, the matrix elements of the $S^3$ coordinates are identified as the fuzzy three-sphere.
- For the non-relativistic model, the fuzzy three-sphere geometry emerges in each Landau level, and only in the degenerate lowest-energy sub-bands.
- The Dirac–Landau operator accommodates two fuzzy three-spheres in each Landau level, and the mass term induces an interaction between them.

The statements below are restated from the introduction, with citation markers kept:

- The fuzzy two-sphere geometry [3, 4, 5] is realized in the $SO(3)$ Landau model [6, 7], which sets up the 2D quantum Hall effect [8].
- The $SO(5)$ Landau model [9, 10] is used for the 4D quantum Hall effect [11], whose underlying geometry is the fuzzy four-sphere [12, 13, 14].
- The correspondence was extended to $S^{2k}$ [15, 16]. The $SO(2k+1)$ Landau model realizes the geometry of the fuzzy $2k$-sphere [17, 18].
- Landau models have also been constructed on $\mathbb{C}P^n$, supermanifolds, hyperboloids, and other manifolds [19–29].
- The Landau model on $S^3$, first proposed by Nair and Randjbar-Daemi [30], is called the $SO(4)$ Landau model here because it respects the $SO(4)$ global symmetry.¹
- In that model, the $SU(2)$ monopole charge quantization was assumed. The Chern number is not defined in odd dimensions, so the quantization is not guaranteed.
- The quantum Nambu three-bracket [31] leads to other problems, such as violation of the Jacobi identity [32].
- Refs. [33, 34, 35] treat the odd-dimensional bracket as a sub-bracket of the one-dimension-higher even bracket.
- The $SO(4)$ Landau model is embedded in the $SO(5)$ Landau model, and monopole charge quantization is accounted for by that on $S^4$ [36].
- A similar dimensional relation holds for arbitrary odd- and even-dimensional Landau models [37]. This relation is attributed to the dimensional ladder of anomaly, or the spectral flow of Atiyah–Patodi–Singer.
- Odd-dimensional topological insulator Landau models based on the Dirac oscillator are discussed in [39, 40, 41].

The paper's main results, restated:

- **(i)** The Schwinger and Dirac gauges for $S^3$ solve the Landau problem with explicit wavefunctions and operators. The Dirac–Landau operator is $SO(4)$-invariant, and the $SO(4)$ Landau-level eigenstates are covariant.
- **(ii)** Relativistic Landau operators on $S^3$ with spin connection are analysed. The Dirac–Landau operator of [30] was taken without spin connection.² The eigenvalues and a coordinate representation of the eigenstates are derived. The results include the known formulae for the free relativistic operator [45, 46, 47] in the free-background limit.
- **(iii)** The matrix elements of arbitrary Landau levels of the $SO(4)$ Landau models are derived. The matrix geometry is identical to the fuzzy three-sphere. The mass parameter of the Dirac–Landau model induces an interaction between the two fuzzy three-spheres in each relativistic Landau level.
