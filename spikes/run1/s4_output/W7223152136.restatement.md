# Restatement

## Abstract

We develop a general theory of analytic prismatization with $\mathbb{Q}_p$-coefficients in rigid analytic geometry over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. We prove an equivalence of perfect complexes on the perfect analytic prismatization modulo Frobenius with perfect complexes on the relative Fargues–Fontaine curve, for any smooth partially proper rigid analytic variety. We explain how this equivalence conceptualizes the decompletion techniques, passing to locally analytic vectors, which appear in (geometric) Sen theory.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1. Rational $\delta$-spaces and rational analytic prismatization

**Setting.** The category $\mathsf{GelfRing}$ of Gelfand rings is a subcategory of the category of solid $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebras, stable under colimits. It contains Banach $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebras. Every Gelfand ring $A$ admits a uniform completion $A^u$, which is a uniform Banach $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebra. Gelfand rings are assumed to be separable and quasi-finite dimensional. With the descendable topology, a basis of $\mathsf{GelfRing}$ is given by nilperfectoid rings. A Gelfand ring $A$ is nilperfectoid if $A$ modulo its $\dagger$-nilradical is perfectoid. A Gelfand stack is (to first approximation) a sheaf of anima on $\mathsf{GelfRing}$ for the descendable topology. The category $\mathsf{GelfStk}$ contains fully faithfully the category of $\dagger$-rigid spaces over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and the category of derived Berkovich spaces over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ from [ABLB+25].

For $S\in\mathsf{Perf}$ (qfd perfectoid spaces in characteristic $p$), one attaches the Berkovich spaces $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}$ and $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}^{\times}=\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}\backslash V(p)$. For $S=\mathrm{Spa}(A)$ affinoid perfectoid with pseudo-uniformizer $\varpi$, $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}=\mathrm{Spa}(W(A^{\circ}))\backslash V([\varpi])$.

**Remark 1.1 (Gelfand stacks and arc-stacks).** Any Gelfand stack gives rise to an arc-stack over $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{arc}}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. The perfectoidization functor
$$(-)^{\diamond}\colon\mathsf{GelfStk}\to\mathsf{ArcStk}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$$
is colimit-preserving and sends $\mathrm{GSpec}(A)$ to $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{arc}}(A^u)$ for $A$ Gelfand. The perfectoidization functor admits both left and right adjoints. Its right adjoint, the analytic de Rham stack functor $(-)^{\mathrm{dR}}$, is studied in [RC24] (in a slightly different setup) and [ABLB+25].

Many definitions below are extensions to nilperfectoid rings of constructions already known for perfectoid rings. The theory of analytic prismatization is set up as a theory in which both prismatic cohomology and diamonds appear. The key definitions are those of $\delta$-rings and prisms, in the following form.

**Definition 1.2 (Definition 3.3.1, Definition 4.1.1).**

1. A *rational $\delta$-space* is a triple $(S,\mathcal{X},\iota,\varphi_{\mathcal{X}})$, where $S\in\mathsf{Perf}$, $\mathcal{X}$ is a Berkovich space with an endomorphism $\varphi_{\mathcal{X}}$ (the Frobenius of the $\delta$-space), and $\iota\colon\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}^{\times}\to\mathcal{X}$ is a morphism of Berkovich spaces inducing an isomorphism of diamonds $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}^{\times,\diamond}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{X}^{\diamond}$, such that $\varphi_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\iota\cong\iota\circ\varphi$. Here $\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}^{\times}\to\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}^{\times}$ is the morphism induced by the Frobenius on $S$. One often drops $\varphi_{\mathcal{X}}$ and $\iota$ from the notation. The category of rational $\delta$-spaces is denoted $\mathsf{Spc}^{\delta}$.

2. A *morphism* $(S,\mathcal{X})\to(S',\mathcal{X}')$ of rational $\delta$-spaces consists of a morphism $\alpha\colon S\to S'$ of arc-stacks, a $\varphi$-equivariant morphism $\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}'$ of Gelfand stacks, and a commutative diagram of Gelfand stacks
$$\begin{array}{ccc}\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}^{\times}&\to&\mathcal{X}\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ \mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S'}^{\times}&\to&\mathcal{X}',\end{array}$$
where the left vertical arrow is induced by $\alpha$.

3. A *perfect rational $\delta$-space* is a rational $\delta$-space $(S,\mathcal{X})$ such that $\varphi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}$ is an automorphism.

4. Let $(S,\mathcal{X})$ be a rational $\delta$-space. A (derived) Cartier divisor $(s\colon\mathcal{L}\to\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{X}})$ on $\mathcal{X}$ is *of degree 1* if its pullback to $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}^{\times}$ along the natural map $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},S}^{\times}\to\mathcal{X}$ is of degree 1, in the sense of [FS26] (it represents the closed Cartier divisor of an untilt of $S$). A *rational analytic prism* $(S,\mathcal{X},D)$ is a rational $\delta$-space $(S,\mathcal{X})$ together with a Cartier divisor $D\subseteq\mathcal{X}$ of degree 1.

*Footnote 4 (attached to Definition 1.2):* The notation $D\subseteq\mathcal{X}$ is used for simplicity; the morphism $D\to\mathcal{X}$ is not a monomorphism in general.

**Remark 1.3.** Rational $\delta$-spaces are analogous to formal spectra of $\delta$-rings, and rational analytic prisms are analogous to prisms. The adjective "rational" refers to the existence of an integral version of the definitions, of which these are the base change to $\mathbb{Q}_p$. From now on the adjectives "rational" and "analytic" are omitted.

Let $\mathsf{AnStk}^{\delta}$ be the localization of $\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{Spc}^{\delta})$ at the $!$-equivalences (defined on underlying Berkovich spaces). Its objects are rational analytic $\delta$-stacks (or $\delta$-stacks). The realization functor
$$(-)\colon\mathsf{AnStk}^{\delta}\to\mathsf{GelfStk}$$
is colimit-preserving and sends $(S,\mathcal{X})$ to $\mathcal{X}$.

**Proposition 1.4 (Proposition 3.4.7, Proposition 3.4.12).** Let $A$ be a nilperfectoid ring. Let $T=\mathrm{GSpec}(A)$, and consider the category $\mathsf{Spc}^{\delta}_{T/-}$ of $\delta$-spaces $(S,\mathcal{X})$ with a morphism $T\to\mathcal{X}$ of Berkovich spaces. Then $\mathsf{Spc}^{\delta}_{T/-}$ admits an initial object $(T^{\flat},\mathcal{X}_{T})$, called the $\delta$-space associated to $T$, described as follows.

1. The perfectoid space $T^{\flat}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ is the tilt of $T^{u}:=\mathrm{GSpec}(A^{u})$. There is a natural map of Berkovich spaces
$$f\colon T^{u}\to\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},T^{\flat}}^{\times},$$
induced by the untilt $T^{u}$ of $T^{\flat}$. Let $f_{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{N}\times T^{u}\to\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},T^{\flat}}^{\times}$ be the unique morphism whose restriction to $\{n\}\times T^{u}$ is $\varphi^{n}\circ f$.

2. The thickening $\mathcal{X}_{T}$ of $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},T^{\flat}}^{\times}$ is the pushout in Berkovich spaces of $f_{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{N}\times T^{u}\to\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},T^{\flat}}^{\times}$ and $\mathbb{N}\times T^{u}\to\mathbb{N}\times T$.

The same result holds for perfect $\delta$-spaces, replacing $\mathbb{N}$ by $\mathbb{Z}$. The quotient of the perfect $\delta$-space associated to $T$ by Frobenius is denoted $\mathrm{FF}_{T}$; when $T$ is perfectoid, this is the usual Fargues–Fontaine curve of the tilt. Consequently, the realization functor sends $Y\in\mathsf{AnStk}^{\delta}$ to the sheafification, in Gelfand stacks (with respect to $!$-equivalences), of the presheaf sending $T=\mathrm{GSpec}(A)$, with $A$ nilperfectoid, to $Y(T^{\flat},\mathcal{X}_{T})$.

**Remark 1.5 (Witt vectors).** The functor $T\mapsto(T^{\flat},\mathcal{X}_{T})$ is compared with the Witt vectors functor on $p$-nilpotent rings. Let $A$ be a nilperfectoid $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebra and $T=\mathrm{GSpec}(A)$. The Berkovich space $\mathcal{X}_{T}$ can be described as follows. Put
$$W^{\prime}(A^{\leq 1}):=W(A^{\leq 1})\times_{W(A^{u,\circ})}\varprojlim_{F}W(A^{u,\circ})=W(A^{\leq 1})\times_{W(A^{u,\circ})}W(A^{u,\circ,\flat}).$$
Then $\mathcal{X}_{T}$ is the Gelfand stack sending a Gelfand ring $B$ to the anima of maps $W^{\prime}(A^{\leq 1})\to B^{\leq 1}$ whose composition to any perfectoid $B$-algebra $C$ factors through $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},A^{u}}^{\times}$.

*Footnote 5 (attached to Remark 1.5):* For each light profinite set $S$, $W(A^{\leq 1})(S)=W(A^{\leq 1}(S))$ are the Witt vectors of the animated ring $A^{\leq 1}(S)$, in the sense of [BL22c, Appendix A].

This description follows from the explicit description of $\mathcal{X}_{T}$ as a pushout, together with a distinguished triangle
$$W(\mathrm{Nil}^{\dagger}(A))\to W(A^{\leq 1})\to W(A^{u,\leq 1})=W(A^{u,\circ})$$
(since $A$ is nilperfectoid), and the isomorphism $W(\mathrm{Nil}^{\dagger}(A))\cong\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{Nil}^{\dagger}(A)$, which holds because the non-unital ring $\mathrm{Nil}^{\dagger}(A)$ is a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebra. With $\mathbb{Q}_p$-coefficients, the difference between "analytic prismatization" and "perfect analytic prismatization" consists in changing $\mathbb{N}$ to $\mathbb{Z}$ in the description of the Witt vectors of the $\dagger$-nilradical. For the analytic prismatization with $\mathbb{Z}_p$-coefficients, the use of Witt vectors is critical.

**Definition 1.6 (Definition 4.1.1).** Let $X$ be a Gelfand stack.

1. The *rational analytic prismatic site* of $X$, denoted $X_{\mathbbl{\Delta}\text{-site}}$, is the category of tuples $(S,\mathcal{X},D,\alpha)$, where $(S,\mathcal{X},D)$ is a rational analytic prism and $\alpha\colon D\to X$ is a morphism of Gelfand stacks. Coverings are induced by $!$-covers in $(S,\mathcal{X})$.

2. Let $\mathbb{Q}_{p,\mathbbl{\Delta}}$ be the $\delta$-stack sending $(S,\mathcal{X})$ to the anima of degree 1 Cartier divisors on $(S,\mathcal{X})$.

3. Let $X_{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$ be the $\delta$-stack over $\mathbb{Q}_{p,\mathbbl{\Delta}}$ mapping $(S,\mathcal{X},D)$ to the anima of maps $D\to X$.

4. If $X$ is a Berkovich space, the *rational analytic prismatization* $X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$ of $X$ is the realization of the $\delta$-stack $X_{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$. Equivalently, it is the functor sending a nilperfectoid $\mathbb{Q}_p$-algebra $A$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}:=\mathrm{GSpec}(\mathbb{Q}_p)^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$ to the anima of maps $D\to X$, where $D\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{GSpec}(A)}$ is the degree 1 divisor classified by the map $\mathrm{GSpec}(A)\to\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$.

5. The *rational perfect analytic prismatization* $X^{\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}}$ of $X$ is
$$X^{\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}}:=\varprojlim_{\varphi}X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}.$$
One writes $\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}:=\varprojlim_{\varphi}\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$.

6. The *rational perfect analytic prismatization of $X$ modulo Frobenius*, or *$p$-adic twistor stack of $X$*, is the Gelfand stack
$$X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}:=X^{\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}}/\varphi^{\mathbb{Z}}.$$
One writes $\mathrm{Div}^{1}:=\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}/\varphi$.

**Remark 1.7.** The restriction to Berkovich spaces in the definition of prismatization ensures that the functor-of-points description on nilperfectoid rings holds as written, without sheafification. More generally, $X$ could be a $\dagger$-cohesive Gelfand stack in the sense of Definition 2.7.7. For any Berkovich space $X$, there is a natural morphism
$$X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_m,$$
given by pullback of Cartier divisors along $\{0\}\times T\to\mathbb{N}\times T\to\mathcal{X}_{T}$, for $T=\mathrm{GSpec}(A)$ with $A$ nilperfectoid.

**Definition 1.8.** Let $X$ be a Berkovich space. The *analytic Hodge–Tate stack* of $X$ is the Gelfand stack
$$X^{\mathrm{HT}}:=X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_m}B\mathbb{G}_m.$$

**Remark 1.9.** For any prism $(S,\mathcal{X},D)$, there are natural maps of Gelfand stacks
$$\mathcal{X}\to D^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathbbl{\Delta}},$$
where $D$ maps to $D^{\mathrm{HT}}\to\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{HT}}$, and the first map corresponds to the identity of $D$ (cf. Remark 4.1.6). For example, there is a morphism $\mathcal{X}_{\rm cyc}\to\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$ (factoring through $\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$) from the cyclotomic prism, given by
$$(S,\mathcal{X}_{\rm cyc}):=\Bigl(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{arc}}\bigl(\mathbb{F}_p((\pi^{1/p^\infty}))\bigr),\ \varprojlim_{q\mapsto q^{p}}(1+\mathbb{D}^{<1})\backslash\{1\}\Bigr).$$
The divisor corresponds to $[p]_q=(q^p-1)/q-1$ (as printed in the source; the parentheses may have been lost in extraction), where $q$ is the coordinate on the open unit disc $\mathbb{D}^{<1}$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and Frobenius is given by $q\mapsto q^p$. This is the analogue in the analytic theory of the $q$-de Rham prism.

**Remark 1.10 (Relative versions).** Let $(S,\mathcal{X},D)$ be a prism and $X$ a Berkovich space over $D$. Define
$$X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}/\mathcal{X}}=X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\times_{D^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\mathcal{X},$$
using the map $\mathcal{X}\to D^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$ from Remark 1.9. (The left-hand side depends on $(S,\mathcal{X},D)$, not only on $\mathcal{X}$; the notation is an abuse, as is common in the $p$-complete theory.)

**Remark 1.11 (Perfectoid case).** The prismatization of a perfectoid space $X$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is not simply given by $\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},X^{\flat}}^{\times}$. However, the Hodge–Tate stack $X^{\mathrm{HT}}$ of a perfectoid space $X$ is isomorphic to $X$: see Proposition 4.2.4. Sections 4.8 and 4.10 make explicit the (perfect) prismatizations of $\mathrm{GSpec}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, the rigid analytic multiplicative group $\mathbb{G}_m$, and the analytic affine line.

For $\mathrm{GSpec}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, the morphism $\mathcal{X}_{\rm cyc}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$ from Remark 1.9 induces isomorphisms
$$\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}\cong\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{cyc}}/\mathbb{Z}_p^{\times,\mathrm{la}},\qquad \mathrm{Div}^{1}\cong\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q}_p^{\times,\mathrm{la}},$$
with the action of $a\in\mathbb{Z}_p^{\times,\mathrm{la}}$ given by
$$a.(q,q^{1/p},\dots):=(q^{a},q^{a/p},\dots).$$
Here, for a $p$-adic Lie group $G$, $G^{\rm cont}$, $G^{\mathrm{la}}$ and $G^{\mathrm{sm}}$ denote the groups in Berkovich spaces given by the sheaves of continuous, locally analytic, and locally constant functions on $G$ valued in $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

*Footnote 6 (attached to Remark 1.11):* This is the notation for $G^{\rm cont}$, $G^{\mathrm{la}}$, $G^{\mathrm{sm}}$ used in the text.

In particular, there is an isomorphism
$$\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{HT}}\cong\mathrm{GSpec}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})\bigr)/\mathbb{Z}_p^{\times,\mathrm{la}},$$
where the action of $\mathbb{Z}_p^{\times,\mathrm{la}}$ on $\mathrm{GSpec}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty}))$ factors through the natural Galois action of $\mathbb{Z}_p^{\times,\mathrm{sm}}\cong\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q}_p)^{\mathrm{sm}}$, provided by the cyclotomic character.

*Footnote 7 (attached to Remark 1.11):* This isomorphism is related to [Col94], [Ram12] and [AHLB22].

For $\mathbb{G}_m$, and then for the analytic affine line, the explicit presentations involve the prismatic logarithm, which takes a simpler form than in [BL22c] and [Dri24b] because only the rational theory is considered.

### 1.2. The $p$-adic twistor stack and (geometric) Sen theory

For a Berkovich space $X$, the analytic de Rham stack of $X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$ is $(\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},X^{\diamond}}^{\times})^{\mathrm{dR}}$. There is a radius map
$$\kappa\colon X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to(0,\infty),$$
intertwining Frobenius with multiplication by $p$. Here $(0,\infty)$ is seen as a Gelfand stack over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ through the Betti stack functor. The following observations hold.

- Over $(1,\infty)$ (via $\kappa$), the morphism $X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to(\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{FF},X^{\diamond}}^{\times})^{\mathrm{dR}}$ is an isomorphism. Frobenius induces an isomorphism $X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{(1,\infty)}\cong X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{(p,\infty)}$, and the quotient by Frobenius on this locus is the Hyodo–Kato stack of $X$.
- Over $(0,p)$, the morphism $X^{\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}}\to X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}$ is an isomorphism. Frobenius induces an isomorphism $X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{(0,1)}\cong X^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{(0,p)}$, and the quotient by Frobenius on this locus is isomorphic on $X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$ (as printed).

For a Berkovich space $X$, there is a diagram of Gelfand stacks
$$\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}\xrightarrow{\alpha_X}X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}\xrightarrow{\beta_X}X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1},\mathrm{dR}}=X^{\mathrm{HK}},$$
whose composition is the natural map $\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}\to X^{\mathrm{HK}}=(\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}})^{\mathrm{dR}}$. Thus $X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$ sits between the relative Fargues–Fontaine curve over $X^{\diamond}$ and its analytic de Rham stack. The left and right terms depend only on the perfectoidization $X^{\diamond}$ of $X$, whereas $X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$ itself does not.

**Proposition 1.12 (Proposition 4.4.3).** Let $X$ be a Berkovich space.

1. The morphism $\beta_X\colon X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}\to\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}^{\mathrm{dR}}=X^{\mathrm{HK}}$ is an isomorphism after base change to
$$\mathrm{Div}^{1}\setminus\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{HT}}\cong\mathrm{Div}^{1,\mathrm{dR}}\setminus\mathrm{GSpec}(\mathbb{Q}_p).$$

2. There exists a natural morphism of Gelfand stacks
$$\pi_X\colon X^{\mathrm{HT}}\to X\times\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{HT}}.$$
If $X$ is a smooth partially proper rigid space over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (or more generally a formally smooth Gelfand stack over $\mathbb{Q}_p$), then $\pi_X$ realizes $X^{\mathrm{HT}}$ as a gerbe over $X\times\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{HT}}$, banded by $\mathbb{T}^{\dagger}_{X/\mathbb{Q}_p}\{1\}$. Here $\{1\}$ refers to the Breuil–Kisin twist on $\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{HT}}$ (cf. Definition 4.2.7 and Construction 4.9.1).

**Remark 1.13 (Relation to the Faltings extension).** The Hodge–Tate stack, and the overconvergent neighborhood of $X^{\mathrm{HT}}$ inside $X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$, are controlled by the Faltings extension of $X$. This admits a relative variant over a perfectoid base, when $X$ lifts to Fontaine's ring $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}$. See Section 4.6.

**Remark 1.14 (Archimedean/non-archimedean analogy).** In [Sch24], an archimedean version of this story is developed. There, a notion of nilperfectoid $\mathbb{C}$-algebra is defined, and the perfectoidization functor takes the underlying topological space (or condensed anima). For any nilperfectoid $\mathbb{C}$-algebra $A$, a twistor projective line relative to $A$ can be defined. For a complex analytic space $X$, [Sch24] defines a stack $X^{\rm tw}$ on nilperfectoid $\mathbb{C}$-algebras, called the twistor stack of $X$, which lives over the twistor projective line $\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^1_{\mathbb{R}}$.

The relative analytic de Rham stack of $X^{\rm tw}$ over $\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^1_{\mathbb{R}}$ is the base change of the Betti stack $X_{\rm Betti}$ of $X$ to $\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^1_{\mathbb{R}}$. The morphism from $X^{\rm tw}$ to its relative de Rham stack is an isomorphism away from the point $\infty\in\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^1_{\mathbb{R}}$. The fiber of $X^{\rm tw}$ at $\infty$ is (a version of) the Hodge stack of $X$.

This is parallel to the $p$-adic story: $X^{\rm tw}$, resp. $X_{\rm Betti}\times_{\mathrm{AnSpec}(\mathbb{Z})}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^1_{\mathbb{R}}$, plays the role of the relative $p$-adic twistor stack $X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}/\mathrm{FF}_C}$ of a partially proper rigid space $X$ over $\mathrm{GSpec}(C)$, where $C$ is a complete algebraically closed non-archimedean extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, resp. of the relative Hyodo–Kato stack $X^{\mathrm{HK}/\mathrm{FF}_C}$.

One difference: the Hodge stack is a split gerbe for the overconvergent tangent bundle of $X$, while the relative Hodge–Tate stack is a gerbe for the twisted overconvergent tangent bundle of $X$, which (by a relative variant of Proposition 1.12) is in general non-split.

**Theorem 1.15 (Section 6).** Let $X$ be a partially proper smooth rigid space over $\mathrm{GSpec}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. The morphism
$$\alpha_X\colon\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}\to X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$$
is prim with trivial codualizing complex. The pullback $\alpha_X^{*}$ on quasi-coherent sheaves is fully faithful, and $\alpha_X^{*}$ induces an equivalence on perfect complexes
$$\alpha_X^{*}\colon\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erf}(X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}})\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erf}(\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}).$$
A similar result holds for the preimages in $X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$ and $\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}$ of any open arc-substack $U$ of $\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}^{\diamond}$, as well as for the Hodge–Tate stack $X^{\mathrm{HT}}$ and $\widehat{X^{\diamond}}$. Moreover, a variant of this result holds for the relative $p$-adic twistor stack of a smooth partially proper rigid space over a perfectoid base.

**Remark 1.16.** Theorem 1.15 is, at least morally, a non-abelian $p$-adic Hodge theory statement.

- Over the Hodge–Tate locus, the equivalence restricts, for vector bundles, to an equivalence between twisted Higgs bundles (vector bundles on the analytic Hodge–Tate stack, which is a non-split gerbe) and v-vector bundles. This is a weak form of the $p$-adic Simpson correspondence.$^{8}$
- Over the whole $p$-adic twistor stack, still for vector bundles, the theorem describes relative $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-modules on $X$ (vector bundles on $\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}$) as vector bundles on $X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$. Away from the Hodge–Tate locus, the latter are analytic $D$-modules on $\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}\setminus X^{\diamond}$, and these are glued to twisted Higgs bundles seen on the Hodge–Tate locus.
- Hence relative $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-modules (e.g. pro-étale $\mathbb{Q}_p$-local systems on $X$) and their cohomology can be described in terms of $p$-adic twistors on $X$; cf. also Remark 1.14.

*Footnote 8 (attached to Remark 1.16):* It is only a weak form, since an important aspect of the $p$-adic Simpson correspondence is providing conditions under which, after additional choices, one can undo the twist.

**Remark 1.17.** In restriction to the Hodge–Tate locus and in the geometric case, Theorem 1.15 explains why v-vector bundles come equipped with a canonical Higgs field ([RC26b]). Indeed, they come from the relative Hodge–Tate stack, which is a gerbe for the overconvergent Breuil–Kisin twisted tangent bundle.

**Remark 1.18.** For $X=\mathrm{GSpec}(K)$, with $K$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, Theorem 1.15 encodes a decompletion result for $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-modules over the Robba ring. Section 6.1 gives a discussion, including a reinterpretation of familiar functors of Fontaine for $p$-adic Galois representations in terms of the geometry of $\mathrm{GSpec}(K)^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}$.

Theorem 1.15 is a local statement on $X$. Étale locally, a partially proper smooth rigid space $X$ looks like $\mathbb{G}_m^{d}$, where $d$ is the dimension of $X$, with coordinate $\underline{T}=(T_1,\dots,T_d)$. Let $\widetilde{X}=\varprojlim_{\underline{T}\mapsto\underline{T}^{p}}X$ denote the perfected $d$-dimensional multiplicative group (in Gelfand stacks). For $X=\mathbb{G}_m^{d}$, the perfect prismatization is
$$X^{\widetilde{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}}\cong\bigl(\widetilde{X}\times_{\mathrm{GSpec}(\mathbb{Q}_p)}\mathcal{X}_{\rm cyc}\bigr)/\Gamma_d^{\mathrm{la}},\qquad \Gamma_d:=\mathbb{Z}_p^{\times,\mathrm{la}}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_p^{d,\mathrm{la}}.$$
After passing to the quotient by Frobenius, this is equivalently
$$X^{\mathrm{Div}^{1}}=\mathrm{FF}^{\mathrm{la}}_{\widetilde{X}^{d,\diamond}_{\mathbb{Q}_p^{\rm cyc}}}\big/\Gamma_d^{\mathrm{la}},\tag{1.2.1}$$
where $\mathrm{FF}^{\mathrm{la}}_{\widetilde{X}^{d,\diamond}_{\mathbb{Q}_p^{\rm cyc}}}$ denotes the Berkovich space obtained by gluing the spectra of the rings of $\Gamma_d$-locally analytic vectors in the rings of functions on $\Gamma_d$-stable affinoid pieces of $\mathrm{FF}_{\widetilde{X}^{d,\diamond}_{\mathbb{Q}_p^{\rm cyc}}}$. This is compared with the formula
$$\mathrm{FF}_{X^{\diamond}}=\mathrm{FF}_{\widetilde{X}^{d,\diamond}_{\mathbb{Q}_p^{\rm cyc}}}/\Gamma_d^{\rm cont}.$$

The approach to Theorem 1.15 is therefore to study the more general problem of comparing quasi-coherent sheaves and perfect complexes on $Y/G^{\rm cont}$ and on $Y^{\mathrm{la}}/G^{\mathrm{la}}$, where $G$ is a $p$-adic Lie group acting on a Berkovich space $Y$. This splits into two questions:

1. decompletions by (derived) locally analytic vectors;
2. vanishing of higher locally analytic vectors.

The usual axioms of Sen theory enter the discussion of (2); the vanishing holds in the examples of interest.

**Theorem 1.19 (Theorem 5.1.1).** Let $Y$ be a Berkovich space endowed with a continuous action of a $p$-adic Lie group $G$, that is, with an action of the Gelfand group stack $G^{\rm cont}$. Let
$$h\colon Y/G^{\rm cont}\to|Y|_{\mathrm{Betti}}/G^{\mathrm{la}}$$
be the natural induced map of Gelfand stacks. Here the action of $G^{\mathrm{la}}$ on $|Y|_{\mathrm{Betti}}$ arises from the morphism of groups $G^{\mathrm{la}}\to G^{\mathrm{sm}}$ and the natural continuous action of $G^{\mathrm{sm}}$ on $|Y|_{\mathrm{Betti}}$ induced by the action on topological spaces. The following hold.

1. The map $h$ is prim with trivial codualizing complex. Hence there is a fully faithful pullback functor
$$\mathrm{Mod}_{h_*\mathscr{O}_{Y/G^{\rm cont}}}\mathrm{D}\bigl(|Y|_{\mathrm{Betti}}/G^{\mathrm{la}}\bigr)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{D}(Y/G^{\rm cont}).$$
One denotes $\mathscr{O}_Y^{\mathrm{la}}:=h_*\mathscr{O}_{Y/G^{\rm cont}}$ and calls it the *sheaf of locally analytic functions on $Y$ with respect to $G$*.

2. Suppose $Y=\mathrm{GSpec}(R)$ is affinoid, and assume moreover that $R$ is classically decompleted, in the sense of Definition 5.4.2. Let $R^{\mathrm{la}}\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{D}(BG^{\mathrm{la}}))$ denote the Gelfand algebra of locally analytic vectors of $R$. Then the natural fully faithful functor
$$\mathrm{Mod}_{R^{\mathrm{la}}}\bigl(\mathrm{D}(BG^{\mathrm{la}})\bigr)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathscr{O}_Y^{\mathrm{la}}}\mathrm{D}\bigl(|Y|_{\mathrm{Betti}}/G^{\mathrm{la}}\bigr)$$
is an isomorphism.

3. Assume that $Y$ is locally strongly decompleted, in the sense of Definition 5.6.8. Then the pullback functor
$$h^{*}\colon\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathscr{O}_Y^{\mathrm{la}}}\bigl(\mathrm{D}(|Y|_{\mathrm{Betti}}/G^{\mathrm{la}})\bigr)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{D}(Y/G^{\rm cont})$$
induces an equivalence of dualizable objects. Hence there is an equivalence of stable categories
