# Restatement

## Abstract

We develop a general technique for gluing subcategories of $\infty$-categories. We obtain categorical equivalences between simplicial sets associated to certain multisimplicial sets. One of our results generalizes Deligne's gluing theory developed in the construction of the extraordinary pushforward operation in étale cohomology of schemes.

## Introduction

### Extraordinary pushforward and Deligne's gluing

The extraordinary pushforward, one of Grothendieck's six operations, in étale cohomology of schemes was constructed in SGA 4 XVII [6].

Let $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'$ be the category of quasi-compact and quasi-separated schemes, with morphisms being separated of finite type, and let $\Lambda$ be a fixed torsion ring. For a morphism $f\colon Y\to X$ in $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'$, the extraordinary pushforward by $f$ is a functor
$$f_!\colon \mathrm{D}(Y,\Lambda)\to \mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda),$$
between unbounded derived categories of $\Lambda$-modules in the étale topoi.

The functoriality of this operation is encoded by a pseudofunctor
$$F\colon \mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'\to \mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_1,$$
sending a scheme $X$ to $\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)$ and a morphism $f\colon Y\to X$ to the functor $f_!$. Here $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_1$ denotes the $(2,1)$-category of categories[^1].

The restrictions $F_P$ and $F_J$ of $F$ to the subcategories $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'_P$ and $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'_J$ of $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'$, spanned respectively by proper morphisms and by open immersions, are the obvious candidates. The construction of $F$ amounts to gluing these two pseudofunctors. Deligne developed a general theory for gluing two pseudofunctors with target $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_1$ [6] (Section 3). Deligne's gluing theory, together with its variants ([1] Section 1.3, [23]), has found several other applications ([1], [3], [24]).

### Higher categorical enhancement

In the language of $\infty$-categories [20], an enhancement of $F$ is a functor
$$F^\infty\colon \mathrm{N}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}')\to \mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_\infty,$$
where $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}')$ is the nerve of $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'$ and $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_\infty$ is the $\infty$-category of $\infty$-categories. For every scheme $X$ in $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'$, $F^\infty(X)$ is an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}(X,\Lambda)$ whose homotopy category is equivalent to $\mathrm{D}(X,\Lambda)$. For every morphism $f\colon Y\to X$ in $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'$, $F^\infty(f)$ is a functor $f^\infty_!\colon \mathcal{D}(Y,\Lambda)\to \mathcal{D}(X,\Lambda)$ such that the induced functor $\mathrm{h}f^\infty_!$ between homotopy categories is equivalent to the classical $f_!$.

### Coherence

By Nagata compactification [4], every morphism $f$ in $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'$ can be factorized as $p\circ j$, where $j$ is an open immersion and $p$ is proper. One can then define $F(f)$ as $F_P(p)\circ F_J(j)$. Such a factorization is not canonical, so coherence with composition must be included as part of the data. Since the target of $F$ is a $(2,1)$-category, Deligne's theory requires coherence only up to the level of $2$-cells. Since the target of $F^\infty$ is an $\infty$-category, coherence of all levels is needed. Moreover, more than two subcategories must be handled; this is already apparent in [24] and is illustrated in the proof of Corollary 0.4 below.

### General framework

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an (ordinary) category and let $k\geq 2$ be an integer. Let $\mathcal{E}_1,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k\subseteq \mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})$ be $k$ sets of arrows of $\mathcal{C}$, each containing every identity morphism.

Define a simplicial set $\delta^*_k\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}$ as follows. Its $n$-simplices are functors $[n]^k\to\mathcal{C}$ such that:

- the image of a morphism in the $i$-th direction lies in $\mathcal{E}_i$ for $1\leq i\leq k$, and
- the image of every square in direction $(i,j)$ is a Cartesian square (also called pullback square) for $1\leq i<j\leq k$.

For example, when $k=2$, the $n$-simplices of $\delta^*_2\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2}$ correspond to $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ diagrams
$$\begin{array}{ccccc}
c_{00} & \to & c_{01} & \to & \cdots \to c_{0n}\\
\downarrow & & \downarrow & & \downarrow\\
c_{10} & \to & c_{11} & \to & \cdots \to c_{1n}\\
\vdots & & \vdots & & \vdots\\
c_{n0} & \to & c_{n1} & \to & \cdots \to c_{nn}
\end{array}$$
where vertical (resp. horizontal) arrows are in $\mathcal{E}_1$ (resp. $\mathcal{E}_2$) and all squares are Cartesian. The face and degeneracy maps are defined in the obvious way.

The simplicial set $\delta^*_k\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}$ is seldom an $\infty$-category. It is the simplicial set associated to a $k$-simplicial set $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}$. The latter is a special case of the restricted multisimplicial nerve of an ($\infty$-)category with extra data (Definition 3.14).

Let $\mathcal{E}_0\subseteq \mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})$ be a set of arrows stable under composition and containing $\mathcal{E}_1$ and $\mathcal{E}_2$. Then there is a natural map of simplicial sets
$$g\colon \delta^*_k\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2,\mathcal{E}_3,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}\to \delta^*_{k-1}\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_0,\mathcal{E}_3,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}, \tag{0.9}$$
sending an $n$-simplex of the source, corresponding to a functor $[n]^k\to\mathcal{C}$, to its partial diagonal
$$[n]^{k-1}=[n]\times[n]^{k-2}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{diag}\times \mathrm{id}_{[n]^{k-2}}}[n]^k=[n]^2\times[n]^{k-2}\to\mathcal{C},$$
which is an $n$-simplex of the target.

**Definition 3.18 (as stated in the Introduction).** A subset $\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})$ is *admissible* if:

- $\mathcal{E}$ contains every identity morphism;
- $\mathcal{E}$ is stable under pullback;
- for every pair of composable morphisms $p\in\mathcal{E}$ and $q$ in $\mathcal{C}$, $p\circ q$ is in $\mathcal{E}$ if and only if $q\in\mathcal{E}$.

### Main results

**Theorem 0.1 (Special case of Theorem 5.4).** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category admitting pullbacks and let $\mathcal{E}_0,\mathcal{E}_1,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k\subseteq\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})$, $k\geq 2$, be sets of morphisms containing every identity morphism and satisfying the following conditions:

1. $\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2\subseteq\mathcal{E}_0$; $\mathcal{E}_0$ is stable under composition; and $\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2$ are admissible.
2. For every morphism $f$ in $\mathcal{E}_0$, there exist $p\in\mathcal{E}_1$ and $q\in\mathcal{E}_2$ such that $f=p\circ q$.
3. For every $3\leq i\leq k$, $\mathcal{E}_i$ is stable under pullback by $\mathcal{E}_1$.

Then the natural map (0.9)
$$g\colon \delta^*_k\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2,\mathcal{E}_3,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}\to \delta^*_{k-1}\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_0,\mathcal{E}_3,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}$$
is a categorical equivalence (Definition 1.7).

Taking $k=2$ and $\mathcal{E}_0=\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})$ in Theorem 0.1 gives the following.

**Corollary 0.2.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category admitting pullbacks. Let $\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2\subseteq\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})$ be admissible subsets. Assume that for every morphism $f$ of $\mathcal{C}$, there exist $p\in\mathcal{E}_1$ and $q\in\mathcal{E}_2$ such that $f=p\circ q$. Then the natural map
$$g\colon \delta^*_2\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2}\to \mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})$$
is a categorical equivalence.

In the situation of Corollary 0.2, for every $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}$, the functor
$$\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{D})\to \mathrm{Fun}(\delta^*_2\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2},\mathcal{D})$$
is an equivalence of $\infty$-categories.

Such equivalences can be used to construct functors in many contexts. For instance, $\mathcal{D}$ may be taken to be $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_1)$[^2], $\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}_\infty$, or the $\infty$-category of differential graded categories.

In the above discussion, $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})$ may be replaced by an $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$ (not necessarily the nerve of an ordinary category), and the simplicial set $\delta^*_k\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}$ is defined analogously.

For every subset $L\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}$, Section 3 defines a variant $\delta^*_{k,L}\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}$ of $\delta^*_k\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\dots,\mathcal{E}_k}$ obtained by taking the opposite in the directions in $L$. For $L\subseteq\{3,\dots,k\}$, the theorem remains valid modulo slight modifications; see Theorem 5.4 for a precise statement.

There is a canonical categorical equivalence from the simplicial set $\delta^*_{2,\{2\}}\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2}$ to the $\infty$-category of correspondences introduced in [9] (Example 4.30).

### Applications to schemes

**Corollary 0.3.** Let $P\subseteq\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}')$ be the subset of proper morphisms and let $J\subseteq\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}')$ be the subset of open immersions. Then the natural map
$$\delta_2^*\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}')^{\mathrm{cart}}_{P,J}\to \mathrm{N}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}')$$
is a categorical equivalence.

*Proof.* This follows immediately from Corollary 0.2 applied to $\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'$, $\mathcal{E}_1=P$, $\mathcal{E}_2=J$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 0.4.** Let $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}''$ be the category of disjoint unions of quasi-compact and quasi-separated schemes, with morphisms being separated and locally of finite type. Let $F=\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'')$ be the set of morphisms of $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}''$. Let $P\subseteq F$ be the subset of proper morphisms, and let $I\subseteq F$ be the subset of local isomorphisms [11] (Définition 4.4.2). Then the natural map
$$\delta_2^*\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'')^{\mathrm{cart}}_{P,I}\to \mathrm{N}(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'')$$
is a categorical equivalence.

Corollary 0.4 still holds if $I$ is replaced by the subset $E\subseteq F$ of étale morphisms.

Corollary 0.2 cannot be applied directly with $\mathcal{E}_1=P$ and $\mathcal{E}_2=I$, because its assumption fails. For example, one may take $f$ to be the structural morphism of the disjoint union of varieties of unbounded dimensions over a field.

*Proof of Corollary 0.4.* Put $\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}''$. Introduce the auxiliary sets $F_{\mathrm{ft}}\subseteq F$, the set of separated morphisms of finite type, and $I_{\mathrm{ft}}=I\cap F_{\mathrm{ft}}$. Consider the commutative diagram
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\delta_3^*\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{P,I_{\mathrm{ft}},I} & \to & \delta_2^*\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{F_{\mathrm{ft}},I}\\
\downarrow & & \downarrow\\
\delta_2^*\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{P,I} & \to & \mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C}),
\end{array}$$
where the upper arrow is induced by composing morphisms in $P$ and $I_{\mathrm{ft}}$, and the left arrow is induced by composing morphisms in $I_{\mathrm{ft}}$ and $I$. Apply Theorem 0.1 to all arrows except the lower one; this shows they are categorical equivalences. It then follows that the lower arrow is also a categorical equivalence.

- *Upper arrow.* Apply Theorem 0.1 with $k=3$, $\mathcal{E}_0=F_{\mathrm{ft}}$, $\mathcal{E}_1=P$, $\mathcal{E}_2=I_{\mathrm{ft}}$, $\mathcal{E}_3=I$. Conditions (1) and (3) are obviously satisfied. For Condition (2), every morphism $f$ in $F_{\mathrm{ft}}$ can be written as a disjoint union $\coprod f_i$ of morphisms $f_i$ of $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}'$. It then suffices to apply Nagata compactification to each $f_i$.
- *Left arrow.* Apply Theorem 0.1 with $k=3$, $\mathcal{E}_0=\mathcal{E}_1=I$, $\mathcal{E}_2=I_{\mathrm{ft}}$, $\mathcal{E}_3=P$. All conditions are obviously satisfied.
- *Right arrow.* Apply Corollary 0.2 with $k=2$, $\mathcal{E}_0=F$, $\mathcal{E}_1=I$, $\mathcal{E}_2=F_{\mathrm{ft}}$. To verify its assumption, let $f$ be a morphism of $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{ch}''$. Then $f$ has the form $\coprod_{i,j}X_{ij}\to\coprod_i Y_i$, induced by morphisms $X_{ij}\to Y_i$, where $X_{ij}$ and $Y_i$ are quasi-compact and quasi-separated schemes. Then $f$ is the composition
$$\coprod_{i,j}X_{ij}\xrightarrow{q}\coprod_{i,j}Y_i\xrightarrow{p}\coprod_i Y_i,$$
with $p\in I$ and $q\in F_{\mathrm{ft}}$. $\blacksquare$

### Proof strategy for Corollary 0.2

The map $g$ factors as
$$\delta_2^*\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{cart}}_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2}\xrightarrow{g'}\delta_2^*\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2}\xrightarrow{g''}\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C}),$$
where $\delta_2^*\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})_{\mathcal{E}_1,\mathcal{E}_2}$ is the simplicial set whose $n$-simplices are diagrams as above without the requirement that every square is Cartesian, $g'$ is the natural inclusion, and $g''$ is the map remembering the diagonal. Both $g'$ and $g''$ are categorical equivalences. The fact that $g''$ is a categorical equivalence is an $\infty$-categorical generalization of Deligne's result [6] (Proposition 3.3.2).

### Organization

Section 1 collects basic definitions and facts on $\infty$-categories [20]. Section 2 develops a general technique for constructing functors to $\infty$-categories. Section 3 introduces notions related to multisimplicial sets, including the restricted multisimplicial nerve. Section 4 proves a multisimplicial descent theorem, which implies that $g''$ is a categorical equivalence. Section 5 proves a Cartesian gluing theorem, which implies that $g'$ is a categorical equivalence. A Cartesian gluing formalism for pseudofunctors between $2$-categories was developed in [23]. Section 6 proves facts about inclusions of simplicial sets.

### Conventions

Unless otherwise specified, a category is an ordinary category. Sets and categories in which the only morphisms are identities are not distinguished. For categories $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$, $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})$ denotes the category of functors from $\mathcal{C}$ to $\mathcal{D}$, whose morphisms are natural transformations. The symbol $\mathrm{id}$ denotes various identity morphisms.

## Section 1: Preliminaries on $\infty$-categories

**Definition 1.1 (Simplicial set and $\infty$-category).** Let $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}$ denote the category of sets[^3].

- The category of simplicial sets is $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}_\Delta=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{op},\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et})$. For a simplicial set $S$, $S_n=S([n])$ is its set of $n$-simplices.
- For $n\geq 0$, $\Delta^n=\mathrm{Fun}(-,[n])$ is the simplicial set represented by $[n]$. Let $\partial\Delta^n\subseteq\Delta^n$ be the simplicial subset obtained by removing the interior, namely the $n$-simplex $\mathrm{id}_{[n]}\colon[n]\to[n]$. In particular $\partial\Delta^0=\emptyset$. For $0\leq k\leq n$, the $k$-th horn $\Lambda^n_k\subseteq\partial\Delta^n$ is obtained by removing the face opposite to the $k$-th vertex, namely the $(n-1)$-simplex $d^n_k\colon[n-1]\to[n]$.
- An $\infty$-category (resp. Kan complex) is a simplicial set $\mathcal{C}$ such that $\mathcal{C}\to\Delta^0$ has the right lifting property with respect to all inclusions $\Lambda^n_k\subseteq\Delta^n$ with $0<k<n$ (resp. $0\leq k\leq n$). Equivalently, every map $\Lambda^n_k\to\mathcal{C}$ with $0<k<n$ (resp. $0\leq k\leq n$) extends to $\Delta^n\to\mathcal{C}$.

**Example 1.2 (Nerve of an ordinary category).** For an ordinary category $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})_n=\mathrm{Fun}([n],\mathcal{C})$. $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})$ is an $\infty$-category, and $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})_0$ and $\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{C})_1$ are identified with $\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$ and $\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})$.

**Definition 1.3 (Object, morphism, equivalence).** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category. Vertices are called objects and edges are called morphisms. A morphism is an equivalence if it defines an isomorphism in the homotopy category $\mathrm{h}\mathcal{C}$.

**Definition 1.4 (Functor, natural transformation, natural equivalence).** Objects of $\mathrm{Fun}(S,\mathcal{C})$ are functors $S\to\mathcal{C}$, morphisms are natural transformations, and equivalences in $\mathrm{Fun}(S,\mathcal{C})$ are natural equivalences.

**Remark 1.5.** Let $f,g\colon S\to\mathcal{C}$ be functors and $\phi\colon f\to g$ a natural transformation. Then $\phi$ is a natural equivalence if and only if, for every vertex $s$ of $S$, the morphism $\phi(s)\colon f(s)\to g(s)$ is an equivalence in $\mathcal{C}$. See [20] Proposition 3.1.2.1 for a generalization (see [20] Remark 2.4.1.4).

**Remark 1.6.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $\infty$-category and $f,g\colon x\to y$ morphisms of $\mathcal{C}$. Then $f$ and $g$ are homotopic (having the same image in $\mathrm{h}\mathcal{C}$) if and only if they are equivalent as objects of the $\infty$-category defined by the fiber of $\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^1,\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Fun}(\partial\Delta^1,\mathcal{C})$. Indeed, the latter condition means that there exist a morphism $h\colon x\to y$ and two $2$-simplices of $\mathcal{C}$ with boundaries $(\mathrm{id}_x, f, h)$ and $(\mathrm{id}_y, h, g)$ respectively, as in the diagram with edges $\mathrm{id}_x$, $f$, $h$, $\mathrm{id}_y$, $g$. By definition (resp. [20] Remark 1.2.3.6), the existence of the $2$-simplex in the upper right (resp. lower left) corner means that $f$ (resp. $g$) and $h$ are homotopic. This proves the "if" part. For the "only if" part, take $h=g$ and take the $2$-simplex in the lower left corner to be degenerate.

**Definition 1.7 (Categorical equivalence).** A map $f\colon T\to S$ of simplicial sets is a categorical equivalence if, for every $\infty$-category $\mathcal{C}$, the induced functor $\mathrm{h}\mathrm{Fun}(S,\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{h}\mathrm{Fun}(T,\mathcal{C})$ is an equivalence of ordinary categories.

**Remark 1.8.** Let $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$ be $\infty$-categories. A functor $f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}$ is a categorical equivalence if and only if there exist a functor $g\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}$ and natural equivalences between $f\circ g$ and $\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}$, and between $g\circ f$ and $\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}$. The "only if" direction follows from [20] Proposition 1.2.7.3; the other direction is clear.

**Lemma 1.9.** A map of simplicial sets $f\colon Y\to Z$ is a categorical equivalence if and only if the following conditions hold for every $\infty$-category $\mathcal{D}$:

1. For $l=0,1$ and every commutative diagram
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
Y & \xrightarrow{v} & \mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^l,\mathcal{D})\\
{\scriptstyle f}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle p}\\
Z & \xrightarrow{w} & \mathrm{Fun}(\partial\Delta^l,\mathcal{D})
\end{array}$$
where $p$ is induced by the inclusion $\partial\Delta^l\subseteq\Delta^l$, there exists a map $u\colon Z\to\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^l,\mathcal{D})$ with $p\circ u=w$ such that $u\circ f$ and $v$ are homotopic over $\mathrm{Fun}(\partial\Delta^l,\mathcal{D})$.
2. For $l=2$ and every commutative diagram as above, there exists a map $u\colon Z\to\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^2,\mathcal{D})$ with $p\circ u=w$.

**Definition 1.10 (Marked simplicial set).** A marked simplicial set is a pair $(X,\mathcal{E})$, where $X$ is a simplicial set and $\mathcal{E}\subseteq X_1$ is a subset containing all degenerate edges. A morphism $f\colon(X,\mathcal{E})\to(X',\mathcal{E}')$ is a map $f\colon X\to X'$ of simplicial sets with $f(\mathcal{E})\subseteq\mathcal{E}'$. The category of marked simplicial sets is denoted $\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}_\Delta^+$.

## Section 2: Constructing functors

**Notation 2.1.** Let $\Phi\colon\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{R}$ be a morphism of $(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}_\Delta)^{\mathcal{I}}$. Let $\Gamma_\Phi(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\Gamma(\mathcal{R})$ denote the simplicial subset which is the union of the images of $\Gamma(\Psi)\colon\Gamma(\mathcal{M})\to\Gamma(\mathcal{R})$ over all factorizations $\mathcal{N}\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\Psi}\mathcal{R}$ of $\Phi$ such that $i$ is anodyne.

**Remark 2.2.** As the referee pointed out, $\Gamma_\Phi(\mathcal{R})$ can be computed using one single factorization $\mathcal{N}\xrightarrow{i'}\mathcal{M}'\xrightarrow{\Psi'}\mathcal{R}$ of $\Phi$, where $i'$ is anodyne and $\Psi'$ is an injective fibration. For every factorization $\mathcal{N}\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\Psi}\mathcal{R}$ of $\Phi$ with $i$ anodyne, there exists a dotted arrow $\mathcal{M}'\to\mathcal{M}$ making the evident diagram commute. Thus $\Gamma_\Phi(\mathcal{R})$ is simply the image of $\Gamma(\Psi')$. Since $\Gamma(\Psi')$ is a Kan fibration, $\Gamma_\Phi(\mathcal{R})$ is a union of connected components of $\Gamma(\mathcal{R})$. Indeed, the inclusion $\Gamma_\Phi(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\Gamma(\mathcal{R})$ has the right lifting property with respect to $\Delta^{\{j\}}\subseteq\Delta^n$ for all $0\leq j\leq n$.

**Remark 2.3.** Given a commutative diagram in $(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}_\Delta)^{\mathcal{I}}$
$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathcal{N} & \xrightarrow{G} & \mathcal{R}\\
{\scriptstyle\Phi}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle F}\\
\mathcal{N}' & \xrightarrow{\Phi'\text{-target}} & \mathcal{R}'
\end{array}$$
(with $\Phi'\colon\mathcal{N}'\to\mathcal{R}'$), the map $\Gamma(F)\colon\Gamma(\mathcal{R})\to\Gamma(\mathcal{R}')$ carries $\Gamma_\Phi(\mathcal{R})$ into $\Gamma_{\Phi'}(\mathcal{R}')$. Indeed, for every factorization $\mathcal{N}\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\Psi}\mathcal{R}$ of $\Phi$ with $i$ anodyne, there is a commutative diagram in which $i'$ is the pushout of $i$ by $G$, hence anodyne, and $\Psi'$ is the induced map $\mathcal{M}'\to\mathcal{R}'$ (with $\mathcal{M}'$ the pushout). 

For a functor $g\colon\mathcal{I}'\to\mathcal{I}$, composition with $g$ induces $g^*\colon(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}_\Delta)^{\mathcal{I}}\to(\mathcal{S}\mathrm{et}_\Delta)^{\mathcal{I}'}$. The pullback map $g^*\colon\Gamma(\mathcal{R})\to\Gamma(g^*\mathcal{R})$ carries $\Gamma_\Phi(\mathcal{R})$ into $\Gamma_{g^*\Phi}(g^*\mathcal{R})$. Indeed, for every factorization $\mathcal{N}\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\Psi}\mathcal{R}$ of $\Phi$ with $i$ anodyne, $g^*\mathcal{N}\xrightarrow{g^*i}g^*\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{g^*\Psi}g^*\mathcal{R}$ is a factorization of $g^*\Phi$ with $g^*i$ anodyne, and the induced square of $\Gamma$-maps commutes.
