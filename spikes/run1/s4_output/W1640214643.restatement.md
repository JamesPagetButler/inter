# Restatement

## Abstract

Let $(M,g)$ be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension $>2$. There is a metric $h$ conformal to $g$ and of volume $1$ such that the first positive eigenvalue of the conformal Laplacian with respect to $h$ is arbitrarily large. A similar statement is proven for the first positive eigenvalue of the Dirac operator on a spin manifold of dimension $>1$.

## Introduction

**Theorem 1.1.** Let $(M,g_0,\chi)$ be a compact Riemannian spin manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$. For any metric $g$ in the conformal class $[g_0]$, denote the first positive eigenvalue of the Dirac operator on $(M,g,\chi)$ by $\lambda_1^+(D_g)$. Then
$$\sup_{g\in[g_0]}\lambda_1^+(D_g)\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)^{1/n}=\infty.$$

**Theorem 1.2.** Let $(M,g_0,\chi)$ be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension $n\geq 3$. For any metric $g$ in the conformal class $[g_0]$, denote the first positive eigenvalue of the conformal Laplacian
$$L_g:=\Delta_g+\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\mathrm{Scal}_g$$
(also called the Yamabe operator) on $(M,g,\chi)$ by $\lambda_1^+(L_g)$. Then
$$\sup_{g\in[g_0]}\lambda_1^+(L_g)\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)^{2/n}=\infty.$$

The Dirac operator and the conformal Laplacian belong to a family of operators defined in subsection 2.3. These are called conformally covariant elliptic operators of order $k$ and of bidegree $\left(\frac{n-k}{2},\frac{n+k}{2}\right)$, acting on manifolds $(M,g)$ of dimension $n>k$. The definition includes formal self-adjointness.

**Theorem 1.3.** Let $P_g$ be a conformally covariant elliptic operator of order $k$, of bidegree $\left(\frac{n-k}{2},\frac{n+k}{2}\right)$, acting on manifolds of dimension $n>k$. Assume that $P_g$ is invertible on $\doba{S}^{n-1}\times\doba{R}$ (see Definition 2.4). Let $(M,g_0)$ be a compact Riemannian manifold. If $P_g$ depends on the spin structure, assume that $M$ is oriented and equipped with a spin structure. For any metric $g$ in the conformal class $[g_0]$, denote the first positive eigenvalue of $P_g$ by $\lambda_1^+(P_g)$. Then
$$\sup_{g\in[g_0]}\lambda_1^+(P_g)\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)^{k/n}=\infty.$$

**Remarks from the introduction (mathematical content only).**

- The infimum $\inf_{g\in[g_0]}\lambda_1^+(D_g)\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)^{1/n}$ is strictly positive [3]. Under a condition preventing the blow-up of spheres, it is attained [4], [2]. See also [7], [6].
- For the Hodge Laplacian $\Delta_p^g$ acting on $p$-forms, $\sup_{g\in[g_0]}\lambda_1(\Delta_p^g)\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)^{2/n}=+\infty$ for $n\geq 4$ and $2\leq p\leq n-2$ [17].
- For the standard Laplacian $\Delta^g$ acting on functions, $\sup_{g\in[g_0]}\lambda_k(\Delta^g)\,\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)^{2/n}<+\infty$. The case $k=1$ is proven in [18] and the general case in [26]. See [24] for a synthetic presentation.
- The proof constructs metrics with longer and longer cylindrical parts. This is called an *asymptotically cylindrical blowup*. Such metrics are also called *Pinocchio metrics* in [1, 5]. The Dirac eigenvalues on such metrics were studied partially in [1, 5].

## Section 2

**Lemma 2.1 (Removal of singularities lemma).** Let $\Omega$ be a bounded open subset of $\doba{R}^{n}$ containing $0$. Let $P$ be an elliptic differential operator of order $k$ on $\Omega$, let $f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)$, and let $u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})$ be a solution of
$$Pu=f \quad \text{on } \Omega\setminus\{0\}$$
with
$$\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_0(2\varepsilon)-B_0(\varepsilon)}|u|\,r^{-k}=0 \quad\text{and}\quad \lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_0(\varepsilon)}|u|=0, \tag{2}$$
where $r$ is the distance to $0$. Then $u$ is a (strong) solution of $Pu=f$ on $\Omega$. The same result holds for sections of vector bundles over relatively compact open subsets of Riemannian manifolds.

**Definition 2.2.** A conformally covariant elliptic operator of order $k$ and of bidegree $\left(\frac{n-k}{2},\frac{n+k}{2}\right)$ is a contravariant functor from $\mathrm{Riem}^n$ (resp. $\mathrm{Riemspin}^n$) to $\mathrm{Laplace}^n_k$ (resp. $\mathrm{Dirac}^n_k$). It maps $(M,g)$ to $(M,g,V_g,P_g)$ in such a way that the coefficients are continuous in the $C^k$-topology of metrics (see Definition 2.3). The notation $P_g$ or $P$ is used for this functor.

**Definition 2.3.** The coefficients of $P$ are said to be continuous in the $C^k$-topology of metrics if the following holds. For any metric $g$ on a manifold $M$ and any compact subset $K\subset M$, there is a neighborhood $\mathcal{U}$ of $g|_K$ in the $C^k$-topology of metrics on $K$ such that for all metrics $\tilde g$ with $\tilde g|_K\in\mathcal{U}$, there is an isomorphism of vector bundles $\hat\kappa:V_g|_K\to V_{\tilde g}|_K$ over the identity of $K$, with induced map $\hat\kappa_*:\Gamma(V_g|_K)\to\Gamma(V_{\tilde g}|_K)$, such that the coefficients of the differential operator
$$P_g-(\hat\kappa_*)^{-1}P_{\tilde g}\hat\kappa_*$$
depend continuously on $\tilde g$ (with respect to the $C^k$-topology of metrics).

**Definition 2.4.** $P$ is said to be invertible on $\doba{S}^{n-1}\times\doba{R}$ if $P_g$ is an invertible operator $H^k\to L^2$, where $g$ is the standard product metric on $\doba{S}^{n-1}\times\doba{R}$. Equivalently, there is a constant $\sigma>0$ such that the spectrum of $P_g:\Gamma_{H^k}(V_g)\to\Gamma_{L^2}(V_g)$ is contained in $(-\infty,-\sigma]\cup[\sigma,\infty)$ for any $g\in U$. The largest such $\sigma$ is called $\sigma_P$.

## Section 3

**Lemma 3.1.** The manifold $(M_\infty,g_\infty)$ is an exact $b$-metric in the sense of [28, Def. 2.8].

**Proposition 3.2.** Let $P$ be a conformally covariant elliptic operator. Then on $(M_\infty,g_\infty)$,
$$\|(\nabla^\infty)^s u\|_{L^2(g_\infty)}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^2(g_\infty)}+\|P_\infty u\|_{L^2(g_\infty)}\right)$$
for all $s\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}$.

**Proposition 3.3.** The map
$$\ker P_0\to\ker P_\infty,\qquad \varphi_0\mapsto\varphi_\infty=F_\infty^{-\frac{n-k}{2}}\varphi_0$$
is an isomorphism of vector spaces.

## Section 4

**Theorem 4.1.** Let $P$ be a conformally covariant elliptic operator of order $k$, of bidegree $\left(\frac{n-k}{2},\frac{n+k}{2}\right)$, on manifolds of dimension $n>k$. Assume that $P$ is invertible on $\doba{S}^{n-1}\times\doba{R}$. If
$$\liminf_{L\to\infty}|\lambda_j^\pm(P_L)|<\sigma_P,$$
then
$$\lambda_j^\pm(P_L)\to\lambda_j^\pm(P_\infty)\in(-\sigma_P,\sigma_P)\qquad\text{for }L\to\infty.$$

**Lemma 4.2.** For any $\delta>0$ and any $\ell\in\{0,\ldots,m\}$, the sequence
$$\left(\|\psi_{i,\ell}\|_{C^{k+1}(M\setminus B_y(\delta),g_\infty)}\right)_i$$
is bounded.

**Lemma 4.3.** For any $\varepsilon>0$ there exist $\delta_0$ and $i_0$ such that
$$\|\psi_{i,\ell}\|_{L^2(B_y(\delta_0),g_\infty)}\leq\varepsilon\,\|\psi_{i,\ell}\|_{L^2(M,g_\infty)}$$
for all $i\geq i_0$ and all $\ell\in\{0,\ldots,m\}$. In particular,
$$\|\psi_{i,\ell}\|_{L^2(M\setminus B_y(\delta_0),g_\infty)}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\,\|\psi_{i,\ell}\|_{L^2(M,g_\infty)}.$$
