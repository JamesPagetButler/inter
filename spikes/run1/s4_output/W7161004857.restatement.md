# Restatement

## Abstract

The Weil group of a number field is a refinement of its absolute Galois group arising from class field theory. The passage from Galois to Weil is important in several places in number theory. The author argues that, from the Galois perspective, a number field is a $K(\pi,1)$, but from the Weil perspective it is not. The Weil group is therefore refined further by constructing an object, the Weil-Moore anima, which has the Weil group as its fundamental group but has nontrivial higher homotopy groups. The cohomological properties of Weil-Moore anima are said to be in several ways nicer than those of the Weil or Galois groups.

## 1. Introduction

**Weil groups.** The Weil group $W_{\mathbb{Q}}$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ is a locally compact Hausdorff group with a surjective homomorphism $W_{\mathbb{Q}}\twoheadrightarrow G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ whose kernel is connected. For a number field $K$, the Weil group $W_K$ is obtained by pulling back along the inclusion $G_K\to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. This construction is due to Weil [67].

**Artin reciprocity.** The Artin map $\mathbb{A}_K^\times/K^\times\twoheadrightarrow G_K^{ab}$ lifts to an isomorphism
$$W_K^{ab}\simeq \mathbb{A}_K^\times/K^\times$$
for every number field $K$. One-dimensional representations $G_K^{ab}\to\mathbb{C}^\times$ correspond to Hecke characters with finite image, and the corresponding Artin and Hecke L-functions are equal. Weil defined Artin-style L-functions for Weil group representations, and Hecke L-functions correspond to the one-dimensional case.

**Cohomological construction.** The Weil group is built from short exact sequences
$$1\to G_K^{ab}\to \operatorname{Gal}(K^{ab}/\mathbb{Q})\to \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\to 1,$$
in which $G_K^{ab}$ is replaced by $\mathbb{A}_K^\times/K^\times$. Such extensions are classified by classes
$$u\in H^2(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q});\mathbb{A}_K^\times/K^\times),$$
and cohomological class field theory gives canonical classes $u=u_{K/\mathbb{Q}}$. Putting these extensions into an inverse system is done using results from [3].

**Higher cohomology.** Tate's work [59] suggests that the Weil group should be refined further. The introduction states that a number field should not be a $K(\pi,1)$: besides the fundamental group (the Weil group), there should be higher homotopy groups. These homotopy groups are to be topological groups, specifically locally compact Hausdorff groups. The tool used is the notion of condensed anima, developed jointly with Peter Scholze.

**Theorem 1.2.** There exists a condensed anima $X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with the following properties:

1. The condensed set $\pi_0 X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is $\ast$.
2. The condensed group $\pi_1 X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is isomorphic to the Weil group $W_{\mathbb{Q}}$ of the rational numbers.
3. For $n>1$, the condensed abelian group $\pi_n X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is compact Hausdorff.
4. For any number field $K$, let $X_K\to X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ denote the corresponding finite étale cover. Then $H^i(X_K;\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})=0$ for $i>1$.

**Motivation for property 4.** The canonical isomorphisms $W_K^{ab}\simeq\mathbb{A}_K^\ast/K^\ast$ can be stated by Pontryagin duality as
$$H^1(BW_K;\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})=(\mathbb{A}_K^\ast/K^\ast)^\vee,$$
where $(-)^\vee$ denotes Pontryagin duality. The higher groups $H^{>1}(BW_K;\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})$ do not all vanish. The aim is to add higher homotopy groups to $BW_K$ so that its abelianization corresponds to $\mathbb{A}_K^\times/K^\times$ in a derived sense.

**Galois cohomology and duality.** The following points are stated imprecisely in the introduction:

1. The cohomological dimension of $X_K$, and of its variants $X_{K,S}$ with restricted ramification at a nonempty finite set of places $S$, is always $2$. This holds whether or not $K$ has a real place, and independently of Leopoldt's conjecture.
2. There are local variants of $X_K$, and versions of global and local Poitou-Tate duality in which archimedean and nonarchimedean places contribute uniformly. At nonarchimedean places $X_{K_\nu}=BW_{K_\nu}$. At archimedean places there is a change.
3. A simpler form of Tate's global Euler characteristic formula holds for $X_{K,S}$ with $S$ finite: the Euler characteristic is always $0$.

**Poitou-Tate duality.** Let $K$ be a number field, $p$ a prime, $S$ a finite set of places of $K$ containing all archimedean places and all places above $p$, and $M$ a finite $G_{K,S}$-module of $p$-power order. Then $R\Gamma(BG_{K,S};M)$ is finite of $p$-power order in each degree, and
$$R\Gamma(BG_{K,S};M)^\vee\simeq \widetilde{R\Gamma}_c(G_{F,S};M^\vee(1)[3]),$$
as printed in the source (with $G_{F,S}$ in place of $G_{K,S}$). Here
$$\widetilde{R\Gamma}_c(BG_{K,S};M):=\operatorname{Fib}\Big(R\Gamma(BG_{K,S};M)\to\bigoplus_{\nu\in S}\widetilde{R\Gamma}(BG_{K_\nu};M)\Big),$$
where $\widetilde{R\Gamma}(BG_{K_\nu};M)$ is the usual continuous group cohomology if $\nu$ is nonarchimedean, and Tate cohomology of the finite group $G_{K_\nu}$ if $\nu$ is archimedean.

The source notes that this resembles Poincaré duality for the interior of a compact 3-manifold with boundary, but only "almost": at complex places the Tate cohomology vanishes, and at real places one obtains $2$-torsion groups in infinitely many degrees, positive and negative. At nonarchimedean places, local Tate duality is like Poincaré duality for a closed surface.

**Brauer groups.** The invariant map $\mathrm{Br}(F_\nu)\simeq\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is an isomorphism at nonarchimedean places. At archimedean places it is an injection with image of order $\leq 2$.

**Weil groups at archimedean places.** The Weil group of $\mathbb{C}$ is $\mathbb{C}^\times$. The Weil group of $\mathbb{R}$ is an extension of $G_{\mathbb{R}}$ by $\mathbb{C}^\times$. For the Brauer group:
$$H^2(BW_{\mathbb{R}};\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}(1))\quad\text{and}\quad H^2(BW_{\mathbb{C}};\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}(1))$$
both equal the full $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$. For an arbitrary number field $K$, as shown by Deligne [15],
$$H^2(BW_K;\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}(1))=\ker\Big(\bigoplus_\nu \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\Big),$$
where the direct sum runs over all places of $K$.

However, the cohomological dimension of $W_{\mathbb{C}}$ and $W_{\mathbb{R}}$ is infinite. For example $H^i(BW_{\mathbb{C}};\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})$ is nonzero in all nonnegative even degrees, since $BW_{\mathbb{C}}$ has the homotopy type of $BS^1\sim\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^\infty$. The $2$-skeleton $S^2$ has $2$-dimensional Poincaré duality, and $G_{\mathbb{R}}$ acts on it by the antipodal map, giving $\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^2$ for $\mathbb{R}$. Chern classes of antipodal twistor structures lie in the cohomology of this $2$-skeleton, analogously to the Chern classes of vector bundles on the Fargues-Fontaine curve ([20], [61], [65]).

The fundamental group of $S^2$ is trivial, so $S^2$ alone does not recover $\mathbb{C}^\times$. Instead, $W_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}^\times$ acts on $|\mathbb{H}^\times|\sim S^3$ by multiplication, and the quotient has fundamental group $W_{\mathbb{C}}$ and discrete-coefficient cohomology equal to that of $S^2$. The same construction works for $\mathbb{R}$, using a realization of $W_{\mathbb{R}}$ inside $\mathbb{H}^\times$. The refinement used in the body of the paper differs slightly from this one.

The paper's task is to make a similar refinement for the Weil group of a number field, compatible with the archimedean story and with the usual Weil groups at nonarchimedean places.

### Tour of applications

**1. Weil-étale cohomology.** Lichtenbaum [39] considers the compactly supported Weil group cohomology of the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_F$ with $\mathbb{Z}$-coefficients, truncated to degrees $\leq 3$. This gives an object in $\operatorname{Perf}(\mathbb{Z})$, and its base change to $\operatorname{Perf}(\mathbb{R})$ has the form $\operatorname{fib}(V\xrightarrow{\Phi}V)$ for a canonical $V\in\operatorname{Perf}(\mathbb{R})$ and canonical endomorphism $\Phi$. On $K$-theory, this yields a natural element of
$$K_1(\mathbb{R})/K_1(\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{R}^\times/\mathbb{Z}^\times,$$
and [39] shows this element (up to sign) equals the leading coefficient of $\zeta_F$ at $s=0$, with order of vanishing given by the Euler characteristic of $V$. Compactly supported cohomology with $\mathbb{Z}$-coefficients is nonzero and large in high degrees [21]. The Weil group of a number field or of an archimedean local field has unwanted cohomology in degrees $>3$. The proposed object removes this, so the argument works without truncation.

**2. Kato's zeta elements.** Let $p$ be a prime, $\Lambda$ a commutative pro-$p$-finite ring, $S$ a finite set of primes with $p\in S$, and $M$ a lisse $\Lambda$-sheaf on $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[1/S])_{et}$. Kato [33] conjectures a canonical isomorphism
$$\zeta_M:\det_\Lambda\big(R\Gamma_c(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[1/S])_{et};M)\big)\simeq\Lambda,$$
where
$$R\Gamma_c(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[1/S])_{et};M):=\operatorname{Fib}\Big(R\Gamma(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[1/S])_{et};M)\to\bigoplus_{\nu\in S\cup\{\infty\}}R\Gamma(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Q}_\nu)_{et};M)\Big).$$
Only usual étale (Galois) cohomology is used. This complex lies in $\operatorname{Perf}(\Lambda)$, and Tate's global Euler characteristic formula implies it has Euler characteristic $0$. The paper states that there is a natural isomorphism
$$R\Gamma_c(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[1/S])_{et};M)\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma_c(X_{\mathbb{Q},S};M),$$
so Kato's conjecture can be formulated in the Weil-Moore setting. Using Poitou-Tate duality for Weil-Moore anima, the conjecture can also be stated as trivializations
$$\det_\Lambda R\Gamma(X_{\mathbb{Q},S};M)\simeq\Lambda,$$
with the two formulations equivalent under $M\leftrightarrow M^\vee(1)$. Kato's original formulation requires an archimedean correction factor, and the case $p=2$ needs extra attention. The Weil-Moore setting avoids these.

**3. Langlands with mod $p$ or $p$-adic coefficients.** Here the conjectural Langlands group [37], [2] does not arise. The basic claim is that on the spectral side one should use Weil-Moore representations: finite rank local systems on $X_K$. Since $\tau_{\leq 1}X_K=BW_K$, these are Weil group representations. If the coefficient ring $R$ is discrete or $p$-adic, they are Galois representations, because $W_K/W_K^\circ=G_K$. When $R$ has nontrivial derived structure, the three notions differ.

For a group scheme $\widehat G$ over $\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$, the functor
$$R\mapsto \{X_F\to (B\widehat G)(R)\}$$
on animated commutative $\Lambda$-algebras defines a formal derived stack $\mathfrak{X}_{F,\widehat G}$, organizing deformations of Weil-Moore representations. It gives a derived structure on Galois deformation functors (Wang-Erickson stacks [66]), different from the naive one in Galatius-Venkatesh [25].

These constructions relate to the conjectures of Emerton-Gee-Hellmann [16] and Zhu [68] on spectral decompositions of compactly supported cohomology of Shimura varieties (see also [24], [1], [36], [51], [19], [16]). At the archimedean places, the proposed analogue of categorical local Langlands is Betti geometric Langlands [5] for $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{C}}$ at complex places, and a twistor-line version for $\mathrm{Tw}_{\mathbb{R}}$ at real places ([50] treats the real case with complex coefficients).

**Betti geometric Langlands for real curves.** Let $X$ be a smooth proper curve over $\mathbb{R}$ with $X(\mathbb{R})=\emptyset$. The dual data is the closed surface
$$|X|=X(\mathbb{C})/\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}),$$
which may be non-orientable and non-connected. For a reductive group $G$ over $\mathbb{R}$, the automorphic side consists of sheaves with nilpotent singular support on the real points of the moduli stack of $G$-bundles on $X$, and the spectral side consists of Ind-coherent sheaves with nilpotent singular support on the stack of $\widehat G$-local systems on $|X|$. For $X=\mathrm{Tw}_{\mathbb{R}}$, the twistor line, $|\mathrm{Tw}_{\mathbb{R}}|=\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^2$, and the notion of $\Lambda$-local system matches Weil-Moore representations with $\Lambda$-coefficients over $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{R})$.

Three technical points are noted:

- Since $|X|$ can be non-orientable, the C-group ([10]) replaces the L-group. The action of $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})=\pi_1|X|$ on $\widehat G$ gives a local system of reductive groups on $|X|$.
- There is a canonical map $\mu_2\to Z(\widehat G)$ (see [7]), so the notion can be further twisted by a $\mu_2$-gerbe, the gerbe of square roots of the Tate twist. This matches [19].
- The canonical bundle $\Omega^1$ of $\mathrm{Tw}_{\mathbb{R}}$ has no square root. For some $G$ (such as $SL_2$) this rules out a Whittaker sheaf. One resolves this by twisting either the automorphic side ([6], C.7) or the spectral side. The spectral twist uses a canonical $\mu_2$-gerbe on the stack of $\widehat G^C$-local systems, obtained by cupping the gerbe of square roots of the canonical bundle of $X$ with the local system and integrating over $|X|$. The Whittaker issue also arises for mod $p$ categorical local Langlands at nonarchimedean places, where [16] avoids it by restricting to adjoint groups.

**Global setting.** Let $K$ be a number field, $G$ a reductive group over $K$, $\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ with $n\geq 1$, and consider
$$R\Gamma_c\big(G(\mathbb{A}_K)/G(K);\Lambda\big).$$
Compactly supported cohomology produces a discrete $\Lambda$-module on a locally compact Hausdorff space, and gives an admissible representation of $G(\mathbb{A}_K)$. The paper works instead with
$$(BG)_c(K):=\operatorname{Fib}\big((BG)(K)\to(BG)(\mathbb{A}_K)\big).$$
In adelic terms,
$$(BG)_c(K)=\bigsqcup_\xi G^\xi(K)\backslash G(\mathbb{A}_K),$$
where $\xi$ runs over representatives of $\ker^1(K,G)$ and $G^\xi$ is the corresponding pure inner twist. If $G$ satisfies the Hasse principle, this is the same as the standard adelic quotient. The known (semi-)local input is $R\Gamma_c(G(\mathbb{A}_K);\Lambda)$ as a $G(\mathbb{A}_K)$-representation, and the goal is to describe $R\Gamma_c((BG)_c(K);\Lambda)$ in terms of it. The text is cut off at this point, during the statement of an adelic version of categorical local Langlands that is conjectural.

## Labelled statements

**Remark 1.1** (terminology). The terms "condensed" and "anima" were chosen by the author and Peter Scholze.

- *Condensed.* Topological structures are encoded as built from profinite sets via colimits. Instead of specifying open subsets, one specifies which families of points may "condense" to a compact Hausdorff space. Every compact Hausdorff space is a quotient of a profinite set by a profinite equivalence relation.
- *Anima.* An anima is a homotopy type, viewed as an object of its natural $\infty$-category rather than its classical homotopy category. Following Beilinson [4], the points of an anima $A$ with underlying set $\pi_0A$ can move within their connected components. A CW complex's anima is what remains after removing its point-set structure.

A condensed anima has both a topological and a homotopy-theoretic aspect, and the topology is not collapsed to homotopy theory. For example, $K(\mathbb{R},n)$, with its topological abelian group structure, exists as a condensed anima. It differs from the ordinary anima $K(\mathbb{R}^\delta,n)$ (its underlying anima) and from $\ast$ (its homotopy type).

**Theorem 1.2.** There exists a condensed anima $X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with the following properties:

1. $\pi_0X_{\mathbb{Q}}=\ast$.
2. $\pi_1X_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong W_{\mathbb{Q}}$.
3. For $n>1$, $\pi_nX_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is a compact Hausdorff condensed abelian group.
4. For any number field $K$, with $X_K\to X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ the corresponding finite étale cover, $H^i(X_K;\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})=0$ for all $i>1$.

**Remark 1.3.** For function fields over finite fields and nonarchimedean local fields, the higher cohomology vanishes by Tate's theorem [59]. Hence $X_K=BW_K$ for nonarchimedean $K$.

**Remark 1.4.** The relation between $X_K$ and $BW_K=K(W_K,1)$ is compared with that between $S^n$ and $K(\mathbb{Z},n)$. The spheres have simple cohomology ("Moore spaces") and the Eilenberg-MacLane spaces have simple homotopy. Serre [56] builds $S^n$ from Eilenberg-MacLane spaces via the Postnikov tower. The same procedure is used to build $X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ from $BW_{\mathbb{Q}}=K(W_{\mathbb{Q}},1)$, with additional technical complications from the nontrivial fundamental group and from working in the condensed setting.

**Remark 1.5.** (The statement and its surrounding claims are summarized in the Introduction section above.)

- $X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is expected to be the condensed homotopy type underlying the space of closed points of a global analogue of the Fargues-Fontaine curve, whose existence Scholze has hypothesized. Analogously, $BW_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ corresponds to the homotopy type of $\operatorname{Div}^1$, the space of closed points of the Fargues-Fontaine curve [19].
- For $\pi_2$, see Lemma 7.11.
- Cohomological dimension of $X_K$ and $X_{K,S}$ is $2$, and the Euler characteristic of $X_{K,S}$ for finite $S$ is $0$, as stated in the Introduction.
