# Restatement

## Abstract (restated)

Clausen and Scholze, in their 2022 lecture notes on condensed sets, remark that the quasiseparated condensed sets are equivalent to the category of compactological spaces defined by Waelbroeck in the 1960s. The paper studies that category, proves the claimed equivalence, and shows that condensed sets are a formal categorical completion of Waelbroeck's compactological spaces. This answers a question of Hanson (2023).

## Introduction (mathematical content)

**Compactological sets.** A compactological set is written $(X,\mathcal{C},(\tau_K)_{K\in\mathcal{C}})$, where $\mathcal{C}$ is the *compactology*. By the coherence conditions, the $\tau_K$ induce a topology $\tau$ on $X$ such that every $K\in\mathcal{C}$ is a compact subspace of $(X,\tau)$. Compactological sets generalise the compact subsets of a topological (cgwh-)space. The small sets in this sense form a bornology, as studied by Waelbroeck, Buchwalter [Wae71, Buc69b], Hogbe-Nlend [HN70, HN71, HN77] and Houzel [Hou73].

**Relation to condensed mathematics.** Compactological sets are related to the condensed mathematics of Clausen and Scholze [Sch19a].

**Results of the article.**

- Section 2 uses Buchwalter's definition (Definition 2.1). It gives a characterisation in which the whole data of a compactological set is encoded in the system $\mathcal{C}$ of plain sets (Proposition 2.11). It describes subspaces, quotients, products, coproducts, limits and colimits in the category $\mathbf{Cmp}$, and shows that $\mathbf{Cmp}$ is complete and cocomplete (Corollary 2.18).
- The category $\mathbf{CGWH}$ of compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces (McCord [McC69]) is related to $\mathbf{Cmp}$ by the following theorem.

> **Theorem** [ThmAdjCGWHCmp]. The two functors $(-)_{\operatorname{top}}$ and $\iota$ are well-defined and form an adjoint pair $\iota \dashv (-)_{\operatorname{top}}$ between $\mathbf{CGWH}$ and $\mathbf{Cmp}$. Moreover, $\iota$ is fully faithful, $(\iota(-))_{\operatorname{top}}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf{CGWH}}$, and therefore $\mathbf{CGWH}$ is a reflective subcategory of $\mathbf{Cmp}$.

  Here $\iota$ endows a cgwh-space with the collection of all its compact subsets. $(-)_{\operatorname{top}}$ takes a compactological set, equips $X$ with the final topology of the inclusions $(K,\tau_K)\hookrightarrow X$, and forgets $\mathcal{C}$ and the $\tau_K$.

  Consequently $\mathbf{CHaus}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CGWH}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Cmp}$, where compact Hausdorff spaces are regarded as compactological sets with the compactology of all compact subsets.

- Section 2 ends with the following structural results: $\mathbf{Cmp}\cong\mathbf{Ind_{emb}}(\mathbf{CHaus})$ (Propositions 2.22 and 2.24).
- Section 3 recalls the basics of condensed sets following Clausen and Scholze [Sch19a]. It works with $\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa$, the compact Hausdorff spaces of cardinality less than $\kappa$.

> **Theorem** [thmcmpcondqs]. Let $\kappa$ be a strong limit cardinal. The restricted Yoneda embedding $\mathbb{H}(C):=\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa(-,C)|_{\mathbf{CHaus}}$ and the assignment $(-)_{\operatorname{cmp}}$ (sending a condensed set $X$ to the underlying set $X(*)$ with compactology $\mathcal{C}_X:=\{\operatorname{ran}(\phi_*)\subseteq X(*)\mid \phi\in X(K),\,K\in\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa\}$) are quasi-inverse, and induce an equivalence
> $$\mathbb{H}\colon \mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa \overset{\sim}{\longleftrightarrow} \mathbf{qsCond}_\kappa\colon (-)_{\operatorname{cmp}}$$
> between $\kappa$-compactological and quasiseparated $\kappa$-condensed sets.

- These equivalences extend along directed colimits to an equivalence between the unrestricted category $\mathbf{Cmp}$ of all compactological sets and the category $\mathbf{Cond}$ of all condensed sets (see 2.15 and 3.2).
- Stempfhuber [Ste24] also surveyed the correspondence between quasiseparated condensed and compactological sets. His work builds on Waelbroeck's definition, whereas this article follows Buchwalter's approach (cf. 2.3).
- Section 4 analyses $\mathbf{Cmp}$ itself. It shows that $\mathbf{Cmp}$ inherits from $\mathbf{CGWH}$ the properties of being total, cartesian closed, regular, coregular and extensive, and it extends $\mathbf{Cmp}$ to a Barr-exact category.

> **Theorem** [thmcmpregex] (third main theorem). The category $\mathbf{Cond}_\kappa$ of $\kappa$-condensed sets is the ex/reg-completion of the category $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ of $\kappa$-compactological sets. Explicitly, it is the universal Barr-exact category containing compactological sets as a full subcategory. The functor $\mathbb{H}\colon \mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Cond}_\kappa$ preserves coequalizers of kernel pairs and is hence a regular functor. Analogously, $\mathbf{Cond}$ is the ex/reg-completion of $\mathbf{Cmp}$.

  This theorem resolves a question of Hanson [Han23].

## Section 2: Compactological sets

**Definition 2.1** (Buchwalter's definition [Buc69b, Dfn 1.1.1]). Let $X$ be a set. A *compactology* $\mathcal{C}$ on $X$ is a non-empty collection of subsets $\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)$, endowed with topologies $\tau_K$, such that each $(K,\tau_K)$ is compact (and hence Hausdorff), and the following hold:

1. $\mathcal{C}$ covers $X$: $\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{C}}K=X$.
2. $\mathcal{C}$ is upwards filtering: $\forall K_1,K_2\in\mathcal{C}\ \exists K_3\in\mathcal{C}:\ K_1\subseteq K_3$ and $K_2\subseteq K_3$.
3. The topologies are coherent: $\forall K_1,K_2\in\mathcal{C}$ with $K_2\subseteq K_1$: $\tau_{K_2}=\tau_{K_1}|_{K_2}$.
4. $\mathcal{C}$ is stable under compact subsets: $\forall K_1\in\mathcal{C}$ and every $K_2\subseteq(K_1,\tau_{K_1})$ compact, $K_2\in\mathcal{C}$.

The triple $\mathcal{C}=(X,\mathcal{C},(\tau_K)_{K\in\mathcal{C}})$ is a *compactological set*.

**Lemma 2.2.** Let $(X,\mathcal{C},(\tau_K)_{K\in\mathcal{C}})$ be a compactological set. Then $\mathcal{C}$ is stable under finite unions and finite intersections.

**Remark 2.3.**

1. An often-cited reference is Waelbroeck's textbook [Wae71, Chapter III]. His definition requires that $\mathcal{C}$ is a bornology (covering, stable under finite unions, stable under arbitrary subsets), that every $K\in\mathcal{C}$ carries a topology $\tau_K$ satisfying condition 2.1(3), and that every $L\in\mathcal{C}$ is contained in some $K\in\mathcal{C}$ such that $(K,\tau_K)$ is compact.
2. The two notions are not strictly equivalent. However, a compactology in the Waelbroeck sense is completely determined by its subsystem of compacts, which is a compactology in the sense of Buchwalter. Conversely, a compactology in the sense of Buchwalter yields one in the Waelbroeck sense by taking the set of all subsets of members of $\mathcal{C}$.
3. Waelbroeck [Wae67, Dfn 4.1] was the first to study compactologies, under the name "compact boundedness".
4. In the remainder, 2.1 is used, but results of Waelbroeck and others are also referred to in the setting described above.

**Proposition 2.4.** Let $(X,\mathcal{C},(\tau_K)_{K\in\mathcal{C}})$ be a compactological set. Let $\tau$ be the final topology on $X$ with respect to the inclusions $\{(K,\tau_K)\hookrightarrow X\mid K\in\mathcal{C}\}$. Then $\tau_K=\tau|_K$ for every $K\in\mathcal{C}$.

**Remark 2.5.**

1. An equivalent definition replaces the system $(\tau_K)_{K\in\mathcal{C}}$ in 2.1 by a single topology $\tau$ on $X$, and requires instead of 2.1(3) that $\tau$ is final with respect to the inclusions $\{(K,\tau|_K)\hookrightarrow X\mid K\in\mathcal{C}\}$.
2. For the Waelbroeck setting (see 2.3(1)), this definition was recently given by Barton and Commelin [BC23, Dfn 1.4.5] in their lecture notes. They used essentially the first line of the proof of 2.4 as a definition.
3. The "topology of a compactological set" refers to the topology $\tau$ from 2.4(2). In 2.11 it is shown that the collection $\mathcal{C}$ of plain sets determines the $\tau_K$.

**Example 2.6.** Let $(X,d)$ be an uncountable metric space. Let $\mathcal{C}:=\{K\subseteq X\mid K\text{ is countable and compact}\}$, where each $K$ carries the topology $\tau_K$ induced by the metric. Then $(X,\mathcal{C},(\tau_K)_{K\in\mathcal{C}})$ is a compactological set.

**Example 2.7** (Stempfhuber [Ste24, Ex 7.1.9]). Let $X:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^2\mid \|x\|\leqslant 1\}$ with the Euclidean metric $d$. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the collection of closed subsets of finite unions of chords in $X$. Endowing each $K\in\mathcal{C}$ with the topology induced by $d$ yields a compactology. Using that there exists a $d$-dense subset of $X$ containing no three collinear points, the final topology of the inclusions $K\hookrightarrow X$ is strictly finer than the topology induced by $d$. For details see [Ste24, Ex 7.1.9].

**Definition 2.8** (Strickland [Str09, Dfns 1.1 and 1.2]). Let $X$ be a topological space.

1. $X$ is *weak Hausdorff* if for every compact space $S$ and every continuous $f\colon S\to X$, the image $f(S)\subseteq X$ is closed.
2. $X$ is *compactly generated* if $F\subseteq X$ is closed if and only if $f^{-1}(F)$ is closed in $S$ for every compact space $S$ and every continuous $f\colon S\to X$.
3. $X$ is a *cgwh-space* if it is compactly generated and weak Hausdorff.

**Definition 2.9.** $\mathbf{Cmp}$ denotes the category of compactological sets. A morphism $f\colon C\to C'$, where $C=(X,\mathcal{C},(\tau_K))$ and $C'=(X',\mathcal{C}',(\tau_{K'}))$, is a map $f\colon X\to X'$ such that $f(K)\in\mathcal{C}'$ for all $K\in\mathcal{C}$, and $f|_K\colon(K,\tau_K)\to(f(K),\tau_{f(K)})$ is continuous for all $K\in\mathcal{C}$. $\mathbf{CGWH}$ denotes the category of cgwh-spaces with continuous maps.

**Remark 2.10.**

1. In general a compactological set carries more data than its associated cgwh-space. In Example 2.6, consider as a second compactology $\mathcal{C}'$ the set of all compact subsets. Then $(X,\mathcal{C}',(\tau_C))$ and $(X,\mathcal{C},(\tau_C))$ cannot be isomorphic as compactological sets, but their final topologies both coincide with the metric topology.
2. $\iota$ can be regarded as a functor from all weak Hausdorff or even all $T_1$-spaces to compactological sets. The composition $(-)_{\operatorname{top}}\circ\iota$ then coincides with the Kelley- or $k$-ification [ML71, p. 186], [Rie17, Ex 4.3.10], [Sch18, p. 690]. In particular, for any weak Hausdorff space $(X,\sigma)$ that is not compactly generated, the topology $\tau$ from Proposition 2.4 is strictly finer than $\sigma$.
3. Buchwalter [Buc69b, Chapter 3.3] defined analogous functors on Hausdorff spaces. The theorem above shows that the cgwh-property suffices for the present purposes. Waelbroeck [Wae71, Prop III.1–2] used a similar construction on $T_1$-spaces, but then $(-)_{\operatorname{top}}\circ\iota$ is not the identity, since $(-)_{\operatorname{top}}$ always produces a weak Hausdorff space.

**Proposition 2.11.** Let $(X,\mathcal{C},(\tau_K)_{K\in\mathcal{C}})$ be a compactological set. Then $\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)$ has the following properties:

1. $\mathcal{C}$ contains the empty set.
2. $\mathcal{C}$ covers $X$.
3. $\mathcal{C}$ is stable under finite unions.
4. $\mathcal{C}$ is stable under arbitrary intersections over non-empty index sets.
5. For any two points $x\neq y$ in $X$ and every $K\in\mathcal{C}$ containing both, there exist $K_1,K_2\in\mathcal{C}$ with $K=K_1\cup K_2$, $x\notin K_1$ and $y\notin K_2$.
6. Every collection $(K_i)_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{C}$ with empty intersection admits a finite subcollection $(K_i)_{i\in I'}$ with empty intersection.

Furthermore, if some $\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)$ satisfies (1)–(6), then there is exactly one system of topologies such that $(X,\mathcal{C},(\tau_K)_{K\in\mathcal{C}})$ is a compactological set. This system is given by $\tau_K:=\{K\setminus K'\mid K'\in\mathcal{C}\}$.

**Lemma 2.12.** Let $X\neq\varnothing$ and assume $\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)$ satisfies 2.11(1)–(4). Put $\tau_K:=\{K\setminus K'\mid K'\in\mathcal{C}\}$ for $K\in\mathcal{C}$. Let $\tau$ be the final topology of the inclusions $\{(K,\tau_K)\hookrightarrow X\mid K\in\mathcal{C}\}$. Then:

1. For $K\in\mathcal{C}$: $\{F\subseteq K\mid F\text{ is }\tau_K\text{-closed}\}=\{K\cap K'\mid K'\in\mathcal{C}\}=\{K'\in\mathcal{C}\mid K'\subseteq K\}$.
2. For every $K\in\mathcal{C}$: $\tau_K=\tau|_K$.
3. If $F\subseteq(X,\tau)$ is closed and contained in some $K\in\mathcal{C}$, then $F\in\mathcal{C}$.
4. If $(K,\tau_K)$ is compact for every $K\in\mathcal{C}$, then $\mathcal{C}$ satisfies 2.11(5) and 2.11(6).

**Remark 2.13.** Compactological sets are written $C=(X,\mathcal{C})$, with the topologies $\tau_K$ recovered via 2.11. Proposition 2.11 and Lemma 2.12 are used to verify that a given collection is a compactology.

**Definition 2.14.**

- A *$\kappa$-small compact space* is a compact space $K$ with $|K|<\kappa$.
- A topological space is *$\kappa$-compactly generated* (resp. *$\kappa$-weak Hausdorff*) if the conditions in 2.8(1) (resp. 2.8(2)) hold for all $\kappa$-small compact spaces $S$.
- A *$\kappa$-cgwh space* is both $\kappa$-compactly generated and $\kappa$-weak Hausdorff. $\mathbf{CGWH}_\kappa$ is the full subcategory of $\mathbf{CGWH}$ formed by the $\kappa$-small cgwh-spaces.
- A *$\kappa$-compactological set* is a compactological set $(X,\mathcal{C})$ such that every $K\in\mathcal{C}$ is $\kappa$-small. $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ is defined accordingly.

**Remark 2.15.**

1. A $\kappa$-compactly generated space is weak Hausdorff if and only if it is $\kappa$-weak Hausdorff. So in the definition of $\kappa$-cgwh spaces, "$\kappa$-weak Hausdorff" may be replaced by "weak Hausdorff".
2. Categories are distinguished by the index $\kappa$. Not every statement transfers from $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ to $\mathbf{Cmp}$ for set-theoretic reasons. Assuming a Grothendieck universe $\mathcal{V}$ and letting "small" mean "element of $\mathcal{V}$", $\kappa$ may be chosen to be $|\mathcal{V}|$, which corresponds to the unrestricted case. Under these assumptions $\mathbf{Cmp}$ is a special case of $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$, and all results transfer.
3. For $\kappa<\kappa'$ the inclusion $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Cmp}_{\kappa'}$ admits a right adjoint, which forgets all elements of the compactology that are not $\kappa$-small. There is an analogous adjunction for cgwh-spaces. $\mathbf{Cmp}$ and $\mathbf{CGWH}$ are the totally ordered colimits over all $\kappa$-restricted subcategories, and every inclusion admits a corresponding adjunction.
4. Section 1 transfers verbatim to an adjunction between $\kappa$-compactological sets and $\kappa$-cgwh spaces.

**Proposition 2.16.** Let $I$ be a set and $(C_i)_{i\in I}$ a family of $\kappa$-compactological sets $C_i=(X_i,\mathcal{C}_i)$.

1. Let $\overline{(-)}^{\cup_{\operatorname{fin}}}$ denote closure under finite unions. The coproduct $\coprod_{i\in I}C_i$ in both $\mathbf{Cmp}$ and $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ exists and is given by
$$\Bigl(X:=\coprod_{i\in I}X_i,\ \mathcal{C}:=\overline{\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_i}^{\,\cup_{\operatorname{fin}}}\Bigr).$$
The associated topology of $\coprod_{i\in I}C_i$ is the usual coproduct topology.

2. For $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)$, let $\overline{\mathcal{B}}^{\,\cap\cup_{\operatorname{fin}}}$ denote the collection of sets that can be written as intersections over a non-empty collection of finite unions of elements of $\mathcal{B}$. The product $\prod_{i\in I}C_i$ in $\mathbf{Cmp}$ exists and is given by
$$\Bigl(X:=\prod_{i\in I}X_i,\ \mathcal{C}:=\overline{\Bigl\{\prod_{i\in I}K_i\Bigm|\forall i\in I:\ K_i\in\mathcal{C}_i\Bigr\}}^{\,\cap\cup_{\operatorname{fin}}}\Bigr).$$
If $I$ is finite, the topology induced by $\mathcal{C}$ on $X$ is the product topology computed in $\mathbf{CGWH}$ [Str09, Dfn 2.3 and Prop 2.4].

3. The product of $(C_i)_{i\in I}$ exists in $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ and is obtained by computing the product in $\mathbf{Cmp}$ and retaining only the $\kappa$-small elements of the compactology. It agrees with (2) whenever the spaces and the index set are sufficiently small relative to $\kappa$. This includes the case when both $|I|$ and $\sup_{K\in\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_i}|K|$ are strictly smaller than $\kappa$, or when $|I|<\operatorname{cof}(\kappa)$.

**Proposition 2.17.** Let $f,g\colon C\to D$ be any two morphisms in $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$, where $C=(X_C,\mathcal{C}_C)$ and $D=(X_D,\mathcal{C}_D)$.

1. The equalizer $\operatorname{Eq}(f,g)$ of $f$ and $g$ in $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ and in $\mathbf{Cmp}$ exists and is given by
$$\bigl(X:=\{x\in X_C\mid f(x)=g(x)\},\ \mathcal{C}:=X\cap\mathcal{C}_C=\{K\in\mathcal{C}_C\mid K\subseteq X\}\bigr).$$
The associated cgwh-space $(\operatorname{Eq}(f,g))_{\operatorname{top}}$ agrees with the equalizer of $(f)_{\operatorname{top}}$ and $(g)_{\operatorname{top}}$ in $\mathbf{CGWH}$.

2. The coequalizer $\operatorname{Coeq}(f,g)$ of $f$ and $g$ in $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ and in $\mathbf{Cmp}$ exists and is given by
$$\bigl(X:=X_D/E,\ \mathcal{C}:=\pi(\mathcal{C}_D)\bigr).$$
Here $E\subseteq X_D\times X_D$ is the smallest closed equivalence relation containing all pairs $(f(x),g(x))$ for $x\in X_C$, where the product carries the topology induced by the product compactology (cf. 2.16(2)). The map $\pi\colon X_D\to X$ is the canonical projection. The associated cgwh-space $(\operatorname{Coeq}(f,g))_{\operatorname{top}}$ agrees with the coequalizer of $(f)_{\operatorname{top}}$ and $(g)_{\operatorname{top}}$ in $\mathbf{CGWH}$.

**Corollary 2.18.** The category of compactological sets is complete and cocomplete.

**Corollary 2.19.** Let $C=(X_C,\mathcal{C}_C)$, $C'=(X_{C'},\mathcal{C}_{C'})$, $C''=(X_{C''},\mathcal{C}_{C''})\in\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$.

1. Let $f\colon C'\to C$ and $g\colon C''\to C$ be morphisms of compactological sets. The pullback $C'\times_C C''$ is given by
$$\bigl(X:=\{(x,y)\in X_{C'}\times X_{C''}\mid f(x)=g(y)\},\ \mathcal{C}:=X\cap\mathcal{C}_{C'\times C''}\bigr).$$
Explicitly, a subset of $X$ belongs to $\mathcal{C}$ if and only if it is a closed subset of some cylinder $K'\times K''$ with $K'\in\mathcal{C}_{C'}$ and $K''\in\mathcal{C}_{C''}$.

2. Let $f\colon C\to C'$ and $g\colon C\to C''$ be morphisms of compactological sets. The pushout $C'\sqcup_C C''$ is given by
$$\bigl(X:=(X_{C'}\sqcup X_{C''})/E,\ \mathcal{C}:=\pi(\mathcal{C}_{C'}\sqcup\mathcal{C}_{C''})\bigr).$$
Here $E$ is the smallest closed equivalence relation containing all $(i_1(f(x)),i_2(g(x)))$ for $x\in X_C$, where $i_1,i_2$ are the inclusions into the disjoint union, and $\pi$ is the corresponding quotient map.

**Lemma 2.20.** The forgetful functor $(-)_{\operatorname{top}}\colon\mathbf{Cmp}\to\mathbf{CGWH}$ is cocontinuous and finitely continuous.

**Proposition 2.21.** The natural functor $\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$, $(K,\tau)\mapsto(K,\mathcal{C}_K)$ with
$$\mathcal{C}_K:=\{(K',\tau|_{K'})\mid K'\subseteq(K,\tau)\text{ compact}\},$$
cf. Section 1, is finitely cocontinuous.

**Proposition 2.22.** For any $\kappa$-compactological set $(X,\mathcal{C})$ we may regard the compactology as a poset-shaped diagram $\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa$. In this sense
$$(X,\mathcal{C})\cong\operatorname*{colim}_{K\in\mathcal{C}}\iota(K,\tau_K)$$
holds for all compactological sets $(X,\mathcal{C})$, where $\iota\colon\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ is defined as in 2.21 and the colimit is taken in $\mathbf{Cmp}$. In particular, the compact spaces $\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ generate the category of compactological sets under filtered colimits of diagrams containing only embeddings, and this presentation is unique up to refinement of diagrams.

**Lemma 2.23.** Let $K$ be a compact space. The functor $\mathbf{Cmp}(\iota(K),-)\colon\mathbf{Cmp}\to\mathbf{Set}$ preserves filtered colimits of compact spaces with all transition maps given by embeddings. The analogous result holds under restriction to some uncountable strong limit cardinal $\kappa$.

**Proposition 2.24.** The category $\mathbf{Cmp}_\kappa$ of compactological sets is equivalent to the category $\mathbf{Ind_{emb}}(\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa)$ of filtered diagrams of ($\kappa$-small) compact spaces with all transition maps given by topological embeddings. The analogous result is true without the restriction to $\kappa$.

## Section 3: Condensed sets

**Definition 3.1** ([Sch19a]). A *$\kappa$-condensed set/group/module/algebra/…*, or more generally a *$\kappa$-condensed $\mathbf{C}$-object*, is a functor $X\colon\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathbf{C}$ such that:

1. $X$ maps finite coproducts in $\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa$ to products in $\mathbf{C}$.
2. Every epimorphism $f\colon K\twoheadrightarrow L$ induces a bijection
$$X(f)\colon X(L)\xrightarrow{\sim}\{\phi\in X(K)\mid X(\pi_1)(\phi)=X(\pi_2)(\phi)\},$$
where $\pi_1,\pi_2\colon K\times_L K\to K$ are the two projections.

The category $\mathbf{Cond}_\kappa(\mathbf{C})$ of $\kappa$-condensed $\mathbf{C}$-objects is the full subcategory of $\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{CHaus}_\kappa^{\operatorname{op}},\mathbf{C})$ on the $\kappa$-condensed objects. Write $\mathbf{Cond}_\kappa:=\mathbf{Cond}_\kappa(\mathbf{Set})$ for the category of $\kappa$-condensed sets. For $X\in\mathbf{Cond}_\kappa$ and $*$ the singleton, $X(*)$ is the *underlying set*.

**Remark 3.2.**

1. The restriction to $\kappa$-small spaces is necessary so that the collection of $\kappa$-condensed sets is well-defined without passing to stronger axiomatic foundations than classical set theory. By [Sch19a, Prop 2.9 and Rmk 2.10], for $\kappa<\kappa'$ the restriction $\mathbf{Cond}_{\kappa'}(\mathbf{C})\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{Cond}_\kappa(\mathbf{C})$ admits a fully faithful left adjoint $\mathbf{Cond}_\kappa(\mathbf{C})\to\mathbf{Cond}_{\kappa'}(\mathbf{C})$, which commutes with $\lambda$-small limits and all colimits. To avoid choosing $\kappa$ altogether, one may pass to the (large) colimit of the totally ordered diagram over all categories of $\kappa$-condensed sets. This category is referred to as $\mathbf{Cond}$.
