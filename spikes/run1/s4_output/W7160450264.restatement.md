# Lectures on Condensed Mathematics

## Preface

These are lecture notes for a course on condensed mathematics, taught in summer term 2019 at the University of Bonn. The material is joint work with Dustin Clausen. This version is an updated, citable version of the original notes, with mostly cosmetic changes and some small corrections. The content has been expanded in [Ásg21], [Bri25], [Dég20], [lS25], [Mai21], [Man22]. Further work by Clausen and the author appears in [Sch20], [CS22], [CS26].

## 0.1. Lecture I: Condensed Sets

**Question 0.1.1.** How to do algebra when rings/modules/groups carry a topology?

Sample situations: representations of topological groups such as $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)$; continuous group cohomology $H^i_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,M)$; and algebraic or analytic geometry over topological fields such as $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

Problems:

1. Topological abelian groups do not form an abelian category. The map $(\mathbb{R},\text{discrete topology})\to(\mathbb{R},\text{natural topology})$ is not an isomorphism, and this cannot be explained by a nontrivial kernel or cokernel in the category of topological abelian groups.
2. For a topological group $G$, a short exact sequence of continuous $G$-modules does not in general give long exact sequences of continuous group cohomology.
3. For a map $A\to B$ of topological $K$-algebras and a topological $A$-module $M$, the base change $M\widehat{\otimes}_A B$ is in general not clearly defined, since it is unclear how to complete.

**Definition 0.1.2.** The pro-étale site $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$ of a point is the category of profinite sets $S$, with finite families of jointly surjective maps as covers. A *condensed set* is a sheaf of sets on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$. Similarly, a *condensed ring/group/…* is a sheaf of rings/groups/… on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$. For any category $\mathcal{C}$, the category $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})$ of condensed objects of $\mathcal{C}$ is the category of $\mathcal{C}$-valued sheaves on $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$.

Equivalently, a condensed set/ring/group/… is a functor
$$T:\{\text{profinite sets}\}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\{\text{sets/rings/groups/…}\},\qquad S\mapsto T(S),$$
satisfying $T(\emptyset)=\ast$ and the following two conditions:

1. For profinite sets $S_1,S_2$, the natural map $T(S_1\sqcup S_2)\to T(S_1)\times T(S_2)$ is a bijection.
2. For a surjection $S'\to S$ of profinite sets, with fibre product $S'\times_S S'$ and projections $p_1,p_2$ to $S'$, the map
$$T(S)\to\{x\in T(S')\mid p_1^*(x)=p_2^*(x)\in T(S'\times_S S')\}$$
is a bijection.

Given a condensed set $T$, $T(\ast)$ is called its underlying set.

**Example 0.1.3.** Let $T$ be a topological space. The associated condensed set $\underline{T}$ sends a profinite set $S$ to the set of continuous maps $S\to T$. If $T$ is a topological ring/group/…, then $\underline{T}$ is a condensed ring/group/… .

**Remark 0.1.4.** Definition 0.1.2 presents set-theoretic problems, since the category of profinite sets is large. To resolve this, fix a suitable uncountable cardinal $\kappa$ and use only profinite sets $S$ with $|S|<\kappa$ to define a site $\ast_{\kappa\text{-}\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$. Throughout, $\kappa$ is an uncountable strong limit cardinal, i.e. $\kappa$ is uncountable and $2^\lambda<\kappa$ for all $\lambda<\kappa$. The case of regular $\kappa$ is also interesting; in particular $\kappa=\aleph_1$ leads to "light condensed sets" [CS26].

Such $\kappa$ exist. Define $\beth_\alpha$ inductively: $\beth_0=\aleph_0$, $\beth_{\alpha^+}=2^{\beth_\alpha}$ for successor ordinals, and for limit ordinals $\beth_\alpha$ is the union of the smaller $\beth_{\alpha'}$. For any limit ordinal $\alpha$, $\kappa=\beth_\alpha$ is an uncountable strong limit cardinal.

For any uncountable strong limit cardinal $\kappa$, the category of $\kappa$-condensed sets is the category of sheaves on $\ast_{\kappa\text{-}\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$.

**Remark 0.1.5.** Let $\kappa'>\kappa$ be uncountable strong limit cardinals. There is a forgetful functor from $\kappa'$-condensed sets to $\kappa$-condensed sets, which admits a natural left adjoint from $\kappa$-condensed sets to $\kappa'$-condensed sets. This left adjoint is fully faithful and commutes with all colimits and many limits (in particular all finite limits), see Proposition 0.2.9. Condensed sets are then defined as the (large) colimit of the categories of $\kappa$-condensed sets along the filtered poset of all uncountable strong limit cardinals $\kappa$.

**Remark 0.1.5 (footnote 2).** In [BH19], Barwick–Haine set up closely related foundations with different set-theoretic conventions, using tiny and small universes and sheaves on tiny profinite sets with values in small sets, which they call pyknotic sets. In the present language, this corresponds to $\kappa$-condensed sets for the first strongly inaccessible cardinal $\kappa$ they consider.

A topological space $X$ is *compactly generated* if a map $f:X\to Y$ to another topological space $Y$ is continuous as soon as $S\to X\to Y$ is continuous for all compact Hausdorff spaces $S$ mapping to $X$. The inclusion of compactly generated spaces into all topological spaces admits a right adjoint $X\mapsto X^{\mathrm{cg}}$, given by the underlying set of $X$ with the quotient topology for the map $\sqcup_{S\to X}S\to X$, where the disjoint union runs over all compact Hausdorff $S$ mapping to $X$. In this definition, compact Hausdorff spaces may be replaced by profinite sets.

Fix $\kappa$ as above. A topological space $X$ is *$\kappa$-compactly generated* if it carries the quotient topology from $\sqcup_{S\to X}S\to X$, where now $S$ runs only over compact Hausdorff spaces with $|S|<\kappa$. The inclusion of $\kappa$-compactly generated spaces into all topological spaces has a right adjoint $X\mapsto X^{\kappa\text{-}\mathrm{cg}}$, given by the underlying set of $X$ with the quotient topology of $\sqcup_{S\to X}S\to X$, where $S$ runs over $\kappa$-small compact Hausdorff $S$.

**Remark 0.1.6.** Any first-countable topological space $X$ (in particular any metrizable space) is compactly generated, and in fact $\kappa$-compactly generated for any uncountable $\kappa$.

**Proposition 0.1.7.**

1. The functor $X\mapsto\underline{X}$ from topological spaces/rings/groups/… to $\kappa$-condensed sets/rings/groups/… is faithful, and fully faithful when restricted to the full subcategory of topological spaces $T$ that are $\kappa$-compactly generated.
2. The functor $X\mapsto\underline{X}$ from topological spaces to $\kappa$-condensed sets admits a left adjoint $T\mapsto T(\ast)_{\mathrm{top}}$. Here $T(\ast)_{\mathrm{top}}$ is the underlying set $T(\ast)$ with the quotient topology of $\sqcup_{S\to T}S\to T(\ast)$, where the disjoint union runs over all $\kappa$-small profinite sets $S$ with a map $S\to T$, i.e. an element of $T(S)$.
3. The counit $\underline{X}(\ast)_{\mathrm{top}}\to X$ agrees with the counit $X^{\kappa\text{-}\mathrm{cg}}\to X$. In particular, $\underline{X}(\ast)_{\mathrm{top}}\cong X^{\kappa\text{-}\mathrm{cg}}$.

**Remark 0.1.8.** If $X$ is a condensed group (or ring, …), it is not necessarily the case that $\underline{X}(\ast)_{\mathrm{top}}$ is a topological group (or ring, …). The functor $X\mapsto X(\ast)_{\mathrm{top}}$ does not necessarily commute with products.

**Example 0.1.9.** Let $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{disc}}$ denote the real numbers with the discrete topology and $\mathbb{R}$ the real numbers with their natural topology. The map $\underline{\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{disc}}}\to\underline{\mathbb{R}}$ is injective, and its cokernel $Q$ is a condensed abelian group with $Q(\ast)=0$. For a general profinite set $S$,
$$Q(S)=\{\text{continuous maps }S\to\mathbb{R}\}/\{\text{locally constant maps }S\to\mathbb{R}\}\neq 0.$$
A priori $Q$ is the sheafification of this functor. Sheafification is unnecessary, by the vanishing $H^1_{\mathrm{cond}}(S,\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{disc}})=0$ proved in Theorem 0.3.2.

**Theorem 0.1.10.** The category of condensed abelian groups is an abelian category satisfying Grothendieck's axioms (AB3), (AB4), (AB5), (AB6), (AB3\*) and (AB4\*). Explicitly: all limits (AB3\*) and colimits (AB3) exist; arbitrary products (AB4\*), arbitrary direct sums (AB4) and filtered colimits (AB5) are exact; and (AB6) holds: for any index set $J$, filtered categories $I_j$ ($j\in J$), and functors $i\mapsto M_i$ from $I_j$ to condensed abelian groups, the natural map
$$\varinjlim_{(i_j\in I_j)_j}\prod_{j\in J}M_{i_j}\to\prod_{j\in J}\varinjlim_{i_j\in I_j}M_{i_j}$$
is an isomorphism. Moreover, the category of condensed abelian groups is generated by compact projective objects.

Recall that an object $M$ of an abelian category $\mathcal{C}$ is *compact* if $\Hom(M,-)$ commutes with filtered colimits.

**Remark 0.1.11.** Sheaves of abelian groups on any site form an abelian category satisfying (AB3), (AB4), (AB5) and (AB3\*). In almost all cases they fail (AB4\*) and (AB6). These axioms follow from the existence of compact projective generators. In the present case, the compact projective generators come from extremally disconnected sets.

## 0.2. Lecture II: Condensed Abelian Groups

**Definition 0.2.1.** The site $\ast_{\kappa\text{-}\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$ is the site of $\kappa$-small profinite sets $S$, with covers given by finite families of jointly surjective maps. A $\kappa$-condensed set/group/ring/… is a sheaf of sets/groups/rings/… on $\ast_{\kappa\text{-}\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$.

**Theorem 0.2.2.** The category of $\kappa$-condensed abelian groups is an abelian category satisfying Grothendieck's axioms (AB3), (AB4), (AB5), (AB6), (AB3\*) and (AB4\*), with the same explicit meaning as in Theorem 0.1.10 (with "$\kappa$-condensed abelian groups" in place of "condensed abelian groups"). Moreover, the category of $\kappa$-condensed abelian groups is generated by compact projective objects.

**Proposition 0.2.3.** Consider the site of all $\kappa$-small compact Hausdorff spaces, with covers given by finite families of jointly surjective maps. Its category of sheaves is equivalent to $\kappa$-condensed sets, via restriction to profinite sets.

**Definition 0.2.4 (Gleason [Gle58]).** A compact Hausdorff space $S$ is *extremally disconnected* if any surjection $S'\to S$ from a compact Hausdorff space splits.

**Example 0.2.5.** If $S=\beta S_0$ is the Stone–Čech compactification of a discrete set $S_0$, then $S$ is extremally disconnected. Any compact Hausdorff space $S$ admits a surjection from the extremally disconnected space $S'=\beta S$, with $|S'|\leq 2^{2^{|S|}}$.

**Warning 0.2.6.** Being extremally disconnected is much stronger than being totally disconnected, and it is hard to find examples that are not Stone–Čech compactifications or closely related. (Any extremally disconnected set is a retract of a Stone–Čech compactification.) For two infinite extremally disconnected sets $S,S'$, the product $S\times S'$ is never extremally disconnected. If $S$ is extremally disconnected and $x_0,x_1,\ldots$ is a convergent sequence of points in $S$, then it is eventually constant, cf. [Gle58].

**Remark 0.2.7.** Extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff spaces play an important role in set theory and forcing. Any such space $S$ can be used to construct a forcing extension of the ground model, and conversely every forcing extension arises this way. In the language ETCSR of the elementary theory of the category of sets with replacement, cf. e.g. [Shu19], the category of sets of the forcing extension is given as follows, cf. [MLM94]. Let $S$ be an extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff space and $s\in S$. Consider the site of open and closed subsets of $S$, where a family $\{U_i\subset U\}_i$ is a cover if $\bigcup_i U_i\subset U$ is dense. Let $\mathrm{Shv}^{\wedge}(S)$ be the resulting category of sheaves. The same construction applies to any open and closed $U\subset S$. The category
$$\mathrm{colim}_{U\ni s}\mathrm{Shv}^{\wedge}(U),$$
where the filtered colimit is along pullback functors in the 2-category of categories, is a model of ETCSR, which can be reinterpreted as a model of ZFC. If $S$ is a Stone–Čech compactification, this is simply an ultraproduct of $\mathrm{Set}$, so passing from $\mathrm{Set}$ to this extension does not change the truth of any ZFC-formula. For general extremally disconnected profinite sets, the extension is very different. See [BLH25], [BB26].

**Proposition 0.2.8.** Consider the site of $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected sets, with covers given by finite families of jointly surjective maps. Its category of sheaves is equivalent to $\kappa$-condensed sets, via restriction from profinite sets.

Using this proposition, the category of $\kappa$-condensed sets/rings/groups/… is equivalent to the category of functors
$$T:\{\kappa\text{-small extremally disconnected sets}\}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\{\text{sets/rings/groups/…}\}$$
such that $T(\emptyset)=\ast$ and, for all $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected $S_1,S_2$, the natural map $T(S_1\sqcup S_2)\to T(S_1)\times T(S_2)$ is a bijection. The analogue of condition (ii) in Definition 0.1.2 is automatic, since every cover of extremally disconnected sets splits.

**Proposition 0.2.9.** Let $\kappa'>\kappa$ be uncountable strong limit cardinals. There is a natural functor from $\kappa$-condensed sets to $\kappa'$-condensed sets, given by sending a $\kappa$-condensed set $T$ to the $\kappa'$-condensed set $T_{\kappa'}$, the sheafification of
$$\widetilde{S}\mapsto\varinjlim_{\widetilde{S}\to S}T(S),$$
where the filtered colimit is over all $\kappa$-small profinite sets $S$ with a map $\widetilde{S}\to S$. In other words, $T_{\kappa'}$ is the pullback of $T$ along the map of sites $\ast_{\kappa'\text{-}\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}\to\ast_{\kappa\text{-}\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$. The functor $T\mapsto T_{\kappa'}$ is fully faithful and commutes with all colimits and all $\lambda$-small limits, where $\lambda$ is the cofinality of $\kappa$.

**Remark 0.2.10.** Except for the commutation with $\lambda$-small limits, this proposition applies verbatim to condensed objects in any category that admits all filtered colimits. For commutation with $\lambda$-small limits one has to be careful in general. It is however certainly true if there is a conservative forgetful functor to sets that commutes with limits and filtered colimits, as for rings, groups, modules, etc.

**Definition 0.2.11.** The category of *condensed sets* is the filtered colimit of the categories of $\kappa$-condensed sets along the filtered poset of all uncountable strong limit cardinals $\kappa$. Equivalently, it is the category of functors
$$T:\{\text{extremally disconnected sets}\}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\{\text{sets}\},\qquad S\mapsto T(S),$$
sending finite disjoint unions to finite products, such that for some uncountable strong limit cardinal $\kappa$, $T$ is the left Kan extension of its restriction to $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected sets.

**Remark 0.2.11 (footnote 6).** Another equivalent characterization: a sheaf $T$ defined on all of $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$ is a condensed set when the corresponding functor from the opposite category of profinite sets to sets, i.e. from $\mathrm{ind}(\text{finite sets})^{\mathrm{op}}$ to sets, is accessible. (Both sets and $\mathrm{ind}(\text{finite sets})^{\mathrm{op}}$ are presentable categories.)

**Remark 0.2.12.** The category of condensed sets is a large category, like the category of all sets. Its essential difference is that it does not admit a set of generators, but only a class of generators. In particular, it is not the category of sheaves on any site. This has been studied in detail by Brink [Bri25], under the term big topos (and big presentable categories).

**Remark 0.2.13.** In forming the colimit over all $\kappa$, one can restrict to $\kappa$ of cofinality at least $\lambda$ for any fixed $\lambda$, and so ensure that formation of $\lambda$-small limits is universal. More strongly, one can define by transfinite induction a sequence $\kappa_\alpha$ of uncountable strong limit cardinals, indexed by ordinals $\alpha$, such that the cofinality of $\kappa_\alpha$ is at least the supremum of the $\kappa_{\alpha'}$ over all $\alpha'<\alpha$. With this definition, the category of condensed sets has all small colimits and all small limits. More generally, $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})=\varinjlim_\kappa\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa(\mathcal{C})$ for any category $\mathcal{C}$ admitting all filtered colimits. The category $\mathrm{Cond}(\mathcal{C})$ is large, but locally small: its $\Hom$ sets are sets, since all transition maps are fully faithful, so any $\Hom$ can be computed in some $\mathrm{Cond}_\kappa(\mathcal{C})$.

**Warning 0.2.14.** The natural functor from topological spaces to sheaves on the full pro-étale site $\ast_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}$ of a point does not land in condensed sets. The problem is nonseparated spaces, such as the Sierpiński space $X=\{s,\eta\}$, where $\{\eta\}$ is open but $\{s\}$ is not. Indeed, $\underline{X}$ sends any extremally disconnected set $S$ to the set of all closed subsets of $S$, and this functor is not the left Kan extension of its restriction to $\kappa$-small extremally disconnected sets for any $\kappa$.
