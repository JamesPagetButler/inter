## Abstract (restated)

Condensed mathematics, developed by Clausen and Scholze, replaces the notion of a topological space with that of a condensed set. Solid abelian groups and liquid vector spaces are central objects of the theory, and are analogues of complete topological groups.

Nöbeling's theorem, dating from the 1960s, concerns the structure of the abelian group of continuous maps from a profinite space to the integers. The abstract states that this theorem is used in the theory of solid abelian groups, and that without it no nonzero examples of solid abelian groups can be given.

The paper discusses a recently completed formalisation of this result in the Lean theorem prover. It gives a proof more detailed than those previously available in the literature. The proof requires induction over ordinals.
