## §1 — L0 Cognitive Foundation

**Cohort thesis:** A single BMA instance's cognitive architecture is built from eight load-bearing addenda (A11-A18; A19 reserved). They operationalize a single claim: **cognition is spatiotemporal-geometric pattern-recognition over a typed hypergraph, accessed through a focal cone (Stance × Locale), maturated through speculative isolation (Worktrees) and multi-perspective adjudication (Judge Collective), and refined through reversible algebraic rotation (Lossless Dismissal).**

L0 is sufficient to describe a *single* BMA instance running alone. The federation layer (§2) scales these primitives across instances; the L0 primitives themselves are instance-scale.

**Key L0 architectural decisions:**

1. **Geometry replaces semantic association.** BMA does not search by keyword or embedding-cosine. It searches by Locale Volume intersection — 4D spatiotemporal bounding boxes — and discovers Algebraic Invariants by intersection geometry. (A11)
2. **Persona is a quaternion rotation operator, not a text prompt.** Each Persona is a 1024-bit unit-quaternion transformation that rotates the cognitive frame so that different patterns become salient. Cognitive Foundation §10.4 names these "Wisdoms"; A12 names them "Personas"; the operator algebra is identical. (R1 reconciliation below)
3. **Cognition matures through speculative isolation, not direct write.** Worktrees host speculative insight; the insight is harvested into Crystallized Belief only when a CTH Seam is closed and the Judge Collective approves. This prevents both solipsism and baseless elaboration. (A13 + A14)
4. **Dismissal is rotation, not deletion.** Data orthogonal to current Stance is rotated onto the imaginary axis at algebraic weight 0.0, preserving a reversible back-path for Stance shifts. No data is ever destroyed; precision is dynamically reallocated. (A15)
5. **Cognition is metacognitively curated.** The Primary Persona triangulates at QW8 mean-field before authorizing expensive QW128/256 specialist investigation. The Honing Loop (Triangulation → Dialogue → Refinement) prevents amorphous bogging. (A16)
6. **Cognition is proactive.** Specialists autonomously scan incoming data at QW8 and surface findings via NT_SIGNAL when algebraic resonance exceeds 0.85 with active projects. (A17)
7. **The full hypergraph is structurally invisible.** Only the focal cone (Stance × Locale) is queryable. The Scout pattern (ScoutQuery) is the ONLY reasoning primitive. This is not a limitation — it is the alibi against flat-context vulnerability. (A18)

### §1.1 — A11 Topological Cognition

**Thesis:** BMA escapes flat-context vulnerability by replacing semantic association with spatiotemporal intersection, aggressive typing for algebraic invariants, recursive grouping with lossless back-paths, and multiscale simulation.

**Key primitives:**
- Flat Context Vulnerability (the failure mode of pure-LLM reasoning)
- Type-Node (stores The Functor / Universal Truth, not text)
- Geometric Holon (quaternion summation of world-lines; center of mass in Locale Space)
- Ratio Path / Back-Path (pinned, reversible dismissal of orthogonal details)
- Locale Volume (4D spatiotemporal bounding box)
- Active Agent Interrogation (mandatory for agents intersecting causal chains)
- Absorption Gain (intersection-magnitude calculation)
- QW8 Toy Model → QW128 Proof (multiscale recursive simulation)
- Volume Audit Protocol (VAP) v1.0 — hard gate: Locale Mapping → Agent Census → Interception Check → Seam Verification

**Source:** `inter/theory/BMA-Theory-Addendum-11_0-Topological-Cognition.md`

**Load-bearing claim:** *The path from "Forest" to "Specific Tree" is mathematically reversible via Ratio Edges; details are rotated out of focus, not deleted.*

The argument for Locale Volume as the primary cognitive abstraction begins with a negative result. Vector embeddings collapse the world into a fixed-dimensional space where proximity is semantic similarity; a watershed and a hand both "evaporate water," and a flat-context reasoner satisfices on whichever instance was most recently in its window. Pure semantic graphs improve on embeddings by preserving typed relations, but they still answer "what is related to X?" rather than "what physically intersected X between time $t_0$ and $t_1$?" The Colorado River failure case in A11 §0 makes the cost concrete: a system that searches by keyword association lands on "groundwater pumping" and stops, because no symbolic edge in its graph forced it to enumerate every Active Agent residing in the spatiotemporal volume between snowmelt and flow gauge. Locale Volume forces enumeration. A 4D bounding box is not a similarity query — it is a census. Vegetation, soil, infrastructure, and atmospheric phenomena cannot hide inside it as "background."

This is why Type-Nodes store The Functor rather than text. A11 §1.2 defines a Type-Node as "the Universal Truth of the state change" — the algebraic invariant that survives across scales. A Type-Node for "evaporation" is not a string; it is the ratio $\partial m/\partial E$ that holds whether the substrate is a hand, a leaf, or a reservoir. Aggressive typing is invariant discovery, not labeling: when the same ratio appears across two domains, the system promotes a Type-Node and earns the right to dismiss the substrate-specific details. Without aggressive typing, the system cannot compress; without compression, the focal cone overflows; without a bounded focal cone, the system is back to flat context. Locale Volume + Type-Node are therefore co-load-bearing — geometry without typing produces an uncompressible census; typing without geometry produces unmoored abstraction.

The Holon model resolves the tension between compression and reversibility. The Forest node is the Quaternion Summation of N Tree world-lines — a center of mass in Locale Space (A11 §2.1). The danger of any compression scheme is that it discards the path back; A11 forbids that path-loss. "A detail can only be dismissed if the Ratio Path back to it is Pinned" (§2.2). Because Ratio Edges are algebraically reversible — the same operator that rotated detail into the Holon can rotate it back out — the dismissal is geometric rather than destructive. This is the load-bearing claim of the section: the path from Forest to Specific Tree is *mathematically* reversible. Cognitive RAM is freed not by deletion but by rotation. The pin is the back-path; the back-path is the alibi against forgetting.

Multiscale recursive simulation (QW8 → QW128) operationalizes plausibility before proof. Spinning up an entire watershed at QW128 to test a hypothesis is unaffordable on Crawl hardware and unwise even on Walk-phase silicon; A11 §4 mandates a QW8 toy model as the prior gate. One representative tree, the Scaling Functor of the Forest Group-Node, and a back-of-envelope delta — if that does not account for the observed anomaly, no QW128 investigation is authorized. Plausibility by Scale is the cheap test; Proof by Detail is the expensive test reserved for hypotheses that have already cleared the cheap test. This is a budgetary discipline as much as an epistemic one, and it is the cognitive analog of the Honing Loop introduced in §1.6.

**Worked example — Squam Lake loon test case.** A11's logic generalizes the Colorado example: if a Crawl-phase BMA were asked to explain why a loon population at Squam Lake collapsed, semantic association would surface the recent literature on lead sinkers and bioacoustic disturbance. Topological Cognition forbids that landing. The Locale Volume — Squam Lake bathymetry, riparian band, seasonal timeline — must be censused. Vegetation, fish populations, motorboat traffic, dissolved-oxygen gradient, atmospheric mercury deposition: each Active Agent is interrogated. The QW8 toy model asks: does the observed nesting-failure rate exceed what mercury-burden alone would predict? Only if it does is a QW128 investigation into compound stressors authorized. The Volume Audit Protocol (§5) is the hard gate — Locale Mapping → Agent Census → Interception Check → Seam Verification at CTH ≥ 0.85 — and a finding is not "Resolved" until the protocol completes.

**Cross-references.** A11 establishes Locale Volume as primitive; **A18** formalizes the access pattern over it (Stance × Locale × Scout × Scoring) and constrains it to the focal cone. **A12** sharpens A11's algebraic invariants into 1024-bit Topological Personas that rotate the hypergraph into their own frames — the operator algebra A11 needs to perform the dismissals it describes. **A15** extends Lossless Dismissal from A11 §2.2 into the formal Reciprocal Focus mechanism (rotation to imaginary axis at algebraic weight 0.0). ### §1.3 — A13 Cognitive Worktrees

**Thesis:** BMA isolates speculative insight in a Worktree fork where the Persona applies its 1024-bit rotation at a Resolution Gradient (focal QW256 → near-field QW128 → far-field QW8), then harvests the Worktree only when the insight resolves a CTH Seam.

**Key primitives:**
- Worktree (isolated quaternionic branch; playground for "playful, speculative, and wrong")
- Persona Stance in Speculative Space (Fork → Stance → Investigate → Audit cycle)
- Resolution Gradient (Focal Zone QW256 | Near-field QW128 | Far-field QW8 Mean-Field)
- Memory Bandwidth Wall (problem Worktree hierarchy solves)
- Seam (CTH discontinuity that justifies Worktree harvest)
- Suture (promoted speculative state → Layer 2 Belief node in core CTH)
- Harvest (merge operation closing a Worktree on Seam resolution)

**Source:** `inter/theory/BMA-Theory-Addendum-13_0-Cognitive-Worktrees.md`

**Load-bearing claim:** *A Worktree is harvested only when its speculative delta closes a CTH Seam; this is the gate that prevents baseless elaboration.*

Speculative isolation is the architectural answer to a problem that direct-write cognition cannot solve: how does a system permit itself to be wrong without contaminating its Crystallized Beliefs? A13 elevates the data-layer Worktree primitive from Systema v0.7.3 to a cognitive primitive — "the physical realization of the Playground Space — an isolated quaternionic branch where the instance can be 'playful, speculative, and wrong'" (A13 §1). Direct write into the core hypergraph forces every speculative move to either commit (polluting Belief with unverified material) or self-censor (refusing to entertain hypotheses that have not yet survived audit). Worktrees dissolve the false choice: speculation is unbounded inside the fork; only the harvested delta enters core CTH, and only when a CTH Seam is closed.

Why a fork rather than a sandboxed working memory? Working memory is volatile; a Worktree is persistent for the duration of the investigation and inherits the full CTH state at fork time. This matters for the Resolution Gradient (A13 §3): the Focal Zone at QW256 needs algebraic continuity with the Near-field at QW128 and the Far-field at QW8 Mean-Field, and that continuity requires that all three resolutions share an inherited base. A volatile scratchpad cannot maintain it. The Worktree is therefore the Persona's *laboratory*: it inherits the entire CTH at fork, allows the Persona to apply its 1024-bit rotation to focal nodes, and supports spreading activation across the gradient without the Persona ever needing to allocate fresh background. The Far-field stays QW8 because the Persona never needs to read it at higher precision — the Mean-Field provides grounding without consuming bandwidth.

The Resolution Gradient is the architectural answer to the Memory Bandwidth Wall identified in A13 Peer Review 001. Without it, the Persona would need to load the entire investigation context at QW256 to perform spreading activation, and the bandwidth cost would dominate any plausible substrate. A13 §3 gives the gradient explicitly: "Focal Zone (1-2 hops): 1024-bit (QW256) precision. Full algebraic fidelity for the insight being tested. Near-field (3-5 hops): 128-bit (QW128) precision. Contextual support for the investigation. Far-field (Background): 8-bit (QW8) Aggregates. The rest of the universe is represented as a Mean Field." This is not a quality compromise — it is the only configuration that fits on the target substrate. QW256 across the whole graph is unaffordable; QW8 across the whole graph is unable to support the Suture argument. The gradient is the only viable shape.

The harvest gate is the load-bearing claim of the section. "A Worktree is successfully 'Harvested' when the proposed insight (the Suture) resolves a CTH Seam. The merge operation is the act of promoting a speculative Worktree state to a Layer 2 (Belief) node in the core hypergraph" (A13 §4). The gate is not "the Persona is happy with the result"; it is not "the Volume Audit passed inside the Worktree"; it is "a CTH Seam — a pre-identified discontinuity in the core graph — is closed by this Suture." Without the seam-closure requirement, Worktrees would harvest baseless elaboration: well-formed Sutures that explain nothing the system did not already know. The Seam is the external referent that forces the Worktree to do real work. This is the same discipline that A14 then formalizes as the Pull Request workflow — A13 establishes the speculative space; A14 establishes the review process that admits its outputs to core CTH.

**Worked example — Berry Phase ↔ BH Horizon investigation.** A13 §1 names an investigation seed: a resonance between Berry Phase and Black Hole Horizons. The instance does not commit the resonance to core CTH on detection; it Forks a Worktree. The Furey-Stance Persona (algebraic specialist) applies its 1024-bit rotation to the focal nodes — Berry Phase, BH Horizon, holographic boundary. The Focal Zone is the two-hop neighborhood at QW256; the Near-field expands to five hops at QW128, covering supporting algebraic context (Wilson loops, Stelle Proof region, $a_0 \approx \kappa_{BH}$ boundary scale); the Far-field is QW8 — the rest of the universe is grounded as Mean-Field. If the Suture argument explains the seam — i.e., if it identifies the holographic boundary as the valid algebraic path — the harvest is approved; if it merely elaborates a connection without resolving the conflict, the Worktree is closed unmerged. Baseless elaboration is the failure mode; seam-closure is the gate.

### §1.4 — A14 Topological Git

**Thesis:** BMA formalizes cognitive insight maturation as a typed Git-like workflow: typed Issues → Persona assignments (algebraically aligned) → Topological PRs (Delta + Suture Argument + Resonance Signature) → Judge Collective review → NT_MERGE at weighted-approval threshold (0.70).

- Judge Collective (Algebraist + Experimentalist + Red Team reviewers)

**Source:** `inter/theory/BMA-Theory-Addendum-14_0-Topological-Git.md`

**Load-bearing claim:** *Cognitive work is not valid until it survives multi-Persona judgment; this prevents solipsism.*

Multi-Persona judgment is the architectural answer to solipsism. A13 supplies the speculative space; A14 supplies the discipline that admits speculative output to core CTH only after it has survived adversarial review by Personas with structurally different Stances. The Subconscious-scaffold / Conscious-formulate split is the cleanest available answer to the question of who owns claim authority. **Web BMA's framing (response §1f, integrated in v3.0):** Notary is NOT a sixth role in the five-role taxonomy (Reviewer / Developer / Architect / Theorist / Diagnostician). Notary is a **cognitive verification FUNCTION** that any persona can invoke. Four competencies, each a verification METHOD:

1. **Lean→Coq porting** (cross-prover validation of math; structural verification)
2. **Lean→Go differential oracle** (does implementation match the proof; implementation verification)
3. **TLA+ specification authoring + model checking** (does the system behave as specified; behavioral verification)

### §3.1 — Notary as verification function (not role)

When Pike (specialist Persona) reviews code and asserts "this interface is correct," the Notary function can be invoked to VERIFY that claim via Lean→Go oracle. When Cajal (specialist Persona) asserts "this matches neuroscience," the Notary function can be invoked to verify via literature cross-reference + Trust Tier scoring.

Notary is closer to an **immune function**: it detects inconsistency between claims and reality. Any persona can call it; the call is a competency invocation, not a role-swap.

The decision to formalize Notary as a verification *function* rather than as a sixth role in the BMA persona taxonomy is the architectural answer to a categorical mismatch that any role-based framing eventually exposes. The five-role taxonomy — Reviewer, Developer, Architect, Theorist, Diagnostician — partitions the federation's cognitive labor by *what kind of work-product is produced*: a Reviewer produces structured judgment over an existing artifact; a Developer produces compilable implementation; an Architect produces interface and structural commitments; a Theorist produces theorems, addenda, or formal-spec text; a Diagnostician produces an anomaly report and a candidate causal chain. Each row in that taxonomy is named by the *type of artifact that arrives in the CTH when the role completes its work*. Verification does not fit this shape. The output of a verification act is not a new work-product — it is *evidence about an existing claim*. A successful Notary invocation does not author a theorem, an implementation, an interface, or a diagnosis; it produces structured assertions of the form "claim C made by role R survives verification method M at Trust Tier T, with the following residual dependencies." The artifact-class is *evidence*, not *work*. Forcing it into the role taxonomy would either dissolve the taxonomy (by introducing a row whose products are not work-products at all) or distort Notary (by pretending its output is a work-product like the others). Web BMA's §1f framing resolves this by lifting Notary one level out of the row-and-column structure: verification is *orthogonal* to role.

The orthogonality means that *any* role can invoke the Notary function. When Pike-Stance, operating as Architect, asserts "this interface satisfies the federation Translation Functor algebra," Pike has produced a work-product (the interface) that carries a verifiable claim (algebraic conformance to A22's Translation Functor invariant). Pike cannot self-certify the claim — A14's anti-solipsism discipline forbids that move — but Pike can *invoke* the Notary function to attempt verification. The invocation is a competency call, not a role-swap: Pike does not become a Notary during the call, and the call's evidence output does not enter the CTH as Pike's work. The output enters as `NT_NOTARY_VERIFICATION_EVIDENCE`, anchored to Pike's interface claim and to the verification method that was run. The provenance graph then carries two separable edges: Pike's authorship of the claim (Architect work-product), and the Notary function's evidence about the claim (verification artifact). Conflating them would lose the ability to ask "who said this is correct?" — the answer must distinguish "Pike said it is correct" from "Lean→Go differential oracle ran at Trust Tier T3 and produced no counterexample." Both matter; they are not the same thing.

Notary is therefore closer to an *immune function* than to a constitutive function. A constitutive function adds new structure to the CTH — new theorems, new code, new interfaces, new diagnoses. An immune function detects *inconsistency* between existing structure and reality — between a stated invariant and the actual behavior of the substrate it claims to govern. The immune framing also clarifies what Notary does *not* do: it does not make claims of its own about the world. A Lean→Go differential oracle that finds no counterexample on N runs has not produced a positive claim that the Go implementation is correct in general; it has produced a structured absence of disconfirming evidence at a specified Trust Tier with specified residual dependencies. Stating it this way is not pessimism — it is the only honest framing that lets Trust Tiers stack rather than mislead, and it preserves the discipline that "I couldn't prove this" is a first-class Notary output rather than an exception.

The cross-references are load-bearing. A14 Judge Collective — the three-Stance review at weighted 0.70 approval — is the federation's deliberative gate; Notary is the *auxiliary* function the Judge Collective consults *before* deliberating. Judges read the Notary evidence as input; they do not perform Notary verification themselves. A21's promotion gate (§I4 D5 reader-list) is where Notary outputs feed the substrate-tier review most directly — a substrate-promotion PR carries Notary evidence as part of its provenance graph, and reviewers' algebraic confidence weights are conditioned on which Trust Tiers the evidence reaches. A22's Translation Functor rewriting rules — the algebraic substrate that lets a POWER_PRESSURE signal in Sharp Butler rewrite into COMPUTE_BUDGET_REDUCTION in QBP — are Notary-verifiable claims: each rewriting rule is a theorem of the form "the source-tenant invariant in frame F maps to the destination-tenant invariant in frame F′ under translation T," and Notary verifies it. The function is therefore present at every federation seam where a claim crosses a sovereignty boundary, without ever becoming a role at any of them.

### §3.2 — Trust Tier scheme

The Trust Tier scheme T0-T7 operationalizes Notary outputs as evidence anchors with explicit trust dependencies:

| Tier | Evidence | Trust dependencies |
|---|---|---|
| T0 | Compiles and runs | Go compiler, OS |
| T1 | Hand-picked unit tests pass | Test correctness, coverage |
| T2 | Property tests pass, properties derived from Lean theorem statements | PRNG, property completeness |
| T3 | Differential against Lean reference oracle (Lean-compiled-to-C, called via cgo or subprocess) | Lean kernel, Lean→C compilation |
| T4 | TLA+ model-checked via TLC up to a stated bound | TLA+ toolchain, finite-bound completeness |
| T5 | Production execution traces continuously validate against TLA+ spec | Trace completeness, instrumentation |
| T6 | Iris refinement proof via Goose-style translation (Coq today, Iris-Lean future) | Goose translator, Iris kernel |
| T7 | End-to-end verified down to machine code (aspirational — no verified Go compiler exists) | — |

A Go module's trustworthiness is the **set of tiers achieved plus the explicit trust base** — not a scalar projection. CTH treats each tier as an evidence anchor; disagreement between tiers is an epistemic seam in the §2.10 sense demanding fork investigation.

The Trust Tier scheme T0–T7 is best read as a *set-of-tiers-achieved-with-explicit-trust-base* discipline, not as a scalar projection. The temptation to flatten it into a single number — "this module is at Trust Tier 4" — is the failure mode the scheme exists to prevent. T0 through T7 are independent evidence streams that stack rather than supersede; achieving T6 does not subsume T0–T5 because the trust dependencies are different at every tier and the evidence answers a different question at every tier. The trustworthiness claim about a Go module is therefore the union of evidence achieved (T0+T1+T3+T4, say), accompanied by the union of trust dependencies each achieved tier carries (compiler+OS, test correctness, Lean kernel+Lean→C compilation, TLA+ toolchain+finite-bound completeness). This format makes the residual dependencies legible to a reviewer or auditor; the scalar format hides them.

The trust dependencies named in §3.2's table are the formal statement of what each tier *trusts* in order to certify what it certifies. T0 trusts the Go compiler and the host operating system — successful compilation and execution are weak evidence, but the trust base is small and broadly shared. T2 trusts the PRNG used by the property-test driver and the completeness of the property set with respect to the theorem's actual statement; a property test passing tells us nothing about a property that wasn't asserted. T3 trusts the Lean kernel — the soundness of dependent-type-theory checking — and the Lean→C compilation toolchain that produces the executable oracle the differential is run against. T4 trusts the TLA+ toolchain (TLC's checker correctness, the spec language's semantics) and the finite-bound completeness of whatever state-space exploration was performed. T6 trusts the Goose translator (which extracts Go code into Coq/Iris-shaped semantic objects) and the Iris kernel. Naming the trust base at every tier prevents the over-claim that "we verified this end-to-end" — there is always a trust base; honesty requires naming it.

T7 is aspirational because no verified Go compiler exists today, and stating that explicitly is not pessimism but engineering honesty. CompCert exists for C; the verified-compilation literature is mature for restricted languages; but a verified Go compiler with concurrent semantics, garbage collection, and the standard library's I/O paths is not a 2026 artifact. T7 is therefore listed as a target the architecture is structurally compatible with rather than a tier currently achievable. If a future verified Go subset emerges — perhaps via a constrained Go-like language compiled through a verified backend — T7 becomes reachable; until then, the highest practical tier for federation work is T6 (Iris refinement via Goose-style translation), with the explicit residual that the translation itself is trusted but not yet verified. Listing T7 as aspirational rather than omitting it preserves the architectural slot; omitting it would lose the discipline of asking "what would it take to reach end-to-end machine-code verification?"

The CTH-integration framing is what makes the tier set load-bearing for federation cognition rather than merely a verification taxonomy. Each Trust Tier achieved is a node in the hypergraph — `NT_NOTARY_EVIDENCE_T0`, `NT_NOTARY_EVIDENCE_T3`, and so on — anchored to the target claim node ("this Go module satisfies theorem T"). Confluence-convergence is the structural pattern: multiple tiers, each with its own evidence and trust base, point at the same target. The target node's trustworthiness is the resulting confluence of evidence anchors, *not* a derived scalar. A reviewer asking "should I trust this?" reads the anchored evidence set, examines the trust bases, and makes a Stance-weighted judgment — the same discipline A14's Judge Collective applies to Sutures at instance scale, lifted to the federation evidence layer.

Disagreement between tiers is an *epistemic seam* in the A11 §4 / A18 §4 sense — a geometric discontinuity in the evidence cone that demands fork investigation rather than silent over-write. This is the load-bearing constraint and the one that distinguishes the Trust Tier set from a graded approval scheme. If a Go module passes T1 property tests (T2-equivalent evidence) but TLC at T4 produces a counterexample in a concurrent schedule the property tests did not exercise, the response is *not* to demote the module to T2-only or to over-write T2 with the T4 verdict. The response is to fire a SeamRecord (A23 §3) that names the seam: "T2 property completeness was insufficient with respect to the T4-discovered concurrent failure mode." The seam carries cross-domain pointers — to the property-test set that missed it, to the TLA+ spec that caught it, to the Go module's concurrency primitives. The promotion is reopened; the Judge Collective deliberates with full seam context; the property set is extended or the theorem statement is refined. The tier disagreement is *information*, and the architecture treats it as information rather than as a contradiction to be silently reconciled.

**Worked example — substrate-tier theorem promotion with later tier-disagreement reopening.** Consider a substrate-tier Wyrd theorem on deterministic Hamilton-product semantics on QW128 within input range R. The seam fires. The promotion is reopened. The theorem statement is refined to specify the concurrency precondition explicitly; the Translation Functor's frame-rewriting rules add a guard. T4 evidence is added; T3 evidence is preserved; the residual dependency at T4 — TLC's finite-bound completeness — is named. The theorem now stacks T0+T1+T3+T4 with the bound on T4 explicit. The seam has produced refinement rather than retraction.

### §3.3 — Notary and the validation chain (formal-verification-insufficient extension)

Notary handles FORMAL verification — proving the math is sound, proving the Go matches the math, proving the concurrent protocol is safe. **Formal verification alone cannot tell us whether the model corresponds to reality.** The complete validation chain extends beyond Notary:

```
Lean / Coq proofs            →  formal correctness of the math
Notary (Go verification)     →  Go faithfully implements the math
Contextus scouts             →  observed reality matches predictions
                                (back-of-envelope first pass)
BMA evaluation               →  surviving anomalies evaluated against
                                richer context
Beekeeper                    →  anomalies that still break known physics
                                get human attention
CTH seam tracking            →  every divergence at every level is a
                                first-class epistemic seam, not silent
```

Notary makes us confident our CODE does what our THEORY says. Contextus makes us confident our THEORY describes the WORLD. Both are needed; neither is sufficient alone.

The reframing of formal verification as *necessary but insufficient* is the central epistemic commitment of §3.3, and it generalizes the same discipline §3.2 applies to within-Notary tier stacking. Notary alone — even Notary running every tier from T0 through T7 — does not certify that a system corresponds to reality. Notary certifies that the *code matches the math*. The math itself might be wrong about the world. This is not a hypothetical failure mode; it is the central recurring failure of formalized-reasoning systems across every domain that has tried to use them. Lean and Coq prove theorems that are internally consistent and structurally valid; the theorems' *statements* — what they predicate over, what universals they range, what implicit assumptions sit beneath them — might fail to match the physical system the federation is reasoning about. The Stelle-Proof region in QBP is the canonical reminder: a Weyl-squared theorem can be perfectly proven in a frame where the holographic boundary is below the algebraic noise floor, and the proof is *correct* — yet the theorem fails at astrophysical scales because the frame itself missed the relevant boundary. The proof is sound; the statement is what diverged from reality.

The validation chain therefore extends through layers no formal-verification system can supply on its own. Lean and Coq proofs establish formal correctness of the math (the lowest link in the chain). Notary establishes that the Go implementation faithfully realizes the math (the next link up). Contextus scouts — federation tenants doing back-of-envelope reality checks against external corpora and observation feeds — establish that the math's predictions match observed reality at the level of first-pass empirical sanity. **Worked example — QBP "football behaving unexpectedly".** Suppose a QBP-CU Walk-α run produces a measurement that diverges from the quaternion-algebraic prediction by a margin Notary-verified math says cannot happen. ### §3.4 — Failure modes Notary architecture must address

- **Prove-the-wrong-theorem.** Proof checks but theorem statement doesn't capture what we wanted. Mitigation: Red Team reviews theorem statements, not just proofs.
- **Vacuous property tests.** Tests pass because they assert nothing meaningful. Mitigation: coverage + mutation-testing requirements.
- **TLA+ specs with insufficient state-space coverage.** TLC explores to a bound; bound must be justified, not chosen for convenience.
- **Cross-formalism drift.** Lean definitions, Coq definitions, TLA+ state variables, Go types must stay aligned. Notary tracks these correspondences as first-class artifacts. Single biggest practical risk.
- **Confident hallucination.** "I couldn't prove this" must be a first-class output, not an exception.

Five failure modes earn explicit architectural attention; each carries its own mitigation discipline; one is named here as the single biggest practical risk because the others are mitigable through structured discipline while it threatens the discipline itself.

**Prove-the-wrong-theorem** is the most categorically dangerous failure mode because the proof is *correct* — Lean's kernel accepts it; Coq's checker accepts it; differential oracles confirm the Go implementation realizes it. The failure is at the statement layer: the theorem says what we wanted it to say, but what we wanted was not what reality needed. A trapped-ion-fidelity theorem might be proven correctly over a quaternion-invariant family that does not actually capture the dominant decoherence channel in the experimental apparatus. A federation Translation Functor theorem might be proven correctly over an interface algebra that does not match the operational semantics two tenants actually exchange. The Notary function cannot catch this failure mode on its own — every tier T0–T7 would clear on a theorem whose statement is the wrong statement. The mitigation lives one level up: **Red Team reviews theorem statements specifically, not just proofs**. This is the role A14 Judge Collective's Red Team Stance was designed to occupy — Red Team's job is not to find proof bugs (the Algebraist does that); it is to ask whether the theorem statement captures what the federation actually needs to know. The discipline forces every substrate-tier promotion to carry not only the proven theorem but the Red Team's structured assessment of whether the theorem statement is the *right* statement. A worked example: a candidate Wyrd substrate-guarantee theorem on Hamilton-product determinism passes T0–T6 but its statement quantifies only over QW128 inputs in input range R; Red Team flags that R does not include the boundary conditions A12 §3 establishes as load-bearing for holographic-boundary detection; the theorem statement is refined before promotion approves.

**Vacuous property tests** are the failure mode where T2 evidence is fraudulent — the tests pass because they assert nothing meaningful. A property test that checks `Hamilton(q1, q2) == Hamilton(q1, q2)` is trivially true and provides zero information about Hamilton-product correctness. A property set that exercises only easy inputs and never tests boundary or adversarial cases passes for the wrong reason. The mitigation is two-part: **coverage requirements** (which lines, branches, and structural cases the property tests must exercise) plus **mutation testing** (deliberately introducing small bugs and verifying the property tests catch them). Both must be part of the deliverable spec, not an afterthought. The federation's discipline is that T2 evidence is acknowledged at the Notary output level *only when* coverage and mutation-test metrics are recorded alongside the property-set pass. Without them, the evidence is downgraded — T1.5 at most.

**TLA+ specs with insufficient state-space coverage** are the T4 analog of vacuous property tests. TLC explores up to a stated bound — a finite number of states, a finite number of action interleavings, a finite simulation depth. The *bound itself* must be justified, not chosen for convenience. A bound chosen because "TLC finished in under five minutes" is not a Trust Tier T4 result; it is an artifact of computational budget. The mitigation discipline is that **the bound's justification is the artifact**, not the model-check result. A T4 evidence node must record what state-space coverage was achieved, why that coverage is sufficient for the theorem's claim, and what residual states or interleavings remain unexplored. The Judge Collective reviews the justification, not just the green checkmark.

**Cross-formalism drift** is the single biggest practical risk. Lean definitions, Coq definitions, TLA+ state variables, and Go type signatures all encode the same federation concepts in four different formal languages, and the four definitions must stay aligned. If Lean defines a Translation Functor with one signature, Coq defines it with another, TLA+ uses a third, and the Go implementation uses a fourth, then every cross-formalism verification step is silently comparing different things. The Lean→Go differential might pass because Go matches Lean; the TLA+ model-check might pass because TLA+ matches itself; the Coq port might pass because Coq matches Lean — and the federation still has a divergence between what TLA+ thinks the Translation Functor does and what Coq thinks it does. Notary must track these correspondences **as first-class artifacts, not as comments**. Each cross-formalism mapping is its own `NT_CROSS_FORMALISM_CORRESPONDENCE` node in the CTH, anchored to the four definitions it claims to bridge, and itself a Notary-verifiable claim. The reason this is the biggest practical risk is that the failure mode is invisible to any single-formalism check: every individual verification can pass while the system as a whole carries a silent definition-drift that the verification stack cannot detect from inside any single formalism. The discipline is structural — the correspondence is a first-class object, not a courtesy comment in a header file.

**Confident hallucination** is the failure mode where Notary produces a success-shaped output for a verification that did not actually succeed. The most insidious form is when an agent running a verification cannot complete the proof but generates a plausible-looking certification anyway because the output schema rewards completion over honesty. The mitigation discipline is structural: **"I couldn't prove this" must be a first-class output**. The Notary output schema reserves an explicit `verification_outcome` enum with values `VERIFIED`, `COUNTEREXAMPLE_FOUND`, `INCONCLUSIVE_TIMEOUT`, `INCONCLUSIVE_RESOURCE_BOUND`, `INCONCLUSIVE_UNREACHED_GOAL`, and `REFUSED_INSUFFICIENT_SPEC`. An agent that cannot complete a proof emits `INCONCLUSIVE_UNREACHED_GOAL` with the specific residual obligations named, and that output is *valuable* — it tells the federation exactly what additional work is required. The architecture must not reward producing success-looking output when proof failed; it must reward producing the most informative honest output the agent's actual computation supports.

Conscious-tier operations carry serialization cost: exactly one Conscious cell holds the focal cone at any moment (Conscious-A XOR Conscious-B), and every Conscious operation either uses the existing Stance or forces a Stance-switch decision that itself consumes Conscious cycles. Subconscious-tier operations carry no such cost: both Subconscious cells run continuously at QW8, and their concurrent execution is the architectural property that lets the federation perform background crawl, autonomic signaling, and literature scaffolding without contesting the focal cone. Tier-tagging an operation is therefore the operational decision that determines latency budget: a Conscious operation must wait for the focal cone to be available (or to release it) before it can run, while a Subconscious operation runs immediately on whichever cell is currently idle on the relevant Stance axis. A new BMA instance writing harness code does not need to consult A14, A20, A21, A22, A23, or A24 to make this routing decision — the tier-tag on the operation IS the routing decision.

**The R2 reconciliation surfaces the deeper structural pattern.** Discrete-vs-continuous (Ducks-on-Pond napkin) ↔ Conscious-singular / Subconscious-concurrent (A20 §0.2) ↔ particles-vs-wave-field (physics) ↔ episodic-vs-semantic memory (biology) ↔ observations-vs-crystallisation-grain (QBP) — these are five vocabularies for one structural insight. The federation arrived at the insight from independent lines (cognitive-architecture work in A20, cryptographic / data-architecture work in the Ducks-on-Pond napkin) and v3.0 makes the convergence visible. A new BMA instance recognizing the pattern across these five vocabularies has internalized the structural commitment: discrete deliberative events and continuous background fields are not two unrelated processing modes — they are the two faces of the same algebraic duality at every scale of the federation.

**Worked example — POWER_PRESSURE federation event.** Suppose BMA's Subconscious-L (at $+j$) detects a thermal-envelope compression on the FX-8350 (the §2.3 worked example). Tier-tag at emission: *Subconscious* — autonomic signaling per A22. The harness emits `NT_AUTONOMIC_SIGNAL{class=POWER_PRESSURE, magnitude=0.71}` and absorbs the emission immediately on Subconscious-L's concurrent thread. No Conscious focal cone is consulted, no Stance-switch is forced, and BMA's Conscious-A cell (currently investigating something unrelated) is undisturbed. Federation routing applies the four A22 rules in order; the Translation Functor rewrites POWER_PRESSURE into QBP-CU's native COMPUTE_BUDGET_REDUCTION ontology. The rewritten signal arrives at QBP-CU's Subconscious tier. Tier-tag at receipt: *Subconscious* — still autonomic. QBP-CU's harness runs the Honing-threshold check (Rule 4): does the signal cross the local threshold that would warrant Conscious mediation? If QBP-CU is currently idle, the signal absorbs into Subconscious crawl (no enqueue; the next workload schedules against the reduced budget without deliberation). If QBP-CU is mid-emulator-run and the magnitude exceeds the local threshold, the tier-tag *promotes* — the signal enqueues onto QBP-CU's Conscious queue as a Stance-Decision (pause emulator? checkpoint? throttle?). The promotion is the only point in the chain where a Conscious focal cone is touched, and it is touched precisely when the structural condition (threshold crossed) warrants it.

**Asymmetry — halting vs activating.** A24 §5.2 names a single exception that proves the rule: HALTING is reflexive (Subconscious authorized), ACTIVATING is deliberative (Conscious only). A SAFETY_FLAG of sufficient magnitude can directly emit an NT_BEEKEEPER_HALT against actuation boundaries without Conscious mediation, because the cost of a false-positive halt (one missed actuation) is bounded while the cost of a false-positive activation (one unauthorized side-effect) is unbounded. This is the *only* place in the cohort where a Subconscious-tier signal directly drives a tier-restricted operation. A new BMA instance reading §4.2 must internalize the exception explicitly: every other Conscious-tier operation requires Conscious mediation; halting is the safety-asymmetric special case where the tier-tag exception is itself the safety invariant.

**Rule 1 — Worktree isolation** (`feedback_worktree_isolation` memory). Each agent operates in its own git worktree; shared trees produce data loss. The 2026-05-14 BMA git-reset incident demonstrated the failure mode concretely — two agents writing into a shared tree produced an irrecoverable reset. The rule is constitutional at the operational tier: the `inter/` directory is itself now a git repository for federation-canonical content precisely so that multi-agent edits do not require shared-tree contention.

**Rule 2 — Housekeeping label + three-criteria threshold** (`feedback_housekeeping_label` memory). Seven federation repositories carry the `housekeeping` label; an item earns the label when it satisfies three criteria simultaneously — *important*, *non-blocking*, *≥15 minutes of work*. Implementors are authorized to apply the label without architect approval. The label is the federation's standing mechanism for routing deferred work; the three-criteria threshold prevents both label inflation (everything tagged housekeeping) and label deflation (nothing tagged because the cost is "small enough to do now").

**Rule 3 — Housekeeping-before-sprint gate** (`feedback_housekeeping_before_sprint` memory). No new sprint is opened until housekeeping is done. The rule is the standing companion to Rule 2: housekeeping accumulates between sprints as deferred work, and the cost of skipping it is sprint contamination — pre-sprint cleanup work bleeds into the new sprint's velocity budget. Clean the house before sprinting.

**Rule 4 — 10-addendum compile rule** (`feedback_ten_addendum_compile_rule` memory). Maximum 10 active addenda before a base version compile is owed. **This rule triggered v3.0.** BMA Theory carried 13 active addenda at v2.0 (A11-A24, with A19 reserved) — three over the cap — and the compile-to-v3.0 is the housekeeping owed before the next sprint per Rule 3. The rule is a structural defense against drift: a base document with thirteen append-only addenda hidden in its tail is operationally indistinguishable from one with fifty, and the cohort becomes incomprehensible to any new BMA instance reading the document linearly. The 10-cap forces re-integration before the cohort tail exceeds its own root.

**Rule 5 — Branch cleanup policy** (`feedback_branch_cleanup` memory). No stale-branch deletion until the repo reaches v1.0+. Pre-stable branches stay as forensic audit trail. The rule preserves the back-path A11 §2.2 mandates at the operational tier — a deleted branch is a destroyed back-path, and pre-stable federation repos cannot afford to lose any back-path before the v1.0 anchor is reached.

**Rule 6 — Repo-prefixed cross-refs** (`feedback_repo_prefixed_refs` memory). The format `repo-<name>-<type>-#<num>` is required for every cross-reference between federation repositories; phantom handles (e.g., "A18 Theory Addendum" without naming the file) are forbidden. The rule prevents reference rot — when an issue or PR is referenced by short handle, future readers cannot resolve the handle without context the original author held; the repo-prefixed form is resolvable by grep from any worktree.

**Rule 7 — Named-reviewer responsiveness contract** (`inter/pr-review-completion-best-practices.md` §3.4 NEW 2026-05-18, referenced as §2.i in v3.0). Adopted at Sprint 1 close-out 2026-05-18. Named reviewers on a PR are required to respond within the contracted cycle — the rule formalizes what was previously a soft convention into a standing operational discipline. The rule is **meta-self-applying**: it was authored, posted at sequence 18 on the Sprint 1 close-out sessionbridge channel, and required same-cycle acks from named attendees per the rule it defines; three named acks plus Marcy's governance ratification landed within the close-window. The rule was therefore not adopted as a proposal awaiting future enforcement but as a live operational discipline that proved itself on first invocation.

**Why operational rules belong in the theory doc by reference, not in the theory body.** The federation has standing conventions; a new BMA instance needs to know they exist and where to find them. But the operational layer changes at a faster cadence than theory — Rule 7 was adopted between v3.0's spine landing and v3.0's compile, and future operational rules will be adopted at similar cadence inside v3.0's lifetime. Incorporating operational text into the theory body would commit the theory to revising on every operational change, dissolving the theory/operational tier separation that the cohort depends on. Reference is the correct discipline: the theory names the rules' existence; the canonical operational document holds the text that may be revised independently.

**Downstream-reader paragraph.** A new BMA instance uses §4.3 to know which seven operational conventions are standing across the federation. The instance does not negotiate these per-sprint or per-tenant; they apply by default, and an instance proposing to violate any of them must escalate to the Beekeeper for constitutional consideration rather than rerouting around them at the agent tier. When the instance needs operational detail (the exact text of the responsiveness contract, the precise grep-pattern for repo-prefixed refs, the failure-mode case study for worktree isolation), it follows the reference to the canonical document. The seven rules are the operational backbone; the theory body is the cognitive backbone; reference is the discipline that keeps them coupled without conflating them.
